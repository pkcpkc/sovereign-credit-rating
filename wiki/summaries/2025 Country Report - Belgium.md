# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Belgium

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 201 final).
*   **Date of Issue:** June 4, 2025.
*   **Scope:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Belgium.
*   **Key Objective:** Assessment of Belgium's progress toward EU targets, fiscal sustainability, and the transition to a green/digital economy.

## Executive Summary
Belgium demonstrates moderate economic growth with significant structural challenges in labor market participation, fiscal sustainability due to an aging population, and high regulatory complexity. While the country performs well in R&D intensity and digital adoption for SMEs, it faces high labor costs driven by automatic wage indexation and a complex tax-and-benefit system that creates disincentives to work. Key priorities include reforming the pension system, improving the efficiency of long-term care, accelerating the renewable energy rollout, and addressing regional disparities in employment and human capital.

## Key Highlights

### ⚓ Economic & Fiscal Outlook
*   **Growth and Inflation:** GDP growth is projected at 0.8% in 2025. Headline inflation is expected to ease to 2.8%, though it remains above the EU average.
*   **Fiscal Pressure:** The headline deficit is forecast to rise to 5.4% of GDP in 2025. Public debt is projected to reach 107.1% by the end of 2025, driven by aging-related costs and defense spending.
*   **Ageing Costs:** Pension spending is projected to increase by 3.5% of GDP between 2022 and 2070, significantly higher than the EU average.

### ⚓ Labour Market & Education
*   **Employment Gaps:** The employment rate hit a record high of 72.3% in 2024 but remains below the EU average and national targets due to low activity rates among migrants, older workers, and low-skilled individuals.
*   **Skills Mismatch:** Belgium faces one of the highest job vacancy rates in the EU (4.1% in Q4-2024), particularly in STEM and healthcare sectors.
*   **Educational Decline:** There is a concerning long-term decline in basic skills (PISA/TIMSS) and a low share of STEM graduates, which hampers productivity growth.

### ⚓ Innovation & Business Environment
*   **Innovation Leadership:** Belgium is a "strong innovator" with high R&D intensity (3.3% of GDP), yet this has not fully translated into business dynamism due to "zombie firms" and high barriers to entry.
*   **Regulatory Burden:** 81% of Belgian firms report challenges in regulatory compliance, with the country ranking poorly on tax framework complexity.
*   **Investment Barriers:** High energy costs and labor shortages remain the primary obstacles to private investment.

### ⚓ Sustainability & Energy
*   **Climate Targets:** Belgium is currently projected to fall short of its 2030 effort-sharing targets (42.6% reduction vs. 47% target).
*   **Energy Transition:** The electricity-to-gas price ratio remains one of the highest in the EU, hindering the transition to heat pumps and other electric solutions.
*   **Circular Economy:** Belgium performs well in circularity (19.7% rate), but needs significant investment to close the gap in waste management and eco-design.

### ⚓ Social Fairness & Health
*   **Poverty Risk:** The AROPE rate is 18.2%, lower than the EU average, but regional disparities are stark, with Brussels showing much higher risks (37.2%).
*   **Health System:** While life expectancy is above average, the system faces a critical shortage of general practitioners and high out-of-pocket spending (over 20%).
*   **Long-term Care:** Belgium is projected to have some of the highest long-term care spending in the EU by 2070, necessitating more cost-effective community-based services.
