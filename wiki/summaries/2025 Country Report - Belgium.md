# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Belgium

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 201 final).
*   **Publication Date:** June 4, 2025.
*   **Scope:** Comprehensive evaluation of economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies in Belgium.
*   **Key Focus Areas:** Fiscal sustainability, aging-related costs, labor market participation, innovation diffusion, and decarbonization.

## Executive Summary
Belgium's economy experienced moderate growth in 2024 (1%), with a projected deceleration to 0.8% in 2025. While the country maintains high labor productivity and a robust R&I system, it faces significant structural challenges: high labor taxes, significant regional disparities in employment, and a rising fiscal burden driven by aging-related costs—particularly pensions and long-term care. The report highlights a pressing need to reform the tax and benefits system to incentivize work, simplify the regulatory environment for SMEs, and accelerate the transition to a low-carbon economy. Key fiscal measures include a multi-year plan to reduce net expenditure growth and reforms to the pension and unemployment benefit systems to ensure long-term sustainability.

## Key Highlights
*   **Fiscal Pressure & Aging:** Age-related spending is projected to reach 32% of GDP by 2070, substantially higher than the EU average. Pensions and long-term care are the primary drivers of this increase.
*   **Labor Market Disparities:** While the employment rate hit a historic high of 72.3% in 2024, it remains below the EU average due to low activity rates among migrants, older workers, and low-skilled individuals.
*   **Taxation & Work Incentives:** The current tax system creates a "low-wage trap" and high disincentives to work. The federal government plans to limit unemployment benefits to two years and abolish the marital quotient to increase labor participation.
*   **Innovation & Productivity:** Belgium is a "strong innovator" with high R&D intensity (3.3% of GDP), but productivity growth is slowing due to low business dynamism and "zombie firms."
*   **Energy Transition:** Belgium faces high electricity prices relative to gas, hindering the switch to heat pumps. The report calls for a faster roll-out of renewables and the phase-out of fossil fuel subsidies.
*   **Education & Skills:** A sharp decline in basic skills among young people and critical shortages in STEM and ICT graduates threaten the country's ability to meet the demands of the green and digital transitions.
*   **Regional Inequality:** Significant disparities exist between Flanders (high employment, high productivity) and Wallonia/Brussels (higher poverty risks, lower employment rates).
