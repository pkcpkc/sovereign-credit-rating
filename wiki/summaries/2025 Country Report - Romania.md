# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Romania

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 223 final)
*   **Date:** June 4, 2025
*   **Subject:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Romania.
*   **Key Objective:** Assessment of Romania's convergence to EU levels, identification of macroeconomic vulnerabilities, and recommendations for structural reforms.

## Executive Summary
Romania is converging toward EU income and productivity levels, with GDP per capita reaching nearly 80% of the EU average by 2024. However, this progress is hampered by significant structural weaknesses: high fiscal and current account deficits, outdated infrastructure, persistent skills shortages, and low R&D capacity. The report highlights a critical need to transition from a low-cost labor model to an innovation-driven economy. Key challenges include high inflation, a widening trade deficit, and a labor market characterized by low participation rates among women, youth, and the Roma community. To ensure sustainable growth, Romania must accelerate the implementation of its Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), pursue resolute fiscal consolidation, and streamline regulatory processes to improve the business environment.

## Key Highlights

### ⚓ Macroeconomic Vulnerabilities and Fiscal Policy
*   **Growth Deceleration:** Real GDP growth slowed to 0.8% in 2024, primarily due to a high negative contribution from net exports and dampened private investment.
*   **Deficit Concerns:** The public deficit rose to 9.3% of GDP in 2024, driven by generous income policies and public investment spending.
*   **Fiscal Adjustment:** Romania has committed to a medium-term fiscal-structural plan (MTP) targeting a 7% budget deficit in 2025, though net expenditure growth in 2024 exceeded recommended limits.
*   **Debt Dynamics:** Government debt reached nearly 55% of GDP by the end of 2024, significantly below the EU average but rising rapidly.

### ⚓ Labor Market and Human Capital
*   **Participation Gaps:** The employment rate (69.5% in 2024) remains among the lowest in the EU, with significant underrepresentation of women, youth, and Roma people.
*   **Wage vs. Productivity:** Nominal wages rose by 15% in 2024, significantly outstripping productivity growth and eroding external cost competitiveness.
*   **Skills Shortages:** Romania faces severe shortages in both skilled (ICT, STEM) and non-skilled labor, exacerbated by outward migration and a mismatch between education and market needs.
*   **Education Quality:** PISA scores indicate that nearly half of Romanian 15-year-olds lack basic proficiency in mathematics, reading, and science.

### ⚓ Innovation and Business Environment
*   **R&D Underinvestment:** Romania recorded the lowest R&D intensity in the EU (0.52% of GDP), hindering its transition to a high-value-added production model.
*   **Regulatory Burdens:** 90% of firms cite uncertainty as the primary barrier to investment. The business environment is hampered by over-regulation and frequent, unpredictable fiscal changes.
*   **Capital Market Development:** Access to finance remains difficult; companies rely heavily on internal funding (73%), and the stock market is underdeveloped compared to EU peers.

### ⚓ Energy, Decarbonisation, and Sustainability
*   **Energy Affordability:** Romania faces some of the highest electricity prices in the EU (average 103 EUR/MWh in 2024), exacerbated by high taxes and levies on electricity.
*   **Green Transition:** While renewable capacity grew by 14% in 2024, progress is slowed by permitting bottlenecks, grid limitations, and a lack of storage solutions.
*   **Circular Economy:** Romania’s circular material use rate (1.3% in 2023) is the lowest in the EU, requiring substantial investment to meet waste and recycling targets.
*   **Water Resilience:** The country faces significant water stress and pollution issues, with a massive investment gap for wastewater treatment and flood risk management.
