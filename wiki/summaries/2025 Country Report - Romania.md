# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Romania

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 223 final)
*   **Date of Issue:** June 4, 2025
*   **Scope:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Romania.
*   **Key Objectives:** Evaluate convergence toward EU productivity, assess fiscal sustainability, monitor labor market participation, and track progress on the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP).

## Executive Summary
Romania is showing steady convergence toward EU income and productivity levels (reaching nearly 80% of the EU average in 2024). However, this progress is threatened by significant macroeconomic vulnerabilities, including high fiscal and current account deficits, and structural weaknesses such as outdated infrastructure, persistent skills shortages, and low R&D capacity. The report highlights a critical need to transition from a low-cost labor model to an innovation-driven economy. Key challenges include the high tax burden on electricity, a lagging digital transformation in both businesses and public services, and a struggling education system that fails to provide sufficient STEM and basic skills. While the government has committed to a Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP), the report notes delays in implementing key tax reforms and pension adjustments.

## Key Highlights

### ⚓ Economic Growth & Fiscal Policy
*   **Slowdown in Growth:** Real GDP growth decelerated to 0.8% in 2024 (down from 2.4% in 2023) due to weak net exports and high inflation pressures.
*   **Fiscal Deficit:** The public deficit reached 9.3% of GDP in 2024, driven by high wage and pension hikes.
*   **Fiscal Strategy:** Romania's MTP aims to cap net expenditure growth at 5.1% in 2025, though current 2024-2025 cumulative growth is projected to exceed recommended limits.
*   **Debt Sustainability:** Government debt rose to nearly 55% of GDP by the end of 2024, well below the EU average but rising rapidly.

### ⚓ Labor Market & Education
*   **Employment Disparities:** The employment rate stands at 69.5%, hampered by low participation among women, young people, and the Roma community.
*   **Wage Dynamics:** Nominal wages rose by 15% in 2024, significantly outpacing productivity growth and eroding external cost competitiveness.
*   **Skills Gap:** Romania faces a massive shortage of skilled workers, particularly in ICT and manufacturing, exacerbated by low tertiary education attainment (23.2% of the 25-34 age group).
*   **Education Reform:** PISA results show high rates of underachievement in mathematics, reading, and science among 15-year-olds.

### ⚓ Innovation & Digitalization
*   **R&D Underinvestment:** Romania has the lowest R&D intensity in the EU (0.52% of GDP), far below the target of 2% by 2027.
*   **Digital Lag:** Only 27.7% of the population has basic digital skills (vs. EU average of 55.6%). AI adoption in businesses is the lowest in the EU at just over 3%.
*   **Capital Markets:** Romania's capital markets are underdeveloped, with firms heavily reliant on internal funding (73%) and trade credit.

### ⚓ Energy & Sustainability
*   **Energy Affordability:** Romania has some of the highest electricity prices in the EU, partly due to a fiscal burden that taxes electricity more heavily than gas.
*   **Decarbonization:** Renewables reached 48% of electricity generation in 2024, but the transition is slowed by regulatory barriers and grid limitations.
*   **Circular Economy:** Romania has the lowest circular material use rate in the EU (1.3%), highlighting a need for massive investment in waste management and recycling.

### ⚓ Social Fairness & Infrastructure
*   **Poverty Risks:** The AROPE rate is high at 27.9%, with significant rural-urban disparities. In-work poverty remains nearly double the EU average.
*   **Housing Crisis:** Romania faces severe housing deprivation and overcrowding, particularly in rural areas.
*   **Infrastructure Gaps:** Transport networks are among the least dense in the EU, and railway infrastructure remains largely unmodernized.
