# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Romania

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Country Report - Romania (SWD(2025) 223 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2024 Data Analysis and 2025-2026 Projections
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Commission Staff Working Document / Country Report

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Country Report for Romania highlights a critical juncture for the economy, characterized by converging living standards but growing macroeconomic vulnerabilities. While Romania has successfully raised its GDP per capita to nearly 80% of the EU average, this growth has been driven largely by domestic demand, loose fiscal policy, and wage increases, particularly in the public sector. Consequently, the country faces a deteriorating external position with a current account deficit widening to 8.4% of GDP in 2024, and a surging government deficit reaching 9.3% of GDP. Public debt increased rapidly to 54.8% of GDP, raising concerns about debt sustainability despite remaining below the EU average.

The report identifies structural weaknesses as key barriers to a new growth model based on innovation and productivity. These include outdated infrastructure, low R&D intensity, persistent skills shortages, and a challenging business environment marked by regulatory uncertainty, legislative instability, and perceived corruption. Implementation of EU-funded reforms, particularly under the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), is significantly delayed due to administrative bottlenecks and political instability.

Fiscal consolidation is the central pillar of the government's strategy, embodied in the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP) endorsed by the Council, which extends the adjustment period to 2031. Immediate measures include wage and pension freezes and tax reforms. However, the report warns that without resolute fiscal consolidation and structural reforms, Romania remains vulnerable to external shocks and shifts in investor sentiment. Social challenges, including high poverty rates, low labor force participation among women and youth, and inadequate healthcare and education systems, further complicate the path to inclusive growth.

## Key Highlights

- **Macroeconomic Imbalances and Fiscal Deficit**: Romania’s real GDP growth slowed to 0.8% in 2024, driven by strong private consumption but dampened by weak net exports and a slowdown in private investment. The government deficit surged to 9.3% of GDP, primarily due to generous public sector wage increases and higher public investment spending. Net expenditure growth reached 19.9% in 2024, far exceeding the recommended maximums under the reformed EU economic governance framework.

- **External Vulnerabilities**: The current account deficit widened to 8.4% of GDP in 2024, reflecting high import demand driven by fiscal expansion and weak external competitiveness. Romania remains a net borrower from the rest of the world, with a negative net international investment position of 41.5% of GDP. High energy prices and currency stability measures have contributed to external pressures.

- **Fiscal Consolidation Plan**: The Council endorsed Romania’s Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP) in January 2025, committing to a seven-year adjustment period (2025-2031). Key immediate measures adopted in late 2024 include freezing public wages and pensions, removing tax exemptions, and reforming the microenterprise tax regime to save 2.0% of GDP in 2025. The government targets a deficit of 7% of GDP in 2025, with a gradual reduction thereafter.

- **Structural Reform Delays**: Implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) is significantly delayed, with only 14% of milestones and targets fulfilled. Key bottlenecks include procurement delays, political instability, and weak administrative capacity. Critical reforms, such as the comprehensive tax framework review and special pension reform, have not been fully implemented or have been partially struck down by the Constitutional Court.

- **Business Environment and Investment Climate**: Regulatory uncertainty and legislative instability are cited by 90% of Romanian firms as the top barrier to long-term investment. The fiscal code has been amended hundreds of times since 2015, creating unpredictability. Foreign direct investment (FDI) flows have declined. The business environment is further hampered by over-regulation, weak public administration, and perceived corruption.

- **Labor Market and Skills Shortages**: Despite an improved employment rate of 69.5% in 2024, Romania suffers from low labor force participation, particularly among women, youth, and low-skilled workers. There are severe skills mismatches, with high demand for STEM and digital skills not met by the education system. Early school leaving remains high, especially in rural areas, and vocational education and training (VET) relevance to the labor market is weak.

- **Energy Transition and Affordability**: Romania made progress in renewable energy deployment, with renewables accounting for 48% of electricity generation in 2024. However, energy prices remain high due to fossil fuel dependence and grid limitations. The country faces challenges in decarbonizing industry and phasing out fossil fuel subsidies. Energy affordability is a concern for households and industry, exacerbated by a tax burden skewed towards electricity.

- **Social Inequality and Poverty**: Romania continues to face high rates of poverty and social exclusion, with the AROPE rate at 27.9% in 2024. Vulnerable groups, including the Roma population, people with disabilities, and those in rural areas, are disproportionately affected. Access to quality healthcare, long-term care, and education remains unequal, with significant disparities between urban and rural areas.

- **Innovation and R&D Weakness**: Romania has the lowest R&D intensity in the EU at 0.52% of GDP. The innovation system is fragmented, with weak public-private collaboration and low business innovation activity. The lack of a dedicated national innovation agency and underdeveloped venture capital market further hinder the transition to a knowledge-based economy.

- **Infrastructure Deficits**: Transport infrastructure remains a bottleneck, with Romania having one of the shortest and least dense motorway networks in the EU. Railway infrastructure is aging and slow. Digital infrastructure is improving, but digital skills and business digitalization lag behind the EU average.

- **Environmental and Climate Risks**: Romania is highly exposed to climate-related risks, including floods and droughts. Water management and wastewater treatment compliance remain poor. The circular economy transition is in early stages, with low material recycling rates. The country is projected to meet its 2030 effort-sharing targets if planned measures are implemented.

- **Regional Disparities**: Significant disparities exist between the Bucharest-Ilfov region and less developed regions in terms of GDP per capita, productivity, education, and access to services. These disparities are exacerbated by population decline and emigration in less developed areas.
