# Summary of: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation: Austria

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF Staff Report for the 2025 Article IV Consultation with Austria
- **Effective Period**: July 2025 (Consultation concluded July 3, 2025)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Staff Report & Press Release

## Executive Summary

The IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation with Austria highlights a challenging economic environment characterized by two successive years of recession (2023 and 2024), driven by the energy price shock and subsequent Euro Area monetary tightening. Growth is expected to remain weak in 2025 (-0.1%) due to fiscal consolidation and global trade uncertainty, with a modest recovery anticipated in 2026. Inflation remains above the Euro Area average (3.2% in 2025) due to sticky services inflation and the expiration of energy support measures, though it is projected to converge toward 2% by 2026.

Fiscally, the deficit widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, pushing public debt to 81.2%. The new coalition government, formed in March 2025, has announced near-term consolidation measures for 2025-2026. However, the IMF emphasizes that substantial further fiscal adjustment is required over the medium term to stabilize the debt ratio against rising pressures from demographic aging, defense spending, and the green transition. The IMF recommends reducing the deficit to below 2% of GDP through pension reforms, healthcare efficiency, and tax mix improvements (e.g., property and carbon taxes).

Structurally, Austria faces headwinds from sluggish productivity and demographic decline. The IMF urges reforms to boost labor supply (particularly among women and the elderly) and reduce regulatory barriers in services and housing. Financially, the banking sector remains well-capitalized, but risks persist from Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposures and the upcoming expiration of borrower-based mortgage lending measures in July 2025. The IMF advises making these lending standards permanent and maintaining vigilance on CRE data gaps.

## Key Highlights

- **Weak Growth and Recessionary Context**: Austria experienced a 1.3% GDP contraction in 2024, following a 0.9% drop in 2023. Growth is projected at -0.1% for 2025, driven by weak domestic demand, fiscal tightening, and global trade policy uncertainty. A return to growth (0.8%) is expected in 2026 as investment and consumption recover.
- **Inflation Divergence and Convergence**: Headline inflation averaged 3.2% in 2025, exceeding the Euro Area average, primarily due to core services inflation and one-off energy price effects. The IMF projects inflation will gradually decline to near 2% by end-2026, contingent on disinflationary fiscal policies and wage moderation.
- **Fiscal Deterioration and Medium-Term Risks**: The fiscal deficit widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, and public debt rose to 81.2%. While near-term measures aim to reduce the deficit to 4.3% in 2025, the IMF stresses the need for a "bold" medium-term adjustment to bring the deficit below 2% of GDP. This is necessary to offset aging costs (estimated at ~2% of GDP by 2030), increased defense spending, and green transition investments.
- **Structural Reform Priorities**: To counteract demographic aging and low productivity, the IMF recommends boosting labor supply by increasing female and elderly participation rates, which could offset more than 20 years of demographic drag. Additionally, reducing regulatory barriers in services, speeding up renewable energy permits, and easing land-use regulations for housing are critical for growth.
- **Financial Sector Stability and CRE Risks**: Austrian banks are well-capitalized (CET1 ratio of 17.7%) and profitable, but face rising Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) in the Commercial Real Estate (CRE) sector. The IMF notes that borrower-based measures for residential mortgages expire in July 2025 and recommends enacting legislation to make these prudent lending standards permanent to prevent a resurgence of macro-financial risks.
- **External Position and Competitiveness**: Austria’s external position is broadly in line with fundamentals, with a current account surplus of 2.4% of GDP in 2024. However, the IMF warns that persistent inflation differentials with the Euro Area could undermine competitiveness over time if productivity-adjusted wage growth exceeds the Euro Area average.
- **New Government Policy Direction**: Following September 2024 elections, a new coalition government (ÖVP-SPÖ-NEOS) took office in March 2025. They have committed to fiscal consolidation, pension reforms, and expanding childcare to support female labor supply, aligning broadly with IMF recommendations, though details on medium-term spending restraint remain under development.
