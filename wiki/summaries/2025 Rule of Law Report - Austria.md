# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Austria

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 920 final
*   **Reporting Period:** 2024–2025
*   **Jurisdiction:** Austria
*   **Key Authorities:** European Commission, Austrian Ministry of Justice, Court of Audit (ACA), Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK).

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Austria highlights a high level of perceived judicial independence among the public and businesses, alongside a generally efficient justice system. However, significant structural challenges remain regarding the autonomy of the prosecution and the appointment processes for administrative court leadership. While the government has committed to establishing an independent Federal Prosecution Office and improving transparency for Members of Parliament, progress has been categorized as limited. The report notes advancements in the digitalization of justice and a positive evolution in the tax framework for civil society organizations. In the media sector, while independence of regulators is maintained, the economic viability of private media is deteriorating. Anti-corruption efforts continue with the implementation of the 2023-2025 National Anti-Corruption Strategy, though high-level corruption investigations still face intense public and media scrutiny.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System and Judicial Independence
*   **Judicial Perception:** 86% of the general public perceive judicial independence as "fairly or very good," though company perception has slightly declined compared to 2024.
*   **Administrative Appointments:** No progress was made on involving the judiciary in the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents, raising concerns regarding European standards.
*   **Prosecutorial Reform:** Limited progress on establishing an independent Federal Prosecution Office. While the 2025 Government programme commits to this, the current system still allows the Minister of Justice to instruct prosecutors in individual cases.
*   **Digitalisation:** The "Justice 3.0" initiative aims for 90% of new cases to be managed digitally by the end of 2025; compulsory online publication of judgments was expanded to Higher Regional Courts in January 2025.
*   **Legal Aid:** The Constitutional Court ruled that restricting legal aid in the Administrative Procedural Code was unconstitutional; these provisions are expected to be suspended by March 31, 2026.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **National Strategy:** The National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2023-2025 is active, with the first evaluation of action plans planned for June 2025.
*   **High-Level Investigations:** Ongoing investigations into high-level corruption continue, though prosecutors face intense public scrutiny and practical challenges regarding security clearances.
*   **Transparency Measures:** Work continues on an asset declaration system for Ministers and a Code of Conduct for Cabinet staff. However, progress on rules for Members of Parliament remains limited.
*   **Lobbying & Revolving Doors:** The lobbying framework remains limited in scope (dating back to 2013). Post-employment "revolving door" restrictions remain narrow, though a new three-year cooling-off period for certain judicial roles was introduced in 2024.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Public Service Media:** Following a Constitutional Court ruling, the ORF Act was reformed in March 2025 to increase the independence of its management bodies.
*   **Economic Pressures:** The private media sector faces a deteriorating economic situation due to a widening revenue gap between online platforms and traditional media.
*   **State Advertising:** There is limited progress on ensuring the fair distribution of state advertising, with concerns raised regarding the concentration of funding in the tabloid press.
*   **Freedom of Information:** The Freedom of Information Act is set to enter into force in September 2025, providing a subjective right to information from public authorities.

### IV. Institutional Checks and Balances
*   **Ombudsman Board:** The Board functions effectively as the National Human Rights Institution; the government plans to review its nomination procedure to ensure greater transparency.
*   **Investment Protection:** 72% of companies express confidence in the legal protection of investments in Austria.
*   **ECHR Judgments:** As of June 2025, the number of leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights pending implementation decreased to 4.
*   **Civil Society:** The tax framework for non-profit organizations has shown positive results, extending tax-exempt status for donations and clarifying advocacy-related work.
