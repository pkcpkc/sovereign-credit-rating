# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Italy

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission (SWD(2025) 212 final)
*   **Date:** June 4, 2025
*   **Subject:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Italy.
*   **Key Frameworks:** Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), REPowerEU, Stability and Growth Pact.

## Executive Summary
Italy's economy shows moderate growth (0.7% in 2024) supported by RRP investments, despite global uncertainties and high public debt. While the primary budget balance turned positive in 2024 (first since 2019), the debt-to-GDP ratio is trending upward due to past tax credits and high interest costs. Key structural challenges include low productivity growth, significant skills mismatches, regional disparities (particularly in the Mezzogiorno), and high exposure to climate risks. The report highlights progress in justice and public administration reforms but stresses the need for deeper industrial strategy, R&D investment, and improved social protection for non-standard workers.

## Key Highlights

### ⚓ Economic Growth & Fiscal Policy
*   **Growth Dynamics:** Real GDP grew by 0.7% in 2024. Growth is expected to continue slowly through 2026, driven by household consumption and corporate investment.
*   **Debt & Deficit:** The government deficit dropped significantly to 3.4% of GDP in 2024 (from 7.2% in 2023). However, the debt-to-GDP ratio is projected to rise to 138.2% by 2026 due to borrowing needs for past housing renovation tax credits.
*   **Primary Balance:** Italy achieved its first primary surplus since 2019 (0.4% of GDP) in 2024.

### ⚓ Productivity & Innovation
*   **Innovation Gap:** Italy’s R&D expenditure (1.31% of GDP) is significantly lower than the EU average (2.24%).
*   **Capital Markets:** The Italian stock exchange remains small; market capitalization is only 28.5% of GDP compared to the EU average of 67%.
*   **Venture Capital:** While growing, Italy's VC market remains fragmented and underfunded for late-stage start-up scaling.

### ⚓ Labour Market & Social Fairness
*   **Employment Trends:** Employment rates reached record highs in 2024, but remain below EU averages.
*   **Skills Mismatch:** Italy has one of the highest macroeconomic skills mismatches in the EU, particularly impacting manufacturing and high-tech sectors.
*   **Gender Gap:** Female labor participation is hindered by insufficient childcare and long-term care (LTC) services, especially in Southern regions.
*   **In-Work Poverty:** Risk of poverty for part-time and fixed-term workers remains high (10.3% in 2024).

### ⚓ Sustainability & Energy
*   **Clean Tech Leadership:** Italy is a leader in solar PV and wind manufacturing but lags in the adoption of electric vehicles (4.2% of new sales vs 14.5% EU average).
*   **Energy Prices:** Wholesale electricity prices in Italy remain significantly higher than in peer countries (e.g., 60% higher than Germany) due to high gas-dependence and taxes.
*   **Climate Risks:** Italy is highly exposed to hydrogeological risks and extreme weather, with an annual infrastructure cost of approx. €2 billion by 2030.

### ⚓ Regional Development
*   **Mezzogiorno Divide:** Significant disparities persist between Northern and Southern Italy. The South faces lower GDP per head (65% of EU average) and higher population decline due to "brain drain."
*   **Infrastructure:** Southern regions suffer from lower accessibility to transport networks and lower-quality water services.
