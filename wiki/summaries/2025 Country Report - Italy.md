# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Italy

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 212 final).
*   **Date:** June 4, 2025.
*   **Scope:** Comprehensive economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policy review for Italy.
*   **Key Frameworks:** Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), REPowerEU, and the National Adaptation Plan (NAP).

## Executive Summary
Italy's economy is characterized by moderate growth and stabilizing inflation, supported significantly by the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP). While the primary budget balance has turned positive, high public debt and weak productivity remain core vulnerabilities. Structural challenges include a significant skills mismatch, regional disparities (particularly the North-South divide), and a high tax burden on labor. The report highlights Italy's leadership in clean technology manufacturing while noting its heavy reliance on imported critical raw materials. Key policy recommendations focus on accelerating RRP implementation, simplifying the business environment, boosting R&D investment, and addressing climate-related risks through infrastructure resilience and insurance coverage.

## Key Highlights

*   **# Economic Growth and Inflation:** Real GDP growth stabilized at 0.7% in 2024. Growth is expected to remain slow but steady through 2026, driven by household consumption and RRP-funded corporate investment. Inflation is forecast to settle near 2% in 2025-2026.
*   **# Fiscal Position and Debt:** Italy achieved its first primary surplus since 2019 (0.4% of GDP in 2024). However, the public debt-to-GDP ratio is on an upward trend due to lagged fiscal impacts of previous housing tax credits and high debt-servicing costs.
*   **# Labor Market and Productivity:** Employment remains robust, but participation rates for women and youth are below EU averages. A "skills mismatch" persists, with a high vacancy rate (2%) and a lack of high-level technical skills. Real wage growth is recovering but only partially offsets past losses.
*   **# Innovation and Business Environment:** Italy's R&D expenditure (1.31% of GDP) is significantly below the EU average (2.22%). The business environment is hampered by bureaucracy, high energy costs, and a lack of venture capital depth.
*   **# Energy and Decarbonization:** Italy is a leader in solar PV and wind manufacturing but faces high electricity prices due to gas-fired generation and taxes. The country is highly vulnerable to supply chain disruptions for critical raw materials (48% of manufacturing inputs are imported).
*   **# Climate Risk and Adaptation:** Italy is among the most exposed EU nations to climate risks (floods, droughts, heatwaves). Economic losses from extreme events reached nearly €134 billion between 1980-2023, yet only 4% of these losses were insured.
*   **# Social Fairness and Education:** Poverty risks remain high for low-qualified workers and non-EU citizens. The education system faces challenges in basic skills proficiency (PISA) and a persistent "brain drain" of young tertiary graduates moving abroad.
*   **# Regional Disparities:** Significant gaps persist between the North and the Mezzogiorno (South). Southern regions suffer from lower investment, higher unemployment, and poorer access to childcare and healthcare.
