# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Malta

### Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 918 final)
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
*   **Subject:** Rule of Law situation in Malta (Justice, Anti-Corruption, Media Freedom, and Institutional Checks)

### Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Malta highlights a landscape of mixed progress. While perceived judicial independence remains high among the public and corporations, significant challenges persist regarding the efficiency of the justice system, particularly the length of legal proceedings. Key achievements include the completion of the transfer of serious prosecutions to the Office of the Attorney General and the introduction of constitutional reforms to involve the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice. However, the report notes a lack of progress in establishing a National Human Rights Institution, enhancing public service media independence, and improving the working environment for journalists. Furthermore, the European Court of Justice recently ruled that Malta's investor citizenship scheme is contrary to EU law.

### Key Highlights



*   **Judicial Independence and Reform** ⚓ `justice_reform`
    *   Perceived judicial independence is high (69% of the general public, 63% of companies).
    *   A comprehensive constitutional reform was tabled on May 13, 2025, to involve the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice.
    *   The Government is proposing to remove executive involvement in the appointment of members of specialized tribunals.

*   **Prosecutorial and Judicial Efficiency** ⚓ `judicial_efficiency`
    *   The transfer of non-summary case prosecutions from the Police to the Office of the Attorney General was completed in September 2024.
    *   Despite recruitment efforts, Malta's judge-to-inhabitant ratio remains among the lowest in the EU.
    *   Disposition times for civil, criminal, and administrative cases remain high, with significant backlogs reported.

*   **Anti-Corruption Framework** ⚓ `anti_corruption`
    *   The 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index scores Malta 46/100 (ranking 24th in the EU).
    *   Progress has been made in high-level corruption investigations, but a "robust track record of final judgments" is still lacking.
    *   The Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC) remains under-resourced, with only three part-time commissioners.

*   **Media Freedom and Journalists' Rights** ⚓ `media_freedom`
    *   While a law on cross-border strategic lawsuits (anti-SLAPP) was enacted in July 2024, no additional safeguards for journalists' working conditions have been implemented.
    *   The Media Pluralism Monitor (MPM) maintains a "very high risk" score for the independence of public service media.
    *   Efforts to improve access to official documents remain limited.

*   **Institutional Checks and Balances** ⚓ `institutional_checks`
    *   The European Court of Justice ruled on April 29, 2025, that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme is illegal under EU law.
    *   A controversial legislative reform (Bill 125) increased the threshold for citizens to petition for magisterial inquiries, drawing criticism from civil society.
    *   Draft legislation for a National Human Rights Institution and a regulatory framework for civil society is still under preparation.
