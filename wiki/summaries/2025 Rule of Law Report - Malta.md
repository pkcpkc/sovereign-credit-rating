# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Malta

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 918 final).
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025.
*   **Geographic Focus:** Malta.
*   **Key Stakeholders:** Office of the Attorney General, Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC), Public Services Commission (PSC), and various civil society organizations (Repubblika, Aditus Foundation, Daphne Caruana Galizia Foundation).

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Malta highlights a complex landscape of progress and stagnation. While perceived judicial independence remains high among the public and corporations, significant systemic challenges persist regarding the efficiency of the justice system and the transparency of anti-corruption efforts. Notable achievements include the completion of prosecution transfers to the Attorney General and ongoing constitutional reforms to involve the judiciary in Chief Justice appointments. However, the report identifies critical gaps: a lack of progress in establishing a National Human Rights Institution, stagnant improvements in public service media independence, and a controversial legislative reform that increases the threshold for citizens to petition magisterial inquiries. Furthermore, the European Court of Justice recently ruled against Malta's investor citizenship scheme, and concerns remain regarding the slow pace of high-level corruption investigations and the "narrowed" civic space for civil society.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System & Efficiency
*   **Judicial Independence:** Perceived independence remains high (69% of the general public; 63% of companies). Constitutional reforms are underway to include the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice.
*   **Prosecutorial Reform:** The transfer of non-summary case prosecutions from the Police to the Office of the Attorney General was finalized in September 2024.
*   **Resource Shortages:** Despite recruitment efforts, Malta still has one of the lowest numbers of judges per inhabitant in the EU, leading to significant delays in civil, commercial, and administrative cases.
*   **Digitalization:** Efforts are underway to launch a Court Information Management System and new case management systems for the Attorney General and State Advocate.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Public Perception:** Corruption remains high in public perception; 83% of respondents consider it widespread in Malta.
*   **Investigation Progress:** While human resources for the Financial Crimes Investigation Department (FCID) and the Attorney General's office have increased, a robust track record of final judgments in high-level corruption cases is still lacking.
*   **PCAC Capacity:** The Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC) is noted for having insufficient specialized staff and lacking a functional case-management system.
*   **Integrity Measures:** Efforts continue for public service and police integrity, but action is still required to improve measures for high-level politicians and officials.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Regulator Competences:** Legislation is being prepared to align the Broadcasting Authority with the European Media Freedom Act, though the Media Pluralism Monitor has raised risk levels to "medium-high."
*   **Public Service Media:** No progress has been made in enhancing the editorial independence of public service media, which continues to be flagged as a "very high risk" area.
*   **Journalist Protection:** While a law on cross-border strategic lawsuits (SLAPP) was enacted in July 2024, critics argue it lacks sufficient protections for domestic lawsuits and that the overarching media environment remains challenging.

### IV. Institutional Checks and Balances
*   **Public Participation:** The government intends to establish a Public Consultation Department by June 2025 to create a formal framework for public engagement in legislation.
*   **Human Rights Institution:** Draft legislation for a National Human Rights Institution (aligned with UN Paris Principles) is expected to be submitted to Parliament by June 2025.
*   **Magisterial Inquiries:** A controversial reform (Bill 125) was approved in April 2025, increasing the threshold for citizens to petition inquiries—a move criticized by stakeholders as potentially hindering the prosecution of high-level offences.
*   **ECtHR Judgments:** Malta continues to have a significant backlog of leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights pending implementation, with some dating back over 16 years.
