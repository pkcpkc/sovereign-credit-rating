# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report France

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission (Staff Working Document)
*   **Reference:** SWD(2025) 910 final / COM(2025) 900 final
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
*   **Subject:** Evaluation of the rule of law situation in France, focusing on justice, anti-corruption, media freedom, and institutional checks and balances.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for France highlights a period of significant legislative reform and administrative reorganization. Key developments include the implementation of a 2023 law reforming magistrate status, intensified efforts to digitalize judicial procedures (notably via the *Portalis* tool), and a robust expansion of anti-corruption frameworks. While judicial independence remains stable despite public and political pressure, the report notes persistent challenges regarding the efficiency of the justice system—particularly in bribery cases—and the practical implementation of the right to access official documents. Media freedom is marked by progress in ownership transparency but remains under scrutiny regarding public broadcasting governance and the practical barriers faced by journalists.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System
*   **Judicial Independence:** Perceived independence remains "average" (51% public, 53% companies). The Superior Council of Magistracy (CSM) has actively defended judges against personal attacks following high-profile decisions.
*   **Magistracy Reform:** Implementation of the 2023 law is underway, removing age limits for admissions and creating a 360° evaluation process for court presidents.
*   **Digitalisation & Access:** The *Portalis* tool is expanding to all labor and local courts. Online access to over one million decisions was achieved by late 2024, with a target for all criminal court decisions by the end of 2025.
*   **Efficiency Challenges:** Civil and commercial cases average 354 days to resolve. Notably, bribery cases at the first instance have the highest resolution time in the EU (approx. 3.8 years).

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **National Strategy:** A new 2024-2029 national anti-corruption plan is expected by summer 2025, focusing on organized drug crime and municipal integrity.
*   **Institutional Strengthening:** The Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) was reorganized into public and private sector departments. The High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP) saw a 48% increase in asset declarations examined in 2024.
*   **Prosecution Efforts:** Increased human resources were allocated to the National Financial Prosecutor (PNF) and OCLCIFF. New legislation (Law Warsmann) was passed to improve the seizure of criminal assets.
*   **Lobbying & Ethics:** While lobbying transparency has improved, disclosure requirements still do not apply to top-executive level officials. New ethics codes were adopted for MPs and the Presidency.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Regulatory Evolution:** ARCOM is expanding its scope to include the Digital Services Act (DSA) mandates and the preparation of a national media ownership database.
*   **Public Broadcasting:** Debate continues over the creation of "France Médias," a holding company to oversee public broadcasters. Funding has shifted from a license fee to a VAT-based mechanism.
*   **Right to Information:** Despite strong legal frameworks, journalists report that the "disproportionate burden" exception is being used to complicate access to administrative documents.
*   **Journalist Safety:** The report notes 50 alerts (harassment, physical attacks, legal pressure) on the Council of Europe's platform.

### IV. Checks and Balances
*   **Legislative Simplification:** Businesses welcome economic simplification, but stakeholders warn against the reduction of public consultation processes.
*   **ECtHR Compliance:** As of June 2025, 22 leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights remained pending implementation, with one case dating back 15 years.
*   **Artificial Intelligence:** The Government and the Court of Cassation are actively studying safeguards for AI use in drafting administrative decisions and judicial tasks.
*   **Civil Society:** The civic space is rated as "narrowed" by CIVICUS, with concerns over funding reductions and the protection of human rights defenders.
