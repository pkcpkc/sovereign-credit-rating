# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Hungary

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission (SWD(2025) 217 final)
*   **Date:** June 4, 2025
*   **Subject:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Hungary.

## Executive Summary
Hungary's economy is experiencing a slow rebound characterized by sluggish GDP growth (0.8% projected for 2025) and persistent high inflation. While the country has made strides in employment rates, it faces significant structural vulnerabilities including low labor productivity, a high debt-to-GDP ratio (projected at 74.5% in 2025), and an unstable business environment marked by regulatory volatility and state intervention. Key challenges include a skills shortage, high energy costs, and a lack of effective anti-corruption frameworks. The report highlights that while Hungary is a leader in EV battery manufacturing, it struggles with R&D investment, social inequality, and the need for a more sustainable fiscal framework to ensure medium-term stability.

## Key Highlights
*   **Economic Growth and Inflation:** GDP growth was subdued in 2024 (0.5%) due to weak external demand and low investment. Inflation is expected to remain elevated in 2025 due to underlying price pressures and high food costs.
*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Public debt is projected to rise to 74.5% of GDP by the end of 2025. The government faces high debt-servicing costs and a budgetary framework that lacks transparency and multiannual spending ceilings.
*   **Business Environment and Competition:** Hungary ranks among the last in the EU for competitiveness. Barriers include high regulatory volatility, state-led market distortions, and a lack of equal treatment for companies. The retail sector is particularly burdened by high taxes and administrative intervention.
*   **Productivity and Skills:** Labour productivity remains stagnant at levels seen 15 years ago. A severe shortage of skilled workers, particularly in STEM and digital fields, hampers innovation and high-value investment.
*   **Energy and Decarbonisation:** Hungary remains heavily dependent on Russian fossil fuels. While solar generation has surged, the lack of grid flexibility and high energy prices for firms hinder the green transition and industrial competitiveness.
*   **Social Fairness and Poverty:** Poverty rates are rising, with significant disparities affecting Roma communities, persons with disabilities, and children. The social protection system is underfunded, and the minimum income remains one of the lowest in the EU.
*   **Healthcare and Education:** The health system is hospital-centric with low spending on prevention and a shortage of professionals. In education, early school leaving remains high, and there is a significant gap in basic skills among disadvantaged students.
*   **RRP Implementation:** Hungary has fulfilled 0% of the milestones and targets in its Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), necessitating urgent acceleration to meet 2026 deadlines.
