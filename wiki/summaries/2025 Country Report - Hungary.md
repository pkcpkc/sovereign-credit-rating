# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Hungary

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission (SWD(2025) 217 final / COM(2025) 217 final)
*   **Date of Issue:** June 4, 2025
*   **Subject:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Hungary.
*   **Key Focus Areas:** Fiscal sustainability, investment barriers, labour productivity, energy transition, and social fairness.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Country Report highlights a period of slow economic recovery for Hungary, characterized by high inflation and significant structural vulnerabilities. While GDP growth is projected to pick up in 2026, the country faces persistent challenges regarding high public debt, low labour productivity, and a business environment hampered by regulatory volatility and state intervention. Key fiscal concerns include an expansionary budgetary bias, elevated debt-servicing costs, and a lack of transparent multiannual spending ceilings. Structurally, Hungary suffers from a shortage of skilled labour and a low R&D intensity. The report also underscores critical environmental risks, including high dependence on Russian fossil fuels and a need for improved water resilience and circular economy practices. Socially, while employment remains high, poverty risks are rising among vulnerable groups, and housing affordability is deteriorating rapidly.

## Key Highlights

### $\text{⚓}$ Economic & Fiscal Outlook
*   **Growth & Inflation:** GDP grew by 0.5% in 2024; growth is forecast to reach 0.8% in 2025 and 2.5% in 2026. Inflation remains high due to underlying price pressures and rising food costs.
*   **Debt Sustainability:** Public debt reached 73.5% of GDP in 2024. Debt-servicing costs are among the highest in the EU, and the implicit interest rate on government debt is near 6% in 2025.
*   **Budgetary Framework:** Hungary's fiscal framework lacks transparency and reliability due to frequent revisions and the lack of multiannual spending ceilings.
*   **Fiscal Risks:** Net expenditure growth in 2025 is forecast at 6.1%, exceeding Council recommendations, though it remains within cumulative limits due to a national escape clause for defence spending.

### $\text{⚓}$ Business Environment & Innovation
*   **Regulatory Volatility:** A surge in decrees over laws and frequent legislative amendments create an unpredictable environment for long-term business planning.
*   **Market Distortions:** The State is active in business transactions (e.g., Budapest airport, telecommunications), and the use of subsidized loans and framework agreements limits competition.
*   **Innovation Stagnation:** R&D intensity remains well below the EU average (1.4% vs 2.2%). Business R&D is concentrated in a few large foreign-owned firms.
*   **Financing Gaps:** Venture capital investment has dropped significantly, and the stock market capitalisation remains underdeveloped compared to the EU average.

### $\text{⚓}$ Energy & Decarbonisation
*   **Energy Dependence:** Hungary remains heavily dependent on Russian fossil fuels (over 70% of natural gas and 80% of crude oil in 2024).
*   **Solar Leadership:** Hungary has the highest share of solar production in the EU (25%), but lacks sufficient grid flexibility and storage infrastructure.
*   **Fossil Fuel Subsidies:** Hungary maintains one of the highest figures in the EU for fossil fuel subsidies (1.01% of GDP), which are not planned to be phased out before 2030.
*   **Water & Circularity:** Hungary faces severe water scarcity and pollution issues; its circular material use rate (5.9%) is significantly below the EU average (11.8%).

### $\text{⚓}$ Labour & Social Fairness
*   **Skills Shortage:** A shortage of skilled workers, particularly in STEM and the green transition, hampers growth. Tertiary education attainment is one of the lowest in the EU (32.3%).
*   **Wage Dynamics:** Nominal wages saw rapid growth in 2022–2024, but rising unit labour costs (ULCs) may undermine competitiveness if not matched by productivity.
*   **Poverty Trends:** Poverty and social exclusion risks are rising, particularly among children, the Roma population, and persons with disabilities.
*   **Housing Crisis:** House prices rose 230% between 2010 and 2024, the highest in the EU, leading to significant housing cost overburdens for low-income households.
*   **Health System:** The system is hospital-centric with some of the lowest public spending on health in the EU, leading to high out-of-pocket costs and shortages of healthcare professionals.
