# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Slovakia

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 925 final.
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025.
*   **Reporting Body:** European Commission.
*   **Geographic Focus:** Slovakia.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Slovakia highlights a complex landscape of legislative progress tempered by significant regressions in judicial independence, media freedom, and anti-corruption efficacy. While the government has engaged in dialogue to align criminal codes with EU financial interest directives (PIF Directive), critics point to a "chilling effect" on the judiciary due to potential criminal liability for judicial decisions. Key concerns include the erosion of public service media autonomy, the expansion of the Prosecutor General's powers to annul lower-level decisions, and a deteriorating environment for journalists characterized by increased verbal attacks and SLAPP-like litigation. Furthermore, the report notes a lack of progress in regulating lobbying and strengthening asset declaration systems, alongside a notable deterioration in the efficiency of administrative courts.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System and Judicial Independence
*   **Perception of Independence:** Public and corporate trust in judicial independence remains low, with only 32% of the general population perceiving it as "fairly or very good."
*   **Criminal Liability for Judges:** While amendments clarified procedural aspects of the "abuse of law" crime, stakeholders warn that the lack of sufficient safeguards creates a chilling effect on independent decision-making.
*   **Judicial Council Oversight:** Concerns persist regarding the Judicial Council's expanded role in disciplinary proceedings and the lack of independent guarantees regarding the dismissal of its members.
*   **Court Efficiency:** Administrative courts have seen a sharp deterioration in efficiency, with resolution times rising to 1,040 days in 2023—one of the highest in the EU.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Institutional Changes:** The dissolution of the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO) has led to delays in investigations and a drop in the number of corruption cases.
*   **Prosecutorial Power:** The Prosecutor General retains broad powers to annul final decisions of lower-ranking prosecutors, raising concerns about undue interference in high-level corruption cases.
*   **Corruption Perception:** Slovakia's score on the 2024 Corruption Perceptions Index (49/100) indicates a regression to levels seen five years ago.
*   **Whistleblower Protection:** Despite existing frameworks, reporting remains low, and the Ministry of Interior faced a EUR 90,000 fine for improperly suspending protected whistleblowers.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Public Service Media:** The establishment of the new Slovak Television and Radio (STVR) entity is viewed as a regression in editorial independence due to the potential for political influence over its Council.
*   **Journalist Safety:** The environment for journalists is described as increasingly hostile; the government has abandoned plans to reform defamation laws or improve physical safety.
*   **Freedom of Information:** New amendments allowing fees for "exceptionally extensive" information requests have been criticized as potential "paywalls" for critical information.

### IV. Checks, Balances, and Civil Society
*   **Fast-Track Legislation:** The frequent use of fast-track procedures (e.g., 28 of 58 laws in 2024) continues to bypass meaningful stakeholder consultation.
*   **NGO Pressures:** A new legal framework imposes heavy reporting and disclosure obligations on NGOs, which stakeholders describe as an increased administrative burden.
*   **Constitutional Concerns:** Proposed amendments to the Constitution have been postponed by Parliament following Commission concerns regarding the primacy of EU law.
*   **ECtHR Judgments:** As of June 2025, the number of leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights pending implementation in Slovakia rose to 33.
