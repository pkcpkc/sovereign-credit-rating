# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Austria

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: SWD(2025) 920 final – 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Austria
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (Reference period covers developments through mid-2025)
- **Issuer**: European Commission (DG Justice and Consumers)
- **Document Type**: Staff Working Document / Country Report

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Austria highlights a jurisdiction with strong overall institutional foundations but identifies critical areas requiring legislative and structural reform to align with European standards. Austria maintains very high public perception of judicial independence and efficiency, alongside low perceived corruption levels. However, systemic vulnerabilities persist in the appointment mechanisms for administrative court presidents, which lack consistent judicial involvement, and in the structure of the prosecution service, which remains subject to executive oversight and reporting obligations.

Key progress includes the commitment to establish an independent Federal Prosecution Office, ongoing digitalization of the justice system ("Justice 3.0"), and reforms to the public service broadcaster (ORF) to enhance management independence. Conversely, significant challenges remain regarding access to justice due to high court fees, incomplete implementation of anti-corruption recommendations (specifically regarding asset declarations for MPs and lobbying transparency), and deteriorating economic conditions for private media providers despite state funding for quality journalism. The report recommends targeted actions to strengthen judicial appointments, prosecute corruption independently, and ensure fair distribution of state advertising.

## Key Highlights

- **[Judicial Independence & Appointments]**: Perceived judicial independence remains very high (86% of general public), yet no progress was made on reforming the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents, which lacks consistent judicial involvement and raises concerns under European standards.
- **[Prosecutorial Reform]**: Limited progress was made on establishing an independent Federal Prosecution Office; while the new Government Programme commits to this reform, draft legislation is still pending, and current structures allow the Minister of Justice to instruct prosecutors in individual cases.
- **[Access to Justice]**: High court fees in specific contexts persist, with a 23% average increase effective April 2025. The Constitutional Court ruled in October 2024 that restrictions on legal aid in administrative proceedings were unconstitutional, with suspensions taking effect by March 2026.
- **[Digitalization]**: The "Justice 3.0" initiative continues to advance, aiming for 90% digital case management by year-end, and compulsory online publication of judgments was extended to Higher Regional Courts in January 2025.
- **[Anti-Corruption Framework]**: Austria ranks 10th in the EU on corruption perception. While investigations into high-level corruption continue, limited progress was made on introducing effective asset and interest declaration rules for Members of Parliament, and the lobbying framework remains limited in scope with no legislative updates.
- **[Media Freedom & Pluralism]**: The media regulator (KommAustria) operates independently, but the economic situation for private media providers is deteriorating due to advertising revenue shifts to online platforms. Reforms to ORF management bodies were implemented in March 2025 to increase independence following a Constitutional Court ruling.
- **[State Advertising]**: Limited progress was made on ensuring fair distribution of state advertising; while transparency rules have improved, stakeholders criticize the lack of effective sanction mechanisms for violations and the large volume of spending influencing media markets.
- **[Whistleblowing & Transparency]**: Web-based reporting channels for whistleblowers function well, with the Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption receiving 147 reports by end-2024. A publicly accessible register of parliamentary groups was established, strengthening transparency.
- **[Investment Protection]**: 72% of companies express confidence in investment protection, and the independence of the national competition authority is perceived as high (85%). However, no judicial mechanisms exist at the Supreme Administrative Court level to ensure implementation of administrative court judgments.
