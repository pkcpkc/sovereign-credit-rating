# Summary of: IMF (2025) Slovakia Country Report

## Context Metadata
- **Source:** International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Report Type:** 2025 Article IV Consultation Staff Report & Press Release
- **Date of Publication:** March 2025
- **Subject Country:** Slovak Republic
- **Key Focus Areas:** Economic growth, fiscal consolidation, financial sector resilience, structural reforms, and demographic challenges.

## Executive Summary
The IMF concludes that the Slovak economy is recovering, with growth accelerating to 2.0% in 2024, outpacing the euro area. However, the outlook is tempered by significant structural headwinds, including geoeconomic fragmentation, an aging population, and low productivity growth. The government has committed to an ambitious 4-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025-2028) to reduce the headline deficit and stabilize public debt. While the financial sector remains resilient with healthy capital buffers, vulnerabilities persist in the residential and commercial real estate markets. Key recommendations from the IMF include accelerating structural reforms to diversify the economy, enhancing innovation, improving governance/anti-corruption efforts, and implementing specific expenditure-based fiscal measures to meet long-term sustainability targets.

## Key Highlights
*   **Economic Performance:** Growth is projected to moderate to 1.8% in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation, before rising to 2.1% in 2026. Inflation is expected to peak at 4.1% in 2025 before moderating toward the 2% target by 2027.
*   **Fiscal Policy:** The authorities aim to reduce the headline deficit to 4.7% of GDP in 2025. The IMF emphasizes the need for further measures beyond 2025, specifically suggesting a shift toward expenditure-based rationalization (e.g., reducing subsidies and targeting social spending) over revenue-based measures.
*   **Financial Stability:** The banking system is assessed as resilient to stress, characterized by high capital ratios and ample liquidity. However, the IMF advises monitoring the impact of the new Financial Transaction Tax (FTT) and refining macroprudential tools for real estate.
*   **Structural Challenges:** Slovakia faces a "secular decline" in labor productivity and high risks of automation. To counter this, the IMF recommends intensifying Active Labor Market Policies (ALMPs), increasing R&D spending, and deepening integration into the European single market.
*   **Demographics & Debt:** Public debt is projected to rise significantly in the long term due to aging-related costs. The IMF warns that without permanent adjustments to the pension system, fiscal costs could become unsustainable.
*   **Governance & Corruption:** The report highlights a need to strengthen judicial independence and anti-corruption frameworks, noting that perceptions of corruption in Slovakia have not improved significantly in recent years.
