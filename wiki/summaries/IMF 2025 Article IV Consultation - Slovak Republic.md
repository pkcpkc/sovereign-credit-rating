# Summary of: IMF (2025) Slovakia Country Report

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** International Monetary Fund (IMF)
*   **Report Type:** 2025 Article IV Consultation — Press Release and Staff Report
*   **Subject Country:** Slovak Republic
*   **Reporting Date:** March 2025
*   **Key Objective:** Assessment of economic developments, fiscal policy, financial sector stability, and structural reforms.

## Executive Summary
The Slovak economy is in a recovery phase following a sharp slowdown in 2022-23, with growth reaching 2.0% in 2024. While the economy is outperforming the euro area in recent growth figures, it faces significant headwinds including an aging population, low productivity growth, and high fiscal deficits. The government has committed to an ambitious 4-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025-2028) to reduce the headline deficit and stabilize public debt. The IMF notes that while the financial sector remains resilient, risks persist in residential and commercial real estate markets. Structural reforms focusing on innovation, labor force expansion (migrants and elderly), and governance are deemed essential for long-term growth.

## Key Highlights

*   **$\text{A1}$ Economic Growth & Inflation:** Growth is projected to moderate to 1.8% in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation before rising to 2.1% in 2026. Inflation is expected to rise temporarily to 4.1% in 2025 before reaching the 2% target by early 2027.
*   **$\text{A2}$ Fiscal Policy & Debt:** The 2025 budget targets a deficit reduction to 4.7% of GDP. However, the IMF suggests that additional measures totaling 3.1% of GDP will be needed over the next three years to meet long-term targets, specifically recommending expenditure-based measures like reducing subsidies and targeting social spending.
*   **$\text{A3}$ Financial Sector Resilience:** The banking sector is well-capitalized with ample liquidity. However, the residential real estate market is identified as a vulnerability due to high house prices and upcoming mortgage refixations.
*   **$\text{A4}$ Structural & Demographic Challenges:** Slovakia faces a shrinking labor force due to aging and outward migration. Recommendations include shortening parental leave, increasing vocational training, and facilitating the integration of migrants.
*   **$\text{A5}$ Energy & Climate:** Slovakia has successfully reduced natural gas reliance on Russia. To meet 2030 climate targets, the IMF recommends faster implementation of the ETS II scheme and phasing out fossil fuel subsidies.
*   **$\text{A6}$ Governance & Corruption:** Perceptions of corruption remain high. The IMF emphasizes the need for a new national anti-corruption strategy and strengthening judicial independence to improve the investment climate.
