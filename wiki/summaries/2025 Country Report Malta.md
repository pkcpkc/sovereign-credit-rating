## Executive Summary

The European Commission's 2025 Country Report for Malta highlights an economy that continues to exhibit strong growth momentum, driven by robust domestic demand and export performance, particularly in tourism, professional services, and IT. Real GDP growth reached 6.0% in 2024, with forecasts projecting continued expansion at approximately 4.0-4.1% through 2026. Unemployment remains exceptionally low at 3.1%, supported by high immigration flows, although significant labor shortages and skills mismatches pose a constraint on future competitiveness and productivity.

Key fiscal challenges include a general government deficit projected to decrease from 3.7% of GDP in 2024 to 2.8% in 2026, with debt stabilizing below 50% of GDP. However, long-term fiscal sustainability is threatened by projected increases in age-related spending, particularly pensions and healthcare. Structural reforms are ongoing through the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), focusing on green and digital transitions, institutional strengthening, and social inclusion.

Critical weaknesses identified include low investment in Research and Development (R&D), administrative burdens, lengthy judicial proceedings, and high dependence on fossil fuels. The report emphasizes the need to address skills shortages, improve energy efficiency, decarbonize transport, and enhance social fairness, particularly for vulnerable groups such as non-EU nationals and persons with disabilities.

## Key Highlights

- **[Strong Economic Growth]**: The Maltese economy maintained high growth momentum with 6.0% real GDP growth in 2024, driven by exports and domestic demand, outperforming most EU countries.
- **[Labor Market Constraints]**: Despite low unemployment (3.1%), severe labor shortages and skills mismatches in ICT, construction, and healthcare are holding back competitiveness. High reliance on immigration is masking domestic skills gaps.
- **[Fiscal Consolidation]**: The general government deficit is projected to fall to 2.8% of GDP in 2026, with debt stabilizing below 50% of GDP, supported by decreasing energy subsidies and increased tax collection efforts.
- **[Low Innovation Investment]**: R&D intensity remains critically low at 0.64% of GDP (second lowest in the EU), hampering innovation potential and digital transition efforts.
- **[Administrative and Regulatory Burdens]**: Malta ranks as one of the weakest performers in the EU regarding administrative burdens and barriers to trade/investment, with lengthy judicial proceedings (454 days for civil cases) deterring investment.
- **[Energy and Climate Challenges]**: Renewable energy uptake is low (17% of electricity mix in 2024), and fossil fuel subsidies distort markets. Transport emissions are rising, and waste management lags behind EU averages, with high landfill rates.
- **[Social Inequalities]**: Poverty risks remain high for non-EU nationals, persons with disabilities, and children. The gender employment gap is wide, and housing affordability is a growing concern for low-income households.
- **[Tax System Vulnerabilities]**: The tax system relies heavily on corporate income taxes, with low labor taxation. There are significant risks of aggressive tax planning, a large VAT compliance gap, and issues with non-domiciled company rules.
- **[Institutional Reforms]**: Establishment of Science Malta (2024) aims to streamline R&D governance. Implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan is progressing, with 39% of milestones/targets fulfilled.
- **[Financial Sector Resilience]**: The banking sector is well-capitalized with strong liquidity, but non-bank financial intermediaries are large yet have limited domestic economic linkages.
