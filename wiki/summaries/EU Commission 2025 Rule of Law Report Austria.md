# Summary of: EU Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report: Austria

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: SWD(2025) 920 final - Commission Staff Working Document: 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Austria
- **Effective Period**: Reporting period covering developments up to mid-2025; Recommendations for 2025-2026 cycle
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Rule of Law Report (Country Chapter)

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Austria, issued by the European Commission, assesses the state of the rule of law across four pillars: the justice system, anti-corruption framework, media pluralism/freedom, and other institutional checks and balances. Austria maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence among the general public (86%) and high overall efficiency in its justice system. However, significant structural reforms remain pending or incomplete.

Key challenges include the lack of progress in introducing judicial involvement in the appointment of administrative court presidents, which raises concerns regarding European standards. The reform to establish an independent Federal Prosecution Office has seen only limited progress, with the Minister of Justice retaining the power to issue instructions in individual cases, a practice that continues despite an investigative commission’s findings of political influence attempts. In the anti-corruption sphere, while corruption perception remains low, there is limited progress on asset declaration rules for Members of Parliament and no progress on strengthening the limited lobbying framework. The media landscape shows strong regulatory independence but faces economic pressures and concerns regarding the fair distribution of state advertising, where spending has more than doubled. Institutional checks and balances are strengthened by effective oversight bodies like the Ombudsman Board and Court of Audit, though appointment procedures for independent authorities remain susceptible to politicization. The Commission reiterates recommendations for Austria to address judicial appointments, finalize prosecutorial independence, enhance parliamentary transparency, strengthen lobbying rules, and ensure fair state advertising distribution.

## Key Highlights

- **Judicial Independence Perceptions Remain High**: In 2025, 86% of the general population and 71% of companies perceive judicial independence as "fairly or very good," showing an increase in public perception compared to 2024 (82%) but a slight decrease in corporate perception (down from 77% in 2024).
  
- **No Progress on Judicial Appointments for Administrative Courts**: There have been no steps to introduce systematic judicial involvement in the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents. The current varied procedures lack consistent judicial input, raising concerns against European standards. A reflection process initiated in 2024 yielded no operational conclusions.

- **Limited Progress on Prosecutorial Independence**: The establishment of an independent Federal Prosecution Office, committed to in the March 2025 Government Programme, is in the drafting stage. However, the Minister of Justice continues to issue instructions to prosecutors in individual cases (31 cases examined by the Council of Directives in 2024). An independent investigative commission in July 2024 confirmed past instances of political influence on prosecutorial action, highlighting the urgency of reform.

- **Justice System Efficiency and Digitalization**: The justice system remains highly efficient, with civil/commercial cases resolved in 141 days on average. Disposition time for administrative cases stagnated at 313 days, while bribery cases saw a significant increase in duration to 518 days. Digitalization advances with "Justice 3.0" aiming for 90% digital case management by end-2025. Compulsory online publication of judgments was expanded to Higher Regional Courts as of January 1, 2025.

- **Court Fees and Access to Justice**: High court fees remain a challenge, with a 23% average increase entering force on April 1, 2025. The Constitutional Court ruled in October 2024 that restrictions on legal aid in the Administrative Procedural Code were unconstitutional, mandating legal aid for all administrative court proceedings, with suspension of old rules expected by March 31, 2026.

- **Anti-Corruption Strategy and Resources**: The National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2023-2025 is being implemented. Austria scores 67/100 in the 2024 Corruption Perceptions Index. The Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK) detected 83 potential corruption cases in 2024. Prosecutors face intense public scrutiny and heavy reporting obligations. Resources for the specialized anti-corruption prosecutor (WKStA) remain stable, though additional posts are needed.

- **Parliamentary Transparency Gaps**: There is limited progress on introducing effective asset and interest declaration rules for Members of Parliament. Current obligations cover income from freelance/salaried work but exclude a full range of assets, interests, debts, or liabilities. No monitoring or sanction mechanisms exist for voluntary declarations. Discussions on live-streaming parliamentary investigative committees may resume.

- **Lobbying Framework Remains Weak**: No progress has been made on strengthening the lobbying framework, which dates back to 2013. The current system lacks comprehensive transparency, supervision, and enforcement mechanisms. GRECO and the Court of Audit have repeatedly called for reform, including a robust transparency register.

- **Media Independence and Economic Pressures**: The media regulator KommAustria operates independently. However, the economic situation for private media operators is deteriorating due to shifts in advertising revenue. Public service broadcaster ORF’s management bodies were reformed in March 2025 following a Constitutional Court ruling to enhance independence. State advertising spending more than doubled in 2024 to EUR 417.9 million, raising concerns about political proximity and fair distribution, with only limited progress made on enforcement.

- **Institutional Checks and Balances**: The Ombudsman Board (National Human Rights Institution) functions effectively, with plans to strengthen its nomination procedure. The Government commits to transparent, merit-based appointments for high-level positions at independent authorities to counter politicization. The implementation of the Freedom of Information Act is well underway, set to enter into force in September 2025, granting subjective rights to information.
