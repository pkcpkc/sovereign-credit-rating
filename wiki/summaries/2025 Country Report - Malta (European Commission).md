# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Malta (European Commission)

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Country Report - Malta (SWD(2025) 218 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (Reviewing 2024 outturns and forecasting 2025-2026)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Country Report (European Semester)

## Executive Summary

The European Commission's 2025 Country Report for Malta highlights an economy maintaining strong growth momentum, driven by robust domestic demand and exports in the service sector. Real GDP grew by 6.0% in 2024 and is projected to expand by 4.1% in 2025 and 4.0% in 2026. The labor market remains tight, with an employment rate of 83.0% in 2024, though this growth relies heavily on immigration to address severe structural skills shortages and mismatches.

Fiscally, Malta is undergoing a significant consolidation phase. The general government deficit is set to decline from 3.7% of GDP in 2024 to 2.8% in 2026, supported by a reduction in energy subsidies and improved tax revenues. The debt-to-GDP ratio, currently at a manageable 47.4%, is expected to stabilize below 50% by 2026. However, long-term fiscal sustainability faces substantial threats from an aging population, with age-related expenditures (pensions, healthcare, and long-term care) projected to rise from 17% of GDP in 2024 to 26% in 2070.

Key structural challenges include very low investment in research and innovation (0.64% of GDP), a highly strained judicial system with lengthy proceedings, and significant risks related to aggressive tax planning and corruption perceptions. The environmental transition is lagging, with low renewable energy uptake (17% of electricity mix), high reliance on fossil fuel subsidies, and critical issues in waste management and water scarcity. Social fairness concerns persist, particularly regarding the gender employment gap, workplace safety for non-EU nationals, and high out-of-pocket healthcare costs. The report emphasizes the need for Malta to implement its Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) effectively and accelerate cohesion policy investments to address these structural weaknesses.

## Key Highlights

- **Strong Economic Growth and Labor Market Dynamics**: Malta’s economy demonstrated resilience with 6.0% real GDP growth in 2024, driven by tourism and professional services. Employment reached 83.0%, one of the highest in the EU, but this is sustained by high immigration flows due to acute domestic labor shortages, particularly in ICT, construction, and healthcare.
- **Fiscal Consolidation and Debt Sustainability**: The government deficit is projected to narrow from 3.7% of GDP in 2024 to 2.8% in 2026. The debt ratio remains low at 47.4% of GDP. However, the cumulative growth in net expenditure for 2024-2025 exceeds Council recommendations, primarily due to energy subsidies and capital expenditures related to the national airline.
- **Long-Term Fiscal Pressures from Aging**: Age-related spending is a critical long-term risk. Pension expenditure is projected to double as a share of GDP (from 6% to 10.5%) by 2070, while healthcare spending is expected to rise from 5.1% to 7.2% of GDP. This represents the second-largest projected increase in pension spending among EU Member States.
- **Structural Weaknesses in Innovation and Business Environment**: R&D investment is among the lowest in the EU at 0.64% of GDP. The business environment is hampered by a high regulatory burden (OECD PMR indicator second weakest in EU) and inefficient judicial processes, with civil and commercial cases taking an average of 454 days to resolve.
- **Taxation Challenges and Aggressive Planning Risks**: Tax revenues are low (26.6% of GDP vs EU 39%) with a high reliance on corporate income tax. Malta faces a significant VAT compliance gap (25.9%) and risks of aggressive tax planning, particularly through outbound payments and the treatment of non-domiciled companies. The Commission notes that while some reforms are underway, withholding taxes on outbound payments remain a key pending measure.
- **Energy Transition and Environmental Sustainability**: Malta’s renewable energy share in electricity generation remains low at 17% (2024). Fossil fuel subsidies account for 1.6% of GDP, distorting market signals. The country also struggles with high municipal waste generation (606 kg/capita), low recycling rates (17.1%), and severe water stress, with the Water Exploitation Index reaching 34% in 2022.
- **Social Inequality and Labor Market Inclusion**: Despite high employment, the gender employment gap remains wide (13.4 percentage points). Non-EU nationals face precarious working conditions, higher risks of poverty, and workplace accidents. Child poverty has increased to 25.9% in 2024, exceeding the EU average, driven by low parental education and housing costs.
- **Recovery and Resilience Plan Implementation**: Malta is progressing with its RRP, having disbursed over 50% of grants. Key investments focus on digitalization, energy efficiency, and waste management. However, the Commission urges faster implementation of cohesion policy programs and better alignment with EU strategic priorities like the Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform (STEP).
