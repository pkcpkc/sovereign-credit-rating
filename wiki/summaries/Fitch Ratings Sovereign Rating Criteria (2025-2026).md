# Summary of: Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Criteria (2025-2026)

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Criteria – Master Criteria
- **Effective Period**: 15 September 2025 to 27 April 2026
- **Issuer**: Fitch Ratings
- **Document Type**: Rating Criteria

## Executive Summary

This document outlines Fitch Ratings' comprehensive framework for assigning and maintaining sovereign and central bank Issuer Default Ratings (IDRs) globally. The criteria define sovereign credit risk as a forward-looking assessment of the government's capacity and willingness to honor debt obligations to private creditors. The methodology is anchored by four analytical pillars: Structural Features, Macroeconomic Performance, Public Finances, and External Finances. 

Fitch employs the Sovereign Rating Model (SRM), a quantitative multiple-regression model using 18 key variables, as the starting point for rating assignment. This output is then adjusted via a Qualitative Overlay (QO) to account for factors not fully captured by the model, such as political stability, financial sector health, and geopolitical risks. The document details the specific variables, weights, and qualitative adjustments for each pillar, defines default events, and explains the differentiation between Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) IDRs. It also introduces the Climate Vulnerability Signal (Climate.VS) as a screening tool for long-term climate-related credit risks.

## Key Highlights

- **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM) Framework**: Fitch utilizes a quantitative model calibrated to the LT FC IDR scale ('AAA' to 'CCC+'), employing 18 variables across four pillars. Structural Features carry the highest weight (53.7%), followed by External Finances (17.3%), Public Finances (19.1%), and Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%).
- **Qualitative Overlay (QO)**: A formalized adjustment system allows analysts to modify SRM output by up to +3/-3 notches based on qualitative factors like political stability, financial sector risks, fiscal flexibility, and external financing resilience.
- **Structural Features Analysis**: This pillar includes governance indicators (World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators), GDP per capita, share in world GDP, years since default/restructuring, and broad money supply. It captures political risk, institutional strength, and economic size/diversification.
- **Macroeconomic Performance**: Assessed via real GDP growth volatility, consumer price inflation, and real GDP growth. The QO adjusts for policy credibility, exchange rate regime flexibility, dollarization risks, and medium-term growth outlook.
- **Public Finances Assessment**: Focuses on gross general government debt/GDP, interest payments/revenue, fiscal balance, and foreign-currency debt share. The QO evaluates fiscal financing flexibility, debt sustainability (using the Debt Dynamics Model), contingent liabilities, and demographic pressures.
- **External Finances Evaluation**: Includes reserve-currency flexibility, sovereign net foreign assets, commodity dependence, FX reserves, external interest service, and current account balance + FDI. The QO assesses external financing flexibility, debt sustainability, and vulnerability to shocks (e.g., using the International Liquidity Ratio).
- **Default Events and Resolution**: Defines specific events triggering an 'RD' rating, including missed payments, distressed debt exchanges (DDEs), and forced redenominations. Grace periods (typically 30 days) and payment force majeure scenarios are detailed.
- **Local-Currency (LC) vs. Foreign-Currency (FC) IDRs**: LC IDRs reflect default likelihood in local currency, while FC IDRs reflect credit risk in foreign currencies. Differentiation arises from factors like debt burden composition, domestic capital market depth, and access to foreign currency.
- **Short-Term Ratings (ST IDR)**: Mapped from LT IDRs using a correspondence table. ST FC IDRs consider reserve-currency flexibility and the International Liquidity Ratio (ILR ≥ 100%), while ST LC IDRs prioritize financing flexibility and money creation capacity.
- **Sovereign Debt Instruments and Loss Severity**: Ratings of specific long-term debt instruments incorporate recovery expectations (Recovery Ratings, RR1-RR6) in addition to the issuer's IDR. Senior unsecured notes at 'BB-' and above are typically rated in line with the IDR, while distressed issuers ('B+' and below) may have instrument ratings notched based on expected recoveries.
- **Climate Vulnerability Signal (Climate.VS)**: A screening tool (0-100 scale) assessing physical risks (e.g., heatwaves, floods) and transition risks (fossil fuel dependence, green energy costs). Sovereigns with a Climate.VS ≥ 50 in 2035 receive additional analytical scrutiny, potentially leading to QO adjustments.
- **Data Sources and Limitations**: Relies on data from issuers, IMF, World Bank, BIS, and proprietary sources like GeoQuant. Data limitations can lead to negative QO adjustments or withdrawn ratings if analysis is deemed insufficiently robust.
