# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on France

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: Commission Staff Working Document: 2025 Rule of Law Report – Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in France
- **Effective Period**: 2024–2025 (Reference period for developments)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Country Report / Staff Working Document

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on France highlights a complex landscape of ongoing reforms and persistent challenges across the pillars of justice, anti-corruption, media freedom, and checks and balances. In the justice system, France is implementing the 2023 reform of the status of magistrates, aiming to enhance judicial independence and diversity. However, perceived judicial independence remains average, and judges face significant personal attacks and political pressure, particularly regarding election-related cases. The Superior Council of the Magistracy has actively defended judicial autonomy. Efforts to digitalize judicial procedures (e.g., Portalis) are progressing but face implementation delays and technical issues. Efficiency remains a concern, with high average durations for resolving cases, particularly in civil/commercial matters and bribery cases, prompting government "flash missions" to propose solutions.

In anti-corruption, France maintains a relatively low corruption perception score but faces challenges in prosecution consistency and high-level transparency. The new 2024-2029 National Anti-Corruption Plan is expected to be adopted in summer 2025. Institutional capacity has been strengthened, with reorganizations at the Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) and increased resources at the High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP). However, a significant gap remains in lobbying transparency, as disclosure rules still do not apply to top-executive officials. Whistleblowing mechanisms are seeing increased usage, but resource constraints for protection persist.

Media freedom is characterized by strong legal guarantees but practical challenges. The media regulator, ARCOM, is expanding its scope under the Digital Services Act and preparing for the European Media Freedom Act. Significant progress has been made in media ownership transparency with the development of a national database. Debates continue over the governance reform of public broadcasting, with concerns about editorial independence and funding stability. Journalists face difficulties in accessing administrative documents despite legal rights, and police violence during protests remains a concern.

Regarding checks and balances, legislative simplification efforts are welcomed by businesses but criticized by civil society for reducing citizen consultation opportunities. The use of accelerated legislative procedures and Article 49-3 has raised concerns about legislative stability. The civic space is rated as "narrowed," with civil society organizations facing budgetary restrictions and occasional intimidation. Implementation of European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) judgments has seen a recent decrease in pending cases, though historical backlogs remain.

## Key Highlights

- **Judicial Independence Under Pressure**: Despite the implementation of the 2023 magistrate status reform, perceived judicial independence remains average. Magistrates face severe personal attacks and threats, particularly after rulings on national elections, prompting the Superior Council of the Magistracy and the President of the Republic to reaffirm judicial independence.
- **Digitalization and Efficiency Challenges**: Progress continues in digitalizing judicial procedures (Portalis tool), but magistrates report slow implementation and technical issues. Case resolution times remain high (354 days for civil/commercial cases; 1,335 days for bribery), leading to government-led "flash missions" to improve efficiency and explore out-of-court dispute resolution.
- **Anti-Corruption Institutional Strengthening**: The Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) was reorganized into public and private sector departments. The HATVP increased its staff to handle a 48% rise in asset declarations. However, lobbying transparency remains incomplete, as top-executive officials are not subject to disclosure requirements, despite new rules for foreign lobbyists.
- **Media Ownership Transparency Improves**: France made significant progress in transparency by preparing a national media ownership database to identify ultimate beneficial owners. ARCOM is expanding its regulatory role under the DSA and preparing for the EMFA, though staff resources remain a concern.
- **Public Broadcasting Reform Debate**: A proposed reform to create a holding company for public broadcasters (France Médias) is under debate, with concerns over editorial autonomy and job security. Funding is shifting from a license fee to a share of VAT, which NGOs view as a temporary and potentially unstable solution.
- **Legislative Simplification vs. Democratic Consultation**: While businesses welcome the draft law to "simplify economic life," stakeholders criticize the reduction in citizen consultation mechanisms and the frequent use of accelerated legislative procedures, which contribute to legal uncertainty.
- **Narrowing Civic Space**: The civic space is rated as "narrowed." Civil society organizations face budgetary constraints, and human rights defenders report intimidation and online harassment. The National Consultative Commission on Human Rights (CNC DH) lacks sufficient means to fully execute its mandate.
- **ECHR Implementation Progress**: As of June 2025, the number of pending leading ECHR judgments decreased from 26 to 22, though historical cases (e.g., land expulsion measures) remain unresolved for over a decade.
