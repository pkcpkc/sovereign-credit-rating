# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: COMMISSION STAFF WORKING DOCUMENT - 2025 Rule of Law Report, Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Malta
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (Report covers developments up to early/mid-2025)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Report / Country Chapter

## Executive Summary

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Malta highlights mixed progress across the four key pillars of the rule of law: justice, anti-corruption, media pluralism, and checks and balances. 

In the **Justice Sector**, perceived judicial independence remains high among the public and companies. Significant progress has been made in removing executive involvement from the appointment of the Chief Justice through ongoing constitutional reform. The transfer of prosecutorial powers for serious offenses to the Office of the Attorney General is complete, enhancing prosecutorial independence. However, critical challenges remain regarding the efficiency of the justice system, with excessive length of proceedings and a backlog of cases negatively impacting business confidence. Resource constraints, including a shortage of judges and court personnel, persist despite recent recruitment efforts.

The **Anti-Corruption Framework** faces significant scrutiny. While the National Anti-Fraud and Corruption Strategy is being implemented, the perceived level of corruption remains high (CPI score 46/100). Progress has been made in addressing the length of investigations for high-level corruption cases, but a robust track record of final judgments is lacking. The Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC) has yet to demonstrate concrete results, citing insufficient resources and operational readiness. Integrity measures for high-level politicians remain inadequate, with ministers' asset declarations unpublished and limited coverage of conflict of interest rules. A major legal development was the Court of Justice of the EU ruling that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme violates EU law.

Regarding **Media Pluralism and Freedom**, there is limited progress. The government is preparing legislation to align with the European Media Freedom Act, which may enhance the media regulator’s competences. However, no progress has been made in safeguarding the independent governance of public service media, which continues to face high risks of political bias. Additional legislation to protect journalists and improve access to official documents remains pending, despite the enactment of a law on cross-border strategic lawsuits (SLAPPs).

In terms of **Checks and Balances**, progress is limited. No formal framework for public participation in the legislative process has been introduced, though a new Public Consultation Department is planned. Draft legislation for a National Human Rights Institution remains unbundled in Parliament. A controversial reform increasing the threshold for citizens to petition magisterial inquiries has raised concerns among stakeholders about reduced accountability for high-level offenses. Meanwhile, civil society space is assessed as "narrowed," with draft regulatory frameworks for NGOs under preparation.

## Key Highlights

- **[Justice Sector Independence & Reform]**: Perceived judicial independence is high (69% of the public, 63% of companies). The government tabled a constitutional reform on May 13, 2025, to involve the judiciary in appointing the Chief Justice. The transfer of prosecutions for non-summary cases to the Attorney General was completed in September 2024, centralizing prosecutorial authority for serious offenses.

- **[Justice System Efficiency Challenges]**: Despite opening new Commercial and Family Courts, the length of proceedings remains a critical issue. In 2023, the disposition time for first-instance civil/commercial cases was 454 days, and criminal cases took 713 days. Administrative justice saw a deterioration to 1,350 days. Backlogs continue to increase, with clearance rates falling below new case intake, adversely affecting business confidence.

- **[Anti-Corruption: Investigations vs. Judgments]**: While investigative bodies (FCID, Attorney General) increased resources and tools, there is no progress in establishing a robust track record of final judgments in high-level corruption cases. The Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC) operates with limited part-time staff and has never referred a case for prosecution. The corruption perception score dropped to 46/100 in 2025.

- **[Investor Citizenship Scheme Ruled Illegal]**: On April 29, 2025, the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) ruled that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme is contrary to EU Law, characterizing it as the "commercialisation" of Union citizenship. This follows a 2023 referral by the European Commission.

- **[Media Freedom and Public Service Media]**: No progress has been made on enhancing the independent governance of public service media, which remains at "very high risk" for political bias according to the Media Pluralism Monitor. Legislation to improve the working environment for journalists and access to official documents is still pending. A new anti-SLAPP law was enacted in July 2024 but criticized for not covering domestic lawsuits.

- **[Checks and Balances & Civic Space]**: No progress was made in establishing a National Human Rights Institution or a formal framework for public participation in legislation. A reform to magisterial inquiries (Bill 125), approved in April 2025, raised the threshold for citizens to trigger investigations, drawing criticism for potentially hindering accountability in high-level corruption cases. Civil society space is considered "narrowed," with regulatory drafts pending.

- **[Integrity of High-Level Officials]**: Action to improve integrity measures for politicians remains limited. MPs are not covered by public administration conflict of interest rules. Ministers filed their 2025 asset declarations in March, but they remain unpublished. The OECD has recommended reforms to the asset declaration system, which the government is considering.
