# Summary of: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Austria

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** International Monetary Fund (IMF)
*   **Report Date:** July 3, 2025
*   **Subject Country:** Austria
*   **Consultation Period:** March 26 – April 8, 2025
*   **Key Stakeholders:** Ministry of Finance (Marterbauer), Central Bank (Holzmann), IMF Staff Team.

## Executive Summary
Austria is navigating a challenging economic landscape characterized by two years of recession following energy shocks and monetary tightening. While the economy is expected to remain near zero growth in 2025, a recovery is projected from 2026 onwards. The IMF highlights significant risks from global trade uncertainty, aging demographics, and sluggish productivity growth. To address these, the IMF recommends a "bold and well-designed" fiscal consolidation plan to reduce the deficit below 2% of GDP, structural reforms to boost labor supply (specifically for women and the elderly), and continued vigilance over commercial real estate (CRE) risks within the financial sector.

## Key Highlights

*   **Economic Outlook:** [RECOVERY_2026] Austria faced a 1.3% GDP contraction in 2024. Growth is expected to be near zero in 2025, with a rebound predicted for 2026 as private consumption recovers and monetary easing takes effect.
*   **Inflation Trends:** [INFLATION_GAP] Headline inflation remains higher than the euro-area average (3.2% projected for 2025), driven by sticky services inflation and the expiration of energy price relief policies.
*   **Fiscal Strategy:** [CONSOLIDATION_PLAN] The government aims to reduce the deficit via measures like abolishing the "climate bonus" and cutting green subsidies. The IMF suggests further structural reforms in pensions and healthcare to manage aging-related costs.
*   **Labor & Productivity:** [DEMOGRAPHIC_HEADWINDS] To offset 20 years of demographic aging, the IMF emphasizes narrowing the gap in female and elderly labor force participation. Regulatory "red tape" in services and housing must be reduced to spur dynamism.
*   **Financial Stability:** [CRE_RISKS] The banking sector remains healthy with strong capital ratios (CET1 at 17.7%), but the IMF warns of rising Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) in the commercial real estate sector.
*   **External Position:** [EXTERNAL_BALANCE] Austria maintains a strong external position with a current account surplus of 2.4% of GDP in 2024, largely due to depressed imports during the economic downturn.
*   **Governance & AML:** [CORRUPTION_REFORM] Austria is undergoing a mutual evaluation of its AML/CFT framework. The IMF encourages continued enforcement against foreign bribery and improved transparency in the corporate sector.
