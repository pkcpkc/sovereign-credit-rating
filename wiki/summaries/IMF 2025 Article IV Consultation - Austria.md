# Summary of: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Austria

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** International Monetary Fund (IMF)
*   **Publication Date:** July 3, 2025
*   **Subject Country:** Austria
*   **Consultation Period:** March 26 – April 8, 2025
*   **Key Stakeholders:** Austrian Ministry of Finance, Central Bank of Austria (OeNB), Financial Market Authority (FMA).

## Executive Summary
Austria is navigating a complex economic landscape following two years of recession driven by energy shocks and monetary tightening. While the economy is expected to remain near-zero growth in 2025, a recovery is projected for 2026. The IMF highlights three primary challenges: **fiscal sustainability** in the face of aging demographics and green transition costs; **inflation management**, as Austrian inflation remains stickier than the euro-area average; and **financial stability**, particularly regarding commercial real estate (CRE) risks and the need for continued bank profit conservation. The report emphasizes the need for "bold" structural reforms to boost labor supply (specifically among women and the elderly) and to reduce regulatory "red tape" to stimulate productivity.

## Key Highlights

*   **Economic Outlook & Growth:** 
    *   Austria faced a 1.3% GDP contraction in 2024.
    *   Growth is projected to be nearly flat (-0.1%) in 2025 before rebounding to 0.8% in 2026.
    *   Downside risks are dominated by global trade policy uncertainty and weak domestic sentiment.

*   **Fiscal Policy & Debt:**
    *   The fiscal deficit widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024; the government aims to reduce this via a 2025-2026 budget.
    *   The IMF recommends a medium-term target of a deficit below 2% of GDP to stabilize the debt ratio.
    *   Key proposed measures include reducing pension replacement rates, increasing healthcare efficiency, and expanding property/inheritance taxes.

*   **Inflation & Prices:**
    *   Headline inflation is expected to average 3.2% in 2025, exceeding the euro-area average due to sticky services inflation and the removal of energy price caps.
    *   Convergence to the 2% target is expected by late 2026.

*   **Financial Sector Stability:**
    *   The banking sector remains healthy with high capital ratios (CET1 at 17.7%).
    *   **CRE Risks:** Nonperforming loans (NPLs) in commercial real estate have risen, requiring continued vigilance and the use of the 1% Sectoral Systemic Risk Buffer (SSyRB).
    *   **Mortgage Lending:** Borrower-based measures (KIM-V) expire in July 2025; the IMF suggests making these permanent to ensure prudent underwriting.

*   **Structural Reform Priorities:**
    *   **Labor Supply:** Closing the gap in female and elderly labor participation could offset 20 years of demographic aging.
    *   **Productivity:** Reducing regulatory barriers in the services sector and speeding up renewable energy permitting are critical.
    *   **EU Integration:** Deepening the Single Market (e.g., Savings and Investment Union) is cited as a primary driver for long-term growth.

*   **Corruption & AML/CFT:**
    *   Austria is undergoing a mutual evaluation of its AML/CFT framework (expected completion Feb 2026).
    *   The IMF encourages continued focus on foreign bribery and the liability of legal persons.
