# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - France

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Country Report - France (SWD(2025) 210 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (Outturn data from 2024; Forecasts for 2025-2026)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Commission Staff Working Document / Country Report

## Executive Summary

The European Commission’s 2025 Country Report for France highlights a slowing economic trajectory amidst heightened political and economic uncertainty. While real GDP grew by 1.2% in 2024, growth is forecast to slow markedly to 0.6% in 2025 due to subdued investment and high uncertainty. The labor market shows signs of weakening, with employment growth turning negative in 2025 and unemployment rates expected to rise. A critical concern is the fiscal situation: France faces persistently high general government deficits (5.8% of GDP in 2024) and a rising public debt burden (113% of GDP), driven by sluggish tax revenues, high interest payments, and rigid expenditures.

The report underscores significant structural challenges, including high corporate indebtedness, stagnant business R&D intensity, and a complex business environment that hinders competitiveness. In the energy sector, while France boasts a low-carbon electricity mix, it faces delays in renewable energy deployment and building decarbonization due to administrative barriers. Socially, the report notes worsening poverty risks, particularly among children and in outermost regions, alongside persistent skills mismatches and educational inequalities. France has committed to a seven-year fiscal adjustment period under the reformed EU economic governance framework, requiring robust spending reviews and targeted reforms to ensure fiscal sustainability and boost potential growth.

## Key Highlights

- **Economic Slowdown and Political Uncertainty**: Economic activity is forecast to slow to 0.6% growth in 2025 following a 1.2% increase in 2024. The fragmented political landscape post-2024 elections has led to unprecedented budgetary delays and reduced fiscal ambitions, complicating the implementation of necessary structural reforms.

- **Deteriorating Fiscal Position**: France recorded a 5.8% of GDP deficit in 2024, with public debt rising to 113% of GDP. Interest payments on debt are projected to increase to 2.9% of GDP by 2026. The Commission forecasts only slight deficit reduction in 2025 (to 5.6%) and a rise in 2026 (to 5.7%), necessitating strict adherence to the endorsed medium-term fiscal-structural plan and effective spending reviews.

- **Labor Market and Productivity Challenges**: Employment growth is set to decline by 0.2% in 2025, with the unemployment rate rising to 7.9%. While labor productivity is recovering from recent losses, it remains hampered by high taxes on production, skills shortages, and low business R&D intensity. Corporate bankruptcies have reached record highs, reflecting the strain of high interest rates and economic uncertainty.

- **Business Environment and Competitiveness**: High administrative burdens and regulatory restrictions persist, particularly in the services and retail sectors. France lags in the digitalization of SMEs and the adoption of advanced technologies like AI. Despite high public support for R&D, the efficiency of schemes like the research tax credit (CIR) needs improvement to boost innovation output and private sector engagement.

- **Energy Transition and Decarbonization Barriers**: Although France has achieved 95% low-carbon electricity production, the deployment of renewable energies is hindered by complex permitting procedures and a lack of a firm national renewable energy target for 2030. Building decarbonization is also lagging, with significant shortfalls in deep renovation targets due to budget constraints and skilled labor shortages.

- **Social Fairness and Inequality**: Poverty and social exclusion risks have worsened, particularly among children and in outermost regions. The report highlights disparities in access to quality education and healthcare. The education system struggles with inequalities linked to socio-economic background, and the teaching profession faces declining attractiveness, impacting long-term human capital development.

- **Implementation of Reforms and EU Funds**: France is advanced in implementing its Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) measures, having fulfilled 82% of milestones. However, the Commission emphasizes the need to accelerate cohesion policy implementation and ensure that fiscal consolidation efforts do not undermine essential investments in the green and digital transitions.
