# Summary of: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation: Slovak Republic

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF Staff Report for the 2025 Article IV Consultation with the Slovak Republic
- **Effective Period**: March 2025 (covering economic developments through early 2025)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation Staff Report and Press Release

## Executive Summary

The IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation with the Slovak Republic assesses an economy recovering from recent headwinds, with growth accelerating to 2.0% in 2024, outpacing the Euro Area average. However, this recovery has been accompanied by a significant widening of the fiscal deficit, which reached 5.8% of GDP in 2024, triggering an Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) by the European Commission. The IMF staff and Executive Board endorse the Slovak government’s ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028), which aims to reduce the deficit to approximately 2.2% of GDP by 2028. The staff projects that while the 2025 budget measures are a step in the right direction, further deficit-reducing measures—particularly on the expenditure side—are required to meet medium-term targets and put public debt on a sustainable downward path.

Key risks to the outlook are tilted to the downside, driven by trade policy uncertainty, potential slowdowns in EU fund disbursements due to governance concerns, and structural challenges including an aging population and dependence on the automotive sector. The financial sector remains resilient with strong capital buffers, though vulnerabilities persist in the commercial real estate sector and from upcoming mortgage refixations. The report emphasizes the need for structural reforms to diversify the economy, enhance productivity, improve governance, and address long-term fiscal sustainability pressures from demographic aging.

## Key Highlights

- **[Fiscal Consolidation Targets]**: The Slovak government has proposed a 4-year consolidation plan to reduce the fiscal deficit from 5.8% of GDP in 2024 to 2.2% by 2028. The IMF staff supports this ambitious agenda as commensurate with Slovakia's fiscal challenges but notes that additional measures beyond the 2025 budget will be needed, prioritizing expenditure-based savings (e.g., reducing subsidies, targeting social spending) over further revenue hikes.
- **[Growth and Inflation Outlook]**: Real GDP growth is projected to moderate to 1.8% in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation and weak external demand, before recovering to 2.1% in 2026. Inflation is expected to rise temporarily to 4.1% in 2025 driven by indirect tax increases, then decline to the 2% target by early 2027.
- **[Debt Sustainability and Risks]**: Public debt is projected to rise in the medium term, reaching 74.3% of GDP by 2030 under the baseline, with a moderate overall risk of sovereign stress. However, long-term risk is assessed as high due to significant aging-related costs, which could increase fiscal burdens by 5.6% of GDP by 2050 without further reforms.
- **[Financial Sector Resilience]**: The banking sector is deemed resilient, with capital adequacy ratios well above regulatory minima and ample liquidity. Stress tests confirm resilience to severe shocks. Primary vulnerabilities lie in the commercial real estate (CRE) market, particularly the office segment, and the refixation of residential mortgages in the coming years. The IMF recommends strengthening the macroprudential framework, including adopting a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer.
- **[Structural Reforms and Governance]**: Slovakia faces structural headwinds from geoeconomic fragmentation and demographics. The report calls for reforms to diversify the economy away from automotive dependence, boost innovation, and increase labor force participation. Crucially, strengthening governance, reducing corruption vulnerabilities, and enhancing judicial independence are highlighted as essential to maintaining investor confidence and ensuring the effective absorption of EU funds.
- **[External Sector Position]**: The current account deficit widened to 2.7% of GDP in 2024, assessed as moderately weaker than fundamentals. The position is expected to improve as fiscal consolidation proceeds and EU funds support investment. The Net International Investment Position (NIIP) remains negative (-53.5% of GDP) but is improving, with high FDI inflows mitigating risks of sudden capital outflows.
