# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Italy

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: COMMISSION STAFF WORKING DOCUMENT 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Italy
- **Effective Period**: Reporting period covering developments up to mid-2025 (Reference dates include 2023 data, 2024 implementations, and 2025 events)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Rule of Law Report Country Chapter

## Executive Summary

The European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report on Italy outlines progress and persistent challenges across four key pillars: the justice system, the anti-corruption framework, media pluralism, and other institutional checks and balances. In the justice system, Italy has made significant strides in digitalising criminal courts and recruiting magistrates, though persistent staff shortages and excessive length of proceedings remain critical issues. A draft constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors is under parliamentary scrutiny but faces strong opposition from judicial bodies.

Regarding anti-corruption, the National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) has updated the National Anti-Corruption Plan and strengthened its resources. However, legislative gaps remain in regulating lobbying and political party financing, with no progress made on establishing an operational lobbying register or addressing the channeling of donations through political foundations. The Constitutional Court has validated the recent abolition of the "abuse of public office" offence.

In the media sector, the regulator (AGCOM) functions independently, and public service broadcaster RAI retained its license fee levels. However, concerns persist regarding RAI’s governance independence, access to judicial information, and the safety of journalists, including alleged spyware use. A draft defamation reform remains stalled. Finally, institutional checks and balances are strained by the government’s frequent use of urgency decrees and a new Security Law that raises concerns about civic space. No concrete steps have been taken to establish a National Human Rights Institution.

## Key Highlights

- **Justice System Reform and Digitalisation**: The High Council for the Judiciary is implementing tasks under the comprehensive justice reform legislation adopted in March 2024. The digital case management system (*APP*) became mandatory in criminal courts on January 1, 2025, though technical challenges have led to derogations for some courts. Recruitment of magistrates and administrative staff continues, aiming to close a 17% magistrate shortage.
- **Constitutional Reform Debates**: The draft constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors was approved by the Chamber of Deputies in January 2025. It faces scrutiny in the Senate and criticism from the High Council for the Judiciary and magistrates’ associations, who argue it undermines judicial autonomy and representation.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework Updates**: ANAC updated the National Anti-Corruption Plan in January 2025 and issued guidelines on "revolving doors." The Constitutional Court declared the law abrogating the offence of abuse of public office compliant with the Constitution in May 2025. However, no progress was made on lobbying regulations or a single electronic register for political party financing.
- **Media Freedom and Safety**: The media regulator AGCOM remains independent and well-resourced. RAI’s license fee was not reduced in 2025, providing financial stability, but governance reforms are pending. Journalists face ongoing challenges, including restrictions on accessing judicial information and threats; the Council of Europe registered 11 alerts concerning Italy in 2025. Allegations of Paragon spyware targeting journalists prompted an investigation by the Parliamentary Committee for the Security of the Republic.
- **Institutional Checks and Balances**: Stakeholders expressed concern over the government’s frequent use of urgency decrees (87 decrees in the current legislature) and the new Security Law adopted in June 2025, which increases penalties for protest-related activities and raises questions about civic space. No concrete measures have been taken to establish a National Human Rights Institution (NHRI), with five draft laws remaining pending.
