# Summary of: EU Commission 2025 Country Report: Poland

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Country Report - Poland (Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 221 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (Review of 2024 developments and outlook for 2025-2028)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Country Report / Economic Policy Recommendation

## Executive Summary

The European Commission’s 2025 Country Report on Poland highlights an accelerating economy that remains one of the fastest-growing in the EU, driven by strong private consumption and investment, including substantial EU-funded projects. However, significant macroeconomic and structural challenges persist. The most pressing fiscal issue is a high budget deficit (6.6% of GDP in 2024) and rising public debt, prompting the Council to initiate the excessive deficit procedure (EDP) and endorse a medium-term fiscal-structural plan. Poland faces the difficult task of consolidating public finances amidst high and rising defense spending, leveraging the "national escape clause" flexibility.

Structurally, Poland struggles with competitiveness, hampered by a complex and volatile regulatory environment, low labor productivity, and persistent skills shortages. The labor market is tight but constrained by an aging population and demographic decline. While unemployment remains historically low, there are significant gaps in the supply of qualified labor, particularly in STEM and vocational fields. The report also emphasizes the need to address the sustainability of the pension system, given the low statutory retirement age for women and special schemes, and to improve the healthcare system, which remains hospital-centric with insufficient preventive care.

In the energy sector, Poland is making progress in decarbonization and renewable energy deployment, particularly in battery manufacturing, but remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels. High electricity prices, driven by taxation and generation costs, pose a threat to industrial competitiveness. The report calls for accelerating the green transition, improving grid connectivity, and phasing out fossil fuel subsidies. Socially, while poverty rates are below the EU average, challenges remain in social inclusion, childcare accessibility, and regional disparities, particularly in rural and eastern areas.

## Key Highlights

- **Fiscal Consolidation Under Pressure**: Poland’s general government deficit reached 6.6% of GDP in 2024, exceeding the Treaty reference value. The Council initiated the excessive deficit procedure and endorsed a fiscal plan aiming to reduce the deficit below 3% by 2028. The fiscal adjustment is complicated by rising defense expenditure, which is expected to reach 2.8% of GDP in 2025, and high public investment needs.
- **Economic Growth and Competitiveness**: Real GDP grew by 2.9% in 2024, with growth expected to surpass this in 2025. Despite this, competitiveness challenges remain due to high unit labor costs, a complex tax framework (ranking last in the EU for tax complexity), and frequent legislative changes that deter investment. Labor productivity is at 67% of the EU average.
- **Labor Market and Demographics**: Unemployment remains at a historic low of 2.9% in 2024. However, the labor market faces severe shortages of skilled workers, particularly in manufacturing, construction, and STEM fields. The shrinking working-age population due to aging and low fertility rates poses a long-term risk to growth. The disability employment gap widened to 35.6%, one of the largest in the EU.
- **Education and Skills Crisis**: Basic skills among 15-year-olds have declined significantly since 2018, with a high proportion of disadvantaged students failing to meet minimum competency levels. Participation in adult learning is low (20.3%), hindering the green and digital transitions. The vocational education and training (VET) system lacks effectiveness in meeting labor market needs.
- **Energy Transition and Industrial Policy**: Poland has emerged as a clean tech hub, producing 60% of Europe’s lithium batteries. However, the energy mix remains dominated by fossil fuels (70% of electricity generation). High electricity prices, exacerbated by taxes and levies, threaten industrial competitiveness. The report urges faster deployment of renewables, grid modernization, and the phase-out of fossil fuel subsidies.
- **Pension and Healthcare Sustainability**: The pension system faces long-term sustainability risks due to demographic changes and a low statutory retirement age for women (60 years). The healthcare system, while undergoing reform via the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), remains hospital-centric with low spending on prevention and high treatable mortality rates compared to the EU average.
- **Regional Disparities**: Significant economic and social inequalities persist between the capital region (Warsaw) and other parts of the country, particularly the eastern regions. Western and capital regions attract the majority of foreign direct investment and exhibit higher productivity, while eastern regions struggle with infrastructure deficits and demographic decline.
- **Implementation of EU Funds**: Poland has made progress in implementing its Recovery and Resilience Plan, fulfilling 25% of milestones and targets as of mid-2025. Acceleration is needed to fully absorb funds and deliver promised reforms, particularly in the green and digital transitions. Cohesion policy implementation is also proceeding, with a focus on infrastructure, skills, and environmental sustainability.
