# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Poland

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: SWD(2025) 921 final - 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Poland
- **Effective Period**: 2025
- **Issuer**: European Commission (Directorate-General for Justice and Consumers)
- **Document Type**: Country Report / Staff Working Document

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Poland highlights significant, yet uneven, progress in addressing long-standing rule of law concerns through the implementation of a government Action Plan. The most notable advancement is the legislative effort to separate the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the Prosecutor General, a move intended to ensure the functional independence of the prosecution service. However, deep-seated institutional crises persist, particularly regarding the Constitutionality and independence of the judiciary. The report details ongoing tensions with the Constitutional Tribunal, whose legitimacy is contested by the government, and the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ), whose composition remains controversial.

While Poland has made strides in anti-corruption measures, including the full operationalization of the European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO) and the establishment of a new Central Office for Combating Corruption, critical gaps remain in lobbying regulations, impunity clauses for top executives, and the comprehensive implementation of asset declaration systems. In media freedom, Poland has reduced politically biased content in public service media and improved state advertising distribution, yet challenges regarding media regulator independence, media ownership transparency, and the safety of journalists endure. The report emphasizes that despite these positive steps, perceived judicial independence remains very low, and significant risks to institutional checks and balances, particularly concerning the rule of law, remain.

## Key Highlights

- **[Judicial Independence and Reform]**: Perceived judicial independence remains very low (26% among general public, 24% among companies). Significant efforts are underway to implement the Action Plan, including drafting laws to address judges appointed by the post-2017 NCJ and to dismantle the Supreme Court’s Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs. The Venice Commission has been consulted, but key amendments to the NCJ and Constitutional Tribunal laws remain pending referral to the Constitutional Tribunal.

- **[Constitutional Tribunal Crisis]**: Since March 2024, the government has refused to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, arguing the body is illegitimate. The Sejm ceased appointing new judges, while the President appointed a new Vice-President. The Tribunal has ruled on the constitutionality of EU treaty provisions, leading to a direct clash with EU law and an ongoing infringement procedure before the Court of Justice of the EU.

- **[Prosecution Service Independence]**: Significant progress has been made in separating the functions of the Minister of Justice and the Prosecutor General through a draft law adopted by the Cabinet. This includes the discontinuation of criminal investigations against Supreme Court judges for their judicial decisions and the cessation of prosecutor secondments used as disciplinary sanctions.

- **[Anti-Corruption Framework]**: The European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO) is fully operational in Poland as of January 2025. A new Central Office for Combating Corruption is being established within the police. However, no progress has been made on introducing lobbying rules, and impunity clauses for top executives remain in force. Large-scale corruption cases involving public officials are being investigated, but systemic risks in public procurement persist.

- **[Media Pluralism and Freedom]**: The government plans a media law reform to address the independence of the National Broadcasting Council (KRRiT) and public service media governance. While politically biased content in public service media has decreased, challenges remain regarding media regulator independence, the safety of journalists, and the transparency of media ownership. State advertising expenditure has become more evenly distributed.

- **[Checks and Balances]**: The Ombudsperson continues to play a key role, enjoying UN 'A' status. Civil society space has improved from 'obstructed' to 'narrowed'. However, implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments remains slow, with 54 leading judgments pending as of June 2025. The government has strengthened legislative consultation rules but stakeholders note that Ombudsperson recommendations are not always reflected in adopted legislation.
