# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Slovakia

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Country Report - Slovakia (SWD(2025) 225 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2024-2026 (Forecasting horizon)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Commission Staff Working Document (CSWD)

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Country Report for Slovakia highlights a critical divergence between short-term economic stability and deepening structural and fiscal vulnerabilities. While the economy grew by 2.1% in 2024 driven by consumption, the outlook has deteriorated, with GDP growth forecast at only 1.5% in 2025 and 1.4% in 2026. This slowdown is attributed to waning foreign direct investment (FDI), trade tensions (specifically US tariffs), and a declining competitiveness position caused by sluggish labour productivity growth failing to keep pace with rising unit labour costs.

Fiscal sustainability is the most significant risk area. Slovakia continues to face an excessive deficit, with the general government deficit remaining high at 5.3% of GDP in 2024. Although a consolidation package involving VAT and corporate tax hikes was implemented in late 2024, the report projects the deficit will remain elevated at 4.9% in 2025 and rise back to 5.1% in 2026 due to permanent spending increases in pensions, healthcare, and military salaries. Government debt is projected to rise to 63.0% of GDP by 2026. The long-term fiscal outlook is severely threatened by demographic ageing, with age-related expenditure projected to increase from 20% to 25% of GDP by 2070.

Institutional and rule of law challenges pose a severe drag on the business environment. Slovakia ranked last in the EU for doing business conditions. Key concerns include the dismantling of specialized anti-corruption law enforcement agencies, relaxed criminal sanctions for corruption, and the dissolution of the Civil Service Council. These actions have undermined public accountability and legal certainty.

Structurally, the economy remains overly reliant on the automotive manufacturing sector, hampered by low private R&D (0.58% of GDP) and an underdeveloped capital market. Furthermore, Slovakia faces an energy security dilemma, maintaining high dependence on Russian crude oil and natural gas despite efforts to diversify. Addressing these interconnected fiscal, institutional, and structural challenges requires accelerated implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) and deeper structural reforms.

## Key Highlights

- **Deteriorating Competitiveness and Growth Outlook**: Labour productivity growth remains weak compared to unit labour cost increases, eroding the international competitiveness of Slovak goods. The drop in FDI inflows (FDI stock fell to 46% of GDP in 2023) and potential exposure to US trade tariffs have led to a downward revision of growth forecasts to 1.5% for 2025.
- **Persistent Fiscal Imbalances**: Despite a consolidation package approved in October 2024, the government deficit is projected to remain above 4.9% in 2025. Public spending remains structurally high due to permanent measures in pensions and healthcare, while revenue collection struggles to keep pace. Debt sustainability risks are compounded by demographic ageing, which threatens to push age-related spending to 25% of GDP by 2070.
- **Severe Rule of Law and Institutional Backsliding**: Slovakia faces significant governance deficits, characterized by the dissolution of the National Crime Agency and Special Prosecution Office, reducing the capacity to investigate and prosecute high-level corruption. Fast-track legislative procedures have become the norm, often bypassing impact assessments and stakeholder consultations, leading to regulatory unpredictability.
- **Structural Weaknesses in Innovation and Business Environment**: The private sector lacks access to diversified financing due to an underdeveloped capital market and low venture capital activity (0.01% of GDP). Businesses cite administrative burdens, skilled labor shortages, and high taxes as primary investment obstacles. The tax system is skewed towards labor taxes, with insufficient use of environmental and property taxation.
- **Energy Security and Green Transition Challenges**: Slovakia's energy mix remains vulnerable due to heavy reliance on Russian fossil fuels (over 80% of crude oil and ~70% of natural gas). While the nuclear fleet is expanding, the transition to renewables is slow, with only 24% of electricity coming from renewable sources in 2024. High energy prices for non-household consumers hinder industrial competitiveness.
- **Labor Market and Social Inclusion**: The labor market is tight with historic lows in unemployment (5.1%), but this masks significant structural issues. There are severe shortages of skilled workers in healthcare and education, exacerbated by an aging workforce. The Roma community remains highly marginalized with an unemployment rate exceeding 53%, highlighting deep socio-economic disparities.
- **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) Implementation**: Slovakia has fulfilled 33% of RRP milestones. However, implementation is slowed by inefficient public procurement, administrative fragmentation, and insufficient project preparation. Accelerating the absorption of these EU funds is critical to supporting the green and digital transitions.
