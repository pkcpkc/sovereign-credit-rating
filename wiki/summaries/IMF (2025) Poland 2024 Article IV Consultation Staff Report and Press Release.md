# Summary of: IMF (2025) Poland 2024 Article IV Consultation Staff Report and Press Release

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: Republic of Poland: 2024 Article IV Consultation—Press Release; Staff Report; and Statement by the Executive Director for the Republic of Poland
- **Effective Period**: January 2025 (covering economic data through Q3/Q4 2024 and projections to 2029)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Staff Report / Press Release / Article IV Consultation

## Executive Summary

The IMF’s 2024 Article IV Consultation with Poland, concluded in January 2025, assesses an economy recovering robustly from recent shocks, driven by strong domestic demand and real wage growth. GDP growth accelerated to 2.8% in 2024 and is projected to reach 3.5% in 2025, supported by the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds. However, the recovery is accompanied by significant fiscal pressures, with the general government deficit widening to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, triggering an EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP). Inflation, while declining from 2023 peaks, remains elevated at approximately 5% due to tight labor markets and administered energy price increases, keeping core inflation sticky.

The IMF Staff and Executive Board commend Poland’s prudent monetary policies, which have anchored inflation expectations, but urge continued tightness until wage growth decelerates. On the fiscal front, the authorities have presented a Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) aimed at reducing the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028. The IMF recommends frontloading some consolidation to 2025 to rebuild buffers, support disinflation, and create space for monetary easing. Structural challenges, including population aging, diminishing cost-competitiveness, and the need for a credible climate transition, require comprehensive reforms. The financial sector remains resilient with strong capital buffers, though litigation risks from foreign-currency mortgages and distortions in private credit provision need addressing.

## Key Highlights

- **Positive Near-Term Growth Outlook**: Poland’s economy is in a cyclical recovery, with GDP growth estimated at 2.8% for 2024, driven by a rebound in private consumption and public investment. Growth is projected to accelerate to 3.5% in 2025 and 3.3% in 2026, fueled by NGEU fund absorption. Medium-term growth is expected to moderate to slightly below 3% due to aging demographics and the unwinding of temporary EU fund boosts.

- **Elevated Fiscal Deficits and EDP**: The fiscal deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, primarily due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending, outweighing savings from reduced energy support. This places Poland under the EU’s Excessive Deficit Procedure. Public debt is projected to rise from ~50% of GDP in 2023 to approximately 65% by 2029 under the baseline scenario.

- **Fiscal Consolidation Strategy**: The Polish authorities have adopted a Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) targeting a deficit of 2.9% of GDP by 2028. The IMF supports this direction but recommends a modest fiscal adjustment of ~0.5% of GDP in 2025 to help disinflation and allow for earlier interest rate cuts. Over the medium term, an additional structural adjustment of ~1.5% of GDP beyond identified measures is advised to stabilize debt around 60% of GDP while preserving public investment.

- **Monetary Policy and Inflation**: Headline inflation declined to 4% in October 2024 but remains above the National Bank of Poland’s (NBP) 2.5% ±1% target. Core inflation remains elevated due to strong wage growth in a tight labor market. The IMF agrees that monetary policy should remain tight, with rate cuts commencing only when there is clear evidence of decelerating wage growth and a sustained decline in inflation toward the target, potentially starting around mid-2025.

- **Financial Sector Resilience and Risks**: The banking sector is well-capitalized and liquid, with profits rising due to wide net interest margins. However, risks persist from litigation over Swiss Franc (CHF) mortgages and potential disputes over the WIBOR benchmark interest rate. The IMF recommends eliminating distortions such as the bank asset tax that favors public debt over private credit and allowing mortgage credit holidays to expire to support a nascent credit recovery.

- **Structural Challenges: Aging and Climate**: Long-term growth prospects are clouded by population aging, which will drag on labor supply, and the need to decarbonize a coal-dependent economy. The IMF urges reforms to boost productivity, including increasing labor force participation among women and older adults, integrating refugees and foreign workers, and accelerating the permitting of green energy projects. Pension reforms, such as equalizing retirement ages and linking them to life expectancy, are highlighted as critical for fiscal sustainability.

- **External Sector Position**: The current account balance narrowed to a surplus of 0.3% of GDP in 2024 and is projected to move into deficit (-1.5% of GDP) over the medium term as imports rise with domestic demand and EU fund spending. Poland’s external position is assessed as stronger than implied by fundamentals, with ample foreign exchange reserves and declining gross external debt (~45% of GDP). The real effective exchange rate has appreciated significantly since 2022, weighing on cost-competitiveness.

- **Refugee Integration**: Approximately 976,000 Ukrainian refugees are registered in Poland. They have made a significant positive contribution to the labor market and potential growth, offsetting some demographic headwinds. The IMF recommends further reforms to facilitate their long-term integration and the attraction of other foreign workers to sustain the labor force.

- **Institutional Improvements**: Poland has strengthened its fiscal framework by amending the stabilizing expenditure rule to align with the new EU fiscal framework and improving transparency of extrabudgetary funds managed by the Polish Development Bank (BGK). The establishment of an independent fiscal council, planned for 2026, is welcomed as a step toward enhanced accountability.
