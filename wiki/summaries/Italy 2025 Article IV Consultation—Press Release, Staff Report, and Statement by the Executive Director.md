## Executive Summary

The IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy highlights an economy that has demonstrated notable resilience, with GDP growing 0.7% in 2024 and reaching record-high employment levels. The near-term outlook remains moderately positive but is clouded by significant global uncertainty, including trade tensions and geopolitical risks. Structural challenges, particularly weak productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-average female labor force participation, continue to weigh on Italy's medium-term growth prospects.

Fiscal performance improved markedly in 2024, with the deficit shrinking to 3.4% of GDP and the country returning to a primary surplus. However, public debt remains high at 135.3% of GDP. The IMF staff recommends a more front-loaded fiscal adjustment to reach a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027 to firmly place debt on a downward trajectory. The authorities committed to a gradual seven-year fiscal adjustment under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), aiming to bring the deficit below 3% by 2026, and dispute the need for additional immediate measures.

The financial sector has strengthened, with banks showing high profitability, sound asset quality, and adequate capital buffers. However, vulnerabilities persist among some Less Significant Institutions (LSIs), and the sovereign-bank nexus remains a concern. The implementation of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) has accelerated, with 54% of milestones achieved, though spending execution remains sluggish. The IMF emphasizes the need for continued structural reforms to boost productivity, enhance labor supply, and transition to renewable energy to offset demographic headwinds.

## Key Highlights

- **Economic Resilience and Outlook**: Italy’s real GDP grew 0.7% in 2024, supported by NRRP investments and net exports. Growth is projected to moderate to 0.5% in 2025 before picking up to 0.8% in 2026. Headline inflation is expected to average 1.7% in 2025, converging to the ECB’s 2% target in 2026. Risks are tilted to the downside due to trade tensions, regional conflicts, and potential delays in NRRP implementation.
- **Fiscal Discipline and Debt Sustainability**: The 2024 fiscal deficit was 3.4% of GDP, better than projected, enabling a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP. Public debt ended at 135.3% of GDP. The IMF staff recommends achieving a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027 to ensure a declining debt path. The authorities propose a more gradual adjustment, targeting a deficit below 3% by 2026 and emphasizing structural reforms to boost growth. The IMF assesses sovereign debt risk as moderate overall, though high in the medium and long term due to aging-related costs.
- **Structural Reforms and Productivity**: Weak productivity growth and population aging are critical challenges. The NRRP implementation has accelerated, with 54% of milestones met, but spending execution lags. The IMF stresses the need for post-NRRP reforms to boost human capital, increase female labor force participation, and improve the business climate. Italy’s R&D investment remains low at 1.3% of GDP, well below the EU average.
- **Financial Sector Stability**: The banking sector’s soundness has improved, with high profitability and low NPLs (2.7%). The Systemic Risk Buffer (SyRB) was increased to 1%. However, weaknesses persist in some Less Significant Institutions (LSIs), and the sovereign-bank nexus remains sizable. The IMF calls for continued vigilance on loan quality, especially regarding firms exposed to trade shocks, and for strengthening the AML/CFT framework.
- **External Sector**: Italy posted a current account surplus of 1.1% of GDP in 2024, driven by lower energy imports and strong exports. The Net International Investment Position improved to 15.3% of GDP. The IMF assesses the external position as weaker than warranted by fundamentals, recommending further fiscal consolidation and productivity-boosting reforms. The authorities disagree, viewing the position as broadly in line with fundamentals.
- **Climate and Energy Security**: Italy faces significant climate risks due to its reliance on imported energy and exposure to extreme weather. The government is advancing the "Mattei Plan" to secure clean energy sources and reduce costs. The IMF emphasizes accelerating the transition to renewables and enhancing climate adaptation measures to meet 2030 targets.
