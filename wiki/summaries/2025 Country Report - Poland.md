# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Poland

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission (Staff Working Document)
*   **Report Date:** June 4, 2025
*   **Subject:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Poland.
*   **Key Frameworks:** Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), REPowerEU, Stability and Growth Pact.

## Executive Summary
Poland remains one of the fastest-growing economies in the EU, characterized by robust private consumption and investment. However, it faces significant structural hurdles, including an ageing population, high energy costs, and a complex regulatory environment. The report highlights a transition toward fiscal consolidation following an "excessive deficit" designation, alongside a push for digital transformation and decarbonization. While labor market participation is strong, there are severe shortages in STEM and green skills. The report emphasizes the need for simplified tax frameworks, improved public investment efficiency, and a more inclusive social protection system to ensure long-term competitiveness and sustainability.

## Key Highlights

### Economic Growth & Fiscal Policy
*   **$\text{A_GDP}$ Growth:** Poland's GDP grew by 2.9% in 2024; 2025 growth is expected to exceed this figure.
*   **Fiscal Adjustment:** Due to a 2023 deficit of 5.3% (exceeding the 3% Treaty limit), the Council initiated an excessive deficit procedure in January 2025.
*   **Net Expenditure:** Poland is working toward a medium-term fiscal-structural plan to reduce the deficit below 3% by 2028.
*   **Fiscal Governance:** A new Fiscal Council is set to become fully operational in January 2026.

### Labor Market & Demographics
*   **Employment:** The employment rate reached 78.4% in 2024, hitting the 2030 target early.
*   **Demographic Crisis:** Poland's population is expected to fall by 16% by 2070, significantly faster than the EU average.
*   **Skills Shortage:** 63% of manufacturing firms report labor shortages; there is a critical mismatch in STEM, digital, and green skills.
*   **Gender Gap:** Women's labor participation is hampered by a lack of affordable childcare and long-term care services.

### Innovation & Digitalization
*   **Productivity Gap:** Poland's labor productivity is at 67% of the EU aggregate, with significant regional disparities (Warsaw leads significantly).
*   **Digital Adoption:** Only 4.9% of Polish SMEs have adopted AI, compared to the EU average of 12.6%.
*   **R&D Performance:** Poland is an "emerging innovator," but suffers from weak science-business collaboration and an unattractive academic career system.

### Energy & Sustainability
*   **Decarbonization:** Renewables reached 30% of the electricity mix in 2024, but coal still dominates the district heating sector (69%).
*   **Energy Prices:** Poland faces some of the highest wholesale electricity prices in the EU, exacerbated by high taxes and a lack of non-fossil grid flexibility.
*   **Water Resilience:** Poland faces an alternating cycle of drought and floods; water productivity is among the lowest in the EU.
*   **Circular Economy:** The circular material use rate (7.5%) is below the EU average (11.8%).

### Social Fairness & Housing
*   **Poverty:** The AROPE rate is 16%, which is better than the EU average (21%), but rural areas remain significantly more vulnerable.
*   **Housing Crisis:** House prices rose by 107% since 2015, and building permits have faced a slowdown, causing significant affordability issues for young people.
*   **Healthcare:** Poland has one of the lowest rates of treatable mortality in the EU and suffers from a severe shortage of general practitioners and nurses.
