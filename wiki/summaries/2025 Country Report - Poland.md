# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Poland

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 221 final).
*   **Date of Issue:** June 4, 2025.
*   **Subject:** Economic, social, employment, structural, and budgetary policies of Poland.
*   **Key Frameworks:** Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), Stability and Growth Pact, European Green Deal.

## Executive Summary
Poland maintains a position as one of the fastest-growing economies in the EU, characterized by high private consumption and significant investment. However, the report highlights critical structural challenges: a rapidly aging population, labor shortages (particularly in STEM and construction), and a complex, overburdened tax framework. While fiscal consolidation is underway to address an excessive deficit (6.6% of GDP in 2023), the economy faces significant hurdles in decarbonizing its heavy industry and transport sectors, improving regional productivity disparities, and transitioning to a circular economy. Strategic priorities include accelerating the RRP implementation, enhancing public spending efficiency, and modernizing the healthcare system to move away from a hospital-centric model toward primary and outpatient care.

## Key Highlights

### ⚓ Economic Growth and Fiscal Policy
*   **Accelerating Growth:** GDP grew by 2.9% in 2024, with projections for higher growth in 2025 driven by consumption and investment.
*   **Fiscal Adjustment:** Poland is under an excessive deficit procedure; a binding expenditure path aims to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2028.
*   **Public Spending:** Net expenditure grew by 12.7% in 2024, largely due to public consumption and defense spending.
*   **Tax Complexity:** Poland ranks last among EU Member States in the Tax Complexity Index 2022, creating significant hurdles for SMEs and innovation.

### ⚓ Labor Market and Demographics
*   **Historic Low Unemployment:** The unemployment rate reached 2.9% in 2024, though the shrinking working-age population poses long-term risks.
*   **Aging Society:** Population is expected to fall by 16% by 2070, putting immense pressure on the pension system and healthcare.
*   **Gender Gap:** Low labor participation among women is linked to insufficient childcare and long-term care services.
*   **Disability Employment:** The disability employment gap remains one of the widest in the EU at 35.6%.

### ⚓ Innovation and Digitalization
*   **Productivity Gaps:** Labor productivity is low (67% of the EU average), with significant regional disparities between the capital and eastern regions.
*   **Digital Adoption:** Only 4.9% of Polish SMEs have adopted AI, compared to an EU average of 12.6%.
*   **Science-Business Linkage:** Collaboration between academic research and private industry remains limited, hindering the commercialization of innovation.
*   **R&D Performance:** Poland is an "emerging innovator," but needs higher R&D intensity to reach the EU average of 2.24%.

### ⚓ Energy and Sustainability
*   **Decarbonization Challenges:** Fossil fuels still account for 70% of electricity generation; the district heating sector remains 69% coal-dependent.
*   **Energy Prices:** Poland has some of the highest wholesale electricity prices in the EU, exacerbated by high taxes and grid bottlenecks.
*   **Clean Tech Hub:** Poland is a leader in lithium battery manufacturing, producing 60% of all European-made batteries.
*   **Water Resilience:** Poland faces alternating cycles of drought and floods; investment is needed in nature-based solutions and wastewater treatment.

### ⚓ Education and Social Fairness
*   **Skills Shortage:** 63% of manufacturing firms report a labor shortage; 82% of SMEs report skills shortages as a primary barrier.
*   **Basic Skills Decline:** Underachievement in mathematics among 15-year-olds has increased significantly since 2018.
*   **Healthcare Reform:** The system is highly hospital-centric; there is a critical need to shift resources toward primary care and address the shortage of general practitioners and nurses.
*   **Housing Crisis:** House prices rose by 107% since 2015, creating an acute shortage and high rental inflation in metropolitan areas.
