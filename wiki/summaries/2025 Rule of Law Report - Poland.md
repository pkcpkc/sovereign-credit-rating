# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Poland

## Context Metadata
- **Source Document:** EU Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 921 final
- **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
- **Subject:** Assessment of the rule of law situation in Poland, focusing on judicial independence, anti-corruption measures, and media freedom.
- **Key Stakeholders:** Polish Government, European Commission, Venice Commission, European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR), and various Polish judicial/civil society bodies.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report highlights a period of mixed progress in Poland. Significant strides have been made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General and improving the procedural framework for judicial appointments. However, profound concerns remain regarding the independence of the Constitutional Tribunal and the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ). While the government is actively working on legislative reforms to address "neo-judges" and media pluralism, the perceived level of judicial independence remains very low. Anti-corruption efforts have seen improvements through the operationalization of the EPPO and new police-led coordination, but the lack of a comprehensive national strategy and persistent "impunity clauses" for top executives remain critical hurdles.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System and Judicial Independence
*   **Judicial Perception:** Perceived judicial independence remains low (26% for the general public, 24% for companies), though there has been a slight increase in confidence among businesses.
*   **Action Plan Implementation:** Poland is consulting the Venice Commission on reforms for the NCJ and Constitutional Tribunal. A draft law aims to remove approximately 1,600 judges appointed via irregular procedures.
*   **Prosecution Reform:** Significant progress was achieved in separating the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General. The National Prosecution Office audited 2016–2023 cases, identifying numerous irregularities.
*   **Constitutional Tribunal Concerns:** The government continues to refuse to publish certain judgments, and the Sejm has ceased appointing judges to vacant posts, citing a lack of constitutional function.
*   **Court Efficiency:** Performance in ordinary and administrative courts remains stable, with some improvements in the rate of resolving administrative cases.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Institutional Shifts:** The Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (CBA) is being liquidated, with its resources and competencies transferred to the police, Internal Security Agency (ABW), and National Revenue Administration (KAS).
*   **EPPO Integration:** The European Public Prosecutor's Office is now fully operational in Poland to investigate crimes affecting EU financial interests.
*   **High-Level Cases:** Investigations are ongoing into large-scale corruption involving public officials, including a significant "visa scandal" and university diploma bribery.
*   **Remaining Gaps:** No comprehensive anti-corruption strategy exists, and there has been no progress on removing broad immunities or "impunity clauses" for top executives.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Regulatory Reform:** A new media law reform is planned to implement the European Media Freedom Act (EMFA), aiming to improve the impartiality of the National Broadcasting Council (KRRiT).
*   **Public Service Media:** While politically biased content has decreased, public broadcasters face instability due to the suspension of license fee funds and the ongoing liquidation process.
*   **SLAPPs and Journalist Safety:** The number of Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs) has decreased, but journalists still face risks from defamation and insult laws.
*   **Ownership Transparency:** Efforts are underway to create a media ownership database, though challenges remain regarding foreign investment and local government-owned media.

### IV. Civil Society and Checks and Balances
*   **Civic Space Rating:** The assessment of civic space in Poland was updated from "obstructed" to "narrowed" in March 2025.
*   **Legislative Consultations:** Mandatory impact assessments and public consultations for draft laws have been introduced to improve the quality of legislation.
*   **Ombudsperson Role:** The Ombudsperson continues to play a key role in checks and balances, maintaining an 'A' status by the UN Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions.
