# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Poland

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** SWD(2025) 921 final (Commission Staff Working Document)
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
*   **Jurisdiction:** Poland
*   **Reporting Body:** European Commission

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Poland highlights a period of mixed progress. Significant strides have been made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General and initiating reforms to address judicial independence, including proposals to restructure the Supreme Court and address the status of judges appointed under irregular procedures. However, perceived judicial independence remains very low, and several key legislative acts remain pending due to presidential referrals to the Constitutional Tribunal. 

In the anti-corruption sphere, Poland is moving toward a "shield" model to protect economic interests and has launched the European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO) operations. Conversely, progress is stalled regarding broad executive immunities and the introduction of lobbying rules. Media freedom shows signs of improvement in terms of pluralism and state advertising distribution, though challenges persist regarding the independence of the National Broadcasting Council (KRRiT) and the protection of journalists from SLAPPs and defamation lawsuits.

## Key Highlights

### Judicial Independence and Reform
*   **Action Plan Implementation:** Poland is consulting the Venice Commission on legislation regarding the prosecution system, the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ), and the Constitutional Tribunal.
*   **Judicial Appointments:** A draft law was proposed in April 2025 to address the status of ~1,600 judges appointed via irregular procedures post-2017.
*   **Structural Changes:** Plans are underway to dismantle the Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs to strengthen Supreme Court independence.
*   **Constitutional Tribunal:** Serious concerns persist as the government refuses to publish certain judgments, and the Sejm has ceased appointing judges to vacant posts there.

### Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Prosecutorial Reform:** Significant progress was achieved in separating the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General, aimed at ensuring functional independence.
*   **Anti-Corruption Shield:** A new law is being developed to coordinate police, Internal Security Agency (ABW), and National Revenue Administration (KAS) efforts to protect state economic interests.
*   **EPPO Integration:** The European Public Prosecutor Office is now fully operational in Poland to investigate crimes affecting EU financial interests.
*   **Whistleblower Protection:** A new law is now in force, though stakeholders call for a broader scope of protection.

### Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Media Law Reform:** The government plans to implement the European Media Freedom Act (EMFA), including stricter eligibility for the KRRiT and a move toward a state-funded model to replace the license fee.
*   **Public Service Media:** There has been a decrease in politically biased content, though management remains under "temporary liquidators."
*   **Journalist Safety:** While SLAPPs have decreased, journalists still face risks from defamation laws and harassment. The government is drafting an anti-SLAPP Directive implementation.

### Checks and Balances
*   **Legislative Quality:** New rules of procedure for the Sejm and Council of Ministers now mandate impact assessments and public consultations.
*   **ECtHR Compliance:** Poland continues to struggle with implementing ECtHR judgments, with a significant backlog and slow average implementation times.
*   **Civil Society:** The assessment of civic space improved from "obstructed" to "narrowed," with new funding programs for small NGOs.
