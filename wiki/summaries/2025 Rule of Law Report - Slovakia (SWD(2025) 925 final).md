# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Slovakia

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 925 final
*   **Issuing Body:** European Commission
*   **Date of Issue:** 2025-07-08
*   **Subject Country:** Slovakia
*   **Primary Focus:** Assessment of judicial independence, anti-corruption frameworks, media pluralism, and legislative transparency.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Slovakia highlights a complex landscape where legislative efforts to align with EU directives (specifically regarding financial interests) coexist with significant concerns over judicial independence, corruption, and media freedom. While the government has engaged in dialogue to adjust criminal codes, several high-level corruption cases have been impacted by new statutes of limitations and lower penalties. The report identifies a "deterioration" in the safety of journalists, a lack of progress on regulating lobbying, and concerns regarding the politicization of public service media. Significant challenges remain in the efficiency of administrative courts and the perceived lack of transparency in the fast-track legislative process.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System and Judicial Independence
*   **Perceived Independence:** Public and corporate confidence in judicial independence remains low, with only 32% of the general population perceiving it as "fairly or very good."
*   **Judicial Safeguards:** There is a noted lack of progress in ensuring independent dismissal guarantees for members of the Judicial Council, who are currently subject to significant executive/legislative influence.
*   **Criminal Liability:** While some procedural clarifications were made for "abuse of law" charges against judges, stakeholders warn of a "chilling effect" on independent decision-making due to a lack of sufficient safeguards.
*   **Prosecutorial Powers:** The Prosecutor General retains broad powers to annul final decisions of lower-ranking prosecutors, a point of ongoing concern for the independence of investigations.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Institutional Changes:** The dissolution of the Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO) and National Crime Agency (NAKA) has led to delays in investigations and a drop in the number of prosecuted corruption cases.
*   **Sanctions and Statutes:** Recent legislative changes have resulted in several high-level corruption cases becoming statute-barred or being reclassified as minor misdemeanors, significantly impacting the ability to seize assets.
*   **Lobbying and Asset Declarations:** Despite political commitments, there is still no formal legislation regulating lobbying or a unified electronic system for asset declarations.
*   **Whistleblower Protection:** While a framework exists, its effectiveness is hampered by low reporting rates and a recent fine imposed on the Ministry of Interior for improperly suspending protected whistleblowers.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Public Service Media:** The creation of the new STVR entity is viewed by stakeholders as an increase in politicization and a regression in editorial independence.
*   **Journalist Safety:** No progress has been made on improving the physical safety of journalists; the environment is described as "increasingly hostile" with rising verbal attacks and SLAPP-like litigation.
*   **Information Access:** Amendments to the Freedom of Information Act have introduced potential "paywalls" for extensive information searches, leading to a Constitutional Court suspension of those specific provisions.

### IV. Checks and Balances
*   **Fast-Track Legislation:** Slovakia frequently uses fast-track procedures, with 28 out of 58 laws enacted in 2024 being adopted under circumstances that limited public participation.
*   **Constitutional Concerns:** Proposed amendments to the Slovak Constitution have been postponed due to concerns regarding the principle of primacy of EU law.
*   **Human Rights Implementation:** Slovakia faces a backlog of European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) judgments, with a low rate of implementation for leading judgments from the past decade.
