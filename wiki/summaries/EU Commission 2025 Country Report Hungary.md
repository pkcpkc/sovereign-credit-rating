# Summary of: EU Commission 2025 Country Report: Hungary

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Country Report - Hungary (SWD(2025) 217 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (covering data up to 2024 and forecasts for 2025-2026)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Commission Staff Working Document / Country Report

## Executive Summary

The European Commission’s 2025 Country Report on Hungary highlights a challenging economic landscape characterized by subdued growth, persistent inflationary pressures, and significant structural vulnerabilities. While GDP growth is projected to rebound slowly in 2026, driven by new manufacturing capacities in the electric vehicle sector, the immediate outlook remains uncertain due to weak external demand and domestic policy uncertainty. 

Key fiscal concerns include a high public debt-to-GDP ratio (73.5% in 2024) and elevated debt-servicing costs, with the Commission identifying high medium-term sustainability risks. The government deficit, while decreasing to 4.9% of GDP in 2024, is projected to remain elevated in 2025 and 2026, complicating Hungary’s path to comply with EU fiscal rules. The report criticizes the lack of a realistic medium-term budgetary framework and the frequent use of emergency powers, which undermine policy credibility and predictability.

Structurally, Hungary faces challenges regarding low labour productivity, which has stagnated for 15 years, and a skills mismatch that hinders innovation. The business environment is described as distorted by heavy state intervention, including subsidized lending, sector-specific taxes, and administrative price controls, which crowd out private actors and limit competition. Energy security remains a critical vulnerability, with Hungary maintaining high dependence on Russian fossil fuels and facing hurdles in grid flexibility and renewable integration. Socially, poverty rates are rising, particularly among children and vulnerable groups, while healthcare and education systems face resource constraints and inefficiencies.

## Key Highlights

- **Economic Growth and Inflation**: GDP grew by only 0.5% in 2024 after a contraction in 2023. Growth is forecast to reach 0.8% in 2025 and 2.5% in 2026, driven by EV battery production. Inflation fell to 3.7% in 2024 but picked up in early 2025 due to underlying price pressures and food costs.
- **Fiscal Position and Debt Sustainability**: Public debt rose to 73.5% of GDP in 2024. The government deficit decreased to 4.9% of GDP but is projected to remain high (4.6% in 2025, 4.7% in 2026). The Commission notes high medium-term fiscal sustainability risks, exacerbated by aging demographics and rigid spending. Net expenditure growth in 2025 is projected at 6.1%, exceeding EU recommendations, though partially justified by defense spending under the national escape clause.
- **Business Environment and State Intervention**: The report identifies widespread state interventions distorting markets, including subsidized loans (38.5% of new SME loans), administratively controlled mortgage rates, and high sector-specific taxes (banking, retail, energy). Regulatory volatility, frequent use of emergency decrees, and weak competition enforcement hinder investment. Corruption remains a significant barrier, with 78% of companies perceiving it as widespread.
- **Energy Security and Green Transition**: Hungary remains heavily dependent on Russian fossil fuels (>70% of gas, >80% of oil). While solar power generation has surged (25% of electricity production), the grid lacks flexibility and storage, leading to price volatility. Progress on wind energy is stalled, and fossil fuel subsidies remain high (1.01% of GDP). The transition to net-zero technologies is hampered by labor shortages and regulatory barriers.
- **Structural Reforms and Productivity**: Labour productivity is among the lowest in the EU and has not improved in 15 years. This is driven by low-skilled employment increases rather than innovation. The education system struggles with early school leaving, teacher shortages, and low tertiary attainment (32.3% for ages 25-34). R&D intensity is well below the EU average (1.38% vs 2.24%).
- **Social Challenges and Poverty**: The at-risk-of-poverty or social exclusion (AROPE) rate increased to 20.2% in 2024. Poverty is rising among children and vulnerable groups. Social protection adequacy is low, with minimum income benefits failing to keep pace with inflation. Healthcare spending is among the lowest in the EU (4.1% of GDP), contributing to low life expectancy and high treatable mortality.
- **Financial Sector**: The banking sector is resilient with high capitalization (CET1 ratio 18.8%) and low non-performing loans. However, the high share of government bonds in bank assets poses systemic risks. Capital markets are underdeveloped, with stock market capitalization at only 17.5% of GDP, and retail investment is skewed towards tax-free government bonds rather than equities.
- **EU Funds and RRP Implementation**: Implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) has stalled, with 0% of milestones achieved as of mid-2025. Delays are attributed to anti-corruption deficiencies and weak control mechanisms. Access to Cohesion Policy funds is also limited by unfulfilled enabling conditions related to the rule of law.
