# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Romania

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** SWD(2025) 923 final (Commission Staff Working Document)
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
*   **Reporting Authority:** European Commission
*   **Subject Country:** Romania
*   **Key Focus Areas:** Justice system independence, anti-corruption measures, media freedom, and civic space.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Romania highlights a period of mixed progress. While the government has made significant strides in completing the Venice Commission's recommendations regarding Justice Laws and improving judicial HR recruitment, several systemic challenges persist. Key issues include a decrease in perceived judicial independence among the public, the continued use of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs), and a lack of progress on lobbying regulations for Members of Parliament. The anti-corruption framework remains active, though it faces a significant "statute of limitations" crisis caused by recent court rulings. Additionally, the media landscape is characterized by high risks regarding ownership transparency and political influence, while civil society faces a "narrowed" space despite the adoption of a new Strategy for Open Government.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System and Judicial Independence
*   **Venice Commission Compliance:** Significant progress was made in aligning the Justice Laws with Venice Commission recommendations, including assessments by high-level expert panels.
*   **Judicial Independence Perception:** Public perception of judicial independence dropped to 44% in 2025 (from 52% in 2024), while company perception dropped to 51%.
*   **Prosecutorial Safeguards:** The Ministry of Justice is drafting a law to extend the mandate of high-ranking prosecutors from three to five years.
*   **Human Resources:** Occupancy rates for judge positions reached 83% in early 2025, though vacancies remain a "continuous concern" due to high workloads.
*   **Legal Aid:** Expansion of legal aid to non-profit legal persons and groups in special situations was noted, though data transparency remains lacking.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Strategy Implementation:** The 2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy remains on track; a new strategy is being drafted for the post-2025 period.
*   **Statute of Limitations Crisis:** A major systemic risk has emerged where courts have closed numerous corruption cases and annulled convictions following High Court of Cassation and Justice (HCCJ) rulings on the *lex mitior* principle.
*   **Agency Performance:** The National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA) maintains a stable occupancy rate (>85%) and continues to issue indictments, despite high workloads.
*   **Integrity and Lobbying:** While some rules on gifts were adopted in March 2025, there has been **no progress** on introducing lobbying rules for Members of Parliament.
*   **Asset Declarations:** A Constitutional Court ruling on May 29, 2025, declared it unconstitutional to require officials to declare assets of spouses and children, creating a potential transparency gap.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Public Service Media:** No progress was made in enhancing the independent governance or editorial independence of public service media.
*   **Media Ownership:** Transparency remains at "high risk" (72%), with significant difficulties in identifying ownership of online news sites.
*   **Political Influence:** Political parties spent approximately EUR 24.5 million on media in 2023, often using advertising agencies to blur the line between news and paid content.
*   **Journalist Safety:** New alerts regarding harassment and intimidation of journalists were recorded in 2025, alongside ongoing concerns about SLAPPs.

### IV. Checks and Balances / Civic Space
*   **Public Consultations:** Some progress was made in training officials on best practices, but CSOs still view consultations as "box-ticking" exercises with tight deadlines.
*   **Legislative Unpredictability:** The frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (156 GEOs in 2024) remains a primary concern for businesses and civil society.
*   **Open Government:** A new Strategy for Open Government (2025-2030) was adopted in April 2025 to address the narrowing civic space.
*   **Human Rights Institutions:** No progress was made on obtaining GANHRI accreditation for the Romanian Institute for Human Rights (RIHR) due to resource cuts and structural issues.
