# Summary of: Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Criteria (Effective 15 September 2025)

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: Sovereign Rating Criteria
- **Effective Period**: 15 September 2025 to 27 April 2026
- **Issuer**: Fitch Ratings
- **Document Type**: Master Criteria / Methodology

## Executive Summary

This document outlines Fitch Ratings' updated methodology for assigning and maintaining sovereign credit ratings, effective from September 15, 2025. The criteria define the framework for assessing the capacity and willingness of sovereign issuers to honor their debt obligations, distinguishing between Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) Issuer Default Ratings (IDRs).

The core of Fitch's analysis rests on four analytical pillars: **Structural Features**, **Macroeconomic Performance/Policies/Prospects**, **Public Finances**, and **External Finances**. The methodology employs a quantitative **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)**, based on an Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression of 18 key variables, which serves as the starting point for ratings. This model is adjusted via a **Qualitative Overlay (QO)** to account for factors not captured by the model, such as geopolitical risk, financial sector stability, and policy credibility.

Key updates and emphases in this criteria version include:
*   **Weighting:** Structural Features carry the highest weight in the SRM (53.7%), followed by Public Finances (19.1%), External Finances (17.3%), and Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%).
*   **Climate Risk Integration:** Introduction of **Sovereign Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)** as a screening tool. Sovereigns with a Climate.VS score of 50 or higher in 2035 are subject to additional analysis. The framework assesses physical risks (e.g., heatwaves, floods) and transition risks (fossil fuel dependence, green energy costs).
*   **Financial Sector Monitoring:** Enhanced focus on macro-prudential risks using the Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI) and Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI).
*   **Rating Adjustments:** The QO allows for notch adjustments of ±2 per pillar, capped at an overall ±3 adjustment from the SRM output, with exceptions for crisis situations or recent defaults.
*   **Loss Severity:** For issuers rated 'B+' or below, Fitch incorporates a view on potential recoveries via Recovery Ratings (RRs) to notch instrument ratings up or down from the IDR.

## Key Highlights

- **Analytical Pillars and SRM Weighting**: Fitch's Sovereign Rating Model (SRM) is built on four pillars. Structural Features dominate the model with a 53.7% weight, driven by governance indicators, GDP per capita, and share in world GDP. Public Finances (19.1%), External Finances (17.3%), and Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%) follow. The SRM generates a score calibrated to the Long-Term FC IDR scale ('AAA' to 'CCC+').

- **Qualitative Overlay (QO) Mechanism**: To adjust for qualitative factors absent in the SRM, Fitch applies a QO with a potential notch adjustment of +2/-2 for each of the four pillars. The total adjustment is generally capped at +3/-3 notches. Exceptions allowing wider adjustments include countries in crisis, recovering from crisis, or with a default history within the last five years.

- **Structural Features Criteria**: This pillar includes governance indicators (using World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators), GDP per capita (as a proxy for development), share in world GDP (small economies are more vulnerable), and years since default/restructuring. Qualitative adjustments consider political stability, geopolitical risks, financial sector health (via BSI and MPI), and other structural factors like business environment quality.

- **Macroeconomic Performance and Policy**: The SRM considers real GDP growth volatility, consumer price inflation, and real GDP growth. The QO assesses policy credibility and flexibility, particularly for fixed exchange rate regimes or dollarized economies. It also evaluates the medium-term GDP growth outlook and macroeconomic stability, including unemployment and sectoral imbalances.

- **Public Finances Assessment**: Key SRM variables include gross general government debt/GDP, interest payments/revenue, fiscal balance/GDP, and foreign-currency debt share. The QO evaluates fiscal financing flexibility (market access, debt structure), debt sustainability (using Fitch’s proprietary Debt Dynamics Model), and fiscal structure (revenue base breadth, budgetary rigidity). Contingent liabilities (e.g., banking sector support, SOE debts) are assessed for potential crystallization.

- **External Finances Analysis**: The SRM incorporates reserve-currency flexibility (based on IMF COFER data), sovereign net foreign assets, commodity dependence, FX reserves, external interest service, and current account balance plus FDI. The QO assesses external financing flexibility (market access resilience), external debt sustainability (net external debt trends), and vulnerability to shocks (balance-sheet structure, remittances). The International Liquidity Ratio (ILR) is used to gauge short-term external liquidity risk.

- **Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)**: A new screening tool to identify sovereigns with high exposure to climate risks. It aggregates physical risks (heat, drought, storms, floods, sea-level rise) and transition risks (fossil fuel dependence, green energy costs) into a 0-100 score for 2025, 2035, and 2050. A score of 50+ in 2035 triggers additional analytical scrutiny. If climate risks are material and not captured by SRM/QO, a notch adjustment may be applied under "Other structural factors."

- **Distressed Debt Exchanges (DDE) and Default Definition**: A DDE is defined by a material reduction in debt terms (principal, interest, maturity, currency) designed to avoid traditional default. Fitch considers a payment failure a default after a grace period (typically 30 days). Sovereigns in default are rated 'RD' (Restricted Default). Ratings are lifted out of 'RD' when the default is cured (payment made or restructuring completed with normalized creditor relations).

- **Local vs. Foreign Currency Ratings**: LC and FC ratings are typically aligned. Differentiation occurs if there is an expectation of preferential treatment, driven by differences in debt burdens, domestic capital market depth, or access to foreign currency. For short-term ratings, Fitch maps LT to ST ratings, choosing the higher of two options for LC ratings and for FC ratings if the sovereign has reserve-currency flexibility or a robust ILR (>100%).

- **Recovery Ratings and Instrument Ratings**: For sovereigns with LT IDRs of 'B+' or below, Fitch assigns Recovery Ratings (RR1 to RR6) to reflect expected loss severity in default. Senior unsecured instrument ratings may be notched up to +3 or down to -2 notches from the IDR based on these recovery prospects. For investment-grade issuers ('BB-' and above), instrument ratings generally align with the IDR, assuming average recovery prospects.
