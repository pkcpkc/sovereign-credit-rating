# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Italy

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Italy (SWD(2025) 912 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (Reference period covers developments from mid-2024 to mid-2025)
- **Issuer**: European Commission, Directorate-General for Justice and Consumers
- **Document Type**: Commission Staff Working Document / Country Report

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Italy highlights significant progress in judicial reform and anti-corruption frameworks, while identifying persistent challenges in political financing, media governance, and the protection of fundamental freedoms. A comprehensive justice system reform is now in place, with the High Council for the Judiciary implementing new directives on magistrate appointments and performance. However, a controversial draft constitutional reform separating the careers of judges and prosecutors faces judicial opposition and parliamentary scrutiny.

In the anti-corruption domain, the National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) has strengthened its resources and issued guidelines on revolving doors, yet comprehensive rules on conflicts of interest and lobbying remain pending. Public procurement continues to be a high-risk area for organized crime infiltration, despite digital safeguards. Regarding media pluralism, the regulator AGCOM functions independently, and public service broadcaster RAI’s funding was stabilized, though governance reforms are stalled. Concerns remain regarding journalist safety, including spyware targeting and SLAPPs, as well as new restrictions on accessing judicial information.

On institutional checks and balances, the draft constitutional reform aiming to enhance governmental stability and introduce direct election of the Prime Minister is advancing. Stakeholders express concern over the frequent use of urgency decrees and a new Security Law that may impact civic space. No progress was made in establishing a National Human Rights Institution, and the implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments remains slow.

## Key Highlights

- **[Judicial Reform Implementation]**: The High Council for the Judiciary has implemented key directives from the March 2024 justice reform, including rules on out-of-tenure magistrates, performance appraisals, and judicial management transparency, though persistent staff shortages remain.
- **[Constitutional Reform Controversy]**: The Chamber of Deputies approved a constitutional reform to separate judges and prosecutors, but it faces strong opposition from the judiciary, who argue it undermines autonomy, and is currently under Senate scrutiny.
- **[Anti-Corruption Progress]**: ANAC updated the National Anti-Corruption Plan and strengthened its staffing, issuing guidelines on revolving doors. However, comprehensive legislation on lobbying and conflicts of interest for political office holders remains stalled in Parliament.
- **[Media Funding Stabilization]**: The decision to reduce RAI’s licence fee was reversed in the 2025 Budget Law, maintaining fees at EUR 90 and preserving the broadcaster’s financial resources, though structural governance reforms are still debated in the Senate.
- **[Journalist Safety Concerns]**: Journalists face increasing threats, including spyware targeting (Paragon case) and legal harassment (SLAPPs). While protection protocols exist, incidents of intimidation have risen, and reforms to defamation laws have not advanced.
- **[Security Law and Civic Space]**: The adoption of a new Security Law to combat terrorism and organized crime has raised concerns among UN and Council of Europe bodies regarding its potential impact on freedom of assembly and expression, with civil society rating Italy’s civic space as 'narrowed'.
- **[Political Financing Stagnation]**: No progress was made in addressing the practice of channeling political donations through foundations and associations, with draft laws on party financing remaining undiscussed in Parliament.
- **[Institutional Gaps]**: No concrete measures have been taken to establish a National Human Rights Institution (NHRI) in line with UN Paris Principles, and the implementation of leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights remains a significant backlog issue.
