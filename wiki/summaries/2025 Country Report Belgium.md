## Executive Summary

The 2025 Country Report for Belgium, issued by the European Commission, provides a comprehensive assessment of the country's economic, social, and structural challenges. The report highlights that while Belgium maintains strong institutional frameworks and a sound banking sector, it faces significant headwinds from high public debt, deteriorating fiscal balances, and structural rigidities in its labor market and business environment.

Key findings indicate that economic growth is projected to decelerate, with GDP growth expected to slow to 0.8% in 2025 due to external shocks and declining exports. Public finances are under pressure, with the headline deficit projected to increase to 5.4% of GDP in 2025, driven by rising age-related costs and defense spending. The report emphasizes the urgent need for structural reforms, particularly in pension sustainability, long-term care efficiency, and labor market activation.

A critical area of concern is the complexity of Belgium's tax system, which creates disincentives for work and investment. The report calls for a shift in the tax burden away from labor, simplification of regulations, and improved coordination between federal and regional levels of government. Additionally, the transition to a green economy requires accelerated deployment of renewable energy, reduction of fossil fuel subsidies, and investment in sustainable infrastructure.

## Key Highlights

- **Moderate Economic Growth**: Economic growth slowed to 1% in 2024 and is projected to decelerate further to 0.8% in 2025, driven by robust private consumption but hindered by declining exports and external shocks.
- **Rising Public Finances Pressure**: The headline deficit is expected to increase to 5.4% of GDP in 2025, with public debt projected to rise to 126.4% by 2035 without policy action, largely due to aging-related costs and defense spending.
- **Labor Market Challenges**: Despite a record employment rate, low activity rates persist, particularly among low-skilled workers, migrants, and older individuals. High labor taxes and complex social benefits create disincentives to work, contributing to high vacancy rates and skills mismatches.
- **Fiscal Framework Weaknesses**: Belgium is the only EU country without a fully developed multiannual fiscal planning framework at the national level, risking undermining of fiscal commitments. Coordination between federal and regional entities remains incomplete.
- **Structural Reforms Needed**: The report calls for comprehensive reforms to improve the efficiency of public spending, strengthen the pension system, and simplify the business environment. Key areas include reducing regulatory burdens, improving access to finance for SMEs, and accelerating the green transition.
- **Green Transition Priorities**: Belgium faces challenges in meeting its 2030 climate targets, with low renewable energy deployment and high fossil fuel dependency. Urgent actions are needed to phase out fossil fuel subsidies, accelerate renewable energy deployment, and improve energy efficiency in buildings.
- **Social Fairness and Inclusion**: Significant regional disparities exist, with higher poverty rates in Brussels and Wallonia compared to Flanders. The report emphasizes the need for targeted policies to integrate disadvantaged groups, including people with disabilities and migrants, into the labor market.
