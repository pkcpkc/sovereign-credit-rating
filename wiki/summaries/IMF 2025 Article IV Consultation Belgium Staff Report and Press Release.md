# Summary of: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation: Belgium Staff Report and Press Release

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation: Belgium Press Release and Staff Report
- **Effective Period**: March 2025 (Discussions ended February 4, 2025; Report completed March 3, 2025; Board concluded March 18, 2025)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation Report

## Executive Summary

The IMF’s 2025 Article IV consultation with Belgium highlights an economy that has demonstrated resilience to multiple shocks but now faces significant structural challenges, particularly regarding fiscal sustainability and labor market efficiency. While growth slowed to 1.0% in 2024, with core inflation remaining sticky due to automatic wage indexation, the broader financial sector remains stable with moderate systemic risks.

The central theme of the report is the urgent need for sustained fiscal consolidation. With public debt at 104.1% of GDP and projected to rise to 123% by 2030 under unchanged policies, the IMF supports the new Belgian government’s plan for a seven-year adjustment under the new EU Economic Governance Framework (EGF). Staff recommends an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of approximately 0.6 percentage points of GDP until 2031 to bring the deficit durably below 3% of GDP and put debt on a downward path. This adjustment must be balanced with growth-enhancing reforms, including rationalizing current spending (social benefits and public wages) while preserving or increasing public investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education.

Structural reforms are deemed essential to boost potential growth, which has been sluggish. Key recommendations include reforming the wage-setting mechanism (specifically automatic indexation and the 1996 wage law) to improve competitiveness, enhancing labor force participation through tax and social benefit adjustments, and deepening product market reforms to foster productivity. Additionally, the report emphasizes the need for coordinated efforts between federal and regional entities to meet climate goals and strengthen the fiscal framework through a revitalized fiscal council.

## Key Highlights

- **Fiscal Consolidation Strategy**: The IMF supports a 7-year fiscal adjustment plan under the EGF, requiring an annual structural primary balance reduction of ~0.6% of GDP. This aims to lower the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2031 and reduce debt to ~78% of GDP, contrasting with a "no policy change" scenario where debt would reach 123% by 2030.
- **Wage and Labor Market Reforms**: Automatic wage indexation and the 1996 wage law are identified as drivers of rising unit labor costs and declining competitiveness. The IMF recommends abolishing automatic indexation and reforming the wage corridor. Other labor reforms should focus on widening the income gap between work and non-work, limiting unemployment benefit duration, and reducing hiring/firing costs.
- **Financial Sector Stability**: Systemic risks remain moderate. The banking sector is well-capitalized with low non-performing loans (2.1%). However, risks are shifting toward commercial real estate (CRE) due to price contractions and rising vacancies, while household indebtedness remains high. The IMF advises maintaining current capital buffer and mortgage lending limits.
- **External Position**: Belgium’s external position was assessed as weaker than implied by fundamentals in 2024, with a real effective exchange rate (REER) overvaluation estimated between 4.9% and 17.4%. The current account deficit narrowed to 0.3% of GDP in 2024. Despite this, the large positive Net International Investment Position (57.3% of GDP) provides a buffer against external shocks.
- **Public Investment and Efficiency**: Fiscal adjustment should prioritize rationalizing current spending rather than cutting investment. The IMF stresses improving the efficiency of public spending in healthcare, education, and infrastructure. For education, reforms should align curricula with market needs and improve teacher time usage to potentially save 1.4 percentage points of GDP while maintaining outcomes.
- **Climate Transition Coordination**: Significant efforts are needed to meet 2030 climate targets, as current projections fall short of EU Effort Sharing Regulation goals. The IMF calls for a coherent national strategy consolidating federal and regional efforts, complemented by carbon taxation and phasing out fossil fuel subsidies.
- **Sovereign Risk Assessment**: The overall risk of sovereign stress is assessed as "moderate." While debt levels are high and rising, risk is mitigated by a long average debt maturity (11.1 years), a large domestic investor base, and high-quality debt management practices.
