# Summary of: S&P Global Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2017 Sovereign Methodology
- **Effective Period**: December 18, 2017 (Immediate)
- **Issuer**: S&P Global Ratings
- **Document Type**: Criteria

## Executive Summary

S&P Global Ratings published its updated Sovereign Rating Methodology on December 18, 2017, replacing previous criteria from 2014 and 2013. The methodology provides a framework for assigning issuer credit ratings (ICR) to sovereign governments and monetary authorities, focusing on their ability and willingness to service financial obligations to nonofficial commercial creditors. The framework is built on five core pillars: Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, and Monetary assessments.

Key updates from previous versions include limiting the uplift of local-currency ratings to no more than one notch above foreign-currency ratings, basing initial fiscal assessments on changes in net general government debt rather than gross debt, and utilizing current account payments (CAP) instead of receipts in certain external assessment ratios. The methodology employs a six-point numerical scale for each pillar, combining quantitative data with qualitative judgments to determine an indicative rating level. Supplemental adjustment factors and caps may further modify this indicative level based on extreme risks (such as high debt burdens or institutional weakness) or strengths (such as large liquid assets). The document also outlines specific treatments for monetary unions, monetary authorities, and various debt structures.

## Key Highlights

- **Five-Pillar Framework**: The methodology assesses sovereign creditworthiness through five distinct areas: Institutional (governance and stability), Economic (income, growth, diversity), External (liquidity and position), Fiscal (performance and debt burden), and Monetary (policy credibility and exchange rate).
- **Updated Rating Mechanics**: Significant changes include capping the local-currency rating uplift at one notch above the foreign-currency rating, shifting the fiscal baseline to net debt changes, and using current account payments for external debt ratios.
- **Indicative Rating Matrix**: Ratings are derived by averaging the Institutional and Economic profiles (Institutional/Economic Profile) and the External, Fiscal, and Monetary profiles (Flexibility/Performance Profile) to identify an indicative rating level, typically within one notch of the final foreign-currency ICR.
- **Supplemental Adjustments and Caps**: Specific conditions, such as extremely weak external liquidity, extremely high fiscal debt, or very high institutional risk, can trigger supplemental adjustments that lower the rating by more than one notch or impose absolute caps on the maximum achievable rating.
- **Monetary Authority Treatment**: Ratings for monetary authorities within unions are determined by combining the weighted average rating of member sovereigns with an uplift based on the authority's monetary flexibility, capped at four notches above the average.
