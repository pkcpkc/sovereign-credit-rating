# Summary of: France 2025 Article IV Consultation: Press Release, Staff Report, and Executive Director Statement

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF (2025) France Country Report
- **Effective Period**: July 2025 (Consultation concluded July 11, 2025; Report completed June 25, 2025)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation Report, Press Release, Staff Report, Informational Annex, Statement by Executive Director

## Executive Summary

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with France, acknowledging the economy's resilience amidst high global uncertainty. While the French economy demonstrated strength in 2024, driven by the Paris Olympics and robust labor market conditions, growth is projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025 due to trade tensions, weak external demand, and policy uncertainty. A primary concern for the IMF is France's high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, coupled with significant fiscal slippages in recent years. The IMF recommends a credible and well-designed package of structural fiscal measures to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029, emphasizing rationalization of current spending across central, local, and social security levels rather than increasing the already high tax burden.

Structural reforms are identified as crucial for boosting productivity and facilitating fiscal consolidation. The IMF highlights France's potential in the green and digital transitions, particularly through Artificial Intelligence (AI) adoption and innovation. Recommendations include easing regulatory burdens, enhancing access to finance for productive firms, and advancing the EU Savings and Investment Union. The financial sector remains resilient with strong capital and liquidity buffers, as confirmed by the 2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP), though risks from corporate indebtedness and commercial real estate require continued monitoring. The French authorities largely concur with staff views, emphasizing their commitment to the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) and ongoing efforts to simplify regulations and support reindustrialization.

## Key Highlights

- **[IMF Executive Board Conclusion]**: The IMF Executive Board completed the Article IV Consultation on July 11, 2025, welcoming France's economic resilience, robust labor market, and progress in disinflation. Directors emphasized the urgent need to strengthen public finances through a credible package of measures and to pursue structural reforms to foster sustainable growth amid high debt levels and external headwinds.
- **[Economic Outlook and Risks]**: Real GDP growth is projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025, recovering to 1% in 2026. Risks are skewed to the downside due to deepening geoeconomic fragmentation, trade tensions, and domestic policy uncertainty. Upsides could arise from eased trade tensions and renewed structural reform momentum.
- **[Fiscal Policy and Debt Sustainability]**: France's public debt reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, with a deficit of 5.8%. The IMF recommends a frontloaded structural fiscal effort of 1.1% of GDP in 2026, followed by 0.9% annually, to place debt on a downward path. The authorities' Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) aims to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029, but implementation risks remain high due to unidentified measures and potential revenue shortfalls.
- **[Structural Reforms and Productivity]**: Advancing structural reforms is critical to boost productivity. The IMF encourages France to leverage its position in digital and green transitions, support innovation (including AI), ease regulatory burdens, and enhance access to finance for high-growth firms. Deepening the EU single market and advancing the Savings and Investment Union are highlighted as key enablers.
- **[Financial Sector Resilience]**: The 2025 FSAP confirms the French banking sector's resilience, with healthy capital (19.8% average regulatory capital ratio) and liquidity buffers (LCR 145%, NSFR 116%). Risks from corporate indebtedness and commercial real estate are manageable, but close monitoring of non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) and cyber/climate risks is advised.
- **[Labor Market and Social Policies]**: The labor market remains robust with high participation (74.6%), but productivity growth is weak. Recommendations include enhancing education, promoting women's and migrants' labor force participation, reforming unemployment benefits to enhance work incentives, and supporting healthy aging in the context of an aging population.
- **[Authorities' Response]**: The French authorities, represented by Executive Director Arnaud Buisse and economic ministers, broadly agree with staff assessments. They reaffirm their commitment to the MTFSP, highlighting ongoing spending reviews, increased defense spending, and efforts to simplify regulations and support reindustrialization through AI and green technologies.
