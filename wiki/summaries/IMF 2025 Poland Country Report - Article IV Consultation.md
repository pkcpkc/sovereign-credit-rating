# Summary of: IMF (2025) Poland Country Report

## Context Metadata
- **Source:** International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Date of Report:** December 16, 2024 (Press Release: January 21, 2025)
- **Subject:** 2024 Article IV Consultation for the Republic of Poland
- **Key Participants:** IMF Staff, Polish Government Officials, National Bank of Poland (NBP)

## Executive Summary
The IMF concludes that Poland's economy is in a recovery phase, with growth expected to accelerate to 3.5% in 2025-2026, primarily driven by domestic demand and the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds. While inflation has declined significantly from its 2023 peak, it remains above the central bank's target due to a tight labor market and strong wage growth. The report highlights a widening fiscal deficit (estimated at 5.9% of GDP in 2024) caused by permanent increases in public sector wages and high defense spending. The IMF recommends a rebalanced policy mix: shifting from tight monetary policy to fiscal consolidation to rebuild buffers, stabilizing debt around 60% of GDP, and implementing structural reforms to address population aging and the climate transition.

## Key Highlights

*   **Economic Growth and Outlook** [#growth]
    *   2024 growth is estimated at 2.8%, with a positive outlook for 2025-2026 (projected ~3.5%) due to NGEU fund absorption.
    *   Medium-term growth is expected to moderate to slightly below 3% as the impact of EU funds unwinds and population aging takes effect.

*   **Inflation and Monetary Policy** [#inflation]
    *   Headline inflation peaked in early 2023 and is expected to reach the target range (2.5±1%) by late 2025.
    *   The NBP maintains a tight stance (5.75% policy rate), with potential for gradual cuts in mid-2025 if wage growth decelerates.

*   **Fiscal Position and Debt** [#fiscal]
    *   The fiscal deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, placing Poland under the EU's Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP).
    *   The authorities have introduced a Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) to reduce the deficit to 2.9% by 2028 and stabilize debt at 60% of GDP.

*   **Financial Sector Stability** [#banking]
    *   The banking system is resilient with high capital and liquidity buffers.
    *   The IMF recommends eliminating the mortgage credit holiday (ending 2024) and redesigning the bank asset tax to remove distortions against private credit.

*   **Structural Reforms and Climate** [#structural]
    *   Critical needs identified: increasing labor supply (integrating refugees/women), fostering innovation through private equity, and decarbonizing the economy.
    *   Poland aims to halve coal-fueled power generation by 2030, requiring significant investment in renewable infrastructure.

*   **Migration and Labor** [#labor]
    *   Ukrainian refugees have significantly bolstered the labor supply; however, integration challenges (language, skills) persist.
    *   A new migration strategy adopted in October 2024 aims to streamline labor market access for foreigners.
