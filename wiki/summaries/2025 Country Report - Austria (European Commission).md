# Summary of: 2025 Country Report - Austria (European Commission)

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 220 final, accompanying Recommendation for a Council Recommendation on the economic, social, employment, structural and budgetary policies of Austria.
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (with forecasts and projections through 2026 and long-term assessments to 2070).
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Country Report (European Semester)

## Executive Summary

This 2025 Country Report on Austria, published by the European Commission on June 4, 2025, highlights a significant economic downturn, with Austria entering its third consecutive year of recession. GDP is projected to contract by 0.3% in 2025, driven by weak industrial production, declining exports, and broad-based cost pressures. The labor market remains resilient with an unemployment rate of 5.3%, though structural challenges such as skills shortages, a shrinking working-age population, and declining labor productivity growth persist.

Fiscally, Austria faces substantial pressure. The government deficit is projected to exceed 4% of GDP in 2025, well above the EU treaty threshold, with the gross debt ratio rising to 84.0% of GDP. Public spending is heavily influenced by inflation indexation and aging-related costs, particularly in pensions and healthcare. The newly formed government has committed to fiscal consolidation measures aiming for EUR 6.4 billion in savings in 2025, with a Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan submitted to the Commission.

The report emphasizes the need for structural reforms to boost competitiveness and sustainability. Key areas include simplifying the regulatory environment, reducing administrative burdens, and improving access to risk capital for startups. In energy, Austria has made progress in renewable generation (87% of electricity in 2024) but faces challenges in grid expansion and high energy costs. Socially, while the welfare system is strong, poverty risks are rising among children, single parents, and those with migrant backgrounds, requiring targeted policy interventions.

## Key Highlights

- **Economic Recession Continues**: Austria is experiencing a three-year recession, with GDP expected to fall by 0.3% in 2025 after declines of 1.2% in 2024 and 1% in 2023. Industrial production dropped by 5% in 2024, and goods exports declined by 5.9%, exacerbated by lost cost competitiveness and potential trade tariffs.

- **Fiscal Deficit and Debt Concerns**: The government deficit is projected to be 4.4% of GDP in 2025 and 4.2% in 2026, remaining above the 3% treaty threshold. Gross government debt is projected to rise from 81.8% of GDP in 2024 to 84.0% in 2025. The new government has committed to consolidation measures, targeting EUR 6.4 billion in savings for 2025.

- **Labor Market Resilience Amid Structural Issues**: Despite the recession, the unemployment rate is contained at 5.3% in 2025, and the employment rate is stable at 77.2%. However, labor productivity growth has slowed significantly since 2008, and skills shortages remain a major constraint, particularly in ICT and care sectors. The working-age population began declining in 2024.

- **High Energy Costs and Transition Challenges**: Austria's electricity price for large consumers is among the highest in the EU. While 87% of electricity was generated from renewables in 2024, grid expansion costs are rising, and permitting procedures remain a bottleneck. Russian gas imports ended in early 2025, requiring diversification of supply sources.

- **Skills and Education Gaps**: Basic skills among students are worsening, with underachievement rates rising in PISA assessments. Adult participation in training is stagnant and below national targets. There is a critical shortage of teachers, with many approaching retirement, and a gap in digital and advanced technical skills among the workforce.

- **Social Protection Strain**: The at-risk-of-poverty or social exclusion (AROPE) rate increased to 16.9% in 2024. Severe material and social deprivation doubled since 2022. Vulnerable groups, including children, single parents, and people with migrant backgrounds, face disproportionately high poverty risks.

- **Regulatory and Business Environment Barriers**: Extensive bureaucracy and complex regulatory landscapes hinder investment. Austria ranks high in services trade restrictiveness, particularly in professional services and retail. Permitting procedures for industrial and renewable projects are lengthy, averaging 22-23 months.

- **Aging-Related Fiscal Pressure**: Age-related public spending is projected to rise from 28% of GDP in 2024 to 30% in 2070, driven by healthcare and long-term care costs. Pension expenditure is expected to increase, partly due to low effective retirement ages compared to the EU average.

- **Green and Digital Transition Needs**: Austria performs well in R&D expenditure (3.29% of GDP) but struggles to translate this into high-impact innovation and business dynamism. Access to venture capital is limited, and institutional investor participation is low. Digital infrastructure, particularly broadband coverage in rural areas, lags behind the EU average.

- **Climate Adaptation Risks**: Economic losses from extreme weather events reached EUR 14.7 billion between 1980 and 2023. Austria faces significant risks from heatwaves, floods, and landslides, necessitating increased investment in adaptation measures and resilient infrastructure.
