# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Romania

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: SWD(2025) 923 final – 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Romania
- **Effective Period**: Reporting period covering developments up to mid-2025
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Staff Working Document / Rule of Law Report

## Executive Summary

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Romania highlights significant progress in addressing judicial independence concerns, particularly through the completion of processes related to the Venice Commission’s recommendations on the Justice Laws. Romania has also taken steps to strengthen safeguards for high-ranking prosecutors and the judicial police. However, challenges persist in several key areas. The perception of judicial independence remains average, and the transparency of judicial promotions and disciplinary sanctions continues to face scrutiny.

In the anti-corruption domain, while the implementation of the 2021-2025 National Anti-Corruption Strategy is on track and authorities maintain a positive record in prosecuting high-level corruption, serious concerns arise from recent case-law on the statute of limitations. Rulings by the High Court of Cassation and Justice regarding *lex mitior* have led to the discontinuation of numerous corruption cases and the annulment of convictions, raising systemic risks regarding impunity and EU law compatibility.

Media freedom and pluralism face ongoing threats, including untransparent political advertising, harassment of journalists, and a lack of progress in enhancing the independent governance of public service media. Legislative unpredictability, driven by the frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances and weaknesses in public consultations, remains a primary concern for businesses and civil society. Additionally, the credibility of the asset declaration system has been weakened by a Constitutional Court ruling, and no progress has been made on lobbying rules for Members of Parliament or the accreditation of National Human Rights Institutions.

## Key Highlights

- **Judicial Independence and Justice Laws**: Significant progress was made in completing the process to implement Venice Commission recommendations on the Justice Laws. Legislative steps are being explored to extend the mandates of high-ranking prosecutors from three to five years. However, perceived judicial independence has decreased among the general public and companies. The Ministry of Justice has shown openness to ensuring judicial independence in the reform of the IT system for courts, addressing concerns about centralized data access.

- **Statute of Limitations and Corruption Cases**: A critical development involves the High Court of Cassation and Justice’s interpretation of *lex mitior*, which has led to the discontinuation of many corruption cases and annulment of convictions due to expired limitation periods. This case-law, extending beyond the 2018-2022 legal void, raises concerns about the effectiveness of anti-corruption efforts and compliance with EU law. The HCCJ has referred a question to the Court of Justice of the European Union for clarification.

- **Anti-Corruption Strategy and Institutions**: The 2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy implementation is on track, with a new strategy under preparation. The National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA) maintains a stable occupancy rate and continues to secure convictions in high-level cases. However, the National Integrity Agency’s (ANI) asset declaration system faces challenges after a Constitutional Court ruling deemed the requirement to declare spouses’ and children’s assets unconstitutional, potentially weakening transparency and accountability.

- **Media Pluralism and Freedom**: No progress was made in enhancing the independent governance and editorial independence of public service media. Untransparent political advertising and financing of private media by political parties have increased, affecting media independence. Journalists continue to face harassment, threats, and political pressure. The National Audiovisual Council (CNA) lacks sufficient human resources and an updated IT system, impacting its effectiveness.

- **Legislative Quality and Public Consultations**: Legislative unpredictability and the frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs) remain major concerns for businesses, with 199 ordinances adopted in 2024. Public consultations are often criticized as ineffective "box-ticking" exercises. No progress has been made on introducing lobbying rules for Members of Parliament, and legislation to improve transparency in political party financing remains pending.

- **Human Rights and Civil Society**: The accreditation process for National Human Rights Institutions (Romanian Institute for Human Rights and People's Advocate) is stalled due to questions from GANHRI regarding independence and resources. The Romanian Institute for Human Rights faces severe budget cuts and staffing shortages, risking its functionality. Civil society space is considered narrowed, with CSOs facing administrative barriers and attacks, although a Strategy for Open Government 2025-2030 was adopted to address these challenges.
