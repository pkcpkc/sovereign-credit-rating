## Executive Summary

The 2025 Country Report for France highlights an economy facing significant headwinds from high economic and political uncertainty, following the fragmented political landscape resulting from the summer 2024 general elections. While economic growth accelerated in 2024 (1.2% GDP growth), activity is expected to slow markedly in 2025 (0.6% growth) due to subdued investment, high energy costs, and policy uncertainty.

Key challenges include a persistent and slippage-prone fiscal deficit, which rose to 5.8% of GDP in 2024, driven by lower-than-expected tax revenues and high public consumption. Public debt is projected to rise to 116% of GDP in 2025. The political fragmentation has led to an unprecedented delay in the adoption of the 2025 budget, complicating deficit reduction efforts.

Structurally, France struggles with high corporate debt, rising bankruptcies, and skills shortages that hinder productivity. While competitiveness indicators have improved recently, long-term growth prospects are constrained by weak total factor productivity and insufficient business R&D intensity. The green transition faces administrative barriers, particularly in permitting for renewable energies and energy-efficient building renovations.

Socially, poverty and social exclusion risks are increasing, particularly among children and vulnerable groups. The labor market shows resilience but is marked by high unemployment (7.4%) and segmentation, with significant gaps for young people, migrants, and low-skilled workers.

## Key Highlights

- **[Political Fragmentation and Budgetary Delays]**: The highly fragmented political landscape following the summer 2024 general elections has resulted in a lack of a clear parliamentary majority. This has caused an unprecedented delay in the adoption of the 2025 budget, reducing its ambitions and creating uncertainty regarding the timely implementation of fiscal consolidation measures.
- **[Fiscal Slippage and Rising Debt]**: France recorded a general government deficit of 5.8% of GDP in 2024, up from 5.4% in 2023. This slippage was driven by tax revenue shortfalls (particularly corporate income and stamp duties) and high public spending. Consequently, public debt is projected to rise to 116% of GDP in 2025, with medium-term fiscal sustainability risks assessed as high.
- **[Slowing Economic Growth and Investment]**: After accelerating to 1.2% in 2024, economic activity is forecast to slow to 0.6% in 2025 and 1.3% in 2026. Investment remains subdued due to restrictive monetary policy, high economic uncertainty, and elevated energy costs. Net exports are expected to stall due to these cost factors.
- **[Corporate Sector Vulnerability]**: Non-financial corporations face high indebtedness levels, above the euro area average. Corporate bankruptcies have reached record highs in 2024, rising sharply since 2022. Late payments to SMEs are significantly above EU averages, disrupting cash flows and posing risks to bank profitability.
- **[Structural Productivity Challenges]**: Despite recent improvements in price competitiveness, France struggles with low total factor productivity and business R&D intensity. The efficiency of public support for business innovation (e.g., the research tax credit) is questioned, as it has not significantly boosted patent output or innovation density compared to other EU peers.
- **[Green Transition Barriers]**: France faces administrative and structural barriers in decarbonizing key sectors. The deployment of renewable energy is hindered by burdensome permitting procedures, limited storage capacity, and a lack of updated national targets for 2030. Building renovation targets are being missed due to budget fluctuations and a shortage of skilled labor for deep renovations.
- **[Labor Market Segmentation and Skills Gaps]**: The unemployment rate remains high at 7.4% in late 2024, significantly above the EU average. Youth unemployment (14.4%) and NEET rates are elevated, particularly in outermost regions. There are persistent skills shortages in key sectors like construction and care, exacerbated by a mismatch between training offers and labor market needs.
- **[Rising Social Inequality]**: The at-risk-of-poverty or social exclusion (AROPE) rate increased to 20.5% in 2024. Child poverty is particularly acute, with 26.2% of children at risk. Single-parent families and non-EU-born individuals face disproportionately higher risks. Housing affordability and energy poverty are growing challenges for vulnerable households.
