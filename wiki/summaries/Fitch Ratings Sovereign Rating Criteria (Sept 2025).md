# Summary of: Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Criteria (Sept 2025)

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** Fitch Ratings
*   **Effective Period:** 15 September 2025 to 27 April 2026
*   **Document Type:** Master Criteria for Sovereign and Central Bank Issuers
*   **Key Purpose:** To provide a transparent, quantitative, and qualitative framework for assigning and maintaining Issuer Default Ratings (IDRs).

## Executive Summary
Fitch Ratings utilizes a multi-pillared approach to assess sovereign creditworthiness, synthesizing quantitative data via the **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)** and qualitative adjustments through a **Qualitative Overlay (QO)**. The analysis is centered on four analytical pillars: Structural Features, Macroeconomic Performance, Public Finances, and External Finances. While the SRM provides a baseline score calibrated to the Long-Term Foreign-Currency IDR scale, the QO allows for adjustments of up to +/- 3 notches to account for nuances like political stability, policy credibility, and fiscal sustainability. The criteria also establish specific protocols for default events, distressed debt exchanges (DDE), and a new screening tool for climate-related risks (Climate.VS).

## Key Highlights

*   **[SRM Framework]** The Sovereign Rating Model is a multiple regression OLS model using 18 variables. **Structural Features** carry the highest weight (53.7%), followed by Public Finances (19.1%), External Finances (17.3%), and Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%).
*   **[Qualitative Overlay]** Analysts can apply a notch adjustment of +/- 2 per pillar (max +/- 3 overall) to address factors like geopolitical risk, financial sector health, and policy flexibility.
*   **[Default Protocols]** A sovereign is moved to 'RD' (Restructured/Default) status upon missed payments, forced redenomination, or execution of a Distressed Debt Exchange (DDE). A 30-day grace period is typically applied for operational interruptions (Payment Force Majeure).
*   **[Currency Differentiation]** Ratings distinguish between Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) IDRs. Differentiation usually occurs based on debt burden distribution, domestic capital market depth, and access to foreign currency.
*   **[Short-Term Mapping]** Short-term ratings (F1+ to D) are mapped from long-term ratings. For LC ratings, Fitch selects the higher option; for FC ratings, the choice depends on Reserve-Currency Flexibility (RCF) or a robust International Liquidity Ratio (ILR > 100%).
*   **[Climate Risk Screening]** The **Climate.VS** framework identifies sovereigns with high exposure to physical (e.g., floods, heatwaves) and transition risks (e.g., fossil fuel dependence). A score of 50 or more in 2035 triggers additional analytical scrutiny.
*   **[Recovery Ratings (RR)]** For sovereigns rated 'B+' or below, Fitch assigns Recovery Ratings (RR1-RR6) to provide transparency on potential creditor recoveries during a default.
