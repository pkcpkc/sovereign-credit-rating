# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Poland

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 921 final
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (Reference period covering developments up to mid-2025)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Country Chapter / Rule of Law Report

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Poland highlights significant efforts by the Polish Government to address long-standing rule of law concerns, particularly regarding judicial independence, as outlined in the Action Plan on the Rule of Law. Key developments include the preparation of legislation to address the status of judges appointed by the post-2017 National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) and reforms to the Supreme Court structure to enhance its independence. While acts amending the laws governing the NCJ and the Constitutional Tribunal have been adopted by Parliament, they remain pending before the Constitutional Tribunal due to a presidential referral. A major milestone is the significant progress made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the Prosecutor General, aiming to ensure the functional independence of the prosecution service.

In the anti-corruption sphere, the perception of corruption remains relatively high, though large-scale cases involving public officials are being investigated. The European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) is fully operational, and a new Central Office for Combating Corruption is to be established within the police. However, challenges persist regarding the lack of a comprehensive anti-corruption strategy, the absence of lobbying rules, and the continued existence of broad immunities for top executives.

Media freedom and pluralism show mixed trends. Politically biased content in public service media has decreased, and state advertising is more evenly distributed. However, concerns remain about the independence of the media regulator (KRRiT) and the governance of public broadcasters, which are currently in liquidation. A planned media law reform aims to address these issues in line with the European Media Freedom Act. Civil society operates in a "narrowed" space, with some progress in improving the framework, although negative rhetoric and operational challenges persist. Overall, Poland is making progress on several key recommendations, but substantial work remains to fully align with EU rule of law standards.

## Key Highlights

- **Judicial Independence Reforms**: Significant efforts have been made to implement the Action Plan on the Rule of Law. A draft law has been proposed to address the status of ~1,600 judges appointed at the request of the NCJ in its post-2017 composition, involving a classification system and potential removal, pending Venice Commission consultation. Plans to amend the Supreme Court structure include dismantling the Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs to strengthen judicial independence.
- **Constitutional Tribunal Crisis**: Legislation addressing the NCJ and Constitutional Tribunal has been adopted by Parliament but has not entered into force due to referral to the Constitutional Tribunal by the President. The Government continues to refuse to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, asserting the body lacks legitimacy, leading to a constitutional crisis and an ongoing infringement procedure by the European Commission.
- **Prosecution Service Independence**: Significant progress has been made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General. A draft law to this end was approved by the Cabinet Standing Committee in December 2024, incorporating Venice Commission recommendations. The National Prosecution Service has also reviewed past cases, identifying irregularities in investigations from 2016-2023.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework**: The EPPO is fully operational in Poland. A new Central Office for Combating Corruption will be established within the police, dissolving the Central Anti-Corruption Bureau. However, there is no comprehensive anti-corruption strategy, and no progress has been made in introducing lobbying rules or addressing broad immunities for top executives, which hinder high-level corruption prosecutions.
- **Media Pluralism and Freedom**: Politically biased content in public service media has decreased. The Government plans a media law reform to implement the European Media Freedom Act, addressing the independence of the media regulator (KRRiT) and public broadcaster governance. Public broadcasters are currently in liquidation, with funds suspended pending a new state-funded model replacing the license fee system.
- **Civil Society**: The civic space in Poland was re-rated from "obstructed" to "narrowed" by CIVICUS in March 2025. The Government has established working groups with civil society organizations and launched a new program for NGOs. However, challenges remain, including negative rhetoric against NGOs and operational restrictions at the border.
- **Checks and Balances**: Poland strengthened rules for legislative consultations, introducing mandatory impact assessments. The Ombudsperson continues to play a key role in checks and balances. However, the number of pending leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights has increased to 54 as of June 2025.
- **Corruption Risks in Public Procurement**: Public procurement remains highly vulnerable to corruption, with significant single-bid rates. Investigations into EU fund misuse and bid-rigging have led to arrests, but systemic transparency issues persist. Stakeholders welcome proposed legislative amendments but caution against reduced transparency due to high thresholds.
