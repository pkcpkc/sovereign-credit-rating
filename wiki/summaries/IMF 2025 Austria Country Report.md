# Summary of: IMF 2025 Austria Country Report

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF (2025) Austria Country Report
- **Effective Period**: July 2025 (Mission: March 26-April 8, 2025; Report Completed: June 9, 2025)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation (Staff Report, Press Release, and Informational Annex)

## Executive Summary

The International Monetary Fund’s 2025 Article IV Consultation with Austria highlights a challenging economic environment characterized by two successive years of recession (2023 and 2024) triggered by the energy price shock, subsequent monetary tightening, and heightened global trade uncertainty. Despite a projected near-zero growth for 2025, a recovery is expected from 2026 onwards, driven by unwinding household savings, monetary easing, and German fiscal expansion. However, medium-term prospects are constrained by demographic aging, sluggish productivity growth, and rising spending pressures from defense, the green transition, and an aging population.

Fiscal policy remains a central focus, with the deficit widening to 4.7% of GDP in 2024 due to weak economic activity and one-off expenditures, pushing public debt to 81% of GDP. The IMF recommends a bold and well-designed fiscal consolidation package, including pension reforms, limits on public-sector wage increases, and increased reliance on property, inheritance, and excise taxes, to put the debt ratio on a declining path. The financial sector is assessed as healthy, with strong capitalization (CET1 ratio of 17.7%) and profitability, though vigilance is warranted regarding risks from Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposures and regional spillovers from Central, Eastern, and Southeastern Europe (CESEE). Structural reforms to boost labor supply, reduce regulatory barriers, and deepen the EU Single Market are critical for enhancing long-term growth potential.

## Key Highlights

- **[2024 Recession and Fiscal Deterioration]** The Austrian economy experienced its second successive year of recession in 2024, with Real GDP falling by 1.3% following a 0.9% contraction in 2023. This downturn was driven by high energy prices, rapid interest rate hikes, and weak domestic/external demand. Consequently, the fiscal deficit widened significantly to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, resulting from weak economic activity, lagged inflation effects, and one-off expenditures, causing public debt to rise to 81% of GDP.

- **[New Coalition Government Formation (March 2025)]** Following elections in September 2024 that resulted in no single party winning a majority, a new coalition government comprising the center-right ÖVP, center-left SPÖ, and liberal NEOS party was formed in March 2025. This new administration has prioritized undertaking reforms to boost growth and reduce the fiscal deficit, including plans for frontloaded consolidation measures.

- **[IMF Mission and Report Completion (April-June 2025)]** An IMF staff team conducted mission discussions with Austrian officials from March 26 to April 8, 2025. The staff report was subsequently completed on June 9, 2025, assessing Austria’s economic developments and policies. The mission met with key figures including Minister of Finance Marterbauer and Central Bank Governor Holzmann, alongside various government bodies, financial institutions, and private-sector representatives.

- **[Executive Board Conclusion (July 2025)]** The IMF Executive Board concluded the Article IV consultation on July 3, 2025. The Board acknowledged Austria’s challenging economic situation, noting that while near-term growth is weak due to fiscal consolidation and trade uncertainty, a recovery is expected from 2026. The Board emphasized the need for substantial medium-term fiscal adjustment to offset rising spending pressures from aging, defense, and the green transition, while maintaining financial stability.

- **[Inflation Dynamics and Monetary Policy Context]** Inflation in early 2025 spiked to 3.3% due to one-off effects from the expiration of electricity price caps and higher network charges, though core inflation remains elevated at 3.1% due to strong services sector wage growth (7-8%). The IMF projects inflation to gradually converge toward the Euro Area average of 2% by end-2026, contingent on productivity-adjusted wage growth aligning with regional peers to maintain competitiveness.

- **[Financial Sector Health and CRE Risks]** Austria’s banking sector remains stable, liquid, and profitable, with a CET1 capital ratio of 17.7% by mid-2024. However, risks persist from Commercial Real Estate (CRE) loans, with NPL ratios on CRE rising to approximately 5% by mid-2024. The IMF welcomes the introduction of a Sectoral Systemic Risk Buffer (SSyRB) set at 1% for CRE assets, effective July 2025, and urges continued vigilance on credit risks, including those from CESEE exposures and global trade policy uncertainties affecting non-financial corporates.

- **[Structural Reforms and Labor Supply Recommendations]** To counteract demographic headwinds, the IMF recommends boosting labor supply by narrowing gaps in female full-time work and elderly labor force participation. Suggested measures include expanding childcare and eldercare facilities, reforming pension systems to incentivize longer working lives, better integrating immigrants, reducing regulatory barriers in services, speeding up green energy project approvals, and easing land-use regulations for housing supply. Deepening the EU Single Market, including through a Savings and Investment Union and a common 28th corporate regime, is highlighted as critical for improving productivity and growth.
