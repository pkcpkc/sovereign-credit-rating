# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Italy

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** SWD(2025) 912 final / COM(2025) 900 final
*   **Origin:** European Commission
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
*   **Subject:** Rule of Law situation in Italy (Justice System, Anti-Corruption, Media Pluralism, and Institutional Checks)

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Italy highlights a period of significant legislative activity and structural reform. While the justice system has seen comprehensive reforms and improved digitalization in several branches (civil, tax, administrative), the criminal justice sector faces technical hurdles in its digital rollout. Judicial independence perception is rising, though tensions exist regarding the proposed separation of judges and prosecutors. Anti-corruption efforts are ongoing, with updates to national plans and the Constitutional Court validating the abrogation of "abuse of office" offenses. Media freedom remains a point of concern due to legislative restrictions on judicial information access and reports of spyware usage against journalists. Finally, the report notes a "narrowed" civic space following the adoption of a new Security Law and ongoing debates regarding the frequency of government urgency decrees.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System & Efficiency
*   **Judicial Independence:** Perceived independence among the general public rose to 46% in 2025, a significant increase from 34% in 2021.
*   **Constitutional Reform:** The Chamber of Deputies approved a reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors. However, the High Council for the Judiciary and various magistrates' associations expressed reservations regarding the authority of separate councils.
*   **Digitalisation:** While tax, civil, and administrative justice are fully digitalized, the criminal justice system is struggling with the *APP* platform due to technical issues and obsolete equipment.
*   **Backlog Management:** Italy continues a positive trend in reducing case backlogs, with clearance rates exceeding 100% across most jurisdictions in 2023.
*   **Mediation:** New rules effective January 2025 aim to encourage mediation to reduce the overall number of legal proceedings.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Corruption Perception:** Italy remains stable at a score of 54/100 (Transparency International), with 82% of citizens considering corruption widespread.
*   **Legislative Updates:** The Constitutional Court confirmed that the law abrogating the offense of "abuse of public office" is compliant with the UN Convention against Corruption.
*   **Lobbying & Conflicts of Interest:** Progress remains limited. While some ministries have voluntary registers, a comprehensive national lobbying register and rules for political office holders remain pending.
*   **Public Procurement:** Remains a high-risk area; 98% of contracts were allocated via direct allocation in 2024, though digital platforms are being used to mitigate infiltration.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Regulator Independence:** AGCOM is noted as well-resourced and functionally independent.
*   **Public Service Media:** RAI's funding was maintained at the 2023 level (EUR 90 fee) for 2025, though stakeholders call for more stable, multi-annual funding.
*   **Journalistic Challenges:** Journalists face a "chilling effect" from the *Costa amendment*, which restricts the publication of direct quotations from pre-trial custody orders.
*   **Safety & Spyware:** There has been a 16% increase in intimidations against journalists in 2024. While the COPASIR report found no evidence of state-sponsored use of "Graphite" spyware against journalists, the allegations remain a point of concern.

### IV. Institutional Checks and Balances
*   **Security Law:** The new Security Law (adopted June 2025) has drawn criticism from the UN and Council of Europe for potentially narrowing civic space and restricting freedom of assembly.
*   **Urgency Decrees:** Stakeholders expressed concern over the frequent use of government decrees (87 in the current legislature), which may impact the separation of powers.
*   **Human Rights:** No progress has been made toward establishing a National Human Rights Institution (NHRI), despite several pending draft laws.
*   **ECHR Judgments:** Italy continues to struggle with the implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments, with an average pending time of over 6 years.
