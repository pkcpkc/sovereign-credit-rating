# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Italy

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission (Commission Staff Working Document)
*   **Reference Number:** SWD(2025) 912 final
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
*   **Subject:** Evaluation of the rule of law situation in Italy, focusing on the justice system, anti-corruption framework, media pluralism, and institutional checks and balances.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Italy highlights a period of significant structural reform and mixed progress. While the justice system has seen comprehensive reforms and improved recruitment of magistrates, it continues to struggle with extreme procedural delays (remaining the longest in the EU for civil cases) and technical hurdles in full digitalization. The judiciary's perceived independence has seen a notable increase among the public and companies. 

In the anti-corruption sphere, Italy has updated its National Anti-Corruption Plan and strengthened integrity measures for police forces; however, the perception of public sector corruption remains high. Legislative progress on lobbying rules and the channeling of donations through political foundations has stalled. Regarding media, while the regulator (AGCOM) is well-resourced and independent, journalists face persistent challenges including threats, intimidation, and the potential "chilling effect" of new judicial information disclosure rules. Finally, the report notes significant constitutional developments, including a draft reform for the direct election of the Prime Minister and a new Security Law that has raised concerns regarding its impact on civic space.

## Key Highlights

### Justice System Reform & Independence
*   **Judicial Independence:** Perceived independence among the general public rose to 46% in 2025 (up from 36% in 2024).
*   **Career Separation:** A major constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors was approved by the Chamber of Deputies in January 2025, though the High Council for the Judiciary expressed significant reservations regarding its impact on autonomy.
*   **Recruitment:** Significant progress in hiring magistrates (578 new ordinary magistrates between July 2024 and April 2025) aims to close a 17% shortage, though administrative staff shortages remain high (37%).
*   **Digitalization:** While civil and tax justice systems are fully digitalized, the criminal justice system faces technical delays in implementing the mandatory *APP* case management system.

### Efficiency and Backlogs
*   **Procedural Length:** Civil and commercial cases still take approximately 6 years to resolve, maintaining Italy's position as the slowest in the EU.
*   **Backlog Reduction:** A positive trend continues with clearance rates exceeding 100% in civil, criminal, and administrative jurisdictions.
*   **Mediation:** New rules enacted in January 2025 aim to promote mediation to reduce the volume of legal proceedings.

### Anti-Corruption & Integrity
*   **Corruption Perception:** Italy ranks 19th globally (54/100) on the Transparency International index; 82% of citizens believe corruption is widespread.
*   **Lobbying & Finance:** There has been "no progress" or "limited progress" on establishing a national lobbying register and addressing the practice of channeling donations through political foundations.
*   **Public Procurement:** Remains a high-risk area for organized crime infiltration, though digital platforms and updated codes are being used to mitigate risks.

### Media Pluralism & Freedom
*   **Regulatory Independence:** AGCOM is noted as a proactive, well-resourced, and independent regulator.
*   **Journalistic Safety:** Threats and intimidations against journalists increased by 16% in 2024. Concerns were raised regarding the use of "Paragon" spyware and the potential for SLAPPs (Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation).
*   **Defamation Reform:** Legislative proposals to abolish imprisonment for press defamation are under review, but stakeholders worry about new criminal pecuniary penalties.

### Institutional Checks & Balances
*   **Constitutional Reform:** Parliamentary scrutiny is advancing on the draft reform to enhance governmental stability and introduce the direct election of the Prime Minister.
*   **Urgency Decrees:** Stakeholders expressed concern over the frequent use of urgency decrees (87 adopted in the current legislature) and their impact on the separation of powers.
*   **Human Rights:** No concrete progress has been made toward establishing a National Human Rights Institution (NHRI).
*   **Security Law:** The new Security Law, adopted in June 2025, has faced criticism from the UN and Council of Europe for potentially narrowing civic space and freedom of assembly.
