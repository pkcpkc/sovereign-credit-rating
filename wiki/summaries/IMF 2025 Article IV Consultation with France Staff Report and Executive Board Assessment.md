# Summary of: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation with France: Staff Report and Executive Board Assessment

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation with France: Staff Report, Press Release, and Informational Annex
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (covering 2024 actuals and 2025-2029 projections)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation Report

## Executive Summary

The IMF's 2025 Article IV consultation with France highlights an economy that demonstrated resilience in 2024, supported by the Paris Olympics and robust labor markets, despite high domestic and external uncertainty. However, the report underscores urgent challenges regarding high and rising public debt (113.1% of GDP in 2024) and persistent fiscal slippages. Growth is projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025 before recovering to 1.0% in 2026, weighed down by trade tensions, geoeconomic fragmentation, and weak private investment.

The IMF Executive Board and staff emphasize the need for a credible, frontloaded fiscal consolidation under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029 and place debt on a downward path. This requires rationalizing current spending across all government levels and implementing structural reforms to boost productivity, particularly through innovation, digital adoption, and labor market improvements. The financial sector remains resilient, with strong capital buffers, though risks from non-bank financial intermediaries and corporate indebtedness require continued monitoring.

## Key Highlights

- **Fiscal Consolidation Imperative**: The IMF stresses the urgent need to approve a well-designed, credible package of fiscal measures to support the MTFSP. Without significant additional efforts, the deficit is projected to remain around 6% of GDP in the medium term, keeping debt on an upward trajectory. Staff recommend a frontloaded structural fiscal effort of 1.1% of GDP in 2026, followed by 0.9% annually, prioritizing spending rationalization over tax increases due to France's already high tax-to-GDP ratio.
- **Economic Outlook and Risks**: Real GDP growth slowed to 1.1% in 2024 and is projected at 0.6% for 2025. Major downside risks include deepening geoeconomic fragmentation, trade tensions, and domestic policy uncertainty. Upside risks hinge on easing trade tensions and renewed structural reform momentum. Inflation has disinfled significantly to 0.6% YoY in May 2025, with expectations well-anchored.
- **Structural Reforms for Productivity**: Raising weak productivity growth is critical. The IMF recommends advancing structural reforms to foster entrepreneurship, enhance access to finance for high-growth firms, and reduce regulatory burdens. Specific focus areas include leveraging AI and green technologies, improving education and skills, and promoting longer, less fragmented careers to address demographic aging.
- **Financial Sector Resilience and Risks**: The 2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP) confirms the banking sector's resilience, with high capital ratios (19.8%) and strong liquidity. However, profitability is weak due to net interest margin compression. Risks from corporate indebtedness and the sovereign-financial sector nexus remain manageable, but close monitoring of non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs) and cyber/climate risks is required.
- **Pension and Local Government Challenges**: The pension system faces long-term pressures from aging demographics, though the 2023 reform (raising retirement age to 64) helps mitigate costs. Local government spending contributed to recent fiscal slippages; the IMF recommends enhancing fiscal oversight, clarifying competencies, and adopting multi-year budgetary frameworks to improve efficiency at the local level.
