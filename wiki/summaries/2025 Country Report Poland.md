## Executive Summary

Poland’s economy is one of the fastest growing in the European Union, with GDP growth reaching 2.9% in 2024 and projected to exceed this figure in 2025, driven by strong private consumption and investment. Despite robust growth, Poland faces significant structural challenges, including competitiveness issues, an ageing population, and fiscal pressures. The general government deficit reached 5.3% of GDP in 2023, triggering an excessive deficit procedure initiated by the Council in January 2025. Poland has submitted a medium-term fiscal-structural plan aiming to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2028, with a binding expenditure path endorsed by the Council.

Macroeconomic stability is largely maintained, with inflation decreasing to 3.7% in 2024 and the current account moving into surplus. However, risks remain from delays in public investments, high energy prices, and exchange rate fluctuations. The banking sector is resilient, with well-capitalised institutions and strong profitability, though legacy issues regarding forex mortgages pose legal and financial challenges.

Structural reforms are critical for long-term sustainability. Poland is addressing challenges in innovation, business environment, skills, and the clean energy transition. Significant investments are being made through EU funds, including the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) and cohesion policy, to boost competitiveness, decarbonise the economy, and improve social fairness. Key priorities include accelerating the implementation of the RRP, improving public spending efficiency, addressing demographic challenges through pension and healthcare reforms, and enhancing digitalisation and innovation.

## Key Highlights

- **Economic Growth**: Poland's GDP grew by 2.9% in 2024 and is expected to surpass this figure in 2025, driven by strong private consumption and EU-funded investment.
- **Fiscal Deficit**: The general government deficit reached 5.3% of GDP in 2023, exceeding the 3% reference value, leading to an excessive deficit procedure initiated in January 2025.
- **Fiscal Plan**: Poland submitted a medium-term fiscal-structural plan with a binding expenditure path to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP over 2025-2028, endorsed by the Council.
- **Inflation**: Inflation decreased to 3.7% in 2024, with further decreases expected in 2025 due to lower energy commodity prices.
- **Current Account**: Poland's current account moved into surplus in 2023 and 2024, improving external sustainability.
- **Banking Sector**: The banking sector remains resilient with well-capitalised banks, strong profitability, and low non-performing loan ratios, though legacy forex mortgage disputes pose some risks.
- **Labour Market**: Unemployment remained at a historic low of 2.9% in 2024, despite a shrinking working-age population and labour market tightness easing somewhat.
- **Housing Market**: Strong house price growth (15% in 2024) was driven by demand-side measures, though financial stability risks from the residential real estate market remain contained.
- **Competitiveness Challenges**: Poland faces challenges in competitiveness, particularly in the business environment, research and innovation, skills, and the clean energy transition.
- **Fiscal Council**: Legislation establishing a Fiscal Council was enacted in December 2024, set to become fully operational in January 2026, initially playing an advisory role.
- **Excessive Deficit Procedure**: The Council initiated the excessive deficit procedure for Poland in January 2025, endorsing a medium-term fiscal-structural plan to correct the deficit.
- **Net Expenditure**: Net expenditure grew by 12.7% in 2024, driven by higher public consumption and defence investments, with 2025 growth forecast at 6.2%.
- **Defence Spending**: Defence expenditure is projected to increase, with Poland requesting activation of the national escape clause to accommodate higher spending within the Stability and Growth Pact.
- **Public Investment**: Public investment efficiency could be improved through more integrated planning, systematic *ex post* evaluations, and enhanced IT systems.
- **Tax Framework**: Poland's tax framework is complex, hindering innovation and sustainable growth, with scope for simplification and elimination of numerous tax reliefs.
- **Ageing Society**: Poland's ageing population is putting pressure on the pension system and health/long-term care, with statutory retirement ages of 60 for women and 65 for men.
- **Pension System**: The pension system's annuity factor is projected to lead to a substantial decline in pension benefits due to rising life expectancy, potentially increasing government spending.
- **Private Pensions**: Uptake of private pension schemes (PPE, IKZE) remains limited, with only about 30% of the eligible population actively participating.
- **Healthcare**: Poland has relatively poor health outcomes, with high mortality among working-age adults and low spending on preventive healthcare.
- **Long-Term Care**: There is a lack of formal long-term care capacity, leading to a heavy burden on informal carers without adequate support or legal recognition.
- **Investment Barriers**: Key barriers to private investment include uncertainty about the future (92% of firms), high energy costs (88%), and shortage of skilled staff (87%).
- **Digital Transformation**: Poland is lagging in 5G roll-out and adoption of advanced digital technologies by SMEs, particularly AI and data analytics.
- **Science-Business Collaboration**: Cooperation between science and business is limited, reducing Poland's ability to commercialise research, though initiatives like the Łukasiewicz Research Network are being implemented.
- **Clean Tech Hub**: Poland has emerged as a clean tech hub in the EU, particularly in battery manufacturing (60% of European-made lithium batteries), but faces skill shortages and regulatory uncertainties.
- **Fossil Fuel Reliance**: Poland remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels, with 70% of electricity generation from fossil fuels in 2024, despite a 30% share of renewables.
- **Energy Prices**: High electricity prices in Poland, driven by taxes and levies, impact industry competitiveness, with a potential need to rebalance energy taxation.
- **Grid Interconnections**: Poland's electricity grid connections are underdeveloped, with only 5% cross-border interconnections compared to the EU target of 15% by 2030.
- **Fossil Fuel Subsidies**: Poland records sizeable fossil fuel subsidies (0.92% of GDP in 2023) without a planned phase-out before 2030, including subsidies to the coal mining industry.
- **Climate Adaptation**: Poland faces growing threats from extreme weather events, including droughts and floods, impacting water resilience and economic security.
- **Water Resilience**: Poland has made progress in water resilience through investments, but scope remains for improving water retention and nature-based solutions.
- **Air Pollution**: Poland has the second-worst industrial air pollution in the EU, with high exposure to fine particulate matter (PM2.5) causing significant health impacts.
- **Circular Economy**: Poland's circular material use rate (7.5% in 2023) is below the EU average, with room for boosting circularity through waste management and recycling.
- **Basic Skills**: Poland has witnessed a decline in basic skills among 15-year-olds since 2018, particularly in vocational programmes, risking long-term competitiveness.
- **STEM Shortages**: Persistently low enrolment in STEM study programmes is limiting the talent pool, including digital and green skills, exacerbating labour shortages.
- **Adult Learning**: Participation in adult learning is falling in Poland, contributing to skills mismatches and hampering the digital and green transition.
- **Labour Market Participation**: Poland's employment rate (78.4% in 2024) surpassed its 2030 target, but challenges remain for integrating underrepresented groups, including women and persons with disabilities.
- **Gender Gap**: The gender employment gap in Poland decreased to 11.6 pps in 2024 but remains above the EU average, hampered by insufficient childcare and long-term care services.
- **Disability Employment Gap**: The disability employment gap widened to 35.6% in 2024, one of the largest in the EU, due to a lack of supported employment and employer knowledge.
- **Social Dialogue**: Social dialogue in Poland has produced limited tangible results, with low collective bargaining coverage (13.4%) and regular violations of labour rights noted.
- **Housing Shortage**: Poland faces an acute housing shortage, leading to spiralling house prices and rental inflation, far exceeding the EU average.
- **Housing Investment**: Investment in housing in Poland is the lowest in the EU (2.2% of GDP), contributing to supply bottlenecks and rapid price rises.
- **Recovery and Resilience Plan**: Poland has fulfilled 25% of the milestones and targets in its RRP, with efforts needed to accelerate implementation, including the REPowerEU chapter.
- **Cohesion Policy**: Poland is leveraging cohesion policy funding to support competitiveness, defence, housing, water resilience, and the energy transition.
- **SDG Progress**: Poland has improved on SDGs related to macroeconomic stability, productivity, and fairness, exceeding the EU average on several indicators.
