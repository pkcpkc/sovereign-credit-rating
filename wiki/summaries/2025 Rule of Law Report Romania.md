# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Romania

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** European Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 923 final)
*   **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
*   **Subject:** Evaluation of the rule of law situation in Romania, focusing on the justice system, anti-corruption framework, media freedom, and institutional checks and balances.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Romania highlights a period of mixed progress. Significant strides were made in finalizing the Venice Commission's recommendations regarding Justice Laws and improving judicial human resources. However, the report notes a concerning increase in the length of legal proceedings and a decrease in perceived judicial independence among the general public. While the National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA) maintains a positive track record in high-level cases, a series of court rulings regarding the statute of limitations has created systemic risks, leading to the closure of numerous corruption cases. Furthermore, while a new Strategy for Open Government was adopted, issues regarding media plurality, the lack of lobbying rules for MPs, and the continued use of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs) remain significant hurdles to full compliance with EU standards.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System
*   **Independence & Progress:** Romania completed the process of incorporating Venice Commission recommendations into its Justice Laws. While the Ministry of Justice is exploring stronger safeguards for high-ranking prosecutors, public perception of judicial independence has declined significantly since 2021.
*   **Resource Management:** Occupancy rates for judges (83%) and prosecutors (74%) improved in 2025, though the Superior Council of Magistracy (SCM) warns that many courts remain understaffed.
*   **Efficiency Challenges:** The length of proceedings has increased steadily since 2021, reaching an average of 228 days in 2023, which acts as a barrier to the right to justice.
*   **IT & Transparency:** The Ministry of Justice is developing a centralized judicial database; however, stakeholders have raised concerns regarding the lack of safeguards to prevent unauthorized access by the executive branch.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Strategy Implementation:** The 2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy remains on track, with a new strategy currently under preparation following an *ex-post* evaluation.
*   **Statute of Limitations Risk:** A critical development involves the High Court of Cassation and Justice (HCCJ) rulings that effectively neutralize the interrupting effect of procedural acts for many crimes committed before 2022, potentially allowing significant corruption cases to escape penalty.
*   **Integrity & Lobbying:** While a draft law on "revolving doors" is being elaborated, Romania has made no progress in introducing formal lobbying rules for Members of Parliament.
*   **Public Procurement:** Authorities identified procurement as a high-risk area for fraud; however, efforts to digitalize the process and implement risk-mapping tools are ongoing.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Public Service Media:** No progress was recorded in enhancing the independent governance of public service media; the bill to reform public broadcasting remains stalled in the Senate.
*   **Ownership & Advertising:** Transparency regarding media ownership remains low (72% risk). Political parties significantly increased spending on media in 2023, often using opaque advertising methods.
*   **Journalist Safety:** Threats and harassment of journalists persist, with several new alerts recorded by the Council of Europe. The use of Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs) remains a major deterrent to media freedom.

### IV. Institutional Checks and Balances
*   **Public Consultations:** While training for officials on consultation practices has improved, civil society organizations (CSOs) continue to describe the process as a "box-ticking" exercise with insufficient timeframes.
*   **Legislative Predictability:** The frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (156 GEOs in 2024) and a lack of impact assessments remain primary concerns for businesses and CSOs.
*   **Human Rights Institutions:** Accreditation for two National Human Rights Institutions remains stalled due to non-compliance with UN Paris Principles and severe budget cuts (50% for the RIHR in 2025).
