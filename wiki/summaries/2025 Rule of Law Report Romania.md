# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Romania

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Romania (SWD(2025) 923 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2025
- **Issuer**: European Commission

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Romania assesses the state of the rule of law, highlighting significant progress in judicial reforms while identifying persistent challenges in anti-corruption enforcement, media freedom, and institutional integrity. Romania has made substantial strides in completing the legislative process initiated by the Venice Commission regarding Justice Laws, with steps taken to strengthen the independence of high-ranking prosecutors and judicial police. However, the perception of judicial independence remains average, and the length of proceedings has increased.

In the anti-corruption domain, while the implementation of the 2021-2025 Strategy remains on track and high-level prosecutions continue, systemic issues persist. Notably, rulings by the High Court of Cassation and Justice regarding the statute of limitations have led to the annulment of many corruption convictions and the discontinuation of cases, creating legal uncertainty. The National Integrity Agency maintains its operational effectiveness but faces resource constraints and a Constitutional Court ruling that weakens the asset declaration system.

Media freedom and pluralism face significant threats, including opaque financing of private media by political parties, insufficient transparency in media ownership, and ongoing harassment of journalists. There has been no progress in enhancing the independent governance of public service media. Furthermore, legislative unpredictability, characterized by the frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances and quality issues in law-making, remains a primary concern for businesses and civil society. Institutional checks and balances show mixed results, with the Constitutional Court’s annulment of the 2024 presidential election first round marking a significant political event, while accreditation processes for National Human Rights Institutions remain stalled.

## Key Highlights

- **[Judicial Reform Progress]**: Significant progress has been made in completing the process initiated by the Venice Commission on Justice Laws, including legislative steps to extend the mandate of high-ranking prosecutors and ensure judicial police independence.
- **[Judicial Perception and Efficiency]**: Perceived judicial independence remains average among the public and companies, and the length of civil and commercial proceedings has increased, posing barriers to access to justice.
- **[Anti-Corruption Legal Uncertainty]**: High Court of Cassation and Justice rulings on the statute of limitations have led to the closure of many corruption cases and annulled convictions, raising systemic risks despite a positive track record in high-level prosecutions.
- **[National Integrity Agency Challenges]**: The National Integrity Agency faces resource shortages and a Constitutional Court ruling that limits asset declaration requirements, potentially weakening transparency and accountability.
- **[Media Freedom Threats]**: No progress has been made in strengthening public service media independence; private media face opaque political financing, and journalists continue to face harassment, surveillance, and strategic lawsuits (SLAPPs).
- **[Legislative Unpredictability]**: Frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances, poor legislative quality, and inadequate public consultations remain primary concerns for businesses and civil society organizations.
- **[Political and Institutional Events]**: The Constitutional Court annulled the first round of the 2024 presidential election due to irregularities, leading to a repeat election in 2025, while accreditation processes for National Human Rights Institutions remain pending.
