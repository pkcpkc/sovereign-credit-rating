# Summary of: Malta 2024 Article IV Consultation Staff Report and Press Release

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF (2025) Malta Country Report – 2024 Article IV Consultation—Press Release and Staff Report
- **Effective Period**: January 2025 (Discussions concluded Nov 15, 2024; Report completed Dec 17, 2024)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation Staff Report and Press Release

## Executive Summary

Malta has experienced remarkable economic growth over the past decade, driven primarily by export-oriented service industries such as tourism and online gaming. While growth is projected to moderate, it is expected to remain among the highest in Europe, supported by tight labor markets and an influx of foreign workers and tourists. However, this rapid growth has led to increased population density, straining infrastructure and public services, and raising concerns about the sustainability of the current labor-intensive growth model.

The IMF Executive Board has concluded the 2024 Article IV Consultation, endorsing staff appraisals that highlight a substantial improvement in Malta’s external position and a sound financial system, though risks remain tilted to the downside due to geopolitical conflicts and domestic capacity constraints. Key policy recommendations include continuing fiscal consolidation, shifting policy away from broad energy subsidies toward targeted support and investment in innovation and infrastructure, and implementing corporate income tax reforms aligned with the EU’s Pillar II directive. The financial sector requires vigilant monitoring, particularly regarding real estate exposures, with suggestions to tighten macroprudential policies. Structural reforms focusing on productivity, digitalization, education, and climate sustainability are essential for long-term social and environmental sustainability.

## Key Highlights

- **[Fiscal Consolidation and Energy Subsidies]**: The authorities are committed to reducing the fiscal deficit to around 2¼ percent of GDP by 2029, with public debt remaining around 50 percent of GDP. However, energy subsidies currently account for 20 percent of the fiscal deficit. The IMF recommends a gradual but decisive exit from the fixed energy price policy, shifting resources toward investment in green infrastructure, health, and innovation support.

- **[Monetary Policy and Inflation]**: Inflation has fallen to around 2 percent, aligning with Euro Area averages, though some inflationary pressures persist in the service sector due to strong tourism demand. The tight labor market, supported by foreign worker inflows, continues to influence wage dynamics.

- **[Financial Sector Risks and Real Estate]**: The financial system is sound with strong capital and liquidity buffers. However, significant risks stem from banks' substantial exposure to real estate (residential, commercial, and construction). The IMF advises raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer rate and broadening its scope beyond residential mortgages to mitigate potential housing market shocks.

- **[Structural Reforms and Innovation]**: Continued efforts are needed to boost productivity through innovation, digitalization, and education. Key initiatives include strengthening STEM enrollment, enhancing digital skills, supporting start-ups via the new Venture Capital Fund, and implementing the "Malta Vision 2050" strategic direction.

- **[External Position and Geopolitical Risks]**: Malta’s external position in 2024 is expected to be substantially stronger than implied by fundamentals, driven by robust service exports. However, downside risks include spillovers from regional conflicts (Ukraine, Israel-Gaza) and geoeconomic fragmentation, which could disrupt trade and supply chains.

- **[AML/CFT and Governance]**: Progress has been made in strengthening the Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) framework. The authorities are urged to remain vigilant against trade-based money laundering and to continue judicial reforms, including improving the efficiency of the justice system and the chief justice appointment process.

- **[Climate Goals and Sustainability]**: Concerted efforts are required to meet ambitious climate goals, including a 19 percent GHG emission reduction target by 2030. This involves completing vulnerability risk assessments, updating adaptation plans, and phasing out fixed energy prices to incentivize conservation and green investment.
