# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Belgium

## Context Metadata
- **Source:** EU Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 901 final)
- **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
- **Subject:** Assessment of the rule of law situation in Belgium, focusing on judicial independence, anti-corruption measures, media freedom, and civic space.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Belgium indicates a high level of perceived judicial independence and ongoing efforts to address structural resource deficiencies in the justice system. While the government is moving toward transferring budgetary management powers to the judiciary and improving digitalization, significant challenges remain regarding the length of proceedings and the implementation of court rulings. The anti-corruption framework is evolving with new investigative structures, though concerns persist regarding high-level cases and police integrity. Media freedom has seen progress through the decriminalization of defamation and anti-SLAPP measures, but public service media face budgetary pressures. Notably, while civic space remains protected by law, there are rising concerns regarding obstacles to the right to demonstrate and the non-implementation of European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) judgments.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System and Judicial Independence
- **Perceived Independence:** 64% of the general public and 72% of companies perceive judicial independence as "fairly or very good" in 2025.
- **Budgetary Autonomy:** The Government intends to transfer budgetary management from the executive to the judiciary, though this is linked to a pending reform of the evaluation and disciplinary system.
- **Resource Deficiencies:** Despite some progress (e.g., publishing open positions), the budget and number of judges per 100,000 inhabitants remain below EU averages.
- **Digitalisation:** The Government is responding to Court of Audit criticisms regarding the lack of an overarching approach and excessive reliance on external consultants.
- **Efficiency:** Backlogs remain a long-standing issue, particularly in the Brussels Court of Appeal; a file management project funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility aims to improve statistics.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
- **Corruption Perception:** Belgium ranks 9th in the EU (69/100) on the Corruption Perceptions Index, showing a significant increase in perception over five years.
- **New Structures:** Plans are underway to create a new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) and a specialized prosecution service for financial crime.
- **Police Integrity:** Concerns remain regarding disparate integrity policies across 181 local police zones; the Government plans to allow voluntary mergers and introduce career-long integrity checks.
- **Asset Declarations:** While 99% of required declarations are submitted, the system lacks adequate verification and transparency, as declarations remain in sealed envelopes.
- **Lobbying & Revolving Doors:** No further progress has been made on lobbying legislation; "revolving door" measures remain non-binding circulars rather than formal rules.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
- **Regulatory Independence:** Media regulators in Flanders (VRM) and the French-speaking community (CSA) maintain strong independence and are adapting to the Digital Services Act (DSA).
- **Journalist Protections:** Belgium decriminalized defamation and introduced harsher penalties for crimes against journalists. Work is ongoing to transpose the EU anti-SLAPP Directive.
- **Public Service Media:** VRT (Flanders) received additional resources, while RTBF (Wallonia-Brussels) faces a projected EUR 78 million shortfall until 2029.
- **Access to Documents:** A 2024 federal law amended the framework on administrative transparency, though some procedural gaps and non-binding appeal bodies persist.

### IV. Institutional Checks and Balances
- **Investment Protection:** 60% of companies express confidence in the protection of investments by law and courts.
- **Human Rights & Equality:** Concerns were raised regarding a 25% budget cut to the interfederal equality body (Unia).
- **ECtHR Compliance:** Belgium continues to face supervision by the Council of Europe for structural issues; as of June 2025, 18 leading judgments remain pending implementation.
- **Civic Space:** Stakeholders warn of a "narrowed" civic space due to proposed sanctions for individuals convicted of illegal acts during previous demonstrations.
