# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Slovakia

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Country Chapter on Slovakia (SWD(2025) 925 final)
- **Effective Period**: 2025 (covering developments up to early 2025)
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Commission Staff Working Document

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Slovakia highlights limited to no progress in addressing key rule of law concerns identified in previous years, despite an intensive dialogue with the European Commission. While legislative amendments were made to align criminal codes with EU financial interest protection directives, significant risks remain in the justice system, anti-corruption framework, media freedom, and institutional checks and balances.

Key issues include the continued low perception of judicial independence, unresolved safeguards for the dismissal of Judicial Council members, and the broad powers of the Prosecutor General to interfere in high-level corruption cases. The dissolution of specialized anti-corruption entities (NAKA and SPO) has led to a drop in investigated corruption cases and operational inefficiencies. Media freedom remains under pressure, with concerns over the politicization of public service media, restrictions on access to information, and a hostile environment for journalists. Furthermore, the frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures and increased burdens on civil society organizations undermine democratic accountability and stakeholder involvement.

## Key Highlights

- **Judicial Independence and Governance**: Perceived judicial independence remains low (32% among the public). No progress was made in establishing sufficient safeguards for the dismissal of Judicial Council members, notably those not elected by judges. Amendments to judges' laws introduced bonuses and evaluation changes, raising concerns about potential influence, though some efficiency measures were welcomed. The role of the Judicial Council in disciplinary proceedings has expanded, posing risks to independence.

- **Anti-Corruption Framework**: There has been no progress in ensuring effective, independent investigation of high-level corruption. The Prosecutor General continues to use powers to annul final decisions, including in high-profile cases. The closure of specialized entities (NAKA and SPO) resulted in delays, a drop in corruption cases investigated, and a lack of specialized investigators. A new National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2024-2029 is in preparation.

- **Criminal Law and Safeguards**: While amendments clarified procedural aspects of the "abuse of law" crime for judges, sufficient safeguards against misuse and chilling effects are still lacking. No progress was made on regulating lobbying or strengthening asset declaration transparency; instead, restrictions on public access to asset disclosures are being considered.

- **Media Freedom and Pluralism**: No progress was made on safeguarding the independence of public service media, with the new STVR governance structure raising concerns about politicization. Amendments to the Freedom of Information Act introduced fees and longer response times, facing constitutional challenge. The safety of journalists remains a concern, with an increasingly hostile environment and no legislative progress on defamation reform.

- **Checks and Balances and Civil Society**: The law-making process continues to suffer from limited stakeholder involvement, with 28% of laws in 2024 adopted via fast-track procedure. Civil society faces increased pressure through new reporting obligations for NGOs and reduced funding for some human rights advocates. Proposed constitutional amendments raising concerns about EU law primacy were postponed after first reading.

- **Justice System Efficiency**: The justice system faces efficiency challenges, particularly in administrative cases where resolution time rose sharply to 1,040 days in 2023. Reforms to the judicial map and legal aid access are ongoing, but logistical and specialization issues persist.

- **Implementation of International Obligations**: Slovakia had 31 leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights pending implementation as of January 2025, rising to 33 by June 2025, indicating weak enforcement of international judicial decisions.
