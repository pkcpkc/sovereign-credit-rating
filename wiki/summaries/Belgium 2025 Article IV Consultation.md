# Summary of: Belgium 2025 Article IV Consultation

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF (2025) Belgium Country Report
- **Effective Period**: 2025
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation Staff Report and Press Release

## Executive Summary

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) concludes that Belgium’s economy, while resilient to recent shocks, faces significant structural and fiscal challenges that require sustained policy action. The primary concern is the high and rising public debt, projected to reach 123 percent of GDP by 2030 under unchanged policies, driven by aging-related spending and elevated interest costs. Although the general government deficit widened to 4.4 percent of GDP in 2024, the IMF endorses the new government’s intention to pursue a seven-year fiscal adjustment under the EU Economic Governance Framework (EGF). This adjustment aims to durably reduce the deficit below 3 percent of GDP and place debt on a solidly downward path.

Key recommendations include rationalizing current spending, particularly social benefits and the public wage bill, while preserving and increasing public investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education. The IMF highlights the need for structural reforms to boost productivity and labor force participation, including changes to the automatic wage indexation mechanism and pension eligibility. Financial sector risks are assessed as moderate, with robust bank capitalization and low non-performing loans, though commercial real estate exposures require monitoring. The report emphasizes the importance of coordinating federal and regional entities to implement these reforms effectively, noting that political fragmentation during the formation of the new government in early 2025 had previously stalled policy progress.

## Key Highlights

- **[Fiscal Consolidation Path]**: The IMF supports a seven-year adjustment under the EGF, requiring an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of 0.6 percent of GDP until 2031 to bring the overall deficit below 3 percent of GDP and reduce gross public debt significantly.
- **[Political Transition]**: Protracted coalition negotiations following the June 2024 elections stalled policymaking; a new federal government was formed in February 2025, initiating a policy agenda that includes significant structural reforms and fiscal consolidation.
- **[Debt Sustainability]**: Public debt stood at 104.1 percent of GDP in 2024, among the highest in the euro area. However, risk of sovereign stress is assessed as moderate due to high-quality debt management, an average residual maturity of 11.1 years, and a large domestic investor base.
- **[Labor Market Reforms]**: Recommendations include increasing the income gap between work and nonwork, limiting unemployment benefits to two years (with exceptions), reforming the automatic wage indexation and 1996 wage law to improve competitiveness, and enhancing labor force participation among low-skilled, older, and immigrant workers.
- **[Financial Sector Stability]**: Overall systemic risks in the financial sector remain moderate. Banks are well-capitalized with robust profitability, though exposure to commercial real estate is rising and requires active monitoring. Prudential limits on mortgage loans are being maintained.
- **[Structural Productivity]**: Despite high R&D spending, total factor productivity growth lags peers. The IMF recommends product market reforms to reduce regulatory barriers, improve the insolvency regime, and deepen the EU single market and capital market union to boost firm dynamics and innovation diffusion.
- **[Green Transition]**: Belgium faces challenges in meeting climate targets, with emissions projected to fall short of EU goals. The IMF recommends consolidating federal and regional climate efforts, implementing carbon taxation, phasing out fossil fuel subsidies, and accelerating investments in renewable energy and energy efficiency.
