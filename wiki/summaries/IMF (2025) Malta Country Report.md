# Summary of: IMF (2025) Malta Country Report

## Context Metadata
*   **Source:** International Monetary Fund (IMF)
*   **Report Type:** 2024 Article IV Consultation—Press Release and Staff Report
*   **Date of Publication:** January 15, 2025
*   **Location of Discussions:** Valletta, Malta (November 4–15, 2024)
*   **Key Stakeholders:** IMF Executive Board, Ministry of Finance and Employment (Malta), Central Bank of Malta.

## Executive Summary
Malta has demonstrated robust economic growth over the last decade, primarily fueled by export-oriented service industries including tourism and online gaming. While growth is expected to moderate, it remains among the highest in Europe. The report highlights a transition toward a productivity-driven growth model to address capacity constraints caused by high population density, infrastructure strain, and labor shortages. Key policy recommendations include a decisive shift away from energy subsidies toward innovation/green investment, the implementation of a Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reform roadmap aligned with EU Pillar II, and the tightening of macroprudential policies to manage significant real estate exposures in the banking sector.

## Key Highlights

*   **Economic Growth & Outlook:** ⚓ **Growth Moderation:** Real GDP growth is projected to slow from 7.5% in 2023 to 5% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2025, though it remains high relative to EU averages.
*   **Fiscal Policy & Consolidation:** ⚓ **Subsidy Reform:** The IMF recommends phasing out the fixed energy price policy to redirect fiscal space toward public services (health, education) and innovation.
*   **Financial Stability:** ⚓ **Real Estate Risks:** Banks face high exposure to the real estate sector (70% of loan portfolios). The IMF suggests raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB) and broadening its scope to include commercial real estate.
*   **Structural & Tax Reform:** ⚓ **CIT Roadmap:** Authorities are urged to develop a clear roadmap for Corporate Income Tax reform to align with the EU’s Minimum Tax Directive (Pillar II) and prevent revenue leakage to other jurisdictions.
*   **Labor & Demographics:** ⚓ **Skills Gap:** While immigration has mitigated labor shortages, there is a critical need for STEM graduates and upskilling to support the "Malta Vision 2050" objectives.
*   **Climate & Sustainability:** ⚓ **Green Transition:** Malta must intensify mitigation measures to meet the 19% GHG reduction target by 2030, focusing on road transport and renewable energy expansion.
*   **Governance & AML:** ⚓ **Judicial Reform:** Continued progress is required in the justice system to improve efficiency and strengthen the appointment process of the chief justice to bolster investor confidence.
