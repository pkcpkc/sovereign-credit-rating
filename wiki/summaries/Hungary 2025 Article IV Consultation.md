# Summary of: Hungary 2025 Article IV Consultation

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF (2025) Hungary Country Report

## Executive Summary

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with Hungary, characterizing the economy as being at a "challenging juncture." Output has stagnated over the past three years, and inflation remains persistently above the Magyar Nemzeti Bank’s (MNB) 3 percent target, driven by core services inflation and external price pressures. While the economy showed a modest 0.5 percent growth in 2024, momentum cooled significantly by early 2025.

The IMF’s Executive Board and staff emphasize the urgent need for high-quality fiscal adjustment to rebuild buffers, citing elevated public debt (projected to reach 79 percent of GDP by 2030) and high financing costs. Key recommendations include broadening the tax base, rationalizing energy subsidies, and strengthening fiscal governance, particularly regarding State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) and contingent liabilities. On the monetary front, the MNB is advised to maintain a tight policy stance into 2026 to durably return inflation to target, phasing out distortive price and margin controls.

Structural reforms are identified as critical for long-term growth, focusing on boosting productivity, enhancing energy security, and improving governance to unlock suspended EU funds. Downside risks are tilted downward, including potential trade escalations, regional conflicts, and delays in fiscal or governance reforms. The authorities broadly agree with the assessment but express greater confidence in their targeted support measures and growth potential, highlighting recent investments in EV and battery production.

## Key Highlights

- **[Stagnant Growth and Inflation]**: Output has stagnated over the past three years, with GDP contracting in Q1:2025. Inflation remains well above the MNB’s 3 percent target, with core services inflation at 7.6 percent year-on-year, driven by wage pressures and food prices.
- **[Fiscal Adjustment Necessity]**: The IMF recommends additional fiscal measures of approximately 0.5 percent of GDP for 2025 to meet budget targets, with a cumulative adjustment of 1.3 percent of GDP in 2026-2027 to bring the deficit below 3 percent by 2027. Current policies project a deficit of 4.5-4.9 percent of GDP.
- **[Debt Sustainability Risks]**: Public debt is projected to rise to 79 percent of GDP by 2030. Risks include high interest expenses, a large stock of inflation-linked retail bonds facing refinancing risk, and significant contingent liabilities from SOE guarantees (14 percent of GDP).
- **[Monetary Policy Stance]**: The MNB is advised to keep its base rate at 6.5 percent (held since September 2024) into 2026 to anchor inflation expectations. The Board urges phasing out retail margin caps, price controls, and voluntary APR ceilings to avoid market distortions.
- **[Financial Sector Vulnerabilities]**: While banks are well-capitalized, vulnerabilities exist in the corporate sector (high FX loan share), sovereign-bank nexus (banks holding significant government debt), commercial real estate vacancies, and buoyant housing prices driven by subsidized lending.
- **[Structural Reform Priorities]**: Key recommendations include fostering firm dynamism by reducing barriers to factor mobility, strengthening access to risk capital for young firms, enhancing energy security through renewables, and improving governance to unlock suspended EU funds (€19 billion).
- **[Downside Risks]**: Significant downside risks include further escalation in trade measures (exposure to US tariffs), intensification of regional conflicts, cancellation of EU funds due to rule of law concerns, and delays in fiscal adjustment ahead of the 2026 national elections.
- **[External Position]**: The external position is assessed as substantially stronger than fundamentals imply, with a current account surplus of 2.2 percent of GDP in 2024. However, gross external debt remains high, and reserve coverage (110 percent of IMF ARA metric) is adequate but could be strengthened.
- **[Authorities’ Response]**: The Hungarian authorities acknowledge the challenges but highlight their resilience, pointing to strong employment, pro-family measures, and recent investments in EV and battery production. They maintain confidence in their Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) and their ability to achieve fiscal targets through targeted measures.
