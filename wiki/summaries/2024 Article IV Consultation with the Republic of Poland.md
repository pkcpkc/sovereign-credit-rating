# Summary of: 2024 Article IV Consultation with the Republic of Poland

## Executive Summary

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has concluded its 2024 Article IV Consultation with Poland, assessing a robust economic recovery driven by domestic demand, strong nominal wage growth, and the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds. Growth accelerated to an estimated 2.8% in 2024, with projections of 3.5% for 2025-2026, supported by private consumption and public investment. However, the IMF highlights significant downside risks to growth from a slowdown in Euro Area demand and geopolitical tensions, alongside upside risks from immigration and NGEU catalytic effects.

Fiscal policy remains a primary concern, with the deficit projected to widen to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending. The IMF recommends frontloading fiscal consolidation to rebuild buffers and support disinflation, aiming for a deficit of 2.9% of GDP by 2028 through identified measures, including VAT reforms, pension adjustments, and better targeting of social benefits. The Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) is welcomed, but the IMF stresses the need to identify all consolidation measures upfront to ensure credibility.

Monetary policy is appropriately tight, with the National Bank of Poland (NBP) maintaining a 5.75% policy rate. The IMF advises that interest rate cuts should commence only when wage growth decelerates and inflation is firmly declining towards the 2.5% target, which is expected to be reached by end-2025. The financial system remains resilient with strong capital and liquidity buffers, though litigation risks related to legacy CHF mortgages and a weak private credit recovery pose challenges. Structural reforms are critical to sustain growth, focusing on labor market integration (including refugees and women), innovation, and a credible climate transition to meet EU 2030 targets.

## Key Highlights

- **[Economic Recovery and Outlook]**: Poland's economy accelerated to 2.8% growth in 2024, driven by a rebound in private consumption fueled by strong nominal wage growth and lower inflation. Growth is projected to reach 3.5% in 2025-2026, supported by the absorption of NGEU funds, before moderating to slightly below 3% in the medium term due to demographic headwinds.

- **[Fiscal Challenges and Consolidation]**: The fiscal deficit widened to an estimated 5.9% of GDP in 2024, driven by permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending. The IMF recommends a modest fiscal adjustment of 0.5% of GDP in 2025 and a medium-term plan to reduce the deficit to 2.9% of GDP by 2028, requiring additional measures of approximately 1.5% of GDP beyond those already identified.

- **[Inflation and Monetary Policy]**: Inflation has declined significantly from peaks of 16.6% in 2023 but remains elevated due to a tight labor market and strong wage growth. The IMF advises maintaining a tight monetary stance, with interest rate cuts contingent on decelerating wage growth and a sustained decline in inflation towards the 2.5% target, expected by end-2025.

- **[Financial Sector Resilience and Risks]**: The banking system remains well-capitalized with ample liquidity buffers. However, legacy litigation risks surrounding CHF mortgages and a weak private credit recovery are noted. The IMF recommends eliminating distortions such as the bank asset tax and allowing mortgage credit holidays to expire to support credit intermediation.

- **[Structural Reforms for Growth]**: To sustain convergence and address long-term challenges, the IMF emphasizes structural reforms including boosting productivity, facilitating resource reallocation, nurturing innovation through private equity and venture capital, and ensuring an adequate labor supply by integrating women, older adults, and refugees into the labor market.

- **[Climate Transition and External Position]**: Poland faces significant challenges in decarbonizing its economy, which remains carbon-intensive. The IMF encourages accelerating permitting for green projects, extending carbon pricing, and leveraging NGEU funds to support the climate transition. The external position is assessed as stronger than implied by fundamentals, with adequate gross reserves and a manageable current account deficit projection.
