# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report France Country Chapter

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in France (SWD(2025) 910 final)
- **Effective Period**: Reference period covering developments up to mid-2025; Document Published 8 July 2025
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Staff Working Document / Country Report

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report for France provides a comprehensive assessment of the country's rule of law situation, highlighting significant progress in judicial independence, anti-corruption frameworks, and media transparency, while noting persistent challenges in judicial efficiency, legislative consultation, and the implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments.

Key developments include the ongoing implementation of the November 2023 law reforming the status of magistrates, which has been defended by the Superior Council of the Magistracy against public and political attacks. The justice system is undergoing extensive digitalization, with the *Portalis* tool being deployed across various jurisdictions. Despite these advancements, judicial efficiency remains a concern, with high average resolution times for civil and criminal cases, particularly bribery cases.

In the anti-corruption sphere, France has strengthened its framework through the reorganization of the Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) and increased resources for the High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP). New legislative tools have been introduced to improve prosecution and asset recovery, and efforts to combat corruption linked to drug trafficking and ports are underway. However, disclosure requirements for lobbyists still do not apply to top executive officials.

Media pluralism and freedom have seen notable improvements, particularly in media ownership transparency, with the preparation of a national database. The proposed reform of public broadcasting governance, involving the creation of a holding company, has sparked debate regarding editorial independence. Journalists face challenges in accessing administrative documents, though legal guarantees remain strong.

Regarding checks and balances, legislative simplification efforts are welcomed by businesses, but stakeholders criticize the use of accelerated legislative procedures that limit citizen consultation. The financial environment for civil society organizations remains favorable despite budgetary restrictions, though civic space is considered narrowed. France continues to address a backlog of pending European Court of Human Rights judgments, with implementation rates improving but still lagging behind EU averages.

## Key Highlights

- **Judicial Independence and Magistrate Reform**: The implementation of the 20 November 2023 law reforming magistrates' status is ongoing, including the repeal of age limits for admissions and the creation of 360° evaluations for court presidents. The Superior Council of the Magistracy has actively defended judicial independence against public attacks on judges, particularly those involving election-related decisions.
- **Justice System Digitalization and Efficiency**: Significant progress has been made in digitalizing civil and criminal proceedings, with the *Portalis* tool expanding to labor courts and planned for local courts by 2025. Online access to court decisions has improved, with over one million decisions accessible via open data. However, efficiency challenges persist, with average resolution times for civil and commercial cases remaining high (354 days in 2023) and particularly long for bribery cases (1,335 days).
- **Anti-Corruption Framework Strengthening**: The 2024-2029 national anti-corruption plan is expected to be adopted by summer 2025. The Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) has been reorganized to enhance effectiveness, and the High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP) has increased its staff to handle a growing workload. New legislative tools have been passed to improve corruption prosecution and asset recovery, including Law Warsmann No. 582 (2024).
- **Media Ownership Transparency**: France has made significant progress in improving media ownership transparency, with the preparation of a single national database listing direct or indirect owners and ultimate beneficial owners. This addresses a previous recommendation from the 2024 Rule of Law Report, although formal mandates for ARCOM remain to be confirmed.
- **Public Broadcasting Reform Debate**: A proposed reform creating a holding company, France Médias, to oversee public broadcasters (France Télévisions, Radio France, INA) has triggered strikes and debates over editorial autonomy. The reform aims to pool resources and share investments, with a common CEO for subsidiaries, but has raised concerns about job security and editorial independence.
- **Legislative Simplification and Consultation Concerns**: While legislative simplification efforts are welcomed by businesses, stakeholders criticize the use of accelerated legislative procedures (27 out of 39 draft bills in 2024 used this procedure), which contributes to legal uncertainty. Citizen consultation processes, such as the Agora application, have been interrupted since June 2024, raising concerns about democratic engagement.
- **European Court of Human Rights Implementation**: France has 26 leading judgments pending implementation as of 1 January 2025, an increase from the previous year. The implementation rate for leading judgments from the past 10 years was 66%, with an average pending time of 3 years. The oldest pending judgment concerns the inaction of authorities in executing judicial measures regarding illegally occupied lands, pending since 2007.
- **Civic Space and Civil Society Organizations**: The civic space in France is considered narrowed, with budgetary restrictions impacting civil society organizations. Despite this, the financial environment remains favorable, and there have been fewer administrative dissolutions or fund withdrawals based on the Republican Engagement Contract in 2024. However, reports of intimidation and attacks against human rights defenders continue.
