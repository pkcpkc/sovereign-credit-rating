## Executive Summary

The European Commission's 2025 Country Report for Austria identifies a nation navigating a persistent economic recession, characterized by three consecutive years of contraction. Austria's economy is strained by industrial weakness, broad-based cost pressures, and declining exports, particularly to the United States. Despite a resilient labor market, structural challenges such as demographic decline, skills shortages, and declining productivity growth pose significant long-term risks. Fiscal consolidation efforts are underway, with the government targeting significant savings to address deficits well above the EU treaty threshold. The report highlights the need for comprehensive structural reforms, including pension and healthcare adjustments, tax mix optimization, and regulatory simplification, to restore competitiveness and ensure long-term fiscal sustainability.

## Key Highlights

- **[Economic Recession]**: Austria is experiencing its third consecutive year of recession, with GDP expected to contract by 0.3% in 2025, following declines of 1.2% in 2024 and 1% in 2023, driven by industrial weakness and lower exports.
- **[Fiscal Deficit and Consolidation]**: The government deficit is projected to exceed 4% of GDP in 2025 (4.4%), with a commitment to achieve EUR 6.4 billion in savings for 2025 and EUR 8.4 billion for 2026 through consolidation measures.
- **[Inflation and Competitiveness]**: Inflation, which peaked at 8.6% in 2022, declined to 2.9% in 2024 but is expected to remain above 2% in 2025. Austria has lost cost competitiveness since 2019, with nominal unit labor costs rising 10% more than the euro area average.
- **[Labor Market Resilience vs. Structural Issues]**: The unemployment rate is expected to remain contained at 5.3% in 2025, but the working-age population is declining, and total weekly hours worked are decreasing due to a rise in part-time work and reduced hours.
- **[Productivity Decline]**: Labor productivity growth has slowed significantly since 2008, averaging 0.6% annually compared to 1.8% pre-2008, with total factor productivity growth also declining, posing a risk to long-term growth prospects.
- **[Public Spending and Demographics]**: Rising public spending, driven by inflation indexation and aging costs, is weighing on public finances. Pension expenditure is projected to increase from 14.2% of GDP in 2024 to 15% in 2030, and healthcare expenditure is expected to rise significantly.
- **[Innovation and Business Environment]**: Despite high R&D expenditure, Austria struggles to translate this into business dynamism and productivity growth, hindered by bureaucratic hurdles, regulatory barriers in professional services, and limited access to risk capital.
- **[Energy Transition and Costs]**: High energy prices, particularly for industry, affect competitiveness. Austria aims to reach climate neutrality by 2040, with ambitious targets for renewable energy, but faces challenges in grid expansion and permitting procedures.
- **[Social Fairness and Skills]**: Skills shortages, particularly in ICT and construction, are a key constraint. The report highlights disparities in poverty risks for women, single parents, and migrants, and calls for improved early childhood education and care to boost female labor market participation.
