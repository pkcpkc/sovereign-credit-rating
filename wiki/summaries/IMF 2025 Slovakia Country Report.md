# Summary of: IMF 2025 Slovakia Country Report

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF (2025) Slovakia Country Report - 2025 Article IV Consultation—Press Release; and Staff Report
- **Effective Period**: March 2025
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Country Report / Article IV Consultation

## Executive Summary

The International Monetary Fund concluded its 2025 Article IV consultation with the Slovak Republic, endorsing staff appraisal on a lapse-of-time basis following discussions in late 2024 and early 2025. The Slovak economy demonstrated resilience, growing by 2.0 percent in 2024, outpacing the euro area average, driven by strong private consumption and public investment. However, growth is projected to moderate to 1.8 percent in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation, before recovering to 2.1 percent in 2026. Inflation, which declined from record highs in 2023, is expected to rise temporarily to 4.1 percent in 2025 due to higher indirect taxes, before moderating to the 2 percent target by early 2027.

The IMF highlights Slovakia's ambitious fiscal consolidation plan targeting a reduction in the headline deficit to 4.7 percent of GDP in 2025 and close to 2 percent by 2028. While commensurate with fiscal challenges, further deficit-reducing measures are required beyond 2025 to ensure long-term debt sustainability, particularly given the significant burden of aging-related costs. The financial sector remains resilient with strong capital buffers, though vulnerabilities persist in the residential and commercial real estate markets. Structural reforms are urgently needed to diversify the economy, sustain productivity growth, address demographic challenges, and strengthen governance and judicial independence to mitigate corruption risks and ensure the effective absorption of EU funds.

## Key Highlights

- **[IMF Board Conclusion]**: The IMF Executive Board concluded the 2025 Article IV consultation on March 24, 2025, endorsing the staff appraisal without a formal meeting.
- **[Growth Trajectory]**: Economic growth accelerated to 2.0 percent in 2024, driven by private consumption and public consumption, but is forecast to decline to 1.8 percent in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation, before rising to 2.1 percent in 2026.
- **[Inflation Dynamics]**: Headline inflation increased in the second half of 2024 and early 2025, reaching 4.2 percent in January 2025 due to higher indirect taxes and global food prices, before being projected to moderate to 3.1 percent in 2026 and reach the 2 percent target by early 2027.
- **[Fiscal Consolidation Plan]**: The government’s 2025 budget targets a headline deficit reduction to 4.7 percent of GDP, from 5.8 percent in 2024. Staff forecasts imply a 5.0 percent deficit, but structural tightening is broadly aligned. Further measures are needed to achieve the medium-term target of a deficit close to 2 percent of GDP by 2028.
- **[Debt Sustainability]**: Without additional consolidation, public debt is projected to rise to 74 percent of GDP by 2030. The IMF recommends further deficit-reducing measures, including expenditure rationalization, reducing reduced VAT rates, raising property taxes, and phasing out temporary energy support and the financial transaction tax (FTT).
- **[Financial Sector Resilience]**: The banking sector is resilient with strong capital and liquidity buffers. However, risks remain in the residential real estate market due to mortgage refixation and in the commercial real estate (CRE) office segment. Macroprudential policies are deemed appropriate but could be strengthened with a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer.
- **[Structural Reforms and Governance]**: The IMF stresses the need for structural reforms to diversify the economy, particularly reducing dependence on the automotive sector and addressing high automation risks. Strengthening governance, reducing corruption, and enhancing judicial independence are critical to improving the investment climate and ensuring timely disbursement of EU funds.
- **[Demographic Challenges]**: Adverse demographic trends and an aging population pose significant medium-term risks. The IMF recommends policies to increase labor force participation, including integrating migrants, improving child and elderly care, and reforming pension rules to eliminate early retirement options.
- **[External Position]**: Slovakia’s external position is assessed to be moderately weaker than fundamentals, with a current account deficit of 2.7 percent of GDP in 2024. However, the net international investment position is improving, and high FDI inflows mitigate some vulnerability.
