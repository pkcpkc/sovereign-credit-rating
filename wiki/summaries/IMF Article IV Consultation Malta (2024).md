# Summary of: IMF Article IV Consultation: Malta (2024)

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: IMF Staff Report for the 2024 Article IV Consultation—Malta
- **Effective Period**: January 2025 (Discussions ended November 15, 2024; Report completed December 17, 2024)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Country Report (Article IV Consultation)

## Executive Summary

The IMF’s 2024 Article IV Consultation with Malta highlights the country’s remarkable economic performance over the past decade, driven primarily by export-oriented service industries such as tourism and online gaming. While growth is expected to moderate, it remains among the highest in Europe. However, this labor-intensive growth model has led to significant capacity constraints, including strained infrastructure, public services, and tight labor markets, raising questions about long-term sustainability.

The IMF Executive Board endorsed staff recommendations urging Malta to shift its growth strategy toward productivity-driven, socially, and environmentally sustainable development. Key policy priorities include:

1.  **Fiscal Consolidation and Reorientation**: While welcoming the authorities' commitment to fiscal consolidation under the new EU framework, the IMF recommends phasing out broad fixed-energy price policies in favor of targeted subsidies. The resulting fiscal space should be redirected toward investment, innovation, and green transition to boost productivity.
2.  **Financial Stability**: The financial system is robust, but risks are concentrated in the real estate sector, which accounts for 70% of bank loans. The IMF advises tightening macroprudential policies, specifically raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer and broadening its scope beyond residential mortgages.
3.  **Structural Reforms**: Efforts to enhance innovation, digitalization, and educational outcomes are critical. The IMF emphasizes the need for a roadmap for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reform aligned with the EU’s Pillar II directive to protect revenue and guide investors.
4.  **Governance and Climate**: Continued strengthening of the AML/CFT framework and judicial reforms is essential. Additionally, concerted public and private sector action is required to meet ambitious climate goals, particularly in reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

Malta’s external position is strong, with a large net international investment position, mitigating external vulnerabilities. Sovereign debt remains manageable, below the EU’s 60% ceiling, with low risk of sovereign stress.

## Key Highlights

- **[Fiscal Policy & Energy Subsidies]**: The IMF recommends gradually phasing out the fixed-energy price policy, which accounts for 20% of the fiscal deficit. Resources saved should be reallocated to investment, innovation, and public services. The authorities should develop a long-term fiscal framework reflecting spending pressures from aging and climate transition.
- **[Financial Sector & Real Estate Risk]**: Banks’ exposure to real estate has reached 70% of the loan portfolio. Although banks are well-capitalized, the IMF advises tightening macroprudential stance by raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB) rate and expanding its scope to include construction and commercial real estate. Vigilant monitoring of cyber risks and commercial real estate data gaps is also warranted.
- **[Growth Model & Productivity]**: Past growth has been driven by labor-intensive sectors (tourism, gaming), leading to capacity constraints. The key challenge is enhancing a productivity-driven growth strategy. The IMF highlights the need to improve educational outcomes, increase STEM enrollment, and support innovation through effective tax incentives and venture capital.
- **[Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Reform]**: Malta has deferred implementation of the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive until end-2029. The IMF urges the development and dissemination of a clear CIT reform roadmap to guide taxpayers and investors, preventing revenue loss to other jurisdictions and reducing distortions between domestic and multinational firms.
- **[External Sector Strength]**: Malta’s external position is substantially stronger than fundamentals imply. The current account surplus is projected at 6.3% of GDP in 2024. The country maintains a large positive net international investment position (~95% of GDP), providing a strong buffer against external shocks.
- **[Geopolitical & Domestic Risks]**: Risks to the outlook are tilted to the downside, including spillovers from regional conflicts (Ukraine, Gaza) and geoeconomic fragmentation. Domestically, higher-than-expected wage growth could fuel inflation, while capacity pressures from tourism and immigration strain public infrastructure.
- **[AML/CFT & Judicial Reform]**: Progress has been made in strengthening the Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) framework. The IMF recommends continuing to monitor emerging threats like trade-based money laundering and advancing judicial reforms, particularly regarding the appointment of the chief justice and system efficiency.
- **[Climate Goals]**: Current policies are insufficient to meet the 19% GHG emissions reduction target by 2030. The IMF calls for robust implementation of the Low Carbon Development Strategy, phasing out energy subsidies to incentivize conservation, and completing climate vulnerability risk assessments.
