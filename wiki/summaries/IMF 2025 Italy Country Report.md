# Summary of: IMF 2025 Italy Country Report

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Article IV Consultation—Press Release; Staff Report; and Statement by the Executive Director for Italy
- **Effective Period**: July 2025 (Discussions concluded May 28, 2025; Report completed July 1, 2025)
- **Issuer**: International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- **Document Type**: Article IV Consultation Report

## Executive Summary

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) completed its 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy, concluding that the Italian economy has demonstrated notable resilience despite heightened global uncertainty. Real GDP grew by 0.7 percent in 2024, supported by National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) investments and net exports, with activity remaining resilient in early 2025. A key achievement was the return to a primary surplus in 2024, driven by improved tax compliance and strong labor market performance. However, significant structural challenges persist, including low productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-EU average female labor force participation.

The IMF Executive Board commended the strong fiscal performance but emphasized the need for sustained consolidation to place the persistently high public debt on a clear downward trajectory. The staff recommended reaching a primary surplus of 3 percent of GDP by 2027 through measures such as improving tax compliance, rationalizing tax expenditures, and streamlining pension-related spending. The banking sector's soundness has improved, with high profitability and adequate capital buffers, though vulnerabilities among less significant institutions remain. The staff stressed that full and timely implementation of the NRRP, followed by comprehensive structural reforms focusing on productivity, labor supply, and the green transition, is essential to durably lift growth and offset demographic headwinds.

## Key Highlights

- **[IMF Executive Board Conclusion]**: On July 18, 2025, the IMF Executive Board completed its consultation, welcoming Italy's economic resilience and strong fiscal performance while stressing the urgency of accelerating structural reforms to address productivity malaise and population aging.
- **[Fiscal Overperformance and Primary Surplus]**: The 2024 fiscal deficit shrank to 3.4 percent of GDP, better than projected, enabling a return to a primary surplus of 0.4 percent of GDP. The IMF recommends an additional fiscal adjustment to reach a 3 percent primary surplus by 2027 to ensure a declining debt trajectory.
- **[Structural Challenges: Productivity and Demographics]**: Weak productivity growth and rapid population aging are identified as pressing issues. The old-age dependency ratio is projected to increase sharply, and low female labor force participation remains a constraint. Reforms under the NRRP and subsequent Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan must focus on boosting human capital, innovation, and labor supply.
- **[Banking Sector Resilience]**: The banking sector has strengthened, with record profits, sound asset quality (low nonperforming loans), and ample capital and liquidity buffers. However, vulnerabilities persist among less significant institutions, and close monitoring of the sovereign-bank nexus is warranted.
- **[National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) Implementation]**: Italy has achieved 54 percent of NRRP milestones and received nearly two-thirds of allocated funds, ranking among top EU performers. However, spending delays persist, and full implementation is critical to addressing structural gaps in infrastructure, justice, and public administration.
- **[External Sector Assessment]**: Italy's current account turned to a surplus in 2024, supported by lower energy imports and robust tourism. The Net International Investment Position strengthened to 15.3 percent of GDP, though the external position is assessed as weaker than implied by medium-term fundamentals, requiring continued fiscal consolidation and productivity-boosting reforms.
- **[Risk Assessment]**: Downside risks are significant, including escalating trade tensions, regional conflicts, tighter global financial conditions, and macro-critical climate-related shocks. Inefficient NRRP implementation or tax reform delays could also result in lower-than-expected growth and weaker fiscal consolidation.
