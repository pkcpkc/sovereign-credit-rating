# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report - Belgium Country Chapter

## Context Metadata
- **Source Document:** SWD(2025) 901 final (Commission Staff Working Document)
- **Date of Issue:** July 8, 2025
- **Jurisdiction:** Belgium
- **Subject Matter:** Rule of law, judicial efficiency, anti-corruption framework, media pluralism, and civic space.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Belgium highlights a high level of perceived judicial independence, though structural challenges regarding resource deficiencies and judicial efficiency persist. While the Government intends to transfer budgetary management to the judiciary, progress on long-term structural funding and the reform of lobbying legislation remains stalled. The anti-corruption framework shows a low perception of public sector corruption, yet concerns remain regarding the handling of high-level cases and the lack of transparency in asset declarations. Media freedom is bolstered by new legal protections for journalists (including the decriminalization of defamation), though concerns regarding political interference and the narrowing of civic space through potential demonstration restrictions are noted.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System
- **Judicial Independence:** Perceived independence remains high (64% for the general public; 72% for companies). The Government is proceeding with transferring budgetary management to the judiciary's three pillars.
- **Resource Deficiencies:** Despite a 5% increase in judges/prosecutors (2020–2024), salaries and working conditions remain unattractive, and a long-term structural budget approach is still required.
- **Digitalisation:** The Government is responding to Court of Audit criticisms regarding the lack of an overarching strategy and excessive reliance on external consultants for justice digitalisation.
- **Efficiency:** Backlogs remain problematic, particularly in the Brussels Court of Appeal. New file management projects funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility aim to improve statistics collection.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
- **Corruption Perceptions:** Belgium ranks 9th in the EU for low corruption. New structures, including a financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF), are planned to target organized crime.
- **Integrity Gaps:** There is a lack of monitoring for integrity policies regarding Ministers and Parliament. Asset declarations lack adequate verification, as they are submitted in sealed envelopes.
- **Lobbying & Revolving Doors:** No significant progress has been made on lobbying legislation. "Revolving door" rules remain limited to non-binding circulars without clear cooling-off periods.
- **Whistleblower Protection:** A new federal law was sanctioned in March 2025 to transpose EU Directive 2019/1937, though some critics argue the scope for parliamentary civil servants is too narrow.

### III. Media Pluralism and Media Freedom
- **Regulatory Independence:** Media regulators in Flanders (VRM) and the French-speaking community (CSA) maintain strong legal foundations and are adapting to the Digital Services Act (DSA).
- **Public Service Media:** Broadcasters (VRT, RTBF, BRF) face budgetary pressures and governance scrutiny. RTBF faces a projected €78 million shortfall until 2029.
- **Journalist Protections:** Belgium has decriminalized defamation and introduced harsher penalties for crimes against journalists. However, legal intimidation (SLAPPs) and political interference remain active concerns.

### IV. Checks and Balances / Civic Space
- **Compliance with Rulings:** Belgium continues to face supervision by the Council of Europe regarding the failure to implement European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) judgments.
- **Civic Space:** Concerns are rising over potential "demonstration bans" for individuals convicted of illegal acts during previous manifestations.
- **Investment Protection:** Over 60% of companies express confidence in the protection of investments by Belgian law and courts.
