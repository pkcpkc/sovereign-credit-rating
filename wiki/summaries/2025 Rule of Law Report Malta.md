# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Malta

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: SWD(2025) 918 final - 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on the rule of law situation in Malta
- **Effective Period**: 2025
- **Issuer**: European Commission
- **Document Type**: Report

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Malta indicates mixed progress in upholding the rule of law, with notable advancements in judicial independence perceptions and prosecutorial reforms, alongside persistent challenges in corruption accountability, media freedom, and institutional checks. Perceived judicial independence remains high among the public and businesses, supported by the completion of transferring non-summary prosecution powers to the Attorney General. However, significant concerns remain regarding the independence of specialised tribunals, the length of proceedings, and the capacity to deliver final judgments in high-level corruption cases.

Corruption perception remains high, with weaknesses identified in the Permanent Commission Against Corruption's operational capacity and a lack of concrete results. Integrity measures for high-level politicians are deemed insufficient, particularly regarding lobbying and codes of conduct. In the media sector, risks remain high regarding the independence of the media regulator and public service media governance, with limited progress on protecting journalists' working environments.

Institutional checks and balances face challenges, including a controversial reform to magisterial inquiries that raises concerns about accountability for high-level offences. The European Court of Justice's 2025 ruling declaring Malta's investor citizenship scheme contrary to EU Law marks a significant geopolitical and legal event. Recommendations focus on strengthening judicial independence, improving corruption investigation outcomes, enhancing media freedom safeguards, and establishing a National Human Rights Institution.

## Key Highlights

- **[Judicial Independence Perception]**: Perceived judicial independence remains high among the general public (69%) and companies (63%), with ongoing constitutional reforms aiming to involve the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice.
- **[Prosecutorial Reform Completion]**: The transfer of non-summary case prosecutions from the Police to the Attorney General was completed in September 2024, significantly increasing the Attorney General's prosecutorial authority.
- **[Specialised Tribunals Independence]**: Concerns persist regarding the independence of specialised tribunals, highlighted by a 2024 European Court of Human Rights judgment on the Immigration Appeals Board, prompting government proposals to remove executive involvement in member appointments.
- **[Justice System Efficiency]**: Despite staff recruitment, the justice system faces significant delays, with disposition times for civil, criminal, and administrative cases remaining among the highest in the EU, adversely affecting business confidence.
- **[Corruption Perception and Accountability]**: Malta scores 46/100 in the Corruption Perceptions Index, with persistent challenges in establishing a robust track record of final judgments in high-level corruption cases and limited capacity of the Permanent Commission Against Corruption.
- **[Investor Citizenship Scheme Ruling]**: On 29 April 2025, the European Court of Justice ruled that Malta's investor citizenship scheme is contrary to EU Law, citing the "commercialisation" of Union citizenship.
- **[Media Freedom Risks]**: Risks to media pluralism remain high, particularly regarding the independence of the media regulator and public service media governance, with limited progress on protecting journalists' working environments and access to official documents.
- **[Magisterial Inquiry Reform]**: The approval of Bill No. 125 in April 2025 increased the threshold for citizen-prompted magisterial inquiries, drawing strong criticism from stakeholders who argue it limits accountability for high-level offences.
- **[Institutional Checks and Balances]**: Limited progress was made in establishing a National Human Rights Institution and introducing a formal framework for public participation in the legislative process, with draft legislation still pending.
