## Executive Summary

This document provides an overview of Standard & Poor's (S&P) sovereign credit rating methodology, as presented by Alex Stomper at HU Berlin in 2024. The framework evaluates a country's creditworthiness through five primary assessments: Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, and Monetary. Each assessment addresses specific fundamental drivers of credit risk, such as policymaking stability, income levels, external liquidity, fiscal sustainability, and monetary policy credibility.

The methodology culminates in an indicative rating level based on these assessments, which is then subject to adjustments for factors like debt payment culture and external security risks. A significant portion of the document highlights the inherent subjectivity in the process, noting that "discretionary judgement" plays a crucial role, particularly in institutional assessments and through supplemental adjustment factors. The presentation concludes by proposing an application of this methodology to European countries, aiming to document instances where the process lacks replicability due to analyst discretion.

## Key Highlights

- **Five-Pillar Assessment Framework**: S&P's sovereign rating is built upon five core assessments: Institutional (policymaking stability, transparency), Economic (GDP per capita, growth, diversity), External (currency status, liquidity, position), Fiscal (deficit sustainability, debt burden, contingent liabilities), and Monetary (exchange rate regime, policy credibility).
- **Institutional Assessment Nuances**: Beyond basic indicators of stability and transparency, the institutional assessment explicitly accounts for "debt payment culture" (which can cap the rating at '6' if weak) and "external security risks" (which can downgrade the rating by one or two categories).
- **Fiscal Assessment Structure**: The fiscal assessment is divided into "Flows" (performance and flexibility) and "Stocks" (debt burden). It also specifically evaluates contingent liabilities, including those from financial institutions, nonfinancial public-sector enterprises, and off-budget guarantees.
- **Monetary Policy Credibility**: The monetary assessment evaluates the central bank's independence, the effectiveness of its policy toolkit, price stability, its role as a lender of last resort, and the depth of financial markets.
- **Discretionary Judgement**: The methodology allows for significant analyst discretion, including "supplemental adjustment factors" (e.g., event risk, extremely weak liquidity) and the ability to override the indicative rating by more than one notch based on unspecified factors.
- **Replicability Concerns**: The presentation notes that the heavy reliance on discretionary judgement, particularly in institutional assessments and supplemental adjustments, creates challenges for the replicability of sovereign credit ratings.
