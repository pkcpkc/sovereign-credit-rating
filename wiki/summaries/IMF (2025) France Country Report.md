# Summary of: IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - France

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Type:** IMF Article IV Consultation Report (Staff Report & Press Release)
*   **Reporting Period:** 2024–2025 (with projections to 2030)
*   **Primary Objective:** Assessment of France's economic resilience, fiscal sustainability, and structural reform progress.
*   **Key Stakeholders:** International Monetary Fund (IMF), French Government (Ministry of Economy & Finance), Banque de France.

## Executive Summary
The IMF concludes that the French economy has demonstrated notable resilience in 2024, supported by a robust labor market and successful disinflation. However, the report highlights significant challenges regarding high and rising public debt (reaching 113.1% of GDP in 2024) and the need for urgent fiscal consolidation. While the French authorities' Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) is viewed as appropriate, the IMF emphasizes that meeting deficit targets (below 3% by 2029) requires a credible, frontloaded package of measures and decisive spending rationalization. Key recommendations focus on boosting productivity through AI and green transitions, easing regulatory burdens for SMEs, and implementing social reforms to enhance labor force participation.

## Key Highlights

*   **# Economic Resilience & Growth:** Real GDP grew by 1.1% in 2024, aided by the Paris Olympics. Growth is projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025 due to trade tensions and subdued demand, before rebounding to 1.0% in 2026.
*   **# Fiscal Challenges:** The 2024 deficit reached 5.8% of GDP, exceeding targets due to lower revenues and social security overruns. France is currently under the EU's Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP).
*   **# Debt Sustainability:** Public debt is highly sensitive to real interest rates and growth paths. The IMF assesses sovereign risk as "moderate," noting that while the debt market is liquid, the primary deficit remains above the debt-stabilizing level.
*   **# Productivity & Innovation:** France faces a widening productivity gap with the US, particularly in tech. The IMF highlights AI adoption as a potential driver for a 1.2% cumulative TFP boost over five years.
*   **# Financial Stability:** The banking sector remains resilient with healthy capital buffers (average 19.8% regulatory capital ratio). Risks are contained, but the IMF advises closer monitoring of non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) and cyber risks.
*   **# Labor & Demographics:** The labor market is robust, but the aging population poses long-term pressure on the pension system (which ran a 0.2% deficit in 2024). Reforms to increase the retirement age and support women in STEM are prioritized.
*   **# Structural Reform Agenda:** Key priorities include the "Simplification Bill" to reduce red tape, enhancing R&D tax credit targeting, and fostering the "silver economy" to manage demographic shifts.
