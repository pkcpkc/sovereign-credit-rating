# Summary of: EU Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report - Austria

## Context Metadata
*   **Source Document:** Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2025) 920 final)
*   **Reporting Date:** July 8, 2025
*   **Scope:** Analysis of the rule of law situation in Austria, covering justice, anti-corruption, media freedom, and institutional checks and balances.
*   **Key Stakeholders:** Austrian Government, Ministry of Justice, Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK), Court of Audit (ACA), and various judicial/media associations.

## Executive Summary
Austria maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence and a generally efficient justice system. However, the report identifies critical areas requiring further reform: the systematic involvement of the judiciary in appointing administrative court presidents, the establishment of an independent Federal Prosecution Office (which is currently in the legislative drafting phase), and the strengthening of lobbying frameworks. While anti-corruption efforts are progressing through the National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2023-2025, challenges remain regarding the disclosure of assets and interests for Members of Parliament. Media freedom is generally protected, though economic pressures on private media and concerns over the distribution of state advertising persist. Significant progress was noted in the implementation of tax reforms for civil society and the upcoming Freedom of Information Act.

## Key Highlights

### I. Justice System & Judicial Independence
*   **Perception of Independence:** 86% of the general public and 71% of companies perceive judicial independence as "fairly or very good" in 2025.
*   **Administrative Appointments:** No progress has been made on involving the judiciary in the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents, raising concerns regarding European standards.
*   **Prosecutorial Reform:** The Government has committed to creating an independent Federal Prosecution Office as a collegiate body. Draft legislation is currently being prepared.
*   **Instructional Powers:** The Minister of Justice continues to exercise the right to instruct prosecutors in individual cases, highlighting the ongoing need for structural prosecution reform.
*   **Digitalization & Resources:** The "Justice 3.0" initiative aims for 90% digital case management by year-end. While judicial posts are largely filled, there is an identified need for ~200 additional posts to manage workload.
*   **Court Fees:** The Constitutional Court ruled that restrictions on legal aid in the Administrative Procedural Code were unconstitutional, expanding access to justice.

### II. Anti-Corruption Framework
*   **Strategy Implementation:** The National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2023-2025 is active, with the Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK) detecting an increase in potential corruption cases in 2024.
*   **Asset Disclosure:** Progress remains limited on establishing effective rules for assets and interests' declaration for Members of Parliament.
*   **Lobbying & Revolving Doors:** The lobbying framework remains limited in scope and lacks sufficient sanctions. Post-employment "revolving doors" restrictions remain narrow, though a three-year cooling-off period for some constitutional judges was recently introduced.
*   **Whistleblower Protection:** Web-based reporting channels established in 2023 are functioning well and have received numerous reports.

### III. Media Pluralism and Freedom
*   **Regulatory Independence:** The media regulator (KommAustria) and the Austrian Press Council continue to function independently.
*   **Public Service Media:** Following a Constitutional Court ruling, the ORF Act was reformed in March 2025 to increase the independence of its management bodies.
*   **Economic Pressures:** The private media sector faces a deteriorating economic situation due to revenue shifts toward online platforms.
*   **State Advertising:** Public spending on state advertising more than doubled in 2024. Stakeholders have criticized the concentration of funding among certain outlets and the lack of effective sanction mechanisms for violations.
*   **Freedom of Information:** Preparations are underway for the Freedom of Information Act, set to provide a subjective right to information from public authorities in September 2025.

### IV. Institutional Checks and Balances
*   **Ombudsman Board:** The board functions effectively as the National Human Rights Institution; a review of its nomination procedure is planned to increase transparency.
*   **Investment Protection:** 72% of companies express confidence in the legal protection of investments in Austria.
*   **ECHR Judgments:** The number of leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights pending implementation decreased to 4 as of June 2025.
*   **Civic Space:** The tax framework for civil society organizations has shown positive results, broadening eligibility for tax-exempt status.
