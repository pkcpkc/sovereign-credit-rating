# Summary of: 2025 Rule of Law Report Slovakia Country Chapter

## Context Metadata

- **Source Document**: 2025 Rule of Law Report Slovakia Country Chapter (SWD(2025) 925 final)
- **Effective Period**: Reference period covering developments in 2024 and early 2025
- **Issuer**: European Commission (DG JUST)
- **Document Type**: Commission Staff Working Document / Country Report

## Executive Summary

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Slovakia highlights a stagnation or deterioration in key rule of law indicators, particularly regarding judicial independence, anti-corruption frameworks, media pluralism, and institutional checks and balances. While the government has engaged in dialogue with the European Commission and enacted some amendments to align with EU law (specifically the PIF Directive), systemic risks remain high.

Key findings include:
1.  **Justice System**: Perceived judicial independence remains low. The Judicial Council's independence is compromised by vague dismissal procedures and political appointments. Reforms to the Criminal Code, particularly regarding statutes of limitations and sanctions for corruption, have negatively impacted high-level prosecutions. The Prosecutor General's power to annul decisions and redirect cases undermines investigative independence.
2.  **Anti-Corruption**: The dissolution of specialized anti-corruption bodies (NAKA, SPO) has led to operational delays and a drop in case numbers. There is no effective regulation of lobbying, and asset declaration systems remain fragmented. Public procurement rules have been weakened, increasing corruption risks in strategic investments.
3.  **Media and Society**: Public service media (STVR) has become more politicized following its restructuring. Journalist safety has deteriorated, with no progress on legal safeguards against SLAPPs. Civil society faces increased administrative burdens and political pressure through new NGO reporting laws.
4.  **Institutional Checks**: The frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures bypasses stakeholder consultation. Constitutional amendments threatening the primacy of EU law are under discussion. Slovakia has a significant backlog of unimplemented European Court of Human Rights judgments.

## Key Highlights

- **[Judicial Independence Erosion]**: Perceived judicial independence remains low (32% general public, 34% companies). The Judicial Council's dismissal process lacks sufficient safeguards, allowing political influence over its composition. Proposals for judicial bonuses with discretionary elements raise concerns about manipulation.
- **[Corruption Legislation Impact]**: The 2024 Criminal Code reforms, which lowered sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption, have led to the closure or reclassification of several high-level cases. While subsequent amendments aligned with the PIF Directive, the initial impact has materialized in reduced prosecution rates.
- **[Dissolution of Anti-Corruption Bodies]**: The closure of the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO) has fragmented competence, leading to delays, loss of specialized expertise, and a significant drop in registered corruption cases. The Prosecutor General's broad powers to annul decisions and reassign cases persist as a major concern.
- **[Public Service Media Politicization]**: The July 2024 Act on Slovak Television and Radio increased political control over the broadcaster's governance (Council appointments by Parliament and Minister of Culture), leading to a regression in editorial independence according to the Media Pluralism Monitor.
- **[Journalist Safety Deterioration]**: There has been no progress in improving the safety of journalists. The Platform for Support of Press Freedom is non-operational, and incidents of verbal attacks, intimidation, and SLAPPs have increased. Defamation reform plans have been abandoned.
- **[Fast-Track Legislation]**: In 2024, 58 out of 131 laws were adopted with limited public participation, including 28 via fast-track procedures. This practice limits stakeholder involvement and transparency, contrary to previous recommendations.
- **[NGO Pressure]**: The law adopted in April 2025 imposing annual reporting and disclosure obligations on NGOs has increased administrative burdens and is viewed by stakeholders as a tool for political control, narrowing the civil society space.
- **[Constitutional Concerns]**: Proposed constitutional amendments in April 2025, which could affect the primacy of EU law, have been postponed but remain a point of intense dialogue between the Commission and Slovak authorities.
