## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Minister for Institutional Reforms plays a pivotal role in Italy's governance stability and institutional modernization, directly influencing sovereign credit rating factors related to policy predictability and institutional strength. Under their tenure, significant progress has been made in advancing a comprehensive justice system reform, which includes implementing legislation approved by the High Council for the Judiciary regarding magistrate appointments, performance appraisals, and judicial management.

A key area of focus has been the draft constitutional reform aimed at enhancing governmental stability and introducing the direct election of the Prime Minister. While this initiative has received stakeholder support for its potential to strengthen executive stability, it faces scrutiny from the judiciary and requires careful parliamentary handling. The Minister works closely with the [[Minister of Justice]] and legislative bodies to navigate these complex reforms.

Regarding anti-corruption and transparency, the Ministry collaborates with the National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) to update the National Anti-Correction Plan and issue guidelines on revolving doors. However, challenges remain in establishing comprehensive rules on conflicts of interest for political office holders and advancing legislation on lobbying and political party financing, which have stalled in Parliament.

The Minister also oversees institutional frameworks affecting media pluralism and civic space. This includes engaging with the [[RAI CEO]] to stabilize funding through the maintenance of the license fee, while structural governance reforms for public broadcaster RAI remain under discussion. Furthermore, the implementation of the Security Law, intended to combat terrorism and organized crime, has raised concerns from international bodies like the Council of Europe and UN Special Rapporteurs regarding its potential impact on freedom of assembly and expression. The absence of a National Human Rights Institution (NHRI) remains a notable institutional gap identified in oversight reports, prompting ongoing dialogue with international human rights monitors.
