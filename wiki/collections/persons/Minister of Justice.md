## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Minister of Justice plays a pivotal role in shaping the institutional framework and rule of law environment in multiple jurisdictions, directly impacting sovereign credit rating factors such as policy credibility, institutional strength, and geopolitical stability.

In **Italy**, the Minister's portfolio is central to the ongoing debate surrounding the draft constitutional reform aimed at separating the careers of judges and prosecutors, which was approved by the [[Chamber of Deputies]] in January 2025 but faces significant opposition from judicial bodies like the [[High Council for the Judiciary (Italy)]], raising concerns regarding separation of powers. The Minister oversees the implementation of comprehensive justice reforms, including the mandatory deployment of the digital case management system (APP) in criminal courts starting January 1, 2025. This initiative addresses persistent issues with judicial efficiency and case backlogs, which are cited as threats to growth prospects due to the excessive length of proceedings. Additionally, the Ministry is involved in addressing persistent staff shortages in the judiciary, with recruitment efforts aiming to close a 17% gap in magistrates. In the realm of anti-corruption and institutional checks, the Minister's domain intersects with the work of the [[National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC)]]. While the National Anti-Corruption Plan was updated in January 2025, legislative gaps remain regarding lobbying regulation and political party financing transparency, areas where the Government has faced criticism for lack of progress. The Minister also operates within a context of frequent use of urgency decrees by the Government, a practice that stakeholders argue raises concerns about the separation of powers and governance stability.

In **Poland**, the Minister holds a critical position in addressing long-standing rule of law concerns, particularly regarding judicial independence, as outlined in the Action Plan on the Rule of Law. A major milestone under this tenure is the significant progress made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the Prosecutor General, aiming to ensure the functional independence of the prosecution service. A draft law to this end, incorporating [[Venice Commission]] recommendations, was approved by the Cabinet Standing Committee in December 2024. The Minister is also central to preparations for legislation addressing the status of judges appointed by the post-2017 [[National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)]], involving a classification system and potential removal. While acts amending the laws governing the NCJ and the [[Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)]] have been adopted by Parliament, they remain pending before the Constitutional Tribunal due to a presidential referral. The Government, under the Minister's oversight, continues to refuse to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, asserting the body lacks legitimacy, leading to a constitutional crisis and an ongoing infringement procedure by the [[European Commission]].

Furthermore, the Minister in Poland oversees the anti-corruption framework where the [[European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)]] is fully operational, and a new Central Office for Combating Corruption is to be established within the police. However, challenges persist regarding the lack of a comprehensive anti-corruption strategy, the absence of lobbying rules, and the continued existence of broad immunities for top executives, which hinder high-level corruption prosecutions. In the media sector, the Government plans a media law reform to implement the European Media Freedom Act, addressing the independence of the media regulator ([[KRRiT]]) and public broadcaster governance. Poland also strengthened rules for legislative consultations, introducing mandatory impact assessments under the Minister's regulatory domain, although the number of pending leading judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] has increased to 54 as of June 2025.
