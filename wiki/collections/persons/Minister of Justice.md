## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Minister of Justice plays a pivotal role in Italy's sovereign credit rating profile, particularly within the dimensions of **Institutional and Governance Strength** and **Policy Predictability and Reform Continuity**. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the Minister's ministry has overseen a comprehensive reform of the justice system, which is currently being implemented through specific legislation.

Key achievements attributed to this tenure include:
- The High Council for the Judiciary (HSC) taking forward tasks under implementing legislation, including directives on out-of-tenure appointments, magistrate performance appraisals, and the Code of Judicial Management.
- Legislative progress on the digitalisation of criminal courts and prosecutors' offices, contributing to a positive trend in resolving judicial cases and reducing backlogs across civil, criminal, administrative, and tax jurisdictions.

However, significant challenges remain that impact the country's institutional risk profile:
- **Staffing Shortages**: Persistent shortages in judicial and administrative staff across all court levels continue to hinder efficiency.
- **Constitutional Reform Controversy**: The draft constitutional reform, which separates the careers of judges and prosecutors, has been approved by the Chamber of Deputies but faces strong opposition from the judiciary (including critical opinions from the HSC) and pending Senate scrutiny. This stalemate reflects broader tensions between political executive and judicial autonomy.
- **Regulatory Gaps**: Despite progress in justice administration, comprehensive rules on conflicts of interest for political office holders have not been established, and draft laws on lobbying remain stalled in Parliament.

The Minister's office is also central to the government's response to criticisms regarding civic space and the application of the Security Law, which has raised concerns among international bodies such as the United Nations Special Rapporteurs and the Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights regarding its potential arbitrary application and impact on fundamental freedoms.
