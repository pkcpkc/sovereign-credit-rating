## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Prosecutor General in Poland holds a critical position within the country's institutional framework, directly influencing sovereign credit rating assessments related to **Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength**. A key development noted in the 2025 Rule of Law Report is the significant progress made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the Prosecutor General. This separation is a structural strength aimed at ensuring the functional independence of the prosecution service, aligning with Venice Commission recommendations and mitigating risks associated with political interference in judicial matters.

This institutional independence is vital for the **Anti-Corruption Framework**, a core component of sovereign risk analysis. While the European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) is fully operational in Poland, the domestic role of the Prosecutor General remains pivotal. The National Prosecution Service has conducted reviews of past cases (2016–2023), identifying irregularities in investigations, which signals efforts to restore integrity. However, challenges persist in the broader anti-corruption landscape, including the lack of a comprehensive national strategy and the absence of lobbying rules. Furthermore, broad immunities for top executives continue to hinder high-level criminal accountability, creating a threat to institutional strength and policy credibility. These factors contribute to the perception of corruption and affect the **Economic Strength and Tax Base** by influencing investment protection confidence and the effectiveness of public procurement oversight.
