## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Prosecutor General serves as the head of the Prosecution Service of Hungary, a position marked by significant political and structural controversy in recent years. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report issued by the European Commission, the appointment process for this role underwent a fundamental shift with the Fifteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law. This amendment removed the requirement for the Prosecutor General to be selected from among prosecutors, thereby lowering eligibility criteria and increasing the risk of political influence over the selection process.

Despite maintaining a hierarchical structure that allows for potential political pressure on individual cases, the role remains central to Hungary's rule of law framework. The report highlights systemic deterioration in judicial independence, noting that the Prosecutor General's office operates within a system where undue pressure on judges persists. This includes public criticism from the Kúria President and smear campaigns against service courts, which contribute to an environment of legal uncertainty.

Furthermore, the Prosecution Service's effectiveness is constrained by broader anti-corruption shortcomings. The Integrity Authority faces obstacles in accessing state databases, and there is no robust track record for high-level corruption convictions. The role is further complicated by the "state of danger" regime, which has been extended to November 2025, allowing the government to override parliamentary acts and adopt emergency measures that impact the independence of judicial and prosecutorial functions. The 2025 election of a new Prosecutor General following the Fifteenth Amendment signifies a pivotal moment in these ongoing rule of law disputes between Hungary and EU institutions.
