## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The National Anti-Corruption Directorate serves as the primary law enforcement agency within the Romanian Government, tasked with investigating and prosecuting corruption offenses. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the Directorate maintains a positive track record in high-level prosecutions and the implementation of the 2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy remains on track.

However, the effectiveness of the Directorate is significantly impacted by systemic legal uncertainties arising from rulings by the High Court of Cassation and Justice regarding statutes of limitations. These judicial decisions have led to the annulment of many corruption convictions and the discontinuation of active cases, creating an environment of legal uncertainty that undermines enforcement efforts. Additionally, the Directorate's operations intersect with broader institutional integrity challenges, including resource constraints faced by partner agencies like the National Integrity Agency and ongoing scrutiny regarding judicial independence.

Recent developments include surveillance activities involving investigative journalists, which have raised concerns regarding media freedom and the operational transparency of state institutions. The Directorate's work continues to be monitored by international bodies, including the European Commission and the Council of Europe, as part of the broader assessment of Romania's rule of law and institutional stability.
