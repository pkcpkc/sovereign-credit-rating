## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Emily Fisher served as a member of the IMF staff team during the 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy. The consultation, which concluded in July 2025, assessed Italy’s macroeconomic stability, fiscal policy, and financial sector health amid a backdrop of resilient economic growth and persistent structural challenges.

As part of the IMF team, Fisher contributed to the analysis of Italy's fiscal performance, noting a return to a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP in 2024 and a reduction in the fiscal deficit to 3.4% of GDP. The staff report, for which Fisher provided input, highlighted Italy's high public debt level of 135.3% of GDP and recommended a more front-loaded fiscal adjustment to achieve a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027. This recommendation aimed to firmly place debt on a downward trajectory, contrasting with the Italian authorities' proposal for a more gradual seven-year adjustment path under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP).

Fisher’s work also covered the assessment of Italy's financial sector stability. The report acknowledged the banking sector's improved soundness, characterized by high profitability, low non-performing loans (2.7%), and adequate capital buffers. However, the staff emphasized continued vigilance regarding vulnerabilities among Less Significant Institutions (LSIs) and the sizable sovereign-bank nexus. Additionally, the consultation addressed structural reforms under the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP), noting that while 54% of milestones had been achieved, spending execution remained sluggish. The IMF emphasized the need for post-NRRP reforms to boost productivity, enhance labor supply, and address rapid population aging, which continues to weigh on medium-term growth prospects.
