## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As Prime Minister of Slovakia, Robert Fico leads a government facing significant scrutiny regarding its adherence to European Union rule of law standards. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report issued by the European Commission, the administration has been associated with a stagnation or deterioration in key institutional indicators, particularly concerning judicial independence and anti-corruption frameworks.

Key policy developments under his tenure include:

1.  **Judicial Reform and Independence**: The government has pursued reforms that have raised concerns about political interference in the judiciary. Notably, changes to the Criminal Code, including the introduction of lenient sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption offenses effective August 2024, have negatively impacted high-level prosecutions. The power of the Prosecutor General to annul decisions and redirect cases remains a central point of contention regarding investigative independence.
2.  **Anti-Corruption Framework**: The dissolution of specialized anti-corruption bodies, specifically the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO), has fragmented competence and led to operational delays. While the government enacted amendments in December 2024 to align with the PIF Directive, stakeholders report a drop in registered corruption cases and weakened public procurement transparency.
3.  **Media and Civil Society**: Under Fico's leadership, Slovakia has seen a regression in media pluralism. The Act on Slovak Television and Radio (STVR), which entered into force in July 2025, increased political control over the broadcaster's governance. Additionally, legislation adopted in April 2025 imposed new reporting obligations on NGOs, which critics argue narrows the civil society space and increases administrative burdens.
4.  **Legislative Process**: The administration has frequently utilized fast-track legislative procedures, with 28 out of 131 laws in 2024 adopted via such methods. This practice has been criticized for limiting stakeholder consultation and transparency. Proposed constitutional amendments affecting the primacy of EU law, though postponed in April 2025, remain a point of intense dialogue between the Commission and Slovak authorities.

Despite these challenges, Slovakia retains its status as an EU Member State, providing a geopolitical stability anchor. The government maintains commitment to new Anti-Corruption Strategy 2024-2029 goals and benefits from integration into the EU single market and access to EU funds.
