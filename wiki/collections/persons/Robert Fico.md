## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Robert Fico, as the Prime Minister of Slovakia, plays a central role in the country's sovereign credit rating factors, particularly concerning geopolitical and political stability, policy credibility, and policymaking stability. Under his leadership, the Slovak government has faced significant scrutiny regarding the rule of law, which directly impacts investor confidence and institutional strength.

Key risks associated with his administration include political polarization and rhetoric perceived as hostile towards NGOs and media, which threatens geopolitical stability. There are also concerns regarding potential constitutional amendments that could challenge the primacy of EU law, introducing legal uncertainty. The government's frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures has bypassed standard consultations, contributing to unpredictable administrative conduct and affecting investment protection.

In the realm of policy credibility, Fico's government has faced criticism for low perceived judicial independence and the dissolution of specialized anti-corruption entities such as the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Specialized Criminal Prosecutor’s Office (SPO). These actions have led to a fragmented anti-corruption framework and reduced oversight, negatively impacting the accountability dimension of the country's risk profile. Additionally, the broad powers held by the Prosecutor General, who can interfere in high-level corruption cases, remain a point of contention in institutional checks and balances.

Despite these threats, the government has taken steps to align criminal codes with the EU PIF Directive and has adopted new Legislative Rules. An ongoing dialogue with the [[European Commission]] aims to resolve rule of law issues, which is a positive factor for policymaking stability. However, the overall perception of a poor business climate due to rule of law risks continues to pose challenges for economic growth prospects and tax base reliability.
