## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Bogdan Swieczkowski serves as the President of the [[Constitutional Tribunal]] in Poland, a role that places him at the center of significant institutional and political tensions regarding the rule of law. As detailed in the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the Tribunal's legitimacy is contested by the government led by Prime Minister [[Donald Tusk]]. Since March 2024, the government has refused to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, arguing the body is illegitimate, while the Sejm ceased appointing new judges. President [[Andrzej Duda]] has appointed a new Vice-President to the Tribunal, further complicating its operational status.

Swieczkowski's leadership is characterized by a direct clash with EU law, as the Tribunal has ruled on the constitutionality of EU treaty provisions, leading to an ongoing infringement procedure before the Court of Justice of the EU. This institutional crisis contributes to perceived very low judicial independence (26% among the general public, 24% among companies), which is a key threat factor in Poland's sovereign credit rating under "institutional_and_governance_strength." Despite these challenges, the government has initiated legislative efforts to address judges appointed by the post-2017 National Council for the Judiciary, a process in which Swieczkowski's Tribunal plays a critical adjudicative role. The ongoing legitimacy crisis and political polarization surrounding the Tribunal remain significant risks to institutional checks and balances in Poland.
