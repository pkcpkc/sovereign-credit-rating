## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Clicq serves as an Executive Director at the International Monetary Fund, representing Romania within the IMF's Executive Board. As part of the IMF's oversight structure, Clicq participates in the Article IV Consultation process, which critically assesses Romania's macroeconomic stability and sovereign credit profile.

During the 2025 consultation cycle, the IMF Executive Board, including Clicq, reviewed Romania's economic trajectory, noting a slowdown in real GDP growth to 0.9 percent in 2024. The Board welcomed the Romanian coalition government's adoption of a comprehensive fiscal consolidation package in 2025, which included VAT rate hikes and tax measures aimed at reducing the fiscal deficit from 8.7 percent of GDP in 2024 to below 6 percent in 2026, with a long-term target of below 3 percent by 2031.

Clicq's role involves engaging with key Romanian policymakers, including Prime Minister [[Ilie Bolojan]], Finance Minister [[Alexandru Nazare]], and National Bank of Romania Governor [[Mugur Isarescu]]. These interactions focus on ensuring the execution of fiscal reforms and maintaining macroeconomic stability amidst risks such as high inflation (peaking at 9.9 percent in September 2025), widening twin deficits, and potential sovereign credit rating downgrades due to fiscal slippage risks. The IMF's stance, reflected in Clicq's engagement, emphasizes the need for prudent monetary policy, continued fiscal consolidation, and accelerated structural reforms to improve governance and unlock EU funds, thereby supporting long-term debt sustainability and growth prospects.
