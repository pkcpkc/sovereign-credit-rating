## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As Romania's Minister of Finance, Alexandru Nazare plays a central role in the country's macroeconomic stability and sovereign credit profile. Following the 2025 Romanian Presidential Elections and the formation of a new coalition government, Nazare was instrumental in adopting a comprehensive fiscal consolidation package in mid-2025. This package, which included VAT rate hikes and excise increases, aimed to reduce the fiscal deficit from 8.7 percent of GDP in 2024 to below 6 percent in 2026, with a long-term target of below 3 percent by 2031.

Nazare's ministry works closely with the National Bank of Romania, led by Governor [[Mugur Isarescu]], to manage inflationary pressures that spiked to 9.9 percent in September 2025 due to the removal of energy price caps and tax adjustments. The IMF has highlighted the adoption of this fiscal reform package as a strength, noting Romania's commitment to EU fiscal rules despite risks related to high fiscal deficit execution and potential rating downgrades. Nazare's tenure is characterized by efforts to balance robust private consumption and wage growth with the need for structural reforms, including improved public investment management and labor force participation, to unlock EU funds and ensure long-term debt sustainability.
