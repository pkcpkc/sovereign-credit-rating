## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As the Minister for the Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, Lombard played a central role in France’s engagement with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) during the 2025 Article IV Consultation. The consultation, concluded in July 2025, highlighted France's economic resilience amidst high global uncertainty, though it projected a slowdown in growth to 0.6% for 2025 due to trade tensions, weak external demand, and domestic policy uncertainty.

A primary concern for the IMF was France's high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, alongside a deficit of 5.8%. Lombard and the French authorities emphasized their commitment to a credible and well-designed package of structural fiscal measures aimed at bringing the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. This effort is anchored in the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), which prioritizes the rationalization of current spending across central, local, and social security levels rather than increasing the already high tax burden.

Lombard also championed the importance of structural reforms to boost productivity and facilitate fiscal consolidation. Key recommendations supported by the Ministry included leveraging France's position in digital and green transitions, particularly through Artificial Intelligence (AI) adoption and innovation. The Minister highlighted ongoing efforts to simplify regulations, support reindustrialization, and enhance access to finance for high-growth firms. Additionally, the Ministry worked closely with the Banque de France, led by Governor Villeroy de Galhau, to ensure the financial sector remained resilient, with strong capital and liquidity buffers confirmed by the 2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP).
