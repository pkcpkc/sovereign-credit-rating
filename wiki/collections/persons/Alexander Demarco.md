## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Alexander Demarco serves as the Acting Governor of the [[Central Bank of Malta]], playing a pivotal role in the nation's macroeconomic oversight and financial stability framework during the 2024 [[IMF]] Article IV Consultation. His leadership at the Central Bank is integral to maintaining the country's strong institutional strength, a key factor in sovereign credit rating assessments.

Under Demarco's tenure, the [[Central Bank of Malta]] has maintained an independent monetary policy within the [[Eurosystem]] while exercising significant macroprudential mandates. During the 2024 consultation, the IMF highlighted the Central Bank's effective oversight, particularly in monitoring the financial sector's substantial exposure to real estate, which accounts for approximately 70% of the bank loan portfolio. Despite these concentrations, the sector remains robust, characterized by well-capitalized banks (Tier 1 ratio ~21%) and strong liquidity buffers (LCR >350%), metrics overseen by the Central Bank.

Demarco’s administration is also central to addressing emerging risks such as cybersecurity threats and data gaps in the commercial real estate sector. The IMF commended the "high data adequacy rating (SDDS Plus)" associated with Malta's statistical infrastructure, reflecting strong collaboration between the [[Central Bank of Malta]] and other national institutions. Furthermore, Demarco’s role extends to supporting broader sovereign stability goals, including the implementation of structural reforms and the supervision of Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) frameworks in conjunction with the [[Malta Financial Services Authority]].
