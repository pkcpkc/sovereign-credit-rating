## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Barnier served as the Prime Minister of France during a period of significant economic scrutiny and structural adjustment, notably culminating in the fall of his government in December 2024. His tenure was marked by intense focus on fiscal consolidation and debt sustainability, with public debt reaching 113.1% of GDP in 2024. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with France under his administration's successor or transitional context, highlighting the urgent need for credible structural fiscal measures to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029.

Key aspects of his policy context included:

- **Fiscal Challenges**: The administration faced high and rising public debt, fiscal slippages, and revenue shortfalls. The IMF recommended a frontloaded structural fiscal effort of 1.1% of GDP in 2026, followed by 0.9% annually, to place debt on a downward path.
- **Government Fall**: Barnier's government fell in December 2024, an event cited as a threat to policy predictability and reform continuity. This abrupt policy reversal contributed to domestic policy uncertainty, which was identified as a key risk factor alongside trade tensions and geoeconomic fragmentation.
- **Structural Reforms**: Despite the political turbulence, there was an emphasis on advancing structural reforms to boost productivity, including leveraging digital and green transitions, supporting innovation (including AI), and easing regulatory burdens. The Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) was a cornerstone of the authorities' response to IMF recommendations.
- **Economic Outlook**: The French economy demonstrated resilience in 2024, driven by the Paris Olympics and robust labor market conditions. However, growth was projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025 due to weak external demand and policy uncertainty.
