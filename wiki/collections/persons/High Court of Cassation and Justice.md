## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The High Court of Cassation and Justice serves as the highest judicial body in Romania, playing a pivotal role in determining the country's institutional integrity and rule of law rating. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the Court has made significant strides in completing legislative processes initiated by the Venice Commission, particularly concerning the independence of high-ranking prosecutors and judicial police.

However, the Court's jurisprudence has introduced substantial legal uncertainty within the sovereign credit framework. Specific rulings regarding the statute of limitations have resulted in the annulment of numerous high-level corruption convictions and the discontinuation of active cases. This judicial activity has raised systemic risks, contributing to an "average" perception of judicial independence among businesses and the public.

Key policy impacts include:
- **Legal Uncertainty**: The closure of corruption cases due to statute of limitations rulings has created regulatory unpredictability, impacting investor confidence in contract enforcement and anti-corruption efficacy.
- **Judicial Delays**: The length of civil and commercial proceedings has increased, acting as a barrier to justice and negatively affecting economic efficiency.
- **Reform Implementation**: Despite these challenges, the Court remains central to ongoing judicial reforms aimed at strengthening transparency, including the publication of disciplinary case-law and openness to IT system modernization.
