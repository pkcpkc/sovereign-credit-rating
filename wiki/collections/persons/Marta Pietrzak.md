## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Marta Pietrzak serves in the capacity of Advisor to the Executive Director for Poland during the 2024 Article IV Consultation with the Republic of Poland. This consultation, conducted by the IMF European Department in late 2024 and early 2025, focused on assessing Poland's macroeconomic stability, fiscal policy, and structural reforms.

During this period, Poland's economy demonstrated a robust recovery, with growth accelerating to an estimated 2.8% in 2024, driven by private consumption and the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds. However, significant fiscal challenges persisted, with the deficit projected to widen to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending.

As an advisor to the Executive Director, Pietrzak’s role intersects with the IMF's recommendations for fiscal consolidation. The IMF advised frontloading fiscal adjustments to rebuild buffers, aiming to reduce the deficit to 2.9% of GDP by 2028 through measures such as VAT reforms, pension adjustments, and better targeting of social benefits. The Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) was welcomed, but stress was placed on the need for credible policy frameworks to ensure sustainability.

Additionally, the consultation highlighted monetary policy stability, with the National Bank of Poland maintaining a 5.75% policy rate to combat inflation, which had declined from peaks but remained elevated due to tight labor markets. The financial sector was noted as resilient, though litigation risks regarding legacy CHF mortgages and weak private credit recovery posed ongoing challenges. Structural reforms focusing on labor market integration, innovation, and climate transition were identified as critical for sustaining long-term growth and convergence.
