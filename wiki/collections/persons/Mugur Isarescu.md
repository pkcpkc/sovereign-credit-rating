## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Mugur Isarescu, as the Governor of the National Bank of Romania (NBR), plays a pivotal role in Romania's macroeconomic stability and sovereign credit profile. During the 2025 Article IV Consultation, Isarescu’s leadership was central to the monetary response against rising inflation, which spiked to 9.9 percent in September 2025 due to the removal of energy price caps and value-added tax (VAT) increases.

Under Isarescu's cautious monetary policy stance, the NBR paused policy rate cuts, maintaining the key interest rate at 6.5 percent. The bank's strategy was to wait for a firm downward trend in wage growth and inflation before resuming rate reductions, aiming to return inflation to the NBR’s target band by the end of 2026. This approach was designed to anchor inflation expectations and manage the risks associated with high headline inflation.

Isarescu's institution also oversees a banking sector that remains well-capitalized and liquid, though it faces vulnerabilities from growing sovereign exposure and substantial unhedged foreign exchange (FX) corporate loans. The IMF mission, led by Joong Shik Kang, commended the NBR's prudent stance while highlighting the need for continued flexibility and strong supervision to mitigate sovereign-bank nexus risks. His cooperation with Prime Minister Ilie Bolojan and Finance Minister Alexandru Nazare was critical in aligning monetary policy with the government's comprehensive fiscal consolidation package, which aims to reduce the fiscal deficit from 8.7 percent of GDP in 2024 to below 3 percent by 2031.
