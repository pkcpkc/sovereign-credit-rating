## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As President of the Republic, Andrzej Duda plays a central role in Poland's political and institutional landscape, particularly regarding rule of law mechanisms and judicial independence. His tenure has been characterized by significant tensions with the [[Constitutional Tribunal]], which he appoints judges to, leading to a legitimacy crisis contested by the government. A major point of friction occurred in March 2024 when the government, under his administration, refused to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, arguing the body was illegitimate. This action led to a direct clash with EU law and an ongoing infringement procedure before the Court of Justice of the EU.

Despite these institutional crises, Duda's administration has seen progress in other areas through the implementation of a government Action Plan. Notably, efforts were made to separate the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the [[Prosecutor General]], aiming to ensure the functional independence of the prosecution service. The European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO) became fully operational in Poland as of January 2025 under this framework. However, perceived judicial independence remains very low (26% among the general public), and significant risks to institutional checks and balances persist, including issues with lobbying regulations and impunity clauses for top executives. The President's role is critical in navigating these domestic legal challenges while managing Poland's compliance with EU rule of law mechanisms and accessing EU funds.
