## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As the Executive Director representing Poland in the [[International Monetary Fund]], Patryk Loszewski plays a central role in the country's engagement with the [[IMF Article IV Consultation]]. During the 2024 consultation, which concluded in early 2025, Loszewski oversaw Poland's position regarding the assessment of macroeconomic stability and sovereign creditworthiness.

The consultation highlighted significant fiscal challenges, with Poland's deficit projected to widen to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending. Loszewski's office is responsible for navigating the implementation of the [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)]], which aims to reduce the deficit to 2.9% of GDP by 2028 through identified consolidation measures, including [[VAT]] reforms and pension adjustments.

Key policy areas under his purview include maintaining [[Fiscal Discipline]] and ensuring [[Debt Sustainability]]. The IMF noted Poland's strong record of timely debt repayment and credible commitment to honoring obligations, citing low sovereign spreads and favorable access to financing. However, risks remain from rising debt-to-GDP ratios (projected at 65% by 2028) and refinancing pressures.

On the monetary front, Loszewski supports the [[National Bank of Poland]]'s tight monetary stance, with interest rate cuts contingent on decelerating wage growth and a sustained decline in inflation towards the 2.5% target. The financial sector remains well-capitalized, though legacy litigation risks surrounding [[CHF Mortgages]] and a weak private credit recovery are monitored closely. Structural reforms focus on boosting productivity, facilitating resource reallocation, and integrating refugees and older adults into the labor market to sustain economic convergence.
