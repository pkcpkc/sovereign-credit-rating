## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Kotaro Ishi served as the Mission Chief for the IMF's 2024 Article IV Consultation with [[Malta]]. Under his leadership, the IMF staff conducted a comprehensive assessment of Malta’s macroeconomic stability, fiscal policy, and financial sector resilience. The mission concluded its discussions with Maltese officials in November 2024, culminating in a staff report completed in December 2024 and an Executive Board conclusion in January 2025.

Ishi’s mission focused on advising the Maltese government on shifting its growth strategy from labor-intensive sectors (tourism and online gaming) toward productivity-driven, sustainable development. Key policy recommendations overseen by the mission included:

1.  **Fiscal Consolidation**: Urging the phase-out of broad fixed-energy price policies, which accounted for 20% of the fiscal deficit, and reallocating resources toward investment, innovation, and green transition initiatives to support long-term productivity.
2.  **Financial Stability**: Addressing significant concentration risks in the banking sector, particularly the 70% exposure to real estate. The mission recommended tightening macroprudential policies, including raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB) and expanding its scope beyond residential mortgages to include construction and commercial real estate.
3.  **Structural Reforms**: Advocating for a clear roadmap for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reform aligned with the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive to protect revenue and guide investors, as Malta had deferred implementation until 2029.
4.  **Governance & Climate**: Emphasizing the need for continued strengthening of the Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) framework and judicial reforms, alongside robust action to meet climate goals, particularly reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

The mission highlighted Malta’s strong external position, characterized by a large positive net international investment position (~95% of GDP) and a current account surplus, which mitigated external vulnerabilities. However, Ishi’s team noted downside risks from regional geopolitical conflicts and domestic capacity constraints.
