## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As Prime Minister, Donald Tusk has overseen Poland's implementation of a comprehensive Action Plan on the Rule of Law, aimed at addressing long-standing concerns highlighted by the European Commission. A cornerstone of his administration's judicial reform agenda is the legislative effort to separate the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the Prosecutor General, a move designed to ensure the functional independence of the prosecution service. This initiative includes discontinuing criminal investigations against Supreme Court judges for their judicial decisions and ending the practice of using prosecutor secondments as disciplinary sanctions.

Tusk's government has also facilitated the full operationalization of the European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO) in Poland as of January 2025 and established a new Central Office for Combating Corruption within the police. Despite these advancements, the administration faces significant challenges regarding judicial independence, with perceived independence remaining very low among the public and businesses. The government's refusal since March 2024 to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments has led to a direct clash with EU law and ongoing infringement procedures, creating political and geopolitical risks that impact Poland's institutional stability.

In the realm of anti-corruption and media freedom, Tusk's government has made strides in reducing politically biased content in public service media and improving the distribution of state advertising. However, systemic risks persist in public procurement, and lobbying regulations remain underdeveloped. The administration's focus on digitalization, business deregulation, and utilizing EU funding through the Recovery and Resilience Plan aims to bolster economic growth prospects while navigating the complexities of EU rule of law mechanisms.
