## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Marian Jančík, serving as the Prosecutor General of Slovakia, holds a central role in the country's legal and anti-corruption framework, a sector that has faced significant scrutiny in the 2025 Rule of Law Report. His office possesses broad powers, including the ability to annul decisions and redirect cases, which has raised concerns among stakeholders regarding investigative independence and political interference.

The operational environment for Jančík's office has been complicated by the dissolution of specialized anti-corruption bodies, specifically the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO). This restructuring has fragmented competencies, leading to operational delays and a loss of specialized expertise, resulting in a significant drop in registered corruption cases. Furthermore, legislative reforms to the Criminal Code in 2024, which lowered sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption offenses, have negatively impacted high-level prosecutions. Although subsequent amendments aligned with the EU's PIF Directive, the initial legislative changes have contributed to reduced prosecution rates and perceived erosion of judicial independence. Jančík's tenure is thus characterized by these systemic challenges, including political pressure and the need to navigate a fragmented institutional landscape.
