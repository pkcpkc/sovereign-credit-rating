## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Fabian Bornhorst serves as the Deputy Director of the European Department at the International Monetary Fund (IMF). In this capacity, he provides oversight for Article IV consultations involving European economies, including the 2024 Article IV Consultation with the Republic of Poland.

During the Poland consultation cycle (concluding in early 2025), Bornhorst’s department assessed key macroeconomic and sovereign risk factors, including:
- **Fiscal Sustainability**: Analysis of Poland’s widening fiscal deficit (5.9% of GDP in 2024) and the entry into the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP). The IMF recommended frontloading fiscal consolidation to support disinflation and stabilize public debt, which is projected to rise to ~65% of GDP by 2029.
- **Monetary Policy**: Oversight of the National Bank of Poland’s (NBP) independence and inflation-targeting framework, noting that while headline inflation declined to ~4%, core inflation remained sticky due to wage pressures. The IMF advised maintaining tight monetary policy until wage growth decelerated.
- **Structural Reforms**: Identification of long-term growth headwinds, such as population aging and climate transition costs, alongside the positive impact of Ukrainian refugee integration on the labor market.
- **Financial Stability**: Assessment of the resilience of the Polish banking sector, noting strong capital buffers but highlighting risks from CHF mortgage litigation and distortions in private credit allocation.

As Deputy Director, Bornhorst works under Director [[Uma Ramakrishnan]] and collaborates with Mission Heads such as [[Jan Kees Martijn]] to ensure rigorous economic surveillance and policy advice for the region.
