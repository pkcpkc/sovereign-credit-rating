## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Anke Weber served as the Head of Mission for the [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV Consultation with [[Hungary]]. In this capacity, she led the assessment of Hungary’s economic stability, focusing on critical vulnerabilities such as stagnant output, persistently high inflation, and elevated public debt levels. The mission, concluded by the IMF Executive Board in August 2025, highlighted significant downside risks stemming from geoeconomic fragmentation and the suspension of €19 billion in EU funds due to rule of law concerns.

Weber’s oversight of the consultation emphasized the need for additional fiscal adjustment to ensure debt sustainability, with the IMF recommending a structural primary surplus of around 1.75% of GDP. The mission criticized regulatory distortions, including price and margin caps, which undermined monetary policy transmission, and advocated for maintaining a tight monetary stance by the [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB)]] to anchor inflation expectations. Key recommendations under her mission's guidance included phasing out distortive controls, boosting productivity through structural reforms, and addressing financial sector vulnerabilities linked to the sovereign-bank nexus and corporate FX exposure.
