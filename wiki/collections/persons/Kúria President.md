## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Kúria President plays a central role in the ongoing erosion of judicial independence in Hungary, as identified in the 2025 Rule of Law Report. The office has been characterized by public criticism of service courts and involvement in smear campaigns against judges, contributing to an environment of undue pressure on the judiciary.

Key policy impacts include:

- **Judicial Independence Erosion**: The Kúria President's public stance has exacerbated tensions within the judiciary. The Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law, which raised the minimum age for judges to 35 and extended retirement to 70, was implemented under this tenure. Judicial associations argue these changes compromise independence, yet the Kúria President has supported or failed to counteract these structural shifts.
- **Legal Certainty & Emergency Powers**: The implementation of Act XLIX of 2025 strengthened the Kúria's role alongside court compensation schemes, yet this occurred amidst a "state of danger" that allowed the government to override parliamentary acts. The Kúria's inability to effectively check executive overreach during this period has contributed to legal uncertainty, a key risk factor for sovereign credit ratings and foreign investment.
- **Political Influence**: The report highlights that the hierarchical structure of the judiciary, overseen by the Kúria President, allows for potential political pressure on individual cases. The lack of functional independence in case allocation processes remains a critical barrier to rule of law compliance with EU standards.
