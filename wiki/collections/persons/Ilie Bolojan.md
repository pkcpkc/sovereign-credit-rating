## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As Prime Minister of Romania, Ilie Bolojan played a central role in steering the country through a critical juncture for macroeconomic stability during 2025. Following the formation of a new coalition government in June 2025, his administration adopted a comprehensive fiscal consolidation package aimed at restoring fiscal and macroeconomic sustainability. This package included significant VAT rate hikes and other tax measures designed to reduce the fiscal deficit, which had risen to 8.7 percent of GDP in 2024 largely due to costly pension reforms and public wage increases. The government’s target was to lower the deficit to below 6 percent of GDP in 2026 and eventually below 3 percent by 2031.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) Executive Board welcomed these reforms as a necessary step. However, the Bolojan administration faced persistent risks regarding sovereign credit ratings due to doubts about the full execution of the consolidation plan and high current fiscal deficits. Inflation spiked to 9.9 percent in September 2025, driven by the removal of energy price caps and VAT increases, requiring close coordination with Central Bank Governor [[Mugur Isarescu]] to maintain a cautious monetary policy stance. The IMF mission, led by [[Joong Shik Kang]] and including Executive Director [[Helge Berger]], engaged in discussions throughout the year, concluding its Article IV Consultation in November 2025. The administration’s efforts were focused on accelerating structural reforms to unlock EU funds, improve governance, and support long-term debt sustainability amidst regional geopolitical uncertainties.
