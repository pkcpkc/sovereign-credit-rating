## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Gábor Horváth serves as the Chairman of Hungary's Fiscal Council, an institution highlighted by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in its 2025 Article IV Consultation as a key strength in Hungary's institutional and governance framework. The IMF noted the existence of an "established fiscal council" as a positive factor mitigating risks related to rule of law concerns and governance reforms.

In the context of Hungary's sovereign credit rating, the Fiscal Council under Horváth’s leadership plays a critical role in monitoring fiscal sustainability and contingent liabilities. The IMF report emphasizes the importance of transparency and monitoring regarding State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs), noting that the Fiscal Council contributes to this oversight. This function is vital given the high contingent liabilities from SOE guarantees, which amounted to 14 percent of GDP, and the government's efforts to manage off-balance sheet financing vehicles.

Horváth’s role intersects with broader macroeconomic stability efforts, including the implementation of the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The IMF recommends that authorities continue to strengthen fiscal governance, a domain where the Fiscal Council provides independent oversight. While the IMF raised concerns about delays in fiscal adjustment and distortive taxes, the presence of a robust fiscal council like the one led by Horváth is viewed as a stabilizing factor in Hungary's attempt to rebuild fiscal buffers and ensure debt sustainability amidst high interest expenses and refinancing risks.
