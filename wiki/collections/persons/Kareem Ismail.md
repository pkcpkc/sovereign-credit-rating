## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Kareem Ismail served as staff member within the [[International Monetary Fund]] European Department during the 2024 Article IV Consultation with the Republic of Poland. He was part of the IMF mission team led by Jan Kees Martijn, which concluded discussions in Warsaw in October 2024.

The consultation assessed Poland's robust economic recovery, with growth estimated at 2.8% in 2024 and projected to reach 3.5% in 2025-2026. Ismail and the mission team highlighted significant downside risks from Euro Area demand slowdowns and geopolitical tensions, alongside upside potential from immigration and Next Generation EU (NGEU) fund absorption.

A key focus of the mission was fiscal policy, with the deficit projected to widen to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending. The IMF recommended frontloading fiscal consolidation to rebuild buffers, aiming for a deficit of 2.9% of GDP by 2028 through measures including VAT reforms, pension adjustments, and better targeting of social benefits. The Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) was welcomed, but the mission stressed the need for credible implementation.

Regarding monetary policy, the National Bank of Poland maintained a tight stance with a 5.75% policy rate. The mission advised that interest rate cuts should only commence when wage growth decelerates and inflation is firmly declining towards the 2.5% target. The financial system was assessed as resilient with strong capital and liquidity buffers, though legacy litigation risks regarding CHF mortgages were noted as a challenge. Structural reforms to boost productivity, integrate labor market segments (including refugees and women), and facilitate the climate transition were emphasized as critical for sustaining long-term convergence.
