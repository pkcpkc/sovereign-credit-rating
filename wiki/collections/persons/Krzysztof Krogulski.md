## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Krzysztof Krogulski serves as the Resident Representative for the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in Poland. In this capacity, he was a key member of the IMF staff mission that concluded its 2024 Article IV Consultation with the Republic of Poland in October 2024. The consultation, led by Head of Mission Jan Kees Martijn, assessed Poland's robust economic recovery, which accelerated to an estimated 2.8% growth in 2024, driven by domestic demand and the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds.

During the discussions, Krogulski and his team highlighted significant downside risks to growth, particularly from a slowdown in Euro Area demand and geopolitical tensions stemming from Russia's invasion of Ukraine. A primary focus of his engagement was fiscal policy, where the IMF noted the fiscal deficit had widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending. The IMF recommended frontloading fiscal consolidation to rebuild buffers, aiming for a deficit of 2.9% of GDP by 2028 through measures including VAT reforms and pension adjustments.

Krogulski also engaged with Polish authorities, including Executive Director Patryk Loszewski and Advisor Marta Pietrzak, regarding monetary policy and financial sector resilience. The IMF advised maintaining a tight monetary stance with the National Bank of Poland (NBP) keeping interest rates at 5.75% until inflation firmly declines toward the 2.5% target. The mission emphasized the need for structural reforms to sustain convergence, including labor market integration and climate transition efforts. Krogulski's role involves ongoing monitoring of Poland's debt sustainability, institutional strength, and external vulnerability as part of the broader IMF European Department strategy.
