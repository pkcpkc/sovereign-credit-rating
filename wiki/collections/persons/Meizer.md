## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Meizer serves as the Executive Director for Hungary at the International Monetary Fund (IMF). In this capacity, Meizer participated in the 2025 Article IV Consultation, a process concluding discussions in Budapest in June 2025 and finalizing the staff report by July 2025. The consultation characterized Hungary's economy as being at a "challenging juncture," noting stagnant output over the preceding three years and inflation persistently above the Magyar Nemzeti Bank’s (MNB) target.

As an Executive Director, Meizer is involved in the oversight of IMF staff assessments regarding Hungary's macroeconomic stability, fiscal policy, and sovereign credit rating factors. The 2025 consultation highlighted significant downside risks, including deepening geoeconomic fragmentation, suspension of EU funds due to rule of law concerns, and political economy challenges ahead of the 2026 national elections. Meizer's role involves engaging with Hungarian authorities, including Minister Márton Nagy and Central Bank Governors such as Barnabás Virág, Csaba Kandrács, and Zoltán Kurali, to discuss fiscal adjustment needs, debt sustainability (projected to reach 79% of GDP by 2030), and structural reforms. The IMF's recommendations emphasized the need for high-quality fiscal adjustment, broadening the tax base, and strengthening governance to unlock suspended EU funds, all of which fall under the purview of Meizer's representation at the Fund.
