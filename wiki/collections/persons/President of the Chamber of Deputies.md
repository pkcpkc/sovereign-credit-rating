## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The President of the [[Chamber of Deputies]] plays a critical institutional role in the legislative process affecting Italy’s sovereign credit rating and rule of law framework, particularly regarding governmental stability and separation of powers. In January 2025, the Chamber approved the draft constitutional reform aimed at separating the careers of judges and prosecutors, a significant development intended to enhance governmental stability. However, this reform faces scrutiny from judicial bodies, including the [[High Council for the Judiciary]], raising concerns about its impact on judicial independence—a key factor in the [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Rule of Law Report.

The office is situated within a broader political context where the government’s frequent use of urgency decrees and the passage of the new Security Law in June 2025 have strained institutional checks and balances. While the Chamber advances reforms aimed at stability, persistent legislative gaps in lobbying regulation and political party financing transparency remain unresolved, impacting the policy credibility component of Italy's credit assessment. The President’s leadership within the Chamber is thus pivotal in navigating these constitutional and legislative challenges that directly influence investor confidence and institutional strength metrics.
