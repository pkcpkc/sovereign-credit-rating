## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Alexander De Croo serves as the Prime Minister of Belgium, leading the Federal Government. His tenure is marked by significant political and fiscal transitions, particularly following the protracted coalition negotiations after the June 2024 elections. A new federal government was formed in February 2025, initiating a comprehensive policy agenda focused on fiscal consolidation and structural reforms.

Under De Croo's leadership, the government has committed to a seven-year fiscal adjustment program endorsed by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) under the 2025 Article IV Consultation. This agenda aims to durably reduce the general government deficit below 3 percent of GDP and place Belgium’s public debt, which stood at 104.1 percent of GDP in 2024, on a solidly downward path. The primary drivers of this debt are aging-related spending and elevated interest costs, projected to reach 123 percent of GDP by 2030 under unchanged policies.

Key policy priorities include rationalizing current spending, particularly social benefits and the public wage bill, while preserving public investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education. The administration is tasked with implementing structural reforms to boost productivity and labor force participation, including changes to the automatic wage indexation mechanism and pension eligibility. De Croo’s government must navigate complex fiscal federalism, coordinating with regional entities to implement these reforms effectively amidst political fragmentation.
