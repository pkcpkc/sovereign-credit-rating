## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The National Integrity Agency (NIA) is a key regulatory body within the Romanian Government, tasked with ensuring integrity in public office. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the NIA maintains operational effectiveness but faces significant systemic challenges. A primary concern identified by the European Commission is the inadequate resource allocation for the NIA, which hampers its capacity to enforce integrity standards fully.

The agency's operational landscape was significantly impacted by a Constitutional Court decision in June 2025, which ruled on the unconstitutionality of certain aspects of the asset declaration system. This ruling, alongside rulings by the High Court of Cassation and Justice regarding the statute of limitations, has created legal uncertainty. These judicial decisions have led to the annulment of many corruption convictions and the discontinuation of cases, raising systemic risks regarding accountability and transparency.

While the implementation of the 2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy remains on track, the perception of judicial independence and the effectiveness of anti-corruption enforcement remain average. The NIA's struggle with resource constraints and changing legal precedents highlights the fragility of institutional integrity mechanisms in Romania, despite the government's stated commitment to combating corruption.
