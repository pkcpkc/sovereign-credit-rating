## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Eugenio Cerutti served as part of the IMF staff involved in the 2025 Article IV Consultation with France, contributing to the assessment of the country's economic policies and structural reforms. The consultation, concluded in July 2025, highlighted France's economic resilience despite high global uncertainty, noting robust labor market conditions and progress in disinflation driven by the 2024 Paris Olympics. However, significant concerns were raised regarding France's high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, alongside fiscal slippages and a projected slowdown in growth to 0.6% for 2025 due to trade tensions and policy uncertainty.

Cerutti’s work focused on the recommendations for fiscal consolidation, emphasizing the need for a credible package of structural fiscal measures to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. The IMF advised against increasing the already high tax burden, instead recommending rationalization of current spending across central, local, and social security levels. The analysis also underscored the importance of structural reforms to boost productivity, particularly through leveraging France’s strengths in the green and digital transitions, including Artificial Intelligence (AI) adoption.

The financial sector assessment confirmed the resilience of French banks, supported by strong capital and liquidity buffers, though risks from corporate indebtedness and commercial real estate required monitoring. The French authorities, represented by Executive Director Arnaud Buisse and various ministers, broadly agreed with the staff assessments, reaffirming their commitment to the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The consultation process involved extensive dialogue with key French policymakers, including Prime Minister Barnier, Economy Minister Lombard, and Central Bank Governor Villeroy de Galhau, to align on policy priorities and implementation risks.
