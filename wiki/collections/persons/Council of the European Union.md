## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Council of the European Union serves as a primary legislative body within the EU framework, playing a critical role in shaping the legal and institutional environment that influences sovereign credit ratings for member states such as Romania. In the context of the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the Council operates alongside the European Commission and the European Parliament to oversee legislative quality and institutional integrity.

The Council's legislative output directly impacts Romania's sovereign risk profile, particularly regarding "Policy Predictability and Reform Continuity." The report highlights concerns over legislative unpredictability, including the frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs) and inadequate public consultation quality. As a co-legislator, the Council is responsible for ensuring that these legislative processes meet EU standards for transparency and democratic scrutiny.

Furthermore, the Council's role in approving or rejecting legislative acts affects Romania's compliance with EU fiscal frameworks and its access to funds such as the Recovery and Resilience Plan. The institutional stability provided by the Council's adherence to established legislative procedures is a strength factor for Romania, contrasting with threats posed by domestic political pressure and judicial independence perceptions. The Council also interacts with advisory bodies like the Venice Commission to ensure that national justice laws align with European standards, thereby supporting the rule of law which is a key determinant in sovereign credit assessments.
