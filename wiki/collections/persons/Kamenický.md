## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As the Minister of Finance for Slovakia, Kamenický is a central figure in the nation's fiscal consolidation efforts and macroeconomic management. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with the Slovak Republic, endorsing staff appraisals that highlight Slovakia's ambitious fiscal consolidation plan. Under Kamenický's leadership, the government targeted a reduction in the headline deficit to 4.7 percent of GDP in 2025, aiming for close to 2 percent by 2028.

The IMF has noted that while the consolidation targets are commensurate with current fiscal challenges, further deficit-reducing measures are required beyond 2025 to ensure long-term debt sustainability. Key recommendations implemented or considered under his tenure include expenditure rationalization, reducing reduced VAT rates, raising property taxes, and phasing out temporary energy support and financial transaction taxes. Without additional consolidation, public debt is projected to rise to 74 percent of GDP by 2030.

The ministry, under Kamenický's direction, operates within a polarized political environment that complicates policymaking. The IMF has expressed concerns regarding governance and institutional quality, including vulnerability to corruption and perceived weaknesses in judicial independence, which impact the effective disbursement of EU funds. Kamenicky's role is critical in addressing these governance risks to maintain investor confidence and ensure the timely absorption of EU funds.
