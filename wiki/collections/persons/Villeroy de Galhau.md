## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Villeroy de Galhau serves as the Governor of the Banque de France, playing a pivotal role in France's macroeconomic stability and monetary policy implementation. During the 2025 Article IV Consultation with the International Monetary Fund (IMF), he was a key figure in representing the French authorities' stance on fiscal and structural reforms.

The IMF highlighted the resilience of the French economy, driven by robust labor market conditions and the economic impact of the 2024 Paris Olympics. However, growth projections for 2025 were revised downward to 0.6% due to trade tensions, weak external demand, and domestic policy uncertainty. A central concern for the IMF was France's high public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, alongside significant fiscal slippages. The Fund recommended a credible package of structural fiscal measures to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029, emphasizing rationalization of current spending rather than increasing the already high tax burden.

Villeroy de Galhau and the French authorities broadly concurred with staff assessments, reaffirming their commitment to the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The Banque de France, under his leadership, is integral to maintaining financial sector health. The 2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP) confirmed the resilience of the banking sector, characterized by healthy capital ratios and liquidity buffers. Risks from corporate indebtedness and commercial real estate remain under close monitoring. The IMF also noted the progressive disinflation process and well-anchored inflation expectations, strengths attributed to effective monetary policy management. Structural reforms to boost productivity, particularly in digital and green transitions, are seen as crucial for long-term fiscal consolidation and economic diversification.
