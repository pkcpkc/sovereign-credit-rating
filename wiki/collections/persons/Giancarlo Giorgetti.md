## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Giancarlo Giorgetti serves as the Minister of Economy and Finance for Italy, playing a central role in the country's fiscal strategy and engagements with international financial institutions. During the IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation, Giorgetti represented the Italian authorities in discussions regarding the nation's economic outlook, fiscal consolidation path, and structural reforms.

Key aspects of his policy context and involvement in sovereign analysis include:

- **Fiscal Consolidation Strategy**: Giorgetti’s administration committed to a gradual seven-year fiscal adjustment under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The authorities aim to bring the general government deficit below 3% of GDP by 2026 and target a deficit below 2% of GDP by 2029. This stance contrasts with the IMF staff’s recommendation for a more front-loaded adjustment to achieve a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027 to firmly place public debt on a downward trajectory.
- **Debt Sustainability**: Under his oversight, Italy’s public debt stood at 135.3% of GDP at the end of 2024. Despite the high debt burden, the fiscal performance improved markedly in 2024, with the deficit shrinking to 3.4% of GDP and the country returning to a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP. The administration emphasizes structural reforms to boost growth as a complement to fiscal discipline.
- **Structural Reforms and NRRP**: Giorgetti’s ministry is responsible for the implementation of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP). While the implementation of milestones has accelerated (reaching 54% completion), spending execution remains a point of discussion with the IMF. The administration focuses on post-NRRP reforms to address weak productivity growth, increase female labor force participation, and improve the business climate.
- **International Engagement**: As the primary fiscal policymaker, Giorgetti engaged directly with the IMF staff team, led by Lone Christiansen, and other stakeholders including the Bank of Italy and the European Commission, to align Italy’s fiscal path with EU agreements and international best practices.
