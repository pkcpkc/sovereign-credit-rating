## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Peter Kresák serves as the President of the Supreme Court in Slovakia, a position situated within a judicial system facing significant scrutiny regarding independence and political interference. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report, perceived judicial independence in Slovakia remains low, with only 32% of the general public and 34% of companies expressing confidence in the judiciary.

Kresák's tenure and institutional role are contextualized by several critical developments affecting the Slovak legal framework:

1.  **Judicial Independence Erosion**: The independence of the Judicial Council, which plays a pivotal role in judicial appointments and dismissals, is described as compromised due to vague dismissal procedures and political appointments. As President of the Supreme Court, Kresák operates within this environment where political influence over judicial composition is a systemic risk.
2.  **Legislative Impact on Justice**: The 2024 reforms to the Criminal Code, which lowered sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption, have negatively impacted high-level prosecutions. These changes, adopted under the current government led by Prime Minister [[Robert Fico]], have led to the closure or reclassification of several cases, raising concerns about the effectiveness of legal oversight.
3.  **Institutional Dynamics**: The report highlights fragmented competence within the justice sector, particularly following the dissolution of specialized anti-corruption bodies like NAKA and SPO. The broad powers of the Prosecutor General, held by [[Marian Jančík]], to annul decisions and reassign cases persist as a major concern for investigative independence, creating a complex operational landscape for the Supreme Court.
4.  **Political Interference Risks**: Proposed constitutional amendments in April 2025, which could affect the primacy of EU law, have raised concerns about the rule of law. While these amendments were postponed, they underscore the political pressure on judicial institutions, including the Supreme Court, to navigate tensions between national sovereignty and EU legal obligations.

The combination of political polarization, fast-track legislative procedures bypassing stakeholder consultation, and the weakening of anti-corruption frameworks contributes to a high-risk environment for judicial integrity and predictability, directly impacting Slovakia's sovereign credit rating factors related to institutional governance.
