## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The National Judicial Council President serves as the head of Hungary's primary judicial self-governance body, a role central to the European Commission's assessment of judicial independence in Hungary. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the Council continues to exercise powers acquired through 2023 reforms, yet its consultation processes are frequently deemed ineffective. The President's office is implicated in systemic issues affecting legal certainty and investor confidence, key factors in sovereign credit ratings.

Key developments under this leadership include the persistence of undue pressure on judges, highlighted by public criticisms from the Kúria President. The administration of judicial appointments and case allocation has been criticized for lacking transparency, contributing to a "state of danger" framework that overrides parliamentary oversight. Furthermore, the Council's handling of judicial salaries and structural reforms has not sufficiently addressed concerns about political influence, particularly following the Fourteenth Amendment which raised the minimum age for judges to 35 and extended the retirement age to 70. These changes, coupled with the Council's operational independence deficits, have been flagged as significant governance threats by EU institutions.
