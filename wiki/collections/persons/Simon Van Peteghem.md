## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Simon Van Peteghem serves as Deputy Prime Minister of [[Belgium]], playing a key role in the federal government's policy agenda following the formation of the new government in February 2025. His tenure is characterized by a focus on fiscal consolidation and structural reforms aimed at addressing the country's high public debt, which stood at 104.1 percent of GDP in 2024.

Under the supervision of the International Monetary Fund (IMF), Van Peteghem's government has committed to a seven-year fiscal adjustment plan under the EU Economic Governance Framework. The primary objective of this agenda is to durably reduce the general government deficit below 3 percent of GDP and place public debt on a solidly downward path. Key responsibilities include rationalizing current spending, particularly regarding social benefits and the public wage bill, while maintaining public investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education.

Van Peteghem is also involved in the implementation of structural reforms recommended by the IMF to boost labor productivity and force participation. These reforms include changes to the automatic wage indexation mechanism, reforms to pension eligibility, and measures to enhance competitiveness by limiting unemployment benefits and addressing the high labor-tax wedge. Additionally, the government is tasked with coordinating federal and regional efforts to meet climate targets and manage the costs associated with the green transition, ensuring compliance with EU economic governance standards.
