## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Fazurin Jamaludin serves as IMF Staff involved in the 2025 Article IV Consultation with Romania. The consultation highlights a critical juncture for Romania's macroeconomic stability, characterized by slowing growth (0.9% real GDP in 2024) and widening twin deficits. A comprehensive fiscal consolidation package adopted in 2025, including VAT hikes and tax measures, aims to reduce the fiscal deficit to below 6 percent of GDP in 2026 and below 3 percent by 2031.

Key risks monitored during this period include a sovereign credit rating downgrade due to doubts about the full execution of consolidation plans and high current fiscal deficits. Inflation spiked to 9.9 percent in September 2025 due to energy price cap removals and VAT increases, expected to fall within the National Bank of Romania’s target band by end-2026. The banking sector remains resilient but faces vulnerabilities from the sovereign-bank nexus and unhedged foreign exchange loans. Structural reforms, including strengthening state-owned enterprise (SOE) governance and improving public investment management, are deemed crucial for unlocking EU funds and supporting long-term growth.
