Matej Števčík serves as the President of the [[Judicial Council]], a position central to Slovakia's rule-of-law indicators and sovereign credit risk assessment. The Council faces challenges related to the frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures that bypass stakeholder consultation, limiting transparency and increasing political leverage over judicial appointments and dismissals.

Furthermore, the broader context involves reforms to the [[Criminal Code]], including lowered sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption, which have materially impacted high-level prosecutions. While the [[Judicial Council]] is tasked with ensuring judicial administration and digitalization efforts, its ability to maintain institutional checks and balances is strained by the political climate under the current government, including concerns over constitutional amendments threatening the primacy of [[European Union]] law. The Council's role is further complicated by the persistence of the Prosecutor General's broad powers to annul decisions and reassign cases, creating friction between judicial administration and prosecutorial independence.
