## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Christian Karner Marterbauer serves as the Minister of Finance for Austria, leading the [[Austrian Ministry of Finance]] during a critical period of economic transition and structural reform. He was identified as a key counterpart during the [[International Monetary Fund]]'s 2025 Article IV Consultation with Austria, where his administration's fiscal strategy and policy credibility were central to the review.

Under Marterbauer's leadership, the new coalition government (ÖVP-SPÖ-NEOS), which took office in March 2025, committed to significant fiscal consolidation measures. His tenure is marked by the challenge of addressing a widened fiscal deficit of 4.7% of GDP in 2024 and a public debt ratio that rose to 81.2%. The IMF assessment highlighted that while near-term consolidation measures were announced for 2025–2026, substantial further medium-term adjustment would be required to stabilize the debt ratio against rising pressures from demographic aging, defense spending, and the green transition.

Marterbauer's policy direction aligns with IMF recommendations to reduce the deficit to below 2% of GDP through pension reforms, healthcare efficiency improvements, and tax mix adjustments, such as increases in property and carbon taxes. Additionally, his government has prioritized structural reforms to boost labor supply, particularly among women and the elderly, aiming to offset demographic drag and enhance productivity growth. The Ministry, under his guidance, is also navigating financial stability concerns, including risks associated with Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposures in the banking sector and the expiration of borrower-based mortgage lending measures in July 2025.
