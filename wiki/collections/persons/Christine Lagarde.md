## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As Managing Director of the [[International Monetary Fund]], [[Christine Lagarde]] oversaw the conclusion of the 2025 Article IV Consultation with France, marking a significant assessment of the nation's macroeconomic stability and fiscal trajectory. The consultation, finalized in July 2025, highlighted France's economic resilience despite global headwinds such as trade tensions and geoeconomic fragmentation. Under Lagarde's leadership, the IMF acknowledged France's robust labor market and progress in disinflation but emphasized urgent concerns regarding high public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024.

Lagarde's office endorsed the IMF's recommendation for a credible package of structural fiscal measures aimed at reducing the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. This involved encouraging the French authorities, including Prime Minister [[Barnier]] and Economy Minister [[Lombard]], to prioritize rationalization of current spending over increasing the already high tax burden. The IMF also stressed the importance of structural reforms to boost productivity, particularly in digital and green transitions, leveraging France's strengths in AI and innovation.

Furthermore, the IMF's assessment, conducted under Lagarde's tenure, confirmed the resilience of the French banking sector through the 2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP), noting healthy capital and liquidity buffers. However, risks from corporate indebtedness and commercial real estate remained subjects of close monitoring. The consultation underscored the need for continued commitment to the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) and effective coordination between central, local, and social security levels to ensure fiscal sustainability amidst an aging population and rising defense spending pressures.
