## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Jan Kees Martijn served as the Head of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) Mission to Poland during the 2024 Article IV Consultation. Leading the IMF European Department staff, Martijn oversaw a mission that concluded its discussions in Warsaw in October 2024, with the staff report completed in December 2024 and discussed by the Executive Board in January 2025.

Under Martijn’s leadership, the IMF assessed Poland's robust economic recovery, noting growth of 2.8% in 2024 projected to reach 3.5% in 2025-2026. However, the mission highlighted significant fiscal challenges, including a deficit projected at 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending. The IMF, led by Martijn, recommended frontloading fiscal consolidation to rebuild buffers, aiming for a deficit reduction to 2.9% of GDP by 2028 through measures such as VAT reforms and pension adjustments.

The mission also evaluated monetary policy, noting the National Bank of Poland's tight stance with a 5.75% policy rate, advising that interest rate cuts should await decelerating wage growth and inflation declining toward the 2.5% target. Structural reforms were emphasized as critical for sustaining growth, focusing on labor market integration, innovation, and climate transition. The assessment covered sovereign credit rating factors including political stability, institutional strength, debt sustainability, and external liquidity, concluding that Poland's external position remained strong despite geopolitical risks from Russia's invasion of Ukraine.
