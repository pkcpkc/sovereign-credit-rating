## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Jan Kees Martijn served as the Mission Head for the IMF’s 2024 Article IV Consultation with Poland, a process concluded in January 2025. In this capacity, he led the assessment of Poland’s economic recovery, which was characterized by strong domestic demand and real wage growth, yet accompanied by significant fiscal pressures. The mission highlighted a general government deficit of 5.9% of GDP in 2024, triggering the EU’s Excessive Deficit Procedure. Martijn’s report emphasized the need for fiscal consolidation to stabilize debt levels, which were projected to rise to approximately 65% of GDP by 2029 under baseline scenarios.

Key aspects of the policy context under his oversight included:

- **Fiscal Sustainability**: Advocated for frontloading fiscal adjustment in 2025 to rebuild buffers and support disinflation, recommending a medium-term plan to reduce the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028.
- **Monetary Policy Coordination**: Assessed the effectiveness of the [[National Bank of Poland (NBP)]]’s inflation-targeting framework, noting that while inflation expectations remained anchored, core inflation stayed elevated due to tight labor markets.
- **Structural Reforms**: Identified population aging and climate transition costs as long-term risks to growth, recommending reforms to increase labor force participation and accelerate green energy permitting.
- **Financial Stability**: Evaluated the resilience of the Polish banking sector, noting strong capital buffers but highlighting risks from litigation over Swiss Franc (CHF) mortgages and distortions in private credit provision.
