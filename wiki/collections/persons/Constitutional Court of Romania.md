## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

The Constitutional Court of Romania plays a pivotal role in maintaining institutional integrity and the rule of law, directly influencing sovereign risk assessments and judicial efficiency metrics. As the highest judicial authority, it has recently engaged in high-impact political and legal interventions that define the country's stability profile.

A landmark decision occurred in December 2024, when the Court annulled the first round of the 2024 presidential elections due to significant irregularities. This ruling necessitated a repeat of the first round in May 2025, highlighting the Court's active role in safeguarding democratic processes against procedural failures. This intervention underscores the institution's capacity to check executive and legislative powers, a key factor in evaluating political stability.

In the realm of judicial reform, the Court has been central to completing the legislative process initiated by the Venice Commission. It has reviewed laws aimed at strengthening the independence of high-ranking prosecutors and judicial police, contributing to the partial completion of justice sector reforms. However, the Court's rulings on asset declarations have introduced complexities; a June 2025 decision declared certain aspects of asset declaration unconstitutional, creating legal uncertainty and impacting the operational effectiveness of the National Integrity Agency. This tension between transparency mandates and constitutional interpretation remains a focal point for anti-corruption efforts.

Furthermore, the Court's jurisprudence intersects with broader economic and social indicators. By annulling election rounds and addressing conflicts of interest, it influences the "Political and Geopolitical Risk" factors of sovereign credit ratings. The Court's actions contribute to a stable democratic framework, yet its decisions on legislative quality and the frequency of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs) reflect ongoing challenges in legislative predictability. The Court's role in validating or invalidating laws related to media freedom, public procurement, and civil society rights directly impacts the "Institutional and Governance Strength" indices used in rule of law assessments. Its interactions with the European Court of Human Rights ensure that domestic judgments align with international human rights standards, further integrating Romania into the European legal area.
