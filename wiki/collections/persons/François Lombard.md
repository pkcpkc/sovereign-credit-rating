## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

François Lombard serves as the Minister for the Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty for France, playing a central role in the nation's fiscal policymaking and economic surveillance. His tenure has been characterized by the management of significant fiscal challenges, including rising public debt which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024 and persistent fiscal slippages that contributed to Moody's downgrade of France's sovereign credit rating to Aa3 in December 2024.

During the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation, Lombard and his administration were engaged in discussions regarding the implementation of the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The IMF emphasized the urgent need for credible, frontloaded fiscal consolidation under his oversight to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029 and place public debt on a sustainable downward path. Key aspects of this mandate involved rationalizing current spending across all levels of government and addressing the complexities of local government fiscal oversight.

Lombard's policy focus has also included navigating domestic political uncertainty and building consensus for fiscal adjustments amid social discontent. Notably, a political compromise on the 2025 budget was reached in February 2025, marking a step forward in fiscal management. The IMF highlighted the importance of the authorities' commitment to structural reforms aimed at boosting productivity, particularly through digital adoption and AI, as a means to support long-term growth prospects and mitigate the risks associated with geoeconomic fragmentation and trade tensions.
