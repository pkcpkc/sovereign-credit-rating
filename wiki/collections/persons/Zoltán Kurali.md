## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

Zoltán Kurali serves as a Deputy Central Bank Governor at the Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB), playing a key role in Hungary’s monetary policy framework during a period characterized by the IMF as a "challenging juncture." The MNB, under his and the Governor’s leadership, maintains a tight monetary stance with the base rate held at 6.5 percent since September 2024, aiming to anchor inflation expectations and return inflation durably to the 3 percent target.

The IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation highlights that inflation remains persistently above target, driven by core services inflation and external price pressures. Kurali’s role involves navigating these challenges, including the phasing out of retail margin caps, price controls, and voluntary APR ceilings to avoid market distortions. The MNB is advised to maintain this tight policy stance into 2026, balancing the need for price stability against stagnant output growth and high financing costs.

Key responsibilities and policy contexts include managing the sovereign-bank nexus, where banks hold significant government debt, and addressing vulnerabilities in the corporate sector and commercial real estate. The MNB’s independent actions are critical for maintaining financial sector health, ensuring well-capitalized and liquid banks with low non-performing loan ratios despite broader economic headwinds such as high gross external debt and suspended EU funds.
