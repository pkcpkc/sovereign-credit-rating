## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As Deputy Central Bank Governor of the [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]], Csaba Kandrács plays a key role in Hungary’s monetary policy framework and macroeconomic management. During the 2025 IMF Article IV Consultation, Kandrács was part of the Hungarian authorities' engagement with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) mission led by [[Anke Weber]], alongside Department Heads [[Kristina Kostial]] and [[Jacques Miniane]].

The consultation highlighted that Hungary’s economy was at a "challenging juncture," with output stagnating over the past three years and inflation remaining persistently above the MNB’s 3 percent target. The IMF advised maintaining a tight monetary stance, with the base rate held at 6.5 percent into 2026 to anchor inflation expectations. Kandrács, alongside his colleagues [[Barnabás Virág]] and [[Zoltán Kurali]], represents the central bank’s commitment to this policy approach, which includes phasing out distortive price and margin controls to avoid market distortions.

The authorities, including Kandrács, acknowledged the challenges but emphasized resilience through strong employment and pro-family measures. They maintain confidence in their Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) and their ability to achieve fiscal targets through targeted measures, despite downside risks from trade escalations, regional conflicts, and suspended EU funds. The central bank’s actions are critical in managing external vulnerabilities, including high gross external debt and FX exposure, while maintaining adequate reserves.
