## Sovereign Credit Rating & Policy Context

As Minister of Interior, Robert Kaliňák serves in the government led by Prime Minister [[Robert Fico]], which has been assessed for significant risks regarding the Rule of Law, judicial independence, and anti-corruption frameworks by the European Commission in its 2025 Rule of Law Report. The report highlights systemic deterioration in key institutional checks, including the politicization of public service media and the erosion of judicial independence, factors directly impacting Slovakia's sovereign risk profile and investment climate.

Key policy areas under Kaliňák's purview or influenced by his government's legislative agenda include:

1.  **Judicial Independence and Legal Frameworks**: The government has enacted reforms to the Criminal Code, including lowering sanctions and shortening statutes of limitations for corruption, which has led to reduced prosecution rates and the closure of high-level cases. The report notes that these changes, alongside the broad powers of the Prosecutor General to annul decisions, undermine investigative independence.
2.  **Anti-Corruption Institutions**: The dissolution of specialized anti-corruption bodies, specifically the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO), has fragmented competence, leading to operational delays and a significant drop in registered corruption cases. This restructuring is cited as a major threat to institutional governance strength.
3.  **Public Procurement and Transparency**: Weakened public procurement rules under the current administration have increased corruption risks in strategic investments, affecting business confidence and the economic climate.
4.  **Legislative Procedures**: The frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures (58 out of 131 laws in 2024 adopted with limited public participation) bypasses standard stakeholder consultation, raising concerns about transparency and democratic backsliding.

The government's engagement with the European Commission includes ongoing dialogue regarding these issues, including the adoption of amendments to align with the EU's Anti-Money Laundering Package (PIF Directive) and the implementation of a new Anti-Corruption Strategy for 2024-2029. However, perceived high risks in corruption and fraud, particularly in the agriculture sector and public procurement, continue to pose threats to Slovakia's economic diversification and external liquidity position.
