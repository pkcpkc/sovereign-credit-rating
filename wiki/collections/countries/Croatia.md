## Economic Overview

Croatia is situated within a European economic landscape characterized by a focus on fiscal consolidation and the transition toward green and digital economies. As part of the European Commission's 2025 regional analysis, Croatia is positioned amidst a broader push for administrative simplification, investment in R&D, and the management of public debt-to-GDP ratios to ensure long-term fiscal sustainability. 

The country operates within a regional context—including peers like Austria, Germany, and Italy—navigating high capital costs, potential disparities in GDP per head, and the necessity of balancing growth with structural reforms. In alignment with broader European trends discussed in recent IMF Article IV consultations, the region faces challenges such as sluggish productivity growth and the need to manage inflation gaps driven by sticky services prices. For Croatia, maintaining stability involves balancing regional growth dynamics with the specific requirements of the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRP).

## Key Risks & Outlook

Key risks for Croatia, consistent with regional trends in the 2025 report and IMF observations, include:
- **Fiscal Sustainability:** The need for aggressive fiscal consolidation to manage public debt and maintain long-term stability, specifically aiming for sustainable deficit targets.
- **Labor Market:** Addressing skills gaps and ensuring high labor productivity to support the twin green and digital transitions, while potentially narrowing participation gaps in the workforce.
- **Energy Transition:** Navigating the administrative complexities of decarbonizing transport and building sectors while seeking a shortage of skilled renovation workers.
- **Economic Growth:** Managing growth amidst high capital costs and potential regional disparities in GDP per head, while navigating global trade uncertainties.
- **Structural Alignment:** Ensuring alignment with broader EU trends regarding R&D intensity, administrative efficiency, and the reduction of "red tape" to remain competitive within the single market.
- **Financial Stability:** Monitoring regional risks such as Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) in specific sectors like commercial real estate.
