## Economic Overview

Malta has demonstrated robust economic growth over the last decade, primarily driven by export-oriented service industries such as tourism and online gaming. While growth is projected to moderate—slowing from 7.5% in 2023 to 5% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2025—it remains among the highest in Europe. The economy is currently transitioning toward a productivity-driven growth model to address capacity constraints caused by high population density, infrastructure strain, and labor shortages. 

The IMF monitors Malta within the broader European economic landscape, focusing on fiscal sustainability and public debt management. Key focus areas include:
- **Fiscal Policy:** A recommended shift away from fixed energy price subsidies toward funding public services (health, education) and innovation.
- **Taxation:** Development of a Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reform roadmap to align with the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive.
- **Labor Market:** Addressing a critical skills gap by focusing on STEM graduates and upskilling to meet "Malta Vision 2050" objectives.
- **Trade & Investment:** Managing the balance between social spending and structural reforms to boost Total Factor Productivity (TFP).

## Governance & Rule of Law

Malta's governance framework is undergoing continuous assessment regarding judicial efficiency and investor confidence. Key areas of focus include:
- **Judicial Reform:** Ongoing requirements to improve the efficiency of the justice system and strengthen the appointment process of the chief justice.
- **AML/CFT Framework:** Monitoring of anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism measures.
- **Institutional Quality:** Efforts to bolster judicial independence and anti-corruption measures to maintain a stable environment for international investment.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Malta's economic outlook is linked to broader regional trends, including the transition to green energy and managing sovereign risk. Specific risks include:
- **Financial Stability:** High exposure of the banking sector to the real estate market (comprising 70% of loan portfolios). The IMF suggests raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB) and expanding its scope to commercial real estate.
- **Climate Change:** The need to intensify mitigation measures to meet a 19% GHG reduction target by 2030, specifically in road transport and renewable energy expansion.
- **Structural Headwinds:** Managing labor-cost competitiveness, productivity growth, and the transition to a green economy while maintaining long-term fiscal stability.
