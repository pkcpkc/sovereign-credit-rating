## Economic Overview

Spain is a key member of the European Union's economic landscape, operating within a regional context characterized by high capital costs and restrictive monetary policies. While specific 2025 growth figures are not detailed, the country is navigating a period of aggressive fiscal consolidation and structural adjustments required by the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) reforms. Spain faces high inflation pressures and must balance net expenditure growth with significant debt-servicing costs. The economy is currently shaped by the need to match nominal wage growth with productivity gains to maintain international competitiveness while managing a rising debt-to-GDP ratio, a trend observed across the EU.

## Key Risks & Outlook

*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** A primary challenge is managing public debt and deficits. The report highlights a general trend of rising debt-to-GDP ratios across the EU, necessitating permanent spending reviews and fiscal discipline.
*   **Energy & Transition:** Spain is part of the collective effort toward a 50% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030. This includes the development of public and private recharging infrastructure and the transition to a circular economy.
*   **Labor & Skills:** A critical risk factor is the "skills gap" and the shortage of skilled staff required for green and digital transitions. Spain must address these mismatches to bolster productivity in manufacturing and high-tech sectors.
*   **Regional Disparities:** Spain must navigate territorial gaps and social exclusion risks, particularly in regions that may fall below EU GDP per head averages. Addressing "brain drain" and infrastructure disparities remains a priority.
*   **Structural Competitiveness:** Like other EU nations, Spain faces the challenge of balancing wage growth with productivity improvements to remain competitive in the global market.
