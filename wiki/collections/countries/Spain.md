## Economic Overview

Spain is part of a broader European economic landscape currently under review by the [[IMF]]. As a key peer country within the European economic governance framework, Spain's economic trajectory is linked to regional themes of sustained fiscal consolidation, specifically targeting deficits to remain below 3% of GDP and managing high public debt levels. The regional context emphasizes addressing core inflation, improving labor-market efficiency, and boosting Total Factor Productivity (TFP) by reducing regulatory barriers. Like other major European economies in the peer group (including [[Italy]], [[Germany]], and [[France]]), Spain is subject to assessments regarding its ability to maintain a stable debt-to-GDP ratio while navigating demographic headwinds and the need for structural reforms to enhance private dynamism and human capital.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The economic landscape is influenced by the new [[EU]] economic governance framework (EGF), which necessitates structural reforms to rationalize social spending and improve the insolvency regime. Governance priorities include aligning national strategies with the [[Green Transition]] and maintaining financial stability through macroprudential policies. The framework encourages a predictable regulatory environment to help firms scale and promote a transition toward renewable energy to enhance energy security.

## Key Risks & Outlook

The primary risks identified for the region and specifically relevant to Spain's peer group include:
*   **Fiscal Vulnerability:** The necessity of multi-year adjustment periods to manage high debt-to-GDP ratios and achieve primary surplus targets.
*   **Labor Market Competitiveness:** Challenges related to wage indexation, the need to widen the income gap between work and non-work, and the expansion of childcare to boost labor force participation.
*   **Productivity Stagnation:** Lagging TFP requiring product market reforms, lower exit costs for businesses, and increased R&D investment to overcome high-skilled worker shortages.
*   **Sovereign Risk:** While systemic risks may be moderate, high debt levels require high-quality debt management and long average residual maturities.
*   **Demographic Drag:** Potential constraints on long-term potential growth due to aging populations and increasing old-age dependency ratios.
