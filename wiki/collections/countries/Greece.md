## Economic Overview

Greece remains an integral part of the EU's fiscal and economic framework. The country is navigating a period characterized by high public debt-to-GDP ratios and the necessity for aggressive fiscal consolidation. While specific 2025 figures are part of a collective European report, Greece shares the broader Eurozone's challenges regarding high public debt-servicing costs and the need to balance net expenditure growth against cumulative fiscal limits. 

The economic landscape for Greece is shaped by the push for administrative simplification (the "tell us once" principle) and the requirement to navigate complex licensing and retail regulations to stimulate investment. Like its regional peers, Greece faces high capital costs and must implement structural reforms to address "skills gaps" that hinder green and digital transitions. The broader regional context emphasizes the importance of growth-enhancing reforms in labor markets, education, and product market dynamics to maintain international competitiveness and address structural deficits caused by aging populations.

## Governance & Rule of Law

Greece operates within the European Commission's framework for structural and budgetary policies. A primary focus remains on enhancing institutional quality through "simplification of economic life" laws designed to reduce bureaucracy and improve the ease of doing business. The governance framework aims to streamline regulatory environments to foster a more dynamic investment climate.

## Key Risks & Outlook

The primary risks for Greece include:
*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Managing public debt in a high-interest-rate environment and addressing elevated debt-servicing costs.
*   **Labor Market Dynamics:** Addressing unemployment and productivity gaps similar to the broader EU challenges, specifically overcoming "skills gaps" in STEM and green transition sectors.
*   **Energy Transition:** The need to decarbonize transport and building sectors while overcoming administrative complexity and ensuring grid flexibility.
*   **Digitalization:** Scaling high-growth firms and ensuring sufficient equity financing for innovation to bridge the investment-to-GDP ratio gaps seen across the EU.
*   **Structural Reform:** Navigating regulatory volatility and high capital costs while pursuing administrative simplification to improve investment climates.
*   **Productivity & Competitiveness:** Addressing the need for structural reforms to improve Total Factor Productivity (TFP) and ensuring that fiscal consolidation does not stifle growth-enhancing initiatives.
