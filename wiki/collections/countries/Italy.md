## Economic Overview

Italy's economic profile is characterized by notable resilience, with real GDP growth of 0.7% in 2024 and a robust labor market reaching a record-high employment rate of 62.7% in April 2025. The country has successfully returned to a primary surplus (0.4% in 2024), driven by improved tax compliance and labor market strength. However, Italy operates within a challenging EU fiscal landscape, facing high public debt levels and the necessity of bringing deficits below 3% of GDP. While headline inflation is moderating toward the ECB's 2% target, the economy faces significant structural headwinds, including weak productivity growth, a shortage of high-skilled workers, and a current account surplus (1.1% in 2024) that remains weaker than long-term fundamentals suggest. The IMF emphasizes the need for a primary surplus target of 3% by 2027 to place debt on a downward trajectory while balancing public investment in infrastructure and healthcare.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The economic outlook is tied to the new EU economic governance framework (EGF), requiring coordinated fiscal adjustments and adherence to regional stability targets. Governance efforts are currently focused on balancing public investment with fiscal sustainability, specifically through the implementation of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP), where Italy has achieved 54% of milestones by end-2024. Key reform priorities include justice reform to reduce court backlogs, modernization of public administration, and enhancing tax compliance. The Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) is intended to sustain this reform momentum beyond the NRRP horizon.

## Key Risks & Outlook

*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Italy faces challenges in managing high public debt. A primary surplus of 3% by 2027 is recommended to ensure the debt-to-GDP ratio declines.
*   **Labor & Productivity:** Structural headwinds include a "productivity malaise," a shortage of high-skilled workers, and low R&D investment. There is a critical need to boost female labor force participation by expanding childcare and removing policy-induced disincentives.
*   **Demographic Drag:** A low fertility rate is projected to sharply increase the old-age dependency ratio, potentially constraining long-term potential growth.
*   **Green Transition:** A coherent national strategy is required to accelerate the transition to renewables and enhance energy security to mitigate exposure to volatile international energy prices.
*   **Sovereign Risk:** Overall risk is assessed as "Moderate." While debt remains high, risks are mitigated by long average maturities and healthy retail appetite for government bonds. The banking sector remains sound with high profitability and the activation of a 1% Systemic Risk Buffer (SyRB).
*   **Regional Disparities:** Significant gaps persist between Northern and Southern Italy regarding poverty, labor inactivity, and export diversification.
*   **Geoeconomic Factors:** Potential risks include geoeconomic fragmentation and trade policy shocks, which may impact regional economic stability.
