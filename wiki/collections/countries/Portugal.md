## Economic Overview

Portugal is part of a broader group of European economies currently under review by the International Monetary Fund (IMF). While specific individual data for Portugal in this summary is secondary to the primary focus on Belgium, the context suggests a regional focus on fiscal consolidation, managing public debt, and addressing structural headwinds. The broader economic narrative for the region includes a push to bring deficits below 3% of GDP, managing high debt-to-GDP ratios, and tackling persistent core inflation. Key economic levers being monitored include labor-cost competitiveness, Total Factor Productivity (TFP), and the transition toward green energy.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The economic environment is influenced by the new EU economic governance framework (EGF), which emphasizes sustained fiscal discipline and the rationalization of social spending. Governance focuses on balancing public investment in infrastructure and healthcare with the need for structural reforms to improve labor market efficiency.

## Key Risks & Outlook

- **Fiscal Sustainability:** The primary risk involves managing high public debt and the need for structural primary balance improvements.
- **Labor Market:** Challenges include widening the income gap between work and non-work and improving labor participation.
- **Productivity:** Lagging Total Factor Productivity (TFP) remains a concern, necessitating a reduction in regulatory barriers to entry.
- **Green Transition:** Success depends on coherent national strategies, carbon taxation, and the phasing out of fossil fuel subsidies.
- **Sovereign Risk:** While regional risks are assessed as moderate, outcomes depend on high-quality debt management profiles and long average residual maturities.
