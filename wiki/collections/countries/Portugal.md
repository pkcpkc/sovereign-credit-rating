## Economic Overview

Based on the 2025 European Commission Country Report context, Portugal is situated within a broader EU economic landscape characterized by a period of slowing growth and fiscal pressure. While specific national figures for Portugal are part of the collective EU summary, the regional context indicates a focus on fiscal consolidation and managing public debt-to-GDP ratios. The overarching economic narrative for the region emphasizes the need to address "skills gaps," improve business R&D intensity, and navigate high capital costs that have subdued investment. Portugal's economic trajectory is influenced by the broader European trend of attempting to balance labor market productivity with the transition toward a green and digital economy.

## Governance & Rule of Law

Portugal operates within the framework of the European Union, adhering to common standards of fiscal sustainability and administrative simplification. Current policy trends include a move toward reducing bureaucracy (the "tell us once" principle) to enhance the ease of doing business and streamline economic life for domestic and international firms.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Key risks for Portugal include the broader regional challenges of high public debt, rising poverty rates (particularly child poverty), and the difficulty of transitioning transport and building sectors to low-carbon models due to administrative complexity. The outlook remains tied to the success of fiscal consolidation strategies and the ability to attract venture capital and equity financing for high-growth firms to bridge the innovation gap.
