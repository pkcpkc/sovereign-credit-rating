## Economic Overview

Poland is identified as a key participant in the broader European economic landscape described in the 2025 Country Report. While the report focuses primarily on France, Poland is included in the context of European Union fiscal consolidation and structural reforms. The regional economic environment is characterized by a push toward decarbonization, digitalization, and addressing labor market "skills gaps." Like its peers, Poland's economic trajectory is influenced by the European Commission's focus on Real GDP growth, Total Factor Productivity, and the transition to renewable energy infrastructure.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The country operates within the framework of European Commission standards, which currently emphasize administrative simplification (the "tell us once" principle) and the rationalization of tax expenditures to ensure long-term fiscal sustainability across member states.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Key risks for Poland, in alignment with the broader EU context, include:
- **Fiscal Sustainability:** The need for aggressive fiscal consolidation to manage public debt and deficits.
- **Energy Transition:** Challenges in decarbonizing transport and buildings, specifically the shortage of skilled workers for renovation and infrastructure.
- **Labor Market:** Addressing structural weaknesses in business R&D and ensuring the workforce can meet the demands of the green and digital transitions.
- **Investment Climate:** Navigating high capital costs and potential trade tariffs that may impact net exports.
