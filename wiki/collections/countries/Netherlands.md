## Economic Overview

The Netherlands is situated within a European economic landscape characterized by significant structural challenges and transition goals. Based on recent regional assessments, the broader EU context shows a trend of moderate economic growth (projected at 0.8% in 2025) alongside persistent inflationary pressures. Key economic themes include the management of high labor costs, particularly in systems with automatic wage indexation, and the navigation of complex tax-and-benefit structures. Like its neighbors, the Netherlands is navigating fiscal pressures driven by aging populations and the necessity of balancing public debt against rising pension and healthcare expenditures.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The Netherlands operates within a framework of high regulatory complexity. Regional reports indicate that a significant portion of firms face challenges in regulatory compliance, highlighting a need for streamlined tax frameworks and reduced administrative burdens to foster business dynamism. The country remains a key player in the EU's digital and green transitions, emphasizing innovation through R&D intensity.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Structural risks include labor market shortages—particularly in STEM and healthcare—and a mismatch between available skills and job vacancies. Fiscal sustainability is a primary concern as the "ageing cost" of pensions and long-term care projects to rise significantly over the coming decades. Additionally, achieving 2030 climate targets requires overcoming high energy costs and accelerating the transition to renewable energy sources. The outlook emphasizes the need for structural reforms in pension systems, education (specifically STEM output), and circular economy investments to maintain long-term competitiveness.
