## Economic Overview

Belgium is characterized by moderate economic growth, with a projected GDP growth of 0.8% in 2025. The nation faces significant fiscal pressures, with a headline deficit forecast to reach 5.4% of GDP and public debt projected at 107.1% by the end of 2025, with projections reaching 123% of GDP by 2030 under current policies. These pressures are exacerbated by an aging population, which is expected to drive a 3.5% increase in pension spending as a share of GDP between 2022 and 2070.

The labour market presents a dual reality: while the employment rate reached a record high of 72.3% in 2024, it remains below EU averages due to low activity rates among migrants and older workers. A critical skills mismatch persists, evidenced by one of the highest job vacancy rates in the EU (4.1% in Q4-2024), particularly in STEM and healthcare. Furthermore, high labor costs driven by automatic wage indexation—a primary driver of fiscal deficits and a deterrent to international competitiveness—and a complex tax-and-benefit system create significant hurdles for business dynamism. The IMF suggests considering the abolition of automatic indexation and the 1996 wage law to improve competitiveness.

Belgium's Total Factor Productivity (TFP) growth lags behind peers, highlighting a need to reduce regulatory barriers to entry and improve the insolvency regime. While the 2024 external position was weaker than expected, the current account deficit narrowed to 0.3% of GDP.

## Governance & Rule of Law

Belgium operates within a complex regulatory environment where 81% of firms report difficulties with regulatory compliance. The country is noted for its high R&D intensity (3.3% of GDP) and strong innovation leadership, yet it struggles with a complex tax framework and high barriers to entry that can hinder the growth of new enterprises. The government intends to shift the tax burden from labor to capital, introduce capital gains taxation, and reduce tax expenditures.

In terms of the rule of law, Belgium maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence, with 64% of the general public and 72% of companies rating it as "fairly or very good" in 2025. The government is moving toward granting budgetary management powers to the judiciary and improving digitalization, though backlogs remain a persistent issue (notably in the Brussels Court of Appeal). 

The anti-corruption framework is evolving with plans for a new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) and specialized prosecution for financial crime. While Belgium ranks 9th in the EU on the Corruption Perceptions Index (69/100), concerns remain regarding police integrity across local zones and the transparency of asset declarations. Media freedom is supported by the decriminalization of defamation and new anti-SLAPP measures, though public service media face budgetary pressures.

## Key Risks & Outlook

The primary structural risks for Belgium involve fiscal sustainability and demographic shifts. The "Ageing Costs" associated with pensions and long-term care are projected to be among the highest in the EU by 2070. To counter these pressures, the IMF supports raising the effective retirement age and reviewing eligibility for specific pension regimes.

Additionally, Belgium is currently projected to fall short of its 2030 climate effort-sharing targets (42.6% reduction vs. a 47% target), complicated by high electricity-to-gas price ratios that slow the adoption of heat pumps. A cohesive national strategy involving carbon taxation and the phasing out of fossil fuel subsidies is required.

From a financial stability standpoint, risks remain "moderate." The banking sector is resilient, supported by robust capital buffers, though risks in the Commercial Real Estate (CRE) sector have increased due to contracting capital values. Socially, while the AROPE rate is lower than the EU average, there are stark regional disparities, notably in Brussels. The healthcare system faces critical shortages of general practitioners and high out-of-pocket spending, necessitating a shift toward more cost-effective community-based services.

From a legal and institutional standpoint, Belgium faces scrutiny over the non-implementation of 18 leading European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) judgments and a "narrowed" civic space due to proposed sanctions for individuals convicted of illegal acts during demonstrations.
