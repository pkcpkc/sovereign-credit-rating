## Economic Overview

Belgium's economy demonstrates resilience despite successive shocks, though it faces significant structural headwinds characterized by slowing growth and persistent core inflation. A primary concern is declining labor-cost competitiveness driven by the **Automatic Wage Indexation** system. To ensure long-term stability, the IMF emphasizes a need for **Fiscal Consolidation**, aiming to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP and managing a public debt level that reached 104.1% of GDP in 2024. The economic outlook necessitates a seven-year adjustment period under the new EU economic governance framework, focusing on rationalizing social spending and the public wage bill while maintaining investments in infrastructure, healthcare, and the **Green Transition**. Efforts to boost **Total Factor Productivity (TFP)** are also central, requiring a reduction in regulatory barriers and an improved insolvency regime.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The country is currently navigating a period of political adjustment following the 2024 Federal Elections. Economic governance is increasingly aligned with European Union frameworks, particularly regarding fiscal discipline and the transition to a green economy. Coordination between the Belgian Federal and Regional Governments, alongside the National Bank of Belgium (NBB), remains critical for implementing structural reforms.

## Key Risks & Outlook

The primary risks to Belgium's economic stability include high sovereign debt levels and structural inefficiencies in the labor market. The risk of sovereign stress is currently assessed as "Moderate," mitigated by a high-quality debt management profile and a long average residual maturity of 11.1 years. Key priorities for the outlook include:
- **Labor Market Reform:** Addressing the income gap between work and non-work and reviewing the 1996 wage law.
- **Fiscal Sustainability:** Implementing an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of approximately 0.6 percentage points (ppt) of GDP until 2031.
- **Climate Strategy:** Developing a coherent national strategy for carbon taxation and phasing out fossil fuel subsidies.
- **Financial Stability:** Managing systemic risks through maintained capital buffers and prudential limits on mortgage loans.
