## Economic Overview

Ukraine is identified as a key country within the European Commission's 2025 reporting framework, where its economic situation is closely intertwined with EU fiscal policy, energy transition goals, and regional stability. As part of the broader European economic landscape, Ukraine is navigating a context of slowing growth (0.6% for major economies) and high public debt. The report emphasizes the urgent need for structural reforms to address "skills gaps" and facilitate both green and digital transitions. Ukraine's economic trajectory is characterized by the necessity of transitioning toward an innovation-driven model to overcome structural weaknesses, including outdated infrastructure and low R&D capacity.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The context implies a broader European push toward administrative simplification (the "tell us once" principle) and fiscal consolidation. For Ukraine, these themes relate to ongoing efforts to align with European standards of governance, transparency in public procurement, and the reduction of bureaucracy to foster a more competitive business environment. These initiatives are critical for integrating into the European economic framework and ensuring transparency in public spending.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Ukraine faces significant structural hurdles common to the region, including the need for massive investment in R&D and renewable energy infrastructure. Key risks include:
- **Energy Transition:** Challenges in decarbonizing transport and buildings due to administrative complexity and the need for high-capacity renewable energy infrastructure.
- **Labor Market:** Potential shortages of skilled staff, particularly in ICT and manufacturing, which hinder productivity and the "twin green and digital transitions."
- **Fiscal Pressure:** The broader EU trend of rising debt-to-GDP ratios necessitates a focus on permanent spending reviews and rationalizing tax expenditures to ensure long-term sustainability.
- **Structural Reform:** The need to overcome low R&D intensity and lagging digital transformation to move away from a low-cost labor model toward an innovation-based economy.
