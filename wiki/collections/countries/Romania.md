## Economic Overview

Romania is experiencing a complex economic landscape characterized by steady convergence toward EU income and productivity levels, reaching nearly 80% of the EU average in 2024. However, this progress is tempered by significant macroeconomic vulnerabilities. Real GDP growth decelerated to 0.8% in 2024, hampered by weak net exports and high inflation. The country faces a high public deficit of 9.3% of GDP in 2024, driven by substantial wage and pension hikes, while government debt has risen rapidly to nearly 55% of GDP.

The labor market is defined by a dual reality: while nominal wages rose by 15% in 2024, this has significantly outpaced productivity growth, eroding external cost competitiveness. Romania faces a massive shortage of skilled workers, particularly in ICT and manufacturing, coupled with low tertiary education attainment. Furthermore, the economy remains heavily reliant on internal funding (73%) due to underdeveloped capital markets. The country is also navigating high electricity prices—among the highest in the EU due to fiscal burdens—and a lagging digital transformation, with AI adoption in businesses at only 3%.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The governance context for Romania is centered on the "simplification of economic life" initiatives and the implementation of Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) reforms. These measures aim to reduce bureaucratic hurdles for businesses and improve the transparency of public procurement and licensing regulations. These efforts are part of a broader regional strategy to enhance public spending efficiency, modernize institutional frameworks, and facilitate green and digital transitions while addressing the need for administrative simplification to improve the ease of doing business.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Key risks for Romania include a slowing GDP growth trend, high capital costs, and significant fiscal imbalances. Structural risks are prominent, specifically the need for labor market reforms to address skilled worker shortages (STEM) and the necessity of transitioning to a low-carbon economy. Romania faces a critical "innovation stagnation" with the lowest R&D intensity in the EU (0.52% of GDP), far below the 2% target for 2027.

The outlook depends on the successful implementation of the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP) to cap expenditure growth and the ability to attract equity financing for high-growth firms. Long-term sustainability requires addressing the housing crisis, improving infrastructure density (currently among the lowest in the EU), and overcoming regulatory barriers to increase renewable energy integration and circular material use.
