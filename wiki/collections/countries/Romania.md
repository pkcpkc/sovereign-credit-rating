## Economic Overview

Based on the 2025 European Commission Country Report context, Romania is included among the EU nations facing a complex economic landscape characterized by shifting fiscal policies and structural transitions. The country is part of a regional trend where economic growth is being balanced against the need for aggressive fiscal sustainability, specifically aiming to reduce public debt-to-GDP ratios. Romania's economic trajectory is tied to broader EU goals of administrative simplification, R&D investment, and labor market productivity improvements. 

As part of the regional analysis alongside peers like Poland and Hungary, Romania's outlook is influenced by high capital costs, the necessity of transitioning to a low-carbon economy, and the challenge of maintaining competitiveness amidst rising unit labor costs. The regional context highlights a need to address innovation stagnation and improve R&D intensity to reach EU averages. Furthermore, the broader regional economic framework emphasizes the transition toward a circular economy and the management of energy price volatility caused by grid bottlenecks and high taxes.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The governance context for Romania within the 2025 report is linked to the "simplification of economic life" initiatives and the implementation of RRP (Recovery and Resilience Plan) reforms. These measures aim to reduce bureaucratic hurdles for businesses and improve the transparency of public procurement and licensing regulations. This aligns with broader regional efforts to enhance public spending efficiency and modernize institutional frameworks to facilitate green and digital transitions.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Key risks for Romania include the broader European trend of slowing GDP growth and high capital costs which may dampen investment. Structural risks involve the need for significant labor market reforms to address skilled worker shortages (particularly in STEM) and the necessity of transitioning to a low-carbon economy. 

The outlook depends heavily on the successful implementation of fiscal consolidation measures and the ability to attract equity financing for high-growth firms. Regional risks highlighted in the 2025 reports suggest that addressing innovation stagnation, improving R&D intensity, and managing the transition away from fossil fuel dependence are critical for long-term sustainability. Additionally, like its neighbors, Romania must navigate the pressures of an aging population and the need for significant structural shifts in energy generation and healthcare delivery.
