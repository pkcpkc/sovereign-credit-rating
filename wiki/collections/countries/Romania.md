## Economic Overview

Based on the 2025 European Commission Country Report context, Romania is included among the EU nations facing a complex economic landscape characterized by shifting fiscal policies and structural transitions. While specific granular data for Romania is grouped within the broader EU fiscal consolidation framework, the country is part of a regional trend where economic growth is being balanced against the need for aggressive fiscal sustainability. The overarching European strategy emphasizes reducing public debt-to-GDP ratios and addressing the "skills gap" to facilitate green and digital transitions. Romania's economic trajectory is tied to these broader EU goals of administrative simplification, R&D investment, and labor market productivity improvements.

## Governance & Rule of Law

The governance context for Romania within the 2025 report is linked to the "simplification of economic life" initiatives and the implementation of RRP (Recovery and Resilience Plan) reforms. These measures aim to reduce bureaucratic hurdles for businesses and improve the transparency of public procurement and licensing regulations.

## Key Risks & Outlook

Key risks for Romania include the broader European trend of slowing GDP growth and high capital costs which may dampen investment. Structural risks involve the need for significant labor market reforms to address skilled worker shortages and the necessity of transitioning to a low-carbon economy. The outlook depends heavily on the successful implementation of fiscal consolidation measures and the ability to attract equity financing for high-growth firms.
