# Fitch Sovereign Rating Methodology

## Overview

This methodology defines the framework used by [[Fitch Ratings]] to assign and maintain Sovereign Issuer Default Ratings (IDRs) globally. The criteria focus on a forward-looking assessment of a government's capacity and willingness to honor debt obligations to private creditors. The methodology is anchored by four analytical pillars: [[Structural Features]], [[Macroeconomic Performance]], [[Public Finances]], and [[External Finances]]. It applies to both Long-Term (LT) and Short-Term (ST) IDRs, distinguishing between Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) ratings based on factors such as debt composition, capital market depth, and access to foreign currency.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The methodology employs the [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]], a quantitative multiple-regression model calibrated to the LT FC IDR scale ('AAA' to 'CCC+'), as the starting point for rating assignment. The SRM output is adjusted via a [[Qualitative Overlay (QO)]], allowing analysts to modify ratings by up to +3/-3 notches based on factors not fully captured by the model.

The four core pillars and their weights within the SRM are:
1.  **Structural Features (53.7%)**: The highest-weighted pillar, capturing political risk, institutional strength, and economic size/diversification.
2.  **External Finances (17.3%)**: Assesses external vulnerabilities, including reserve adequacy and current account dynamics.
3.  **Public Finances (19.1%)**: Focuses on fiscal sustainability, debt levels, and financing flexibility.
4.  **Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%)**: Evaluates economic stability, growth, and policy credibility.

Additionally, the methodology incorporates the [[Sovereign Climate Risk Model (SCRM)]] as a screening tool (Climate.VS, 0-100 scale) to assess physical and transition risks. Sovereigns with a Climate.VS ≥ 50 in 2035 receive additional analytical scrutiny, which may lead to QO adjustments.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Structural Features Metrics**:
    - **Governance Indicators**: Derived from World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI).
    - **GDP Per Capita**: Measures income level and development status.
    - **Share in World GDP**: Captures economic size and global influence.
    - **Years Since Default/Restructuring**: Historical record of debt servicing.
    - **Broad Money Supply (M2/GDP)**: Indicator of financial depth.
- **Macroeconomic Performance Metrics**:
    - **Real GDP Growth Volatility**: Measures economic stability.
    - **Consumer Price Inflation**: Indicator of price stability.
    - **Real GDP Growth**: Measures economic expansion.
- **Public Finances Metrics**:
    - **Gross General Government Debt/GDP**: Primary measure of fiscal burden.
    - **Interest Payments/Revenue**: Assesses debt servicing costs relative to income.
    - **Fiscal Balance**: Current government budget deficit or surplus.
    - **Foreign-Currency Debt Share**: Proportion of debt denominated in foreign currency.
- **External Finances Metrics**:
    - **Reserve-Currency Flexibility**: Ability to issue debt in hard currencies.
    - **Sovereign Net Foreign Assets**: Net international investment position.
    - **Commodity Dependence**: Reliance on specific commodity exports.
    - **FX Reserves**: Foreign exchange holdings.
    - **External Interest Service**: Debt servicing costs in foreign currency.
    - **Current Account Balance + FDI**: Net external financing position.

## Application

1.  **Calculate SRM Output**: Analysts input data into the [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] using the 18 key variables across the four pillars to generate a baseline quantitative rating.
2.  **Apply Qualitative Overlay (QO)**: Analysts adjust the SRM output by up to +3/-3 notches based on qualitative factors including political stability, financial sector health (via [[Macro-Prudential Indicator Model (MPI)]]), fiscal flexibility, external financing resilience, and geopolitical risks.
3.  **Final Rating Determination**: The [[Rating Committee]] reviews the adjusted rating, considering specific instrument features (e.g., [[Recovery Ratings]] for specific debt instruments) and climate risk signals. The final rating is mapped to the [[Sovereign Credit Rating]] scale, with distinct considerations for LC vs. FC IDRs and short-term mappings.
