# Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI)

## Overview

The **Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI)** is a global indicator published annually by **Transparency International** that ranks countries by their perceived levels of public sector corruption. The index is designed to measure the degree to which corruption is perceived to exist among public officials and politicians in a country. While not a direct sovereign credit rating methodology, the CPI is widely cited by credit rating agencies and sovereign analysts as a key qualitative overlay for assessing **[[Governance quality]]**, **[[Policy credibility]]**, and **[[Accountability]]** within sovereign risk profiles.

In the context of sovereign credit analysis, such as for **[[France]]**, the CPI serves as a benchmark for institutional strength and the effectiveness of anti-corruption frameworks. For instance, France maintains a relatively low corruption perception score (indicating high integrity) compared to global averages, though specific vulnerabilities remain in areas such as lobbying transparency and high-level political accountability.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The CPI does not rely on a single survey or indicator but aggregates data from multiple independent sources. The methodology focuses on three main analytical components:

1.  **Source Aggregation**: The index draws from 13 different data sources, comprising 180+ variables, provided by independent institutions such as the **[[World Bank]]**, **[[World Justice Project]]**, and the **[[European Commission]]**.
2.  **Normalization and Standardization**: All source data is normalized to a common scale (0–100) to allow for comparability across different methodological approaches (e.g., expert assessments vs. survey-based indicators).
3.  **Weighted Averaging**: The final score is calculated as a weighted average of the source scores. Weights are assigned based on the number of countries covered by each source and the reliability of the data, ensuring that sources with broader geographic coverage and higher methodological rigor have greater influence.

The index specifically targets the **public sector**, distinguishing it from broader governance indicators that may include private sector corruption or general administrative inefficiency. It reflects perceptions of corruption in political leaders, parliament, judiciary, and civil service.

## Key Metrics & Variables

The CPI aggregates various underlying metrics from its partner sources. Key variables often include:

- **Expert Assessments**: Ratings from institutions like the **[[World Bank]]** Control of Corruption indicators, which rely on expert opinions on the extent to which public power is exercised for private gain.
- **Country-Specific Surveys**: Data from surveys conducted by organizations like the **[[V-Dem Institute]]** or the **[[Economist Intelligence Unit]]**, which assess the integrity of specific institutions such as the **[[Judicial independence]]** and **[[Anti-corruption framework]]**.
- **Institutional Effectiveness**: Metrics related to the operational capacity of bodies like the **[[Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)]]** and the **[[High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP)]]**, including their ability to investigate and prosecute high-level corruption cases.
- **Regulatory Transparency**: Variables measuring the clarity and enforcement of laws regarding lobbying, conflict of interest, and asset declaration, which are critical components of the **[[Checks and balances]]** system.

## Application

1. **Data Collection and Screening**: Transparency International identifies eligible sources that meet strict criteria for methodology, timeliness, and geographic coverage. Sources must focus on public sector corruption and provide consistent annual data.
2. **Normalization and Weighting**: Selected source data is converted to the 0–100 scale. Each source is assigned a weight based on its sample size and methodological robustness. This step ensures that no single source disproportionately influences the final ranking.
3. **Aggregation and Final Scoring**: The weighted scores are averaged to produce the final CPI score for each country. This aggregate score is then ranked globally. For sovereign analysis, this score is interpreted alongside other rule of law metrics (e.g., **[[EU Rule of Law Mechanism]]** reports) to adjust assumptions on **[[Policy credibility]]** and **[[Debt payment culture]]**.
