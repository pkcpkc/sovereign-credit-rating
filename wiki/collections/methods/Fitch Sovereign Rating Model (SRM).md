# Fitch Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)

## Overview

The Fitch Sovereign Rating Model (SRM) is the quantitative core of Fitch Ratings' methodology for assigning Long-Term Foreign-Currency Issuer Default Ratings (IDRs). Effective from September 15, 2025, this methodology outlines the framework for assessing the capacity and willingness of sovereign issuers to honor their debt obligations. The SRM serves as the starting point for ratings, generating a score calibrated to the Long-Term FC IDR scale ('AAA' to 'CCC+') based on an Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression of 18 key variables.

This quantitative score is subsequently adjusted via a Qualitative Overlay (QO) to account for factors not captured by the model, such as geopolitical risk, financial sector stability, policy credibility, and climate risk. The methodology distinguishes between Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) IDRs, typically aligning them unless specific conditions like reserve-currency flexibility or deep domestic capital markets justify differentiation. For distressed issuers (rated 'B+' or below), Recovery Ratings (RRs) are incorporated to notch instrument ratings based on expected loss severity.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

Fitch's analysis rests on four analytical pillars, which constitute the primary inputs for the SRM and the basis for the Qualitative Overlay:

1.  **Structural Features (Weight: 53.7%)**: This is the dominant pillar in the SRM. It assesses the fundamental economic and political strength of a sovereign. Key quantitative variables include governance indicators (using World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators), GDP per capita (as a proxy for development), and share in world GDP (reflecting vulnerability for small economies). Qualitative adjustments under this pillar consider political stability, geopolitical risks, business environment quality, and financial sector health via the Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI) and Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI).
2.  **Public Finances (Weight: 19.1%)**: This pillar evaluates the fiscal position and debt sustainability of the sovereign. Quantitative inputs include gross general government debt/GDP, interest payments/revenue, fiscal balance/GDP, and the share of foreign-currency debt. The Qualitative Overlay assesses fiscal financing flexibility (market access, debt structure), debt sustainability using Fitch’s proprietary Debt Dynamics Model (DDM), fiscal structure (revenue base breadth, budgetary rigidity), and contingent liabilities (e.g., banking sector support, SOE debts).
3.  **External Finances (Weight: 17.3%)**: This pillar gauges external liquidity and vulnerability to external shocks. SRM variables include reserve-currency flexibility (based on IMF COFER data), sovereign net foreign assets, commodity dependence, FX reserves, external interest service, and current account balance plus FDI. The QO evaluates external financing flexibility, external debt sustainability (net external debt trends), and vulnerability to shocks. The International Liquidity Ratio (ILR) is used to gauge short-term external liquidity risk.
4.  **Macroeconomic Performance, Policies, and Prospects (Weight: 9.8%)**: This pillar focuses on economic stability and growth outlook. SRM variables include real GDP growth volatility, consumer price inflation, and real GDP growth. The QO assesses policy credibility and flexibility (particularly for fixed exchange rate regimes or dollarized economies), medium-term GDP growth outlook, and macroeconomic stability including unemployment and sectoral imbalances.

**Climate Risk Integration**:
A new screening tool, **Sovereign Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)**, aggregates physical risks (heat, drought, storms, floods, sea-level rise) and transition risks (fossil fuel dependence, green energy costs) into a 0-100 score for 2025, 2035, and 2050. Sovereigns with a Climate.VS score of 50 or higher in 2035 are subject to additional analysis. If climate risks are material and not captured by the standard SRM/QO, a notch adjustment may be applied under "Other structural factors."

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Governance Indicators**: Sourced from World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators; a core component of Structural Features.
- **GDP Per Capita**: Proxy for economic development and resource availability; part of Structural Features.
- **Share in World GDP**: Measures economic size and vulnerability; small economies are considered more vulnerable.
- **Gross General Government Debt/GDP**: Primary metric for public finance burden.
- **Interest Payments/Revenue**: Indicator of fiscal stress and service cost burden.
- **Reserve-Currency Flexibility**: Based on IMF COFER data; determines if a sovereign can print its own currency for debt service, significantly impacting FC vs. LC rating alignment and external liquidity.
- **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)**: Used to gauge short-term external liquidity risk; a robust ILR (>100%) influences short-term rating mappings.
- **Climate.VS Score**: Aggregate score (0-100) for climate vulnerability; triggers enhanced scrutiny if ≥50 in 2035.
- **Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI) & Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)**: Metrics used to monitor financial sector stability and systemic risk.

## Application

1.  **Calculate SRM Score**: Compute the quantitative score using the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression of 18 key variables across the four pillars. The output is calibrated to the Long-Term FC IDR scale ('AAA' to 'CCC+'). Structural Features carry the highest weight (53.7%), followed by Public Finances (19.1%), External Finances (17.3%), and Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%).
2.  **Apply Qualitative Overlay (QO)**: Adjust the SRM score to account for qualitative factors not captured by the model. Analysts can assign notch adjustments of ±2 per pillar. The total adjustment is generally capped at ±3 notches from the SRM output. Exceptions allowing wider adjustments include countries in crisis, recovering from crisis, or with a default history within the last five years. Specific checks include:
    *   **Climate Screening**: Apply Climate.VS analysis if the score triggers the threshold.
    *   **Financial Sector Review**: Incorporate BSI and MPI insights into the Structural Features overlay.
    *   **Debt Sustainability**: Validate public finance assumptions using the Debt Dynamics Model (DDM).
3.  **Determine Final Rating and Instrument Ratings**:
    *   **Issuer Default Rating (IDR)**: Finalize the Long-Term IDR based on the adjusted score. Determine LC vs. FC alignment based on reserve-currency flexibility and market depth.
    *   **Short-Term Ratings**: Map LT to ST ratings. For FC ratings, if the sovereign has reserve-currency flexibility or a robust ILR (>100%), choose the higher of two mapping options.
    *   **Recovery Ratings (RR)**: For issuers rated 'B+' or below, assign a Recovery Rating (RR1 to RR6) reflecting expected loss severity. Notch instrument ratings up to +3 or down to -2 notches from the IDR based on these recovery prospects. For investment-grade issuers ('BB-' and above), instrument ratings generally align with the IDR.
