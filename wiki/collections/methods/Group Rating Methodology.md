# Group Rating Methodology

## Overview

The **Group Rating Methodology** is a component of the broader [[S&P Sovereign Rating Methodology]] published by [[S&P Global Ratings]] in December 2017. This framework provides a standardized approach for assessing the creditworthiness of sovereign governments and monetary authorities, specifically regarding their ability and willingness to service financial obligations to nonofficial (commercial) creditors. 

While primarily focused on sovereign entities, the methodology includes specific protocols for rating monetary authorities within monetary unions (e.g., the European Central Bank). In such contexts, the "Group" aspect involves determining ratings through a weighted average of member sovereign ratings, adjusted by the monetary assessment of the authority itself. The framework emphasizes the interplay between institutional stability, economic resilience, external liquidity, fiscal sustainability, and monetary policy credibility.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The methodology is built upon five core pillars, each scored on a scale of 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest). These scores are combined to determine an "indicative rating level," which is subject to supplemental adjustments for extreme risks or strengths.

1.  **Institutional Assessment**: Evaluates policymaking effectiveness, transparency, debt payment culture, and external security risks. A sovereign with a weak debt payment culture (e.g., sustained arrears) receives the weakest assessment ('6'). External security risks, such as war, can lower the assessment by one or two categories if the risk is not imminent within two to three years.
2.  **Economic Assessment**: Focuses on GDP per capita, growth prospects, and economic diversity.
3.  **External Assessment**: Assesses currency status (reserve vs. actively traded), external liquidity, and the overall external position. Metrics use Current Account Payments (CAP) in denominators when external liquid assets exceed external debt.
4.  **Fiscal Assessment**: Analyzes fiscal performance, flexibility, and debt burden. A key update in 2017 shifted the primary metric to the change in *net* general government debt (gross debt minus liquid financial assets) as a percentage of GDP. It also integrates contingent liabilities from the banking sector using the [[Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment (BICRA)]].
5.  **Monetary Assessment**: Evaluates the exchange rate regime and monetary policy credibility. For monetary unions, this assessment is critical for determining potential rating uplifts for the central authority.

### Local vs. Foreign Currency Distinction
The methodology limits the potential uplift for local-currency ratings to one notch above foreign-currency ratings, reflecting a more conservative view on the distinction between local and external debt servicing risks (previously two notches).

## Key Metrics & Variables

-   **Net General Government Debt**: The change in net debt (gross debt minus liquid financial assets) as a percentage of GDP. This is the primary metric for the initial fiscal assessment, replacing gross debt headlines to better capture true fiscal stance.
-   **Current Account Payments (CAP)**: Used in the denominator for external ratios when external liquid assets exceed external debt, providing a more accurate measure of external indebtedness.
-   **Currency Status Classifications**: 
    -   *Reserve Currencies*: Currencies comprising >3% of global FX reserves.
    -   *Actively Traded Currencies*: Currencies comprising >1% of global FX turnover.
-   **Contingent Liabilities**: Categorized as limited, moderate, high, or very high based on [[BICRA]] scores and banking system size. These can lead to negative adjustments in the debt burden assessment of up to three categories.
-   **Net Asset Position**: Sovereigns with exceptionally large liquid financial assets (net asset position >100% of GDP) may qualify for positive supplemental adjustments.

## Application

1.  **Pillar Assessment and Scoring**: Analysts assess the sovereign across the five pillars (Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, Monetary) and assign a score from 1 to 6 for each based on quantitative metrics and qualitative factors (e.g., debt payment culture, security risks).
2.  **Determinative Indicative Rating**: The five pillar scores are combined using a matrix to determine an initial "indicative rating level." For monetary authorities in unions, this involves calculating a weighted average of member sovereign ratings.
3.  **Supplemental Adjustments**: The indicative rating is adjusted for specific extreme factors. Negative adjustments apply for extremely weak external liquidity, very high fiscal debt burdens, or imminent event risks. Positive adjustments may apply for strong net asset positions. For monetary unions, uplifts of zero to four notches may apply depending on monetary flexibility and credibility.
4.  **Final Rating Assignment**: The adjusted indicative rating is mapped to the final sovereign credit rating scale. The relationship between local-currency and foreign-currency ratings is finalized, ensuring the local rating does not exceed the foreign rating by more than one notch.
