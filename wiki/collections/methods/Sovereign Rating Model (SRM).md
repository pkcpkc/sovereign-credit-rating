# Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)

## Overview

The Sovereign Rating Model (SRM) is the core quantitative framework utilized by Fitch Ratings for assigning and maintaining sovereign and central bank Issuer Default Ratings (IDRs) globally. The methodology defines sovereign credit risk as a forward-looking assessment of the government's capacity and willingness to honor debt obligations to private creditors. Anchored by four analytical pillars—Structural Features, Macroeconomic Performance, Public Finances, and External Finances—the SRM serves as the starting point for rating assignment before adjustments are made via the Qualitative Overlay (QO). The model is calibrated to the Long-Term Foreign-Currency (LT FC) IDR scale, ranging from 'AAA' to 'CCC+'.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The SRM employs a multiple-regression model incorporating 18 key variables distributed across four primary pillars. The weighting of these pillars reflects their historical predictive power for default probability:

1.  **Structural Features (Weight: 53.7%)**: This is the most heavily weighted pillar, capturing political risk and institutional strength. It includes governance indicators (World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators), GDP per capita, the country's share in world GDP, years since default or restructuring, and broad money supply.
2.  **Public Finances (Weight: 19.1%)**: Focuses on fiscal sustainability through metrics such as gross general government debt-to-GDP, interest payments as a percentage of revenue, fiscal balance, and the share of foreign-currency debt.
3.  **External Finances (Weight: 17.3%)**: Evaluates the country's external position, including reserve-currency flexibility, sovereign net foreign assets, commodity dependence, Foreign Exchange (FX) reserves, external interest service, and the current account balance combined with Foreign Direct Investment (FDI).
4.  **Macroeconomic Performance (Weight: 9.8%)**: Assesses economic stability via real GDP growth volatility, consumer price inflation, and real GDP growth rates.

The quantitative output is subsequently adjusted by the Qualitative Overlay (QO), a formalized system allowing analysts to modify the SRM score by up to +3 or -3 notches based on factors not fully captured by the model, such as political stability, financial sector health, and geopolitical risks.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **GDP per Capita**: A primary variable within Structural Features, reflecting the economic development level and resilience of the sovereign.
- **World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI)**: Used to quantify institutional strength and governance quality within the Structural Features pillar.
- **Gross General Government Debt-to-GDP**: A core Public Finances metric assessing the overall debt burden relative to economic output.
- **FX Reserves**: Key for External Finances, indicating the country's ability to meet short-term external obligations.
- **Real GDP Growth Volatility**: A Macroeconomic Performance variable measuring economic stability and predictability.
- **Climate Vulnerability Signal (Climate.VS)**: A screening tool (0-100 scale) assessing physical risks (e.g., heatwaves, floods) and transition risks (fossil fuel dependence). Sovereigns with a Climate.VS ≥ 50 in 2035 receive additional analytical scrutiny, potentially leading to QO adjustments.
- **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)**: Used in Short-Term Rating assessments to evaluate liquidity adequacy (threshold ≥ 100%).

## Application

1.  **Quantitative Calculation**: Calculate the initial SRM score using the 18 variables across the four pillars (Structural Features, Macroeconomic Performance, Public Finances, External Finances). This provides a baseline rating on the LT FC IDR scale.
2.  **Qualitative Overlay (QO) Adjustment**: Analysts apply qualitative adjustments to the SRM output, modifying the rating by up to +3 or -3 notches. This step accounts for political stability, financial sector risks, fiscal flexibility, external financing resilience, and geopolitical factors.
3.  **Rating Determination & Mapping**: The final IDR is determined by combining the SRM output with the QO. For specific instruments, Recovery Ratings (RR1-RR6) are applied to determine loss severity. Short-Term IDRs (ST FC/LC) are mapped from the LT IDR using correspondence tables, considering reserve-currency flexibility and financing flexibility.
