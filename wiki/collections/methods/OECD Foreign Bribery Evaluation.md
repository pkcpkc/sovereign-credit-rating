# OECD Foreign Bribery Evaluation

## Overview

The **OECD Foreign Bribery Evaluation** is a peer-review mechanism conducted by the [[OECD Working Group on Bribery]] to assess the effectiveness and implementation of the [[OECD Convention on Combating Bribery of Foreign Public Officials in International Business Transactions]]. It serves as a critical external validation of a country's anti-corruption framework, directly influencing assessments of [[Institutional strength]], [[Policy credibility]], and [[Governance quality]].

The methodology evaluates both the legal framework (efficiency) and the practical enforcement (effectiveness) of anti-bribery measures. Key areas of focus include criminalization of bribery, extraterritorial application, accountability of legal persons, adequate sanctions, enforcement powers, and statistics. The evaluation highlights gaps in legislation and practice, such as inconsistencies in prosecution or insufficient resources for specialized bodies like the [[National Financial Prosecutor]] (PNF) or the [[Anti-Corruption Agency]] (AFA).

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The evaluation method is structured around several core pillars that map to broader governance and credit risk factors:

1.  **Legal Framework Completeness**: Assessing whether domestic laws fully implement the Convention's requirements, including the criminalization of foreign bribery and the exclusion of "facilitation payments."
2.  **Prosecutorial Effectiveness**: Evaluating the independence, resources, and track record of specialized anti-corruption bodies (e.g., [[PNF]], [[OCLCIFF]], [[EPPO]]). This includes analyzing case backlogs, duration of proceedings (e.g., bribery cases averaging 1,335 days in France), and the consistency of outcomes.
3.  **Corporations Accountability**: Reviewing laws and practices regarding the liability of legal persons, internal compliance mechanisms, and the use of deferred prosecution agreements or settlements.
4.  **International Cooperation**: Assessing the country's ability to cooperate in investigations, extradition, and mutual legal assistance.
5.  **Prevention and Integrity**: Examining transparency in public procurement, lobbying regulations (e.g., coverage of top-executive officials), and whistleblower protections.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Legislative Compliance**: Binary and qualitative assessment of whether national laws align with OECD Convention standards.
- **Enforcement Statistics**: Number of investigations, prosecutions, convictions, and final sentences related to foreign bribery.
- **Case Efficiency**: Average duration of bribery cases from investigation to final judgment; backlog size of specialized financial prosecutors.
- **Institutional Independence**: Perceived and actual independence of judicial bodies ([[Superior Council of the Magistracy]]) and anti-corruption agencies ([[AFA]], [[HATVP]]) from political interference.
- **Transparency Gaps**: Extent of coverage in lobbying registers and asset declarations, particularly for high-level executives and public officials.
- **International Peer Review Outcomes**: Recommendations issued by the OECD Working Group on Bribery and the subsequent implementation status by the country.

## Application

1.  **Phase I and II Monitoring Reports**: The OECD Working Group on Bribery produces detailed monitoring reports for each signatory country. Phase I focuses on legislative efficiency, while Phase II assesses enforcement effectiveness. These reports identify specific deficiencies in laws and practices.
2.  **Data Collection and Verification**: Analysts and peers collect quantitative data (case numbers, sentence lengths) and qualitative evidence (expert interviews, case law analysis). In the context of France, this involves reviewing the output of the [[PNF]], the reforms of the magistrate status (2023), and the operational capacity of the [[AFA]].
3.  **Peer Review and Recommendation**: The collected evidence is reviewed by peer countries. Constructive recommendations are made to address identified gaps (e.g., improving lobbying transparency, reducing case durations).
4.  **Integration into Governance Assessments**: The findings are synthesized into broader rule of law and governance scores (such as those used in the [[EU Rule of Law Mechanism]] or [[GRECO Evaluation]]). For sovereign credit and investment analysis, poor OECD evaluations signal higher political risk, weak [[Accountability]], and potential [[Financial_sector_health]] vulnerabilities due to unchecked corruption.
5.  **Follow-up and Continuous Monitoring**: Countries are expected to report on their progress in implementing recommendations. Progress (or lack thereof) is tracked in subsequent reviews, influencing long-term perceptions of [[Policy_credibility]] and [[Institutional_strength]].
