# Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment (BICRA)

## Overview

The **Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment (BICRA)** is a specialized component utilized within the [[S&P Sovereign Rating Methodology]] (2017) to evaluate the credit quality and stability of a country's banking sector. It is not a standalone rating for banks, but rather an analytical framework used to assess the magnitude of sovereign **contingent liabilities** arising from the banking industry.

The primary purpose of BICRA is to quantify the potential fiscal burden on the sovereign government if the banking sector were to experience significant distress or require state support. The assessment considers the size of the banking system relative to the economy and the inherent credit quality of the banks, which is derived from their alignment with the sovereign's rating and specific industry risk factors.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The BICRA framework integrates two main dimensions to determine the level of contingent liability risk:

1.  **Banking System Size**: The relative size of the banking sector within the country's economy, typically measured as a percentage of GDP. A larger banking sector implies greater potential fiscal exposure for the sovereign in the event of a crisis.
2.  **Banking Industry Credit Quality**: An assessment of the overall health and risk profile of the banking industry. This is often inferred from the ratings of individual banks within the country, adjusted for systemic risks, regulatory frameworks, and the strength of the resolution framework.

The output of the BICRA is a categorization of contingent liabilities as **limited**, **moderate**, **high**, or **very high**. This categorization directly influences the **Fiscal Assessment** pillar of the sovereign rating.

## Key Metrics & Variables

-   **Banking Sector Size (% of GDP)**: A quantitative metric representing the volume of banking assets or credit relative to the gross domestic product. Higher percentages indicate higher potential contingent liability.
-   **Contingent Liability Category**: A qualitative-quantitative hybrid output from the BICRA process, classified into four levels:
    -   *Limited*: Minimal risk of fiscal support needed.
    -   *Moderate*: Moderate risk, potentially requiring limited fiscal resources.
    -   *High*: Significant risk, likely requiring substantial fiscal resources.
    -   *Very High*: Severe risk, potentially threatening fiscal sustainability.
-   **Debt Burden Adjustment**: The BICRA result leads to negative adjustments in the sovereign's debt burden assessment, ranging from zero to **three categories** (notches) depending on the severity of the contingent liability classification.

## Application

1.  **Assess Banking Sector Size**: Calculate the size of the banking industry relative to the sovereign's GDP to establish the baseline magnitude of potential exposure.
2.  **Evaluate Industry Credit Quality**: Analyze the creditworthiness of the banking sector, considering individual bank ratings, regulatory oversight, and systemic interconnections. This often involves applying a ceiling to bank ratings based on the sovereign's own rating.
3.  **Determine Contingent Liability Category**: Combine the size and credit quality assessments to classify the sovereign's contingent liabilities from the banking sector as limited, moderate, high, or very high.
4.  **Adjust Sovereign Fiscal Assessment**: Apply the resulting contingent liability classification to the sovereign's **Fiscal Assessment** pillar. High or very high categories result in downgrades (negative adjustments) to the indicative rating level by up to three categories, reflecting the increased fiscal risk to the sovereign.
