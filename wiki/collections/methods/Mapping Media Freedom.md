# Mapping Media Freedom

## Overview

**Mapping Media Freedom** is a methodology and rating system used primarily by **CIVICUS** to assess the state of civic space and media freedom in various countries. Unlike pure media freedom indices that focus solely on press liberty, this framework often contextualizes media health within the broader "Civic Space" rating, which evaluates the environment for civil society, including freedom of expression, association, and assembly.

The methodology identifies whether a country’s civic space (and by extension, media freedom) is classified as **Open**, **Narrowed**, **Obstructed**, **Closed**, or **Vanishing**. The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Poland highlights a specific application of this framework, noting Poland’s re-rating from "Obstructed" to "Narrowed" in March 2025, reflecting improvements in media pluralism and government engagement despite persistent structural challenges.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The assessment of media freedom under this framework is derived from several qualitative and quantitative pillars:

1.  **Regulatory Independence**: Assessment of the autonomy of media regulators (e.g., the National Council for Radio and Television - **KRRiT** in Poland) from political influence.
2.  **Media Pluralism and Ownership**: Evaluation of ownership transparency, state advertising distribution, and the diversity of voices in public and private media.
3.  **Legal Framework**: Analysis of laws governing media licensing, content regulation, and alignment with international standards such as the **European Media Freedom Act**.
4.  **Political Interference**: Monitoring of political bias in public service media, negative rhetoric against journalists/NGOs, and operational restrictions.
5.  **Civil Society Synergy**: Recognition that media freedom is intertwined with the health of civil society; a "Narrowed" space implies that while some channels for expression exist, they face significant pressure.

## Key Metrics & Variables

-   **Civic Space Rating**: The overarching classification (e.g., "Narrowed") that serves as a proxy for the general safety and freedom of media actors.
-   **Public Service Media Bias**: Measurement of political neutrality in state-funded or public broadcasters.
-   **State Advertising Distribution**: Metrics on whether government advertising is allocated equitably across different media outlets or concentrated in friendly press.
-   **Regulator Independence**: Qualitative assessment of the appointment and decision-making powers of the national media regulator.
-   **Legislative Alignment**: Degree of compliance with EU directives like the **European Media Freedom Act** and recommendations from the **Venice Commission**.
-   **Operational Constraints**: Frequency of administrative hurdles, such as slow licensing decisions or legal challenges against media outlets.

## Application

1.  **Data Collection and Monitoring**: Gather data on legislative changes, regulatory appointments, ownership structures, and incidents of harassment or censorship. In Poland, this included monitoring the liquidation of public broadcasters and the status of **KRRiT**.
2.  **Contextual Analysis**: Evaluate the collected data against the five civic space categories. For instance, Poland’s shift from "Obstructed" to "Narrowed" was determined by noting that while threats remain, the space for civil society and media has slightly improved compared to previous periods of intense obstruction.
3.  **Qualitative Overlay**: Adjust the assessment based on specific political contexts, such as the **Constitutional Tribunal crisis** or tensions between domestic authorities and the **European Commission**. This step accounts for systemic risks that quantitative metrics might miss, such as the refusal to publish certain court judgments which indirectly affects legal certainty for media disputes.
4.  **Rating Assignment**: Assign the final civic space/media freedom rating. In the case of Poland (March 2025), the rating was updated to **Narrowed**, acknowledging progress (e.g., decreased political bias in some public media, new NGO programs) while flagging remaining risks (e.g., independence of the media regulator, pending media law reforms).
