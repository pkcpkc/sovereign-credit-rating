# Net expenditure growth

## Overview

Net expenditure growth is a central metric within the [[Reformed EU Economic Governance Framework]] used by the [[European Commission]] to assess fiscal sustainability and monitor compliance with medium-term fiscal-structural plans. Unlike gross expenditure, this metric excludes certain mandatory or discretionary spending categories (such as interest payments, unemployment benefits, and specific EU-funded expenditures) to provide a clearer picture of the government's discretionary fiscal stance and underlying economic momentum.

In the context of the 2025 Country Report for France, net expenditure growth is a critical variable for determining whether the government is adhering to its agreed fiscal adjustment path. The [[European Commission]] uses this metric to evaluate if fiscal consolidation efforts are sufficient to reduce the general government deficit (which stood at 5.8% of GDP in 2024) and stabilize the rising public debt burden (113% of GDP). It serves as a primary indicator for assessing the credibility of the [[Medium-term fiscal-structural plan]] and determining if the [[Excessive deficit procedure]] has been effectively addressed.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The assessment of net expenditure growth relies on three main analytical components derived from the EU's macroeconomic assessment framework:

1.  **Discretionary Fiscal Effort**: Measurement of the change in public spending that is directly controlled by the government, excluding automatic stabilizers. This helps isolate the policy choices made by the [[Council of the European Union]] and national authorities from cyclical economic fluctuations.
2.  **Fiscal Sustainability Alignment**: Comparison of actual net expenditure growth against the trajectory required to meet the debt sustainability targets set in the country's medium-term plan. For France, this involves ensuring that net growth does not outpace potential GDP growth, which is crucial given the [[fiscal sustainability]] risks and aging costs highlighted in the 2025 report.
3.  **Structural vs. Cyclical Decomposition**: Analysis of whether growth in net expenditures is driven by permanent structural reforms (e.g., [[energy transition]] investments, [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] measures) or temporary cyclical factors. This distinction is vital for evaluating the long-term impact on [[competitiveness]] and [[economic strength]].

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Net Expenditure Growth Rate**: The percentage change in government spending net of specific exemptions. In France's 2025 context, this metric is scrutinized to ensure it aligns with the fiscal consolidation path required to reduce the deficit from 5.8% (2024) to projected levels by 2026.
- **General Government Deficit**: While not a component of net expenditure itself, this is the outcome metric heavily influenced by net expenditure decisions. France's deficit of 5.8% of GDP in 2024 necessitated strict controls on net growth.
- **Public Debt-to-GDP Ratio**: Used to contextualize the sustainability of current net expenditure levels. With debt rising to 113% of GDP and projected to reach 118.4% by 2026, the trajectory of net expenditure is critical for long-term [[debt_burden]] management.
- **Potential GDP Growth**: The benchmark against which net expenditure growth is measured. If net expenditure grows faster than potential output, it risks worsening the structural deficit and [[fiscal_performance_and_flexibility]].

## Application

1.  **Data Collection and Exclusion**: The [[European Commission]] collects detailed public finance data from member states, including France. Specific expenditure items such as interest payments (which are projected to rise to 2.9% of GDP in 2026 for France), unemployment benefits, and EU-funded projects are excluded to calculate the "net" figure.
2.  **Benchmarking Against Fiscal Plans**: The calculated net expenditure growth rate is compared against the targets defined in the country’s approved [[Medium-term fiscal-structural plan]]. For France, this involves checking compliance with the reforms endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]] in January 2025 to end the excessive deficit situation.
3.  **Sustainability and Impact Assessment**: Analysts evaluate the impact of net expenditure changes on key economic indicators such as [[labor productivity]], [[business environment]] efficiency, and [[energy security]]. If net expenditure growth is deemed unsustainable or misallocated (e.g., hindering [[green transition]] or [[skills shortages]] resolution), the Commission may issue recommendations or warnings.
