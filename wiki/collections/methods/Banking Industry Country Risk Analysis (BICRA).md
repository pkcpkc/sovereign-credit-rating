# Banking Industry Country Risk Analysis (BICRA)

## Overview

**Banking Industry Country Risk Analysis (BICRA)** is a specialized analytical framework used by **S&P Global Ratings** within its sovereign credit rating methodology. It is not a standalone public rating but a critical component of the sovereign assessment process, specifically integrated into the **Fiscal Assessment** pillar.

BICRA is employed to evaluate the risk of **contingent liabilities** arising from the domestic banking sector. The primary objective is to determine the potential financial exposure a sovereign government might face due to banking sector distress, such as the need for bailouts or guarantees. This analysis helps quantify the "hidden" debt burden that could impact the country's ability to meet its external and domestic obligations.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

BICRA functions as a sub-component of the broader S&P Sovereign Credit Rating Methodology, specifically addressing the **Contingent Liabilities** section of the Fiscal Assessment. Its analytical components include:

- **Integration with Fiscal Assessment**: BICRA is explicitly linked to the analysis of "contingent liabilities," alongside non-financial public enterprises and off-budget guarantees. It assesses the likelihood and magnitude of sovereign support to the banking sector.
- **Risk of Contingent Support**: The analysis evaluates the strength of the banking sector and the legal/institutional framework regarding bail-in vs. bailout mechanisms. A weaker banking sector implies higher potential contingent liabilities for the sovereign.
- **Qualitative Judgment**: Like other parts of the S&P methodology, BICRA involves significant **analytical discretion**. Analysts must judge the systemic importance of banks, the depth of deposit insurance, and the historical "debt payment culture" regarding bank rescues.
- **Impact on Indicative Rating**: The findings from BICRA can lead to adjustments in the final sovereign rating. If contingent liabilities are deemed high, they act as a negative adjustment factor, potentially downstepping the indicative rating derived from primary fiscal metrics.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Banking Sector Strength**: Metrics related to the capitalization, asset quality, and profitability of the domestic banking system.
- **Systemic Importance**: The size of the banking sector relative to GDP, indicating the potential scale of sovereign support required in a crisis.
- **Legal Framework for Resolution**: The clarity and robustness of laws governing bank failures (e.g., bail-in capabilities vs. implicit guarantees).
- **Historical Precedent**: Past instances of government support to the banking sector, reflecting the country's "debt payment culture" and political willingness to absorb banking losses.
- **Fiscal Space**: The sovereign's existing fiscal flexibility and debt burden, which determines its capacity to absorb banking-related shocks without jeopardizing its own creditworthiness.

## Application

1. **Identify Banking Sector Risks**: Analysts assess the domestic banking sector's health, stability, and exposure to external shocks. This involves evaluating regulatory oversight and the resilience of major financial institutions.
2. **Estimate Contingent Liabilities**: Based on the banking sector's risk profile, analysts estimate the potential cost to the sovereign if a systemic crisis occurs. This includes considering implicit guarantees and the likelihood of government intervention.
3. **Integrate into Fiscal Assessment**: The estimated contingent liabilities are factored into the broader **Fiscal Assessment**. If the potential liability is significant, it reduces the "fiscal flexibility" score or increases the effective "debt burden" perception.
4. **Apply Discretionary Adjustments**: The final determination of how much weight BICRA carries in the sovereign rating is subject to **supplemental adjustment factors** and analyst discretion, ensuring that specific event risks or unique institutional factors are considered in the final rating output.
