# GRECO Evaluation

## Overview

The **GRECO Evaluation** is a peer-review methodology conducted by the Group of States Against Corruption (GRECO), a body of the [[Council of Europe]]. Unlike financial credit ratings, GRECO assesses the anti-corruption framework, institutional integrity, and compliance with the [[Council of Europe]]'s standards and recommendations. In the context of the [[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on France]], GRECO evaluations serve as a critical benchmark for assessing the effectiveness of France's anti-corruption architecture, including the independence and capacity of bodies like the [[Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)]] and the [[High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP)]].

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The evaluation framework focuses on both the legislative and practical implementation of anti-corruption measures. Key analytical components identified in the French context include:

- **Institutional Independence & Capacity**: Assessment of whether bodies like the [[Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)]] and the [[National Financial Prosecutor (PNF)]] operate without political interference. The 2025 report highlights the reorganization of AFA into public and private sector departments as a structural strength.
- **Legislative Framework & Transparency**: Evaluation of laws governing lobbying, political financing, and asset declarations. A noted weakness is the exclusion of top-executive officials from lobbying disclosure requirements, a gap often highlighted in GRECO recommendations.
- **Effectiveness of Prosecution**: Analysis of case resolution times and the consistency of prosecuting high-level corruption. The report notes significant delays (e.g., 1,335 days for bribery cases), which impacts the perceived effectiveness of the legal framework.
- **Whistleblower Protection**: Review of legal guarantees and practical resources for protecting whistleblowers. The report indicates insufficient public funding for protection mechanisms despite increased reporting capacity.
- **Political Financing Oversight**: Assessment of the [[National Commission on Campaign Accounts and Political Financing (CNCCFP)]]'s ability to detect and sanction irregularities, noting successful recoveries of misused public funds.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Prosecution Efficiency**: Measured by the average duration of cases (e.g., 354 days for civil/commercial cases, 1,335 days for bribery). High durations negatively impact the rating of institutional effectiveness.
- **Transparency Coverage**: The scope of entities and individuals subject to asset declarations and lobbying registers. The exclusion of top-executive officials from lobbying registers is a critical negative variable.
- **Institutional Resources**: Staffing levels and budgetary constraints of key bodies (e.g., HATVP staff increased to handle a 48% rise in declarations; AFA reorganization).
- **Civic Space & Whistleblowing**: Qualitative assessment of the "narrowed" civic space and the practical implementation of whistleblower protections, including reports of intimidation against civil society.
- **Compliance with Recommendations**: The extent to which the state implements GRECO's previous recommendations, particularly regarding the independence of the [[Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM)]] and the fight against conflicts of interest.

## Application

1. **Legislative Assessment**: Review the national legal framework against GRECO’s recommendations, focusing on laws regarding criminalization of corruption, asset declaration, lobbying, and political financing. Identify gaps such as the lack of lobbying disclosure for top executives.
2. **Institutional Evaluation**: Analyze the structure, independence, and resources of key anti-corruption bodies ([[AFA]], [[HATVP]], [[PNF]], [[CNCCFP]]). Assess recent reforms, such as the 2023 magistrate status reform and AFA's departmental reorganization, for their impact on operational autonomy.
3. **Practical Implementation Review**: Examine data on prosecutions, case durations, and resource allocation. Evaluate practical challenges such as the "narrowed" civic space, police violence affecting journalists, and difficulties in accessing administrative documents.
4. **Compliance Reporting**: Compile findings into an evaluation report, highlighting strengths (e.g., effective oversight by [[Defender of Rights]], strong ethical codes) and weaknesses (e.g., legislative instability, pending ECHR judgments). Issue recommendations for improvement, often integrated into national plans like the 2024-2029 National Anti-Corruption Plan.
