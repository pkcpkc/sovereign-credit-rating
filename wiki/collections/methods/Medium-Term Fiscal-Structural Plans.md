# Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plans

## Overview

The **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plans** (MTFSP) constitute a core component of the [[European Semester]] framework and the updated [[Stability and Growth Pact]]. Administered by the [[European Commission]], this methodology requires Member States to submit comprehensive plans outlining their fiscal trajectory and structural reform agenda for a medium-term horizon (typically 5 years). 

The primary target of this methodology is to ensure **[[fiscal sustainability]]**, **[[debt sustainability]]**, and **[[macroeconomic stability]]** across the Eurozone. Unlike previous stability programs focused solely on deficit and debt reduction, the MTFSP integrates **[[structural reforms]]**—such as labor market adjustments, pension reforms, and green/digital transitions—into the fiscal consolidation narrative. The method assesses whether a country's planned fiscal adjustments are credible, sustainable, and adequately supported by growth-enhancing structural measures.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The analytical framework of the MTFSP evaluates countries through three interdependent pillars:

1.  **Fiscal Sustainability & Consolidation Credibility**:
    *   Assesses the nominal fiscal effort required to bring deficits and debt ratios back within EU treaty thresholds (3% deficit, 60% debt reference values).
    *   Evaluates the credibility of revenue and expenditure projections, including the treatment of discretionary measures versus automatic stabilizers.
    *   Analyzes the impact of **[[contingent liabilities]]** (e.g., banking sector exposure, climate adaptation costs) on long-term fiscal health.

2.  **Structural Reform Alignment**:
    *   Examines whether fiscal consolidation is paired with reforms that enhance **[[productivity growth]]** and **[[labour market resilience]]**.
    *   Key areas include **[[energy transition]]** investments, digitalization, skills development, and regulatory simplification.
    *   The methodology checks for consistency between fiscal savings and investments in **[[green budgeting]]** and **[[institutional strength]]**.

3.  **Demographic & Long-Term Fiscal Pressure**:
    *   Incorporates long-term projections (e.g., up to 2070) regarding age-related spending in **[[pensions]]** and **[[healthcare]]**.
    *   Assesses the adequacy of current structural reforms (e.g., pension tightening, retirement age adjustments) to mitigate rising **[[debt burden]]** from an aging population.

## Key Metrics & Variables

-   **General Government Deficit-to-GDP**: The primary indicator of short-term fiscal stress. Under the MTFSP, countries must demonstrate a clear path to reducing deficits above 3% of GDP. *Example: Austria projected at 4.4% (2025) to 4.2% (2026).*
-   **Gross Government Debt-to-GDP**: Measures the stock of debt relative to economic output. Used to assess **[[debt sustainability]]** and the need for adjustment. *Example: Austria projected to rise to 84.0% of GDP in 2025.*
-   **Net Expenditure Growth**: A key metric in the new fiscal rules, measuring primary spending growth excluding interest payments and cyclical support. MTFSPs must set limits on net expenditure growth to ensure structural balance.
-   **Fiscal Adjustment Effort**: The quantified nominal savings or revenue increases planned (e.g., EUR 6.4 billion in savings for Austria in 2025).
-   **Structural Reform Score/Impact**: Qualitative assessment of reforms aimed at boosting **[[economic growth]]**, reducing **[[skills gap]]**, and improving **[[energy affordability]]**.
-   **Long-Term Fiscal Gap**: The projected gap between sustainable revenue and necessary spending, driven heavily by demographics (e.g., Austria's age-related spending projected to rise to 30% of GDP by 2070).

## Application

1.  **Submission and Pre-Assessment**:
    Member States submit their MTFSP (e.g., Austria submitted its plan on May 13, 2025) to the [[European Commission]]. The Commission conducts an initial check for completeness, ensuring that the plan covers the required medium-term horizon and includes both fiscal targets and structural reform commitments.

2.  **Credibility and Feasibility Analysis**:
    The Commission, often using input from institutions like **WIFO** and **FISK** in the context of Austrian fiscal frameworks, analyzes the technical assumptions. This includes testing the sensitivity of fiscal projections to economic shocks (e.g., recession, energy price volatility) and verifying that **[[policy credibility]]** is high. The analysis checks if the proposed **[[fiscal consolidation]]** measures (e.g., abolition of paid educational leave, pension reforms) are politically feasible and legally sound.

3.  **Structural Reform Verification**:
    The methodology evaluates whether the fiscal plan is complemented by substantive structural reforms. For instance, in Austria, the plan is assessed against reforms in **[[banking sector health]]**, **[[energy transition]]** (grid expansion, renewable integration), and **[[labor market]]** policies (addressing skills shortages). The Commission ensures that fiscal savings are not undermined by weak structural performance.

4.  **Country-Specific Recommendations (CSRs)**:
    Based on the analysis, the Commission issues **[[Country-Specific Recommendations]]** (e.g., the 2025 Country Report for Austria). If the MTFSP is deemed insufficient, the Commission may recommend additional measures or place the country under an Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP). The final output links fiscal targets with specific structural actions to ensure **[[macroeconomic stability]]** and sustainable growth.
