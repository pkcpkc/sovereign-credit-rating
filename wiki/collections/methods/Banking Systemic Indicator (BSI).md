# Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)

## Overview

The **Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)** is a specialized quantitative and qualitative tool employed by **Fitch Ratings** within its **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)** framework. It is designed to monitor and assess the health of a country's financial sector, specifically focusing on macro-prudential risks and systemic banking vulnerabilities.

The BSI serves as a critical input for the **Qualitative Overlay (QO)** process, particularly under the **Structural Features** pillar. While the core SRM relies heavily on governance and economic development metrics, the BSI allows analysts to adjust ratings to reflect the potential contingent liabilities and stability risks posed by the banking sector. It is used in conjunction with the **Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)** to provide a comprehensive view of financial sector stability.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The BSI is integrated into Fitch’s sovereign rating methodology primarily through the following components:

1.  **Structural Features Pillar**: The BSI contributes to the assessment of structural features, which carries the highest weight (53.7%) in the SRM.
2.  **Qualitative Overlay (QO)**: The BSI is a key factor in the QO mechanism. Analysts use the BSI score to determine if a notch adjustment (+2/-2) is warranted for the Structural Features pillar due to financial sector health.
3.  **Contingent Liabilities Assessment**: The BSI helps identify risks related to potential government support requirements for the banking sector, which are assessed under the **Public Finances** pillar as contingent liabilities.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Financial Sector Health**: Assessed via the BSI score, which aggregates various metrics to gauge the overall robustness of the banking system.
- **Macro-Prudential Risks**: Evaluated alongside the **Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)** to identify vulnerabilities such as credit booms, asset price bubbles, or high leverage.
- **Systemic Vulnerability**: Metrics that indicate the likelihood of bank failures or the need for state intervention, which can impact sovereign debt sustainability.

## Application

1.  **Initial Screening**: Analysts utilize the **Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)** and **Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)** to screen sovereigns for elevated financial sector risks.
2.  **Qualitative Adjustment Determination**: Based on the BSI assessment, analysts evaluate whether the financial sector's health deviates significantly from the structural norms captured by the base SRM variables (such as GDP per capita and governance).
3.  **Rating Adjustment**: If the BSI indicates significant fragility or systemic risk that could lead to material contingent liabilities (e.g., bailouts), a negative notch adjustment is applied to the **Structural Features** pillar via the Qualitative Overlay. Conversely, a robust banking sector may support a positive adjustment.
4.  **Integration with Sovereign Rating**: The adjusted Structural Features score is combined with scores from Public Finances, External Finances, and Macroeconomic Performance to finalize the Sovereign Rating Model output, subject to the overall QO cap of ±3 notches.
