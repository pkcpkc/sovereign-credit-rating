# Relative Climate Risk Module (RCRM)

## Overview

The **Relative Climate Risk Module (RCRM)** is a component within the broader **Sovereign Climate Risk Model (SCRM)** utilized by [[Fitch Ratings]] in its Sovereign Rating Criteria (effective September 15, 2025). It serves as a specialized analytical framework to assess and integrate climate-related vulnerabilities into sovereign credit ratings. 

While the core [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] relies on macroeconomic and financial metrics, the RCRM addresses non-traditional risks through the **Sovereign Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)**. This module screens for sovereigns with high exposure to physical climate events (e.g., heatwaves, floods) and transition risks (e.g., fossil fuel dependence). The RCRM acts as a trigger for additional qualitative scrutiny, ensuring that material climate risks not captured by standard financial variables are appropriately reflected in the final rating via the [[Qualitative Overlay (QO)]].

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The RCRM operates as a screening and adjustment mechanism rather than a standalone quantitative rating model. Its components include:

- **Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)**: The primary input for the RCRM. This is an aggregate score (0–100) that quantifies a sovereign's exposure to:
    - **Physical Risks**: Projected impacts of heat, drought, storms, floods, and sea-level rise.
    - **Transition Risks**: Economic exposure to fossil fuel dependence and costs associated with green energy transitions.
- **Screening Thresholds**: The module utilizes specific temporal horizons (2025, 2035, 2050) to assess long-term vulnerability. A **Climate.VS score of 50 or higher in 2035** serves as the critical trigger for enhanced analysis.
- **Qualitative Integration**: Once triggered, the RCRM informs the "Other structural factors" component of the Qualitative Overlay. Analysts evaluate whether climate risks are material and distinct from the risks already captured by the [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] variables (such as GDP volatility or commodity dependence).

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Climate.VS Score**: A composite index ranging from 0 to 100. Higher scores indicate greater vulnerability. It aggregates data on physical hazards and transition dependencies.
- **2035 Projection Horizon**: The specific timeframe used to determine if a sovereign requires additional scrutiny. A score ≥50 in this year mandates deeper analysis.
- **Physical Risk Indicators**: Metrics related to heatwaves, droughts, storms, floods, and sea-level rise.
- **Transition Risk Indicators**: Metrics related to fossil fuel revenue dependence and the cost structure of green energy adoption.
- **Materiality Assessment**: A qualitative judgment call on whether the identified climate risks impact fiscal sustainability or macroeconomic stability beyond standard model inputs.

## Application

1. **Initial Screening via Climate.VS**: 
   Calculate or retrieve the Sovereign Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS) for the sovereign, focusing on the 2035 projection. Assess if the score is 50 or higher.

2. **Trigger Enhanced Analysis**: 
   If the 2035 Climate.VS score is ≥50, initiate a detailed review of physical and transition risks. This step identifies specific vulnerabilities (e.g., infrastructure damage from floods, revenue loss from fossil fuel phase-out) that may not be fully captured by standard macroeconomic variables.

3. **Qualitative Overlay Adjustment**: 
   Evaluate the materiality of the identified climate risks. If risks are significant and distinct from existing SRM factors, apply a notch adjustment (up or down) under the "Other structural factors" category within the [[Qualitative Overlay (QO)]]. The adjustment is subject to the overall QO caps (±3 notches total).

4. **Final Rating Determination**: 
   Combine the adjusted SRM output with the climate-informed QO to derive the final sovereign rating. Ensure that the rationale for any climate-driven adjustments is clearly documented in the credit view.
