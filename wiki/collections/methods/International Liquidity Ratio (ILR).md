# International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)

## Overview

The **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)** is a key metric used by [[Fitch Ratings]] within its [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] to assess a sovereign's short-term external liquidity risk. It serves as a critical component of the **External Finances** pillar, helping analysts gauge a country's ability to meet its external debt obligations and manage balance-of-payments shocks. The ILR is particularly significant in determining short-term sovereign ratings, where a robust ILR (>100%) can support a more favorable short-term rating mapping for Foreign-Currency (FC) issuers, alongside reserve-currency flexibility.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The ILR is integrated into the **External Finances** pillar of Fitch’s Sovereign Rating Model. While the SRM relies on an Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression of 18 variables, the ILR acts as a specific liquidity gauge within this framework. 

*   **Quantitative Assessment**: The ILR is calculated as part of the quantitative input for the External Finances pillar (which carries a 17.3% weight in the SRM). It helps determine the baseline score for external strength.
*   **Qualitative Overlay (QO)**: Analysts apply a Qualitative Overlay to adjust for factors not fully captured by the raw ILR figure. This includes assessing:
    *   **External Financing Flexibility**: The resilience of market access and the sovereign's ability to roll over debt.
    *   **Vulnerability to Shocks**: Balance-sheet structures and reliance on remittances or commodity exports.
    *   **External Debt Sustainability**: Trends in net external debt.
*   **Short-Term Rating Mapping**: The ILR is explicitly used in the logic for mapping Long-Term (LT) ratings to Short-Term (ST) ratings. For Foreign-Currency ratings, if a sovereign has reserve-currency flexibility *or* a robust ILR (>100%), Fitch chooses the higher of two mapping options, potentially resulting in a stronger ST rating relative to the LT IDR.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)**: A ratio gauging short-term external liquidity risk. A value greater than 100% is considered robust and is a specific threshold used in short-term rating determinations.
- **FX Reserves**: Official foreign exchange reserves held by the central bank or monetary authority, a primary numerator component in liquidity assessments.
- **Short-Term External Debt**: Debt maturing within one year, including official and private claims, which forms part of the liquidity pressure side of the ratio.
- **Net Foreign Assets**: The net position of the sovereign's external assets and liabilities, used in the broader External Finances analysis.
- **Current Account Balance plus FDI**: A measure of external current flows and investment income, contributing to the overall external finance score.
- **Reserve-Currency Flexibility**: A binary or qualitative indicator based on IMF COFER data, assessing the sovereign's ability to issue debt in its own currency which is widely held as reserves.

## Application

1.  **Calculate Quantitative Inputs**: Compute the ILR using available data on FX reserves, short-term external debt, and other liquidity buffers. This metric is input into the [[Fitch Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] as part of the External Finances pillar calculation.
2.  **Apply Qualitative Overlay (QO)**: Review the ILR in the context of external financing flexibility and shock vulnerability. Adjust the pillar score if the liquidity position is distorted by off-balance-sheet items, contingent liabilities, or structural weaknesses not reflected in the raw ratio. The QO can adjust the pillar score by up to ±2 notches.
3.  **Map to Ratings**: Use the final External Finances score, informed by the ILR, to contribute to the overall Sovereign Rating. Specifically for Short-Term Foreign-Currency ratings, check if the ILR exceeds 100%. If yes, and combined with reserve-currency flexibility considerations, apply the favorable mapping option to determine the final ST rating.
