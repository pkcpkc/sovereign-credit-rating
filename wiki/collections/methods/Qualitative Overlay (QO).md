# Qualitative Overlay (QO)

## Overview

The Qualitative Overlay (QO) is a critical adjustment mechanism within the Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Methodology. It serves to refine the output of the quantitative Sovereign Rating Model (SRM) by incorporating qualitative factors that may not be fully captured by the model's historical data. The QO allows analysts to adjust the initial rating assigned by the SRM, ensuring that the final Issuer Default Rating (IDR) reflects current political, institutional, and structural realities, such as political stability, financial sector health, and geopolitical risks. This overlay is applied to both Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) IDRs, with specific adjustments made based on the sovereign's access to capital markets and currency regime.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The QO operates as a modification layer atop the four analytical pillars of the SRM: Structural Features, Macroeconomic Performance, Public Finances, and External Finances. The SRM provides a quantitative baseline using 18 variables, but the QO introduces expert judgment on factors that are difficult to quantify or are evolving rapidly.

Key components adjusted via the QO include:
- **Political Stability and Institutional Strength**: Assessed through governance indicators (e.g., World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators) and the credibility of policy-making.
- **Financial Sector Health**: Evaluation of banking sector vulnerabilities, contingent liabilities, and the government's ability to support the financial system.
- **Fiscal and External Flexibility**: Analysis of the government's capacity to raise revenues, manage debt sustainably, and access external financing during stress periods.
- **Geopolitical and Climate Risks**: Integration of long-term risks, such as physical and transition climate risks (via the Climate Vulnerability Signal), which may lead to additional scrutiny or negative adjustments.

The QO allows for an adjustment of up to +3 notches (upgrades) or -3 notches (downgrades) from the SRM output, depending on the strength of the qualitative factors.

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Political Stability Score**: Derived from governance indicators and historical political risk; influences the QO adjustment for institutional strength.
- **Financial Sector Vulnerability**: Assessed via banking sector health, non-performing loans, and government exposure to financial institutions.
- **Fiscal Financing Flexibility**: The ability of the government to raise taxes or cut spending without causing a recession; impacts the Public Finances pillar adjustment.
- **External Financing Flexibility**: The ease with which a sovereign can access international capital markets; crucial for FC IDR assessments.
- **Climate Vulnerability Signal (Climate.VS)**: A screening tool scoring physical and transition risks on a 0-100 scale. Sovereigns with a Climate.VS ≥ 50 in 2035 receive additional analytical scrutiny, potentially leading to QO adjustments.
- **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)**: Used for short-term rating assessments; an ILR ≥ 100% may support a positive QO for short-term FC IDRs.
- **Debt Dynamics Model**: A framework used to assess medium-term debt sustainability, informing the QO for Public Finances.

## Application

1. **Calculate SRM Output**: Begin by determining the initial rating using the Sovereign Rating Model (SRM), which utilizes 18 quantitative variables across four pillars (Structural Features, Macroeconomic Performance, Public Finances, External Finances).
2. **Identify Qualitative Adjustments**: Analysts assess factors not fully captured by the SRM, such as political stability, financial sector risks, fiscal flexibility, and external financing resilience. Determine if these factors warrant an upgrade or downgrade.
3. **Apply QO Adjustment**: Adjust the SRM output by up to +3 or -3 notches based on the qualitative assessment. Ensure that adjustments for LC and FC IDRs are distinct, reflecting differences in debt composition and capital market access.
4. **Final Rating Determination**: The adjusted rating, incorporating the QO, becomes the final IDR. This rating is then mapped to specific debt instruments, considering recovery expectations for rated securities.
