# Qualitative Overlay (QO)

## Overview

The **Qualitative Overlay (QO)** is a critical adjustment mechanism within [[Fitch Ratings]]' Sovereign Rating Methodology, effective from September 15, 2025. It serves to adjust the initial rating indication generated by the quantitative [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] to account for factors that are not captured by the statistical regression. The QO ensures that the final Issuer Default Rating (IDR) reflects the full spectrum of credit risks, including geopolitical stability, financial sector health, policy credibility, and emerging risks such as climate vulnerability.

The QO allows analysts to notch the SRM-derived rating up or down based on qualitative assessments across the four analytical pillars: [[Structural Features]], [[Macroeconomic Performance]], [[Public Finances]], and [[External Finances]]. While the SRM provides a statistically grounded baseline, the QO introduces necessary discretion to handle country-specific nuances, crisis situations, and non-quantifiable structural weaknesses or strengths.

## Key Pillars & Analytical Components

The Qualitative Overlay is applied across four primary pillars, with specific adjustment capabilities for each:

1.  **Adjustment Mechanics**:
    *   **Per-Pillar Adjustment**: Analysts can apply a notch adjustment of **+2 to -2** for each of the four pillars based on qualitative factors.
    *   **Total Cap**: The total adjustment from the SRM output is generally capped at **+3 to -3 notches**.
    *   **Exceptions**: Wider adjustments may be permitted in cases of countries in crisis, countries recovering from crisis, or those with a default history within the last five years.

2.  **Structural Features QO**:
    *   Assesses [[Governance indicators]] beyond the baseline World Bank WGI metrics included in the SRM.
    *   Evaluates political stability, geopolitical risks, and the quality of the business environment.
    *   Incorporates financial sector health using the [[Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)]] and [[Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)]].
    *   Considers "Other structural factors," which now explicitly includes material [[Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)]] if risks are not fully captured by other pillars.

3.  **Macroeconomic Performance QO**:
    *   Evaluates the credibility and flexibility of macroeconomic policies.
    *   Specifically assesses fixed exchange rate regimes or dollarized economies for policy flexibility.
    *   Reviews the medium-term GDP growth outlook and macroeconomic stability, including unemployment trends and sectoral imbalances.

4.  **Public Finances QO**:
    *   Assesses [[Fiscal financing flexibility]], including market access depth and debt structure maturity profiles.
    *   Evaluates debt sustainability using the proprietary [[Fitch Debt Dynamics Model (DDM)]].
    *   Analyzes fiscal structure, such as the breadth of the revenue base and budgetary rigidity.
    *   Quantifies potential [[Contingent liabilities]] (e.g., banking sector support, state-owned enterprise debts) and their potential crystallization.

5.  **External Finances QO**:
    *   Assesses [[External financing flexibility]] and resilience of market access.
    *   Evaluates external debt sustainability trends beyond the static net foreign assets metric.
    *   Analyzes vulnerability to shocks, considering balance-sheet structures and reliance on remittances.
    *   Utilizes the [[International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)]] to gauge short-term external liquidity risk.

6.  **Climate Risk Integration**:
    *   The [[Sovereign Climate Risk Model (SCRM)]], specifically the [[Climate.VS]] score, acts as a screening tool.
    *   Sovereigns with a Climate.VS score of 50 or higher in 2035 undergo additional scrutiny.
    *   If physical risks (e.g., floods, heatwaves) or transition risks (e.g., fossil fuel dependence) are material and not captured by the SRM/QO in other pillars, a specific notch adjustment may be applied under "Other structural factors."

## Key Metrics & Variables

- **Notch Adjustment Range**: ±2 notches per pillar, capped at ±3 notches total (standard case).
- **Climate.VS Threshold**: A score of **50 or higher** in the 2035 scenario triggers enhanced analysis and potential QO adjustments.
- **Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)**: Used in the Structural Features QO to assess financial sector stability.
- **Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)**: Used in the Structural Features QO to monitor macro-prudential risks.
- **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)**: Used in the External Finances QO; an ILR >100% supports higher short-term ratings and reduces negative QO pressure on external liquidity.
- **Grace Period**: Typically **30 days** for payment failures to be classified as default, influencing the "willingness" component of the QO.

## Application

1.  **Calculate SRM Baseline**:
    The process begins by calculating the [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] score based on 18 key variables across the four pillars. The SRM generates an initial rating indication calibrated to the Long-Term FC IDR scale ('AAA' to 'CCC+').

2.  **Apply Qualitative Overlays (QO)**:
    Analysts review qualitative factors for each pillar. Adjustments are made based on:
    *   **Structural**: Political risk, financial sector health (BSI/MPI), and climate risk (Climate.VS).
    *   **Macro**: Policy credibility and regime flexibility.
    *   **Public Finances**: Debt sustainability dynamics, fiscal flexibility, and contingent liabilities.
    *   **External Finances**: Market access resilience, liquidity (ILR), and shock vulnerability.
    Each pillar can be adjusted by up to ±2 notches.

3.  **Aggregate and Cap Adjustments**:
    The individual pillar adjustments are aggregated. The total adjustment is capped at ±3 notches from the SRM output unless the sovereign is in a crisis, recovering from crisis, or has defaulted in the last five years.

4.  **Final Rating Determination and Recovery Assessment**:
    The final IDR is determined by applying the net QO adjustment to the SRM score. For issuers rated 'B+' or below, [[Recovery Ratings (RR)]] are assigned to notch instrument ratings up or down based on expected loss severity, reflecting the "willingness" and legal structure aspects not fully captured in the sovereign's capacity to pay.
