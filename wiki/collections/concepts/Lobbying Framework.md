# Lobbying Framework

The **Lobbying Framework** refers to the regulatory structures and transparency mechanisms designed to oversee the influence of interest groups on public policy and decision-making processes. 

## Current Status in Austria
Based on the *EU Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report*, the current lobbying framework in Austria is characterized by several limitations:

*   **Scope and Enforcement:** The existing framework is noted to be limited in its scope, failing to capture a comprehensive range of lobbying activities. Furthermore, it lacks sufficient sanction mechanisms to hold non-compliant actors accountable.
*   **Transparency and Disclosure:** While there is a push for better oversight, significant challenges remain regarding the disclosure of assets and interests, particularly for Members of Parliament.
*   **Revolving Doors:** The framework includes restrictions on "revolving doors"—the movement of individuals between public office and private sector roles. Currently, these restrictions are considered narrow, though recent progress includes the introduction of a three-year cooling-off period for certain constitutional judges.

## Related Regulatory Contexts
The development of the lobbying framework is interconnected with broader governance and integrity initiatives:
*   **[[Anti-Corruption]]**: The framework serves as a pillar of the *National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2023-2025*.
*   **[[Whistleblower Protection]]**: Complements lobbying transparency by providing channels for reporting internal misconduct.
*   **Freedom of Information Act**: Expected to enter into force in September 2025, this act will provide a subjective right to information from public authorities, potentially increasing the transparency of lobbying interactions.
*   **[[Judicial Independence]]**: Ensuring that legislative and administrative decisions influenced by lobbying remain subject to independent judicial oversight.
