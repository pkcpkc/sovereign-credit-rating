# Policy credibility

**Policy credibility** refers to the perceived reliability, consistency, and institutional support behind government actions, legislative frameworks, and regulatory bodies. In the context of sovereign credit analysis and macroeconomic stability, it serves as a critical determinant of investor confidence, legal certainty, and the effective implementation of fiscal and structural reforms. High policy credibility reduces country risk by ensuring that laws are applied consistently, institutions operate independently of political pressure, and citizens and businesses can anticipate stable regulatory environments. Conversely, low credibility—manifested through legislative unpredictability, weak enforcement, or political interference—increases the risk premium required by investors, hampers long-term investment, and threatens fiscal sustainability.

## Definition and Core Components

Policy credibility is not merely about the existence of laws, but the **consistency of their application** and the **strength of the institutions** enforcing them. Key components include:

1.  **Institutional Independence**: The ability of regulatory and judicial bodies (e.g., competition authorities, central banks, anti-corruption agencies) to operate without undue political interference.
2.  **Legislative Stability and Predictability**: The predictability of the legal framework, characterized by coherent law-making processes rather than rapid, ad-hoc changes. This includes avoiding excessive use of emergency decrees and ensuring that public consultations are substantive rather than symbolic.
3.  **Accountability and Transparency**: Robust mechanisms for monitoring government actions, including freedom of the press, access to administrative documents, effective oversight of public officials, and transparent political financing.
4.  **Implementation Capacity**: The practical ability of the state to enforce laws and deliver services, ensuring that legal guarantees translate into real-world outcomes.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Country Risk

Policy credibility directly influences sovereign credit ratings through several channels:

*   **Investment Protection**: Investors require confidence that contracts will be enforced and assets protected by an independent judiciary. Weak policy credibility increases the risk of expropriation or arbitrary legal changes, leading to higher risk premiums.
*   **Fiscal Sustainability**: Credible institutions ensure prudent fiscal management, effective tax collection, and the recovery of misused public funds. Conversely, weak accountability can lead to corruption, revenue leakage, and fiscal unpredictability (e.g., sudden changes in fiscal codes via emergency ordinances).
*   **Macroeconomic Stability**: Stable policy frameworks encourage long-term investment and economic diversification. Rapid legislative changes or political polarization can disrupt business operations, particularly for SMEs, and hinder growth prospects.
*   **External Confidence**: Strong institutional credibility supports access to international capital markets and maintains stable external liquidity profiles.

## Comparative Analysis: Policy Credibility in the EU (2025 Context)

The *2025 Rule of Law Report* highlights divergent trajectories in policy credibility within the European Union, using France, Poland, and Romania as case studies. While all three nations face challenges related to legislative stability and civic engagement, their specific institutional vulnerabilities and threat profiles differ significantly.

### Case Study: France
France demonstrates a mixed but generally strong profile of policy credibility, with specific strengths in institutional independence and notable vulnerabilities in legislative stability.

#### Strengths in Policy Credibility
*   **Independent Regulatory Bodies**: France maintains high-perceived independence among key regulatory agencies, including **ARCOM** (media regulator), the **High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP)**, and the **Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)**. The **French Competition Authority** is also viewed as highly independent, reinforcing confidence in market regulation.
*   **Strong Anti-Corruption Framework**: The reorganization of the AFA and increased resources for the HATVP reflect a credible institutional response to integrity risks. The **National Financial Prosecutor (PNF)** remains a well-capitalized and effective body for combating financial crimes.
*   **Legal Protections for Investors**: Over half of companies express confidence in investment protection by law and courts. The strong rule of law framework supports contract enforcement, a key pillar of sovereign credit strength.
*   **Effective Oversight Mechanisms**: The **Defender of Rights** and the **Court of Audit** provide strong oversight, with tangible results such as the recovery of EUR 6 million from MPs for misused funds.

#### Threats and Vulnerabilities
*   **Perceived Legislative Instability**: The use of **accelerated legislative procedures** and **Article 49-3** of the Constitution (allowing the government to force votes without parliamentary debate) has raised concerns about democratic friction and legal uncertainty. Businesses, particularly SMEs, report high regulatory burden and legal uncertainty due to rapid legislative changes.
*   **Narrowing Civic Space**: The civic space is rated as "narrowed," with civil society organizations facing budgetary restrictions and occasional intimidation. This undermines the feedback loops necessary for responsive and credible policymaking.
*   **Judicial Independence Pressures**: While the judiciary is formally independent, magistrates face significant personal attacks and political pressure, particularly regarding election-related cases.

### Case Study: Poland
Poland’s 2025 context reflects significant efforts to address long-standing rule of law concerns, yet substantial structural weaknesses in policy credibility persist, posing higher risks to institutional trust and investment confidence.

#### Strengths and Positive Developments
*   **Judicial Reform Efforts**: Significant progress has been made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General, a key step toward ensuring functional independence of the prosecution service. Draft laws have been proposed to address the status of judges appointed by the post-2017 National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) and to dismantle the Supreme Court Chamber of Extraordinary Control.
*   **Enhanced Legislative Consultation**: Poland introduced mandatory impact assessments for draft laws and strengthened rules for legislative consultation, aiming to improve legal certainty.
*   **Anti-Corruption Operational Capacity**: The **European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)** is fully operational, and a new Central Office for Combating Corruption is being established. Large-scale corruption cases involving public officials are actively being investigated.
*   **Economic and Fiscal Fundamentals**: Poland benefits from a resilient external position, manageable public debt levels, and a credible inflation-targeting central bank, providing a strong macroeconomic foundation despite institutional strains.

#### Critical Threats to Policy Credibility
*   **Constitutional Crisis and Judicial Legitimacy**: A major threat to policy credibility is the ongoing refusal by the government to publish **Constitutional Tribunal** judgments, asserting the body lacks legitimacy. This creates a constitutional crisis and deepens political polarization, undermining the supreme court's role as a guarantor of legal stability. Legislation amending NCJ and Tribunal laws remains pending, creating regulatory ambiguity.
*   **Weak Anti-Corruption Framework Structure**: Despite operational anti-corruption bodies, Poland lacks a comprehensive national anti-corruption strategy and lobbying rules. Broad immunities for top executives and impunity clauses in legislation hinder high-level accountability, leading to high corruption perceptions.
*   **Media Independence Concerns**: While biased content in public service media has decreased, concerns remain regarding the independence of the media regulator (**KRRiT**) and the governance of public broadcasters, which are currently in liquidation.
*   **Low Investment Confidence**: Companies report low confidence in investment protection due to legislative instability and frequent changes in legislation. Public procurement remains highly vulnerable to corruption, with significant single-bid rates, affecting the efficiency of public spending and investment flows.
*   **Civic Space Constraints**: Although the civic space rating improved from "obstructed" to "narrowed," negative rhetoric against NGOs and operational restrictions persist, weakening the civil society feedback loop essential for credible governance.

### Case Study: Romania
Romania’s 2025 profile shows progress in specific judicial reforms but suffers from acute legislative unpredictability and systemic risks in anti-corruption enforcement, directly impacting its sovereign credit outlook and business environment.

#### Strengths and Positive Developments
*   **Judicial Independence Reforms**: Significant progress was made in completing the process to implement **Venice Commission** recommendations on the Justice Laws. The Ministry of Justice has shown openness to ensuring judicial independence in the reform of the IT system for courts, addressing concerns about centralized data access.
*   **Anti-Corruption Strategy Implementation**: The 2021-2025 National Anti-Corruption Strategy is on track, with a new strategy under preparation. The **National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA)** maintains a stable occupancy rate and continues to secure convictions in high-level cases.
*   **Open Government Initiatives**: The adoption of the **Strategy for Open Government 2025-2030** aims to address challenges for civil society and improve transparency.
*   **Investment Confidence**: Over half of companies express confidence in the effectiveness of investment protection, suggesting that despite institutional flaws, core legal protections remain perceived as viable by some investors.

#### Critical Threats to Policy Credibility
*   **Legislative Unpredictability via Emergency Ordinances**: The frequent use of **Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs)** is a primary concern. In 2024, 199 ordinances were adopted, contributing to significant legislative unpredictability. This volatility is cited by businesses as a major competitive drawback and a threat to long-term planning.
*   **Weaknesses in Public Consultation**: Public consultations are often criticized as ineffective "box-ticking" exercises, undermining the legitimacy and quality of legislation. No progress has been made on introducing lobbying rules for Members of Parliament.
*   **Systemic Risks in Anti-Corruption Enforcement**: Recent case-law by the **High Court of Cassation and Justice** regarding *lex mitior* (application of more lenient laws) has led to the discontinuation of numerous corruption cases and the annulment of convictions due to expired limitation periods. This raises systemic risks regarding impunity and compatibility with EU law, severely damaging the credibility of the justice system.
*   **Erosion of Asset Declaration Transparency**: A Constitutional Court ruling deemed the requirement to declare spouses’ and children’s assets unconstitutional, weakening the **National Integrity Agency (ANI)**’s ability to detect conflicts of interest and illicit wealth.
*   **Media Freedom and Political Influence**: Untransparent political advertising and financing of private media by political parties have increased, affecting media independence. Journalists face harassment and political pressure, and the **National Audiovisual Council (CNA)** lacks sufficient resources to enforce standards effectively.
*   **Civil Society and Human Rights Deficits**: The civic space is "narrowed," with CSOs facing administrative barriers. The accreditation process for National Human Rights Institutions is stalled, and the **Romanian Institute for Human Rights** faces severe budget cuts, risking its functionality.

## Key Institutions Influencing Policy Credibility

### European Union Institutions
*   **European Commission**: Monitors rule of law compliance and issues annual reports, influencing policy credibility through infringement procedures and funding conditions.
*   **Venice Commission**: Provides legal expertise and recommendations on judicial reforms and constitutional matters, serving as a benchmark for credible institutional design.
*   **Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU)**: Adjudicates disputes regarding EU law compliance, including judicial independence and anti-corruption effectiveness cases, affecting national policy credibility.

### France
*   **Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM)**: Defends judicial independence and autonomy, crucial for the impartial application of policy.
*   **High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP)**: Ensures transparency in public life, managing asset declarations and lobbying registers.
*   **Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)**: Promotes integrity and combats corruption in both public and private sectors.
*   **ARCOM**: Independent regulatory authority for media, ensuring pluralism and editorial freedom.
*   **Constitutional Council**: Reviews the constitutionality of laws, acting as a check on legislative excesses.

### Poland
*   **Constitutional Tribunal**: Faces legitimacy crises due to government non-compliance with judgments, significantly eroding policy credibility.
*   **National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ)**: Disputed appointments and lack of independence have undermined trust in the judicial selection process.
*   **Supreme Court**: Structural reforms, including the dismantling of the Chamber of Extraordinary Control, aim to restore independence.
*   **Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (CBA)**: Being dissolved and replaced by a new Central Office for Combating Corruption within the police.
*   **European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)**: Fully operational, enhancing cross-border criminal prosecution capacity.
*   **Ombudsperson**: Plays a key role in checks and balances and protecting citizen rights.

### Romania
*   **High Court of Cassation and Justice (HCCJ)**: Its recent interpretations of statute of limitations have created systemic uncertainty, impacting the credibility of anti-corruption efforts.
*   **National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA)**: Maintains operational effectiveness in prosecuting high-level corruption, providing a counterweight to other institutional weaknesses.
*   **National Integrity Agency (ANI)**: Its capacity to ensure transparency is compromised by legal rulings limiting asset declaration scopes.
*   **Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM)**: Involved in the ongoing implementation of Venice Commission recommendations, a key area of progress for institutional trust.
*   **Constitutional Court of Romania**: Its rulings, such as those on asset declarations and presidential elections, have at times introduced legal voids or controversies that challenge policy stability.
*   **National Audiovisual Council (CNA)**: Lacks sufficient resources to ensure media pluralism and independence, weakening accountability in the media sector.
*   **Court of Accounts**: Provides oversight of public finances, though its effectiveness is part of the broader governance framework.
