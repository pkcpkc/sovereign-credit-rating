# Compliance with court rulings

**Compliance with court rulings** refers to the obligation of public authorities and the executive branch to fully implement and respect final judicial decisions. In the context of sovereign credit analysis, this concept is a critical indicator of **[[Institutional Checks]]** and the stability of the **rule of law**. Weak compliance signals potential executive overreach, undermines the credibility of the legal system, and increases country risk by creating uncertainty around contract enforcement and property rights.

## Synthesis of Context: Belgium (2025)

Based on the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Belgium**, compliance with court rulings remains an area of **limited progress** and persistent structural challenges. The failure to adequately implement final rulings, particularly those from the **[[European Court of Human Rights]] (ECtHR)**, poses specific risks to **[[Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength]]**.

### Key Indicators and Developments

*   **Non-Implementation of ECtHR Judgments**: As of January 1, 2025, Belgium had **17 leading judgments** of the ECtHR pending implementation. This backlog suggests a systemic lag or reluctance in aligning domestic law and administrative practice with international human rights obligations, potentially triggering reputational costs and international supervision.
*   **Structural Deficiencies and Supervision**: The Belgian Government remains under supervision by the **Council of Europe** (Committee of Ministers) regarding structural issues identified in the case of *Camara v. Belgium*. Insufficient action by the Government to address these identified structural flaws indicates a weakness in the feedback loop between judicial review and executive reform.
*   **Judicial Pushback**: In response to perceived non-compliance, the three highest courts in Belgium (including the **[[Court of Cassation]]** and **[[Constitutional Court]]**) issued a **joint memorandum** in July 2024. This document explicitly required the Government to respect court judgments, highlighting a tension between the executive and the judiciary. Such institutional conflict can signal political instability and undermine the perceived **[[Judicial Independence]]**.
*   **Legislative vs. Executive Action**: While the Constitutional Court has issued rulings on urgent court orders and strikes (November 2024) and annulled specific decree provisions (September 2024), the speed and thoroughness of subsequent executive implementation remain a concern. The lack of an automatic enforcement mechanism or robust monitoring body for administrative compliance contributes to these delays.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Country Risk

The status of compliance with court rulings directly influences several **[[Sovereign Credit Rating Factors]]**:

1.  **Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength**:
    *   **Threat**: Persistent non-compliance with domestic and international court rulings erodes trust in the state's ability to adhere to its own legal framework. This is categorized as a threat to policy credibility, as it suggests that political considerations may override legal obligations.
    *   **Risk**: Investors may perceive higher legal and regulatory risk, knowing that favorable judicial outcomes for private entities might not be enforced by the state.

2.  **Accountability**:
    *   **Threat**: The lack of effective monitoring mechanisms for the enforcement of judgments against public authorities weakens accountability. The reliance on judicial self-defense (e.g., joint memoranda) rather than executive self-correction indicates a fragile accountability structure.

3.  **Geopolitical and Political Stability**:
    *   **Implication**: Tensions between the judiciary and the executive branch, evidenced by the joint memorandum and public criticism of the judiciary, can exacerbate political fragmentation. This instability can lead to policy paralysis or erratic decision-making, negatively impacting long-term fiscal sustainability.

## Related Concepts

*   **[[Judicial Independence]]**: Compliance is a functional outcome of an independent judiciary; without it, judicial independence is symbolic.
*   **[[Rule of Law]]**: A core pillar of the rule of law is the binding nature of final decisions.
*   **[[European Court of Human Rights]]**: The primary international body whose judgments highlight compliance failures in Belgium.
*   **[[Council of Europe]]**: The supervisory body monitoring the implementation of ECtHR judgments.
