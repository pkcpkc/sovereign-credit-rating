# Carbon Rate

The **Carbon Rate** is a metric used to assess the intensity of carbon emissions relative to economic activity, energy consumption, or specific industrial outputs. It serves as a critical indicator for measuring progress toward decarbonization goals and climate resilience.

## Contextual Analysis
In the context of regional economic reporting (e.g., Malta's 2025 Country Report), the Carbon Rate is intrinsically linked to several sustainability hurdles:

- **Energy Mix**: High carbon rates are often driven by a low uptake of renewable energy. For instance, economies with renewables accounting for less than 20% of the electricity mix face significantly higher carbon intensities.
- **Fossil Fuel Subsidies**: High rates of fossil fuel subsidies (e.g., 1.6% of GDP) can artificially maintain high carbon rates by making carbon-intensive energy cheaper than green alternatives.
- **Transport Sector**: A major driver of carbon rates is road transport. High rates are observed in regions where the percentage of vehicles using alternative fuels remains low (e.g., under 3%).
- **Decarbonization Targets**: Addressing the Carbon Rate requires aggressive structural reforms, particularly in transitioning the transport sector and reducing reliance on fossil fuels to meet [[Sustainable Development Goals]].
