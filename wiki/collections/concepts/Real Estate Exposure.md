# Real Estate Exposure

Real Estate Exposure refers to the concentration of financial assets and lending activities within the property sector, which can pose systemic risks to a nation's financial stability if not properly managed. In the context of Malta's economy, this exposure is a critical metric for [[Macroprudential Policy]] and overall financial health.

## Current Status and Risks
Based on the IMF 2025 Malta Country Report, the banking sector faces significant concentration risks:
*   **Portfolio Concentration:** Banks hold a substantial portion of their loan portfolios—approximately 70%—dedicated to the real estate sector.
*   **Systemic Risk:** Due to this high level of concentration, the real estate market is identified as a primary area for potential systemic instability.

## Policy Recommendations and Mitigation
To manage these risks, the following measures have been proposed:
*   **Sectoral Systemic Risk Buffer (sSyRB):** The IMF recommends raising the sSyRB to account for the high volume of real estate loans.
*   **Scope Expansion:** Policy adjustments are suggested to broaden the scope of these buffers to include both construction and commercial real estate, rather than focusing solely on residential properties.
*   **Financial Stability:** These measures are intended to complement Malta's strong banking fundamentals, which currently feature high Tier 1 capital ratios (~21%) and low non-performing loan (NPL) ratios (~2%).
