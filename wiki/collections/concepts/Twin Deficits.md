# Twin Deficits

The **Twin Deficits** refers to the simultaneous occurrence of a government's **fiscal deficit** (where government spending exceeds tax revenue) and a country's **current account deficit** (where the value of goods and services imported exceeds the value of those exported). In macroeconomic theory, these two deficits are often linked because government budget shortfalls can drive up domestic demand, which in turn increases the demand for imports, thereby widening the current account deficit.

## Contextual Analysis: Romania (2025)
In the context of the IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation for Romania, the "twin deficits" represent a primary structural challenge for the nation's economic stability. The IMF emphasizes that addressing these deficits is critical for long-term sustainability.

### Fiscal Component
The fiscal deficit in Romania has necessitated a significant **Fiscal Consolidation** package. Key measures identified to address this side of the twin deficits include:
*   **Tax Reforms:** Implementation of VAT rate increases (to a 21% standard).
*   **Spending Controls:** A freeze on public sector wages and pensions starting in 2026.
*   **Debt Management:** Targets to reduce the fiscal deficit to below 6% of GDP in 2026, with a long-term goal of sub-3% by 2030.

### Current Account Component
The current account deficit is influenced by external trade dynamics and domestic consumption. To mitigate this, the IMF suggests:
*   **Exchange Rate Flexibility:** A gradual move toward increased two-way exchange rate flexibility to improve international competitiveness and build resilience against external shocks.
*   **Structural Improvements:** Addressing administrative bottlenecks to better absorb **Next Generation EU (NGEU)** funds, which can support long-term growth and balance trade.
