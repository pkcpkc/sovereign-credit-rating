# Twin Deficits

The **Twin Deficits** refers to a macroeconomic phenomenon where a country simultaneously experiences a substantial **fiscal deficit** (government spending exceeding revenue) and a significant **current account deficit** (a country importing more goods and services than it exports). 

## Contextual Analysis: Romania (2024-2025)
In the context of the IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation for Romania, the twin deficits represent a primary concern for macroeconomic sustainability:

*   **Fiscal Deficit:** Romania has faced persistent high fiscal deficits, necessitating a large reform package in mid-2025 (including VAT hikes and excise duty adjustments) to aim for a deficit below 6% of GDP by 2026.
*   **Current Account Deficit:** The current account deficit reached 8.2% of GDP in 2024, largely driven by weak export performance and high import demand.

## Economic Implications & Drivers
The coexistence of these deficits can create a cycle of economic instability:
*   **External Imbalances:** High current account deficits often lead to a reliance on foreign capital inflows to finance the gap.
*   **Exchange Rate Pressure:** In Romania, this is compounded by a significant [[REER (Real Effective Exchange Rate)]] overvaluation, which the IMF suggests requires increased exchange rate flexibility to build resilience.
*   **Debt Sustainability:** Persistent fiscal deficits contribute to sovereign stress, requiring aggressive [[Fiscal Consolidation]] to stabilize public debt toward a target of 60% of GDP by 2030.
