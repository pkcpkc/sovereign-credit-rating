# Countercyclical Buffer (CCyB)

The **Countercyclical Buffer (CCyB)** is a macroprudential policy tool designed to ensure the stability of the banking system by requiring financial institutions to build up capital reserves during periods of high credit growth and economic expansion. These buffers are intended to be "drawn down" during economic downturns, providing a cushion that allows banks to continue lending when credit demand is most needed.

### Role in Financial Stability
In the context of the Polish financial sector, as analyzed in the [[IMF (2025) Poland Country Report - Article IV Consultation]], the banking system is characterized by high capital and liquidity buffers. The CCyB serves as a critical mechanism to:
- **Mitigate Systemic Risk:** By curbing excessive credit expansion during booms.
- **Support Lending during Recessions:** By providing a source of internal capital to maintain liquidity.
- **Ensure Resilience:** Maintaining the integrity of the banking system against shocks in domestic demand or external economic pressures.

### Contextual Application
While the Polish banking system is currently noted for its resilience, the IMF highlights the importance of maintaining these buffers while addressing specific regulatory nuances. This includes recommendations to:
- Eliminate temporary measures like the mortgage credit holiday (ending 2024).
- Redesign the bank asset tax to remove distortions against private credit, ensuring that capital requirements and buffers remain effective for supporting sustainable economic growth.
