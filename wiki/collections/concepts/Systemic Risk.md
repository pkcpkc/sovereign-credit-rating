# Systemic Risk

Systemic risk refers to the possibility that an event at the micro level (such as the failure of a single financial institution or a specific asset class) could trigger a cascading failure across the entire financial system or economy.

## Contextual Analysis: Austria (2025)
In the context of the Austrian economy as analyzed by the IMF in 2025, systemic risk is currently monitored through several specific lenses:

*   **Commercial Real Estate (CRE) Vulnerability:** A primary concern for financial stability is the rising level of Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) within the commercial real estate sector. While the banking sector maintains robust capital ratios (CET1 at 17.7%), localized distress in CRE can propagate through the financial system if not managed via [[Macroprudential Policy]].
*   **Fiscal and Demographic Pressures:** Systemic stability is also linked to long-term structural risks, including:
    *   **Demographic Headwinds:** Aging populations and shrinking labor supplies create long-term fiscal pressures that require "bold and well-designed" [[Fiscal Consolidation]].
    *   **Productivity Stagnation:** Sluggish productivity growth poses a systemic threat to long-term economic dynamism and the sustainability of social expenditures.
*   **External and Regulatory Factors:** The stability of the Austrian financial system is further supported by a strong external position (current account surplus) and ongoing efforts to strengthen Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorist Financing (CFT) frameworks to mitigate corruption-related risks.
