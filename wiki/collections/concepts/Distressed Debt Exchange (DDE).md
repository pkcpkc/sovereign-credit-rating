# Distressed Debt Exchange (DDE)

A **Distressed Debt Exchange (DDE)** is a formal mechanism used by sovereign entities to restructure their existing debt obligations, typically when the issuer is facing significant financial distress or an inability to meet original repayment terms. 

Within the framework of the **Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Criteria (Sept 2025)**, the execution of a Distressed Debt Exchange is a critical trigger for credit status reassessment. Specifically:

- **Rating Impact:** The execution of a DDE is a formal event that results in a sovereign being moved to **'RD' (Restructured/Default)** status.
- **Contextual Framework:** DDEs are analyzed as part of the broader **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)** and **Qualitative Overlay (QO)**, which evaluate the sustainability of public finances and external debt burdens.
- **Related Protocols:** DDEs are categorized alongside other default indicators, such as missed payments and forced redenomination, though they are distinguished from temporary operational interruptions (which may be covered under Payment Force Majeure).

### Key Relationships
- **Issuer Default Rating (IDR):** DDEs directly influence the IDR by potentially triggering a move to the 'RD' category.
- **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM):** Provides the quantitative baseline that determines a sovereign's vulnerability to requiring a DDE.
- **Public Finances:** The underlying pillar of the SRM that most frequently drives the necessity for a debt exchange.
