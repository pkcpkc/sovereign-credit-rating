# external position

The **external position** of a country refers to its net worth relative to the rest of the world, encompassing its current account balance, net international investment position (NIIP), and the valuation of its exchange rate. In the context of sovereign credit analysis and macroeconomic stability, the external position serves as a critical indicator of a nation's ability to service foreign debt, absorb external shocks, and maintain competitiveness in global trade.

## Assessment of Belgium’s External Position (2025)

According to the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation** for Belgium, the country’s external position presents a mixed profile characterized by a strong stock of external assets but weaknesses in flow balances and competitiveness metrics.

### 1. External Sector Assessment and Exchange Rate
*   **Misalignment**: The IMF assessed Belgium’s external position as **weaker than implied by fundamentals and desirable policies** in 2024.
*   **REER Overvaluation**: The Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) was estimated to be overvalued by between **4.9% and 17.4%**. This overvaluation suggests that Belgian goods and services are relatively more expensive compared to trading partners, potentially eroding competitiveness.
*   **Drivers of Overvaluation**: The overvaluation is linked to structural issues such as sluggish labor productivity growth and declining labor-cost competitiveness. The persistence of automatic wage indexation contributes to rigidities that prevent the real exchange rate from adjusting efficiently to external shocks.

### 2. Current Account Dynamics
*   **Deficit Trend**: Belgium recorded a **current account deficit**, although it showed signs of improvement by narrowing to **0.3% of GDP** in 2024.
*   **Structural Concerns**: While the deficit is modest in absolute terms, its persistence alongside REER overvaluation indicates underlying competitiveness challenges. The deficit reflects the gap between national saving and investment, exacerbated by high household and corporate consumption relative to productive investment yields.

### 3. Net International Investment Position (NIIP)
*   **Asset Strength**: Despite the current account deficit, Belgium maintains a significantly positive **Net International Investment Position (NIIP)**, estimated at **57.3% of GDP**.
*   **Risk Mitigation**: This large net external asset position acts as a crucial buffer against external vulnerabilities. It indicates that Belgium is a net creditor to the rest of the world, providing resilience against sudden stops in capital flows or adverse terms of trade shocks.
*   **Portfolio Composition**: The external asset strength is partly driven by large positive net portfolio investment positions, which help offset the liabilities associated with the current account deficit.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Fiscal Sustainability

The external position interacts directly with fiscal sustainability and sovereign credit risk through several channels:

*   **Debt Servicing Capacity**: A positive NIIP enhances a country's external liquidity profile. For Belgium, the strong external asset base mitigates some risks associated with high public debt (104.1% of GDP in 2024). It reassures markets that the country has sufficient external resources to service debt obligations, particularly given that a significant share of sovereign debt is held by domestic investors but denominated in local currency (Euro).
*   **Exchange Rate Pass-Through**: Since Belgium is part of the **Eurozone**, it does not have an independent nominal exchange rate. Therefore, REER overvaluation can only be corrected through internal devaluation (wage and price moderation) rather than currency depreciation. This places additional pressure on **fiscal consolidation** and **labor market reforms** to restore competitiveness without the shock of a currency crisis.
*   **Investor Confidence**: The assessment of a "weaker than implied" external position signals potential medium-term risks. However, the substantial NIIP buffer limits the immediate threat to sovereign credit ratings. Rating agencies view the large net external assets as a strength that offsets the fiscal vulnerabilities, contributing to the "moderate" overall sovereign stress risk assessment.

## Policy Recommendations for Improvement

To strengthen the external position and align it with fundamentals, the IMF and relevant stakeholders have outlined key policy directions:

1.  **Labor Market Reforms**: Addressing the root causes of REER overvaluation requires enhancing labor productivity and flexibility. Specifically, reforms to the **wage indexation system** and the **1996 wage law** are recommended to improve unit labor cost competitiveness.
2.  **Productivity Enhancements**: Deepening product market reforms to foster innovation and efficiency can help narrow the productivity gap with peer countries, thereby supporting a more balanced external position.
3.  **Fiscal Coordination**: Ensuring that fiscal consolidation does not inadvertently worsen competitiveness. Rationalizing current spending while preserving public investment in areas that boost long-term productivity (education, infrastructure) is essential.
