# Investment-to-GDP Ratio

The **Investment-to-GDP Ratio** is a key macroeconomic indicator that measures the proportion of a country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) that is spent on investment. It serves as a primary metric for assessing the pace of economic development, industrial modernization, and long-term productivity growth.

## Role in Economic Development
A high investment-to-GDP ratio typically indicates a robust capacity for capital formation, which is essential for:
*   **Infrastructure Development:** Funding for transportation, energy grids, and digital networks.
*   **Innovation:** Supporting [[R&D Intensity]] and the transition to a high-tech economy.
*   **Capacity Expansion:** Increasing the productive capacity of the economy to sustain [[GDP Growth]].

## Contextual Analysis: Hungary (2025 Report)
In the context of the 2025 Country Report for Hungary, the investment landscape is characterized by several structural challenges and specific dynamics:

*   **Innovation Gaps:** While investment is critical for growth, Hungary's [[R&D Intensity]] remains well below the EU average (1.4% vs 2.2%), suggesting that a significant portion of investment may not be directed toward high-value innovation or technological advancement.
*   **Market Distortions:** The report highlights that state intervention and the use of subsidized loans can distort private investment flows, potentially impacting the efficiency of the investment-to-GDP ratio.
*   **Financing Constraints:** A drop in venture capital investment and an underdeveloped stock market capitalisation pose risks to the diversification of investment sources, which are necessary to maintain a healthy ratio.
*   **Fiscal Constraints:** High public debt (73.5% of GDP) and elevated debt-servicing costs may limit the government's ability to provide public investment, shifting the burden toward private capital which currently faces a volatile regulatory environment.
