# Lender of Last Resort

A **Lender of Last Resort (LLR)** is a central banking function and a fundamental pillar of monetary policy and financial stability. The LLR serves as a provider of short-term liquidity to financial institutions (such as commercial banks or other systemic entities) that are experiencing a temporary shortage of funds but are otherwise solvent.

## Role in Monetary Policy and Credit Ratings
In the context of sovereign and monetary authority assessments (e.g., S&P Global Ratings), the **Lender of Last Resort** capability is a critical component of the **Monetary Assessment** pillar. It is used to evaluate:
*   **Policy Credibility:** The ability of a monetary authority to maintain price stability and provide a backstop for the financial system.
*   **Systemic Stability:** The capacity to prevent "runs" on banks or systemic collapses during periods of acute liquidity stress.
*   **Sovereign Creditworthiness:** A credible LLR capability can mitigate risks associated with banking sector instability, influencing the overall credit rating of a nation.

## Key Characteristics
*   **Liquidity Provision:** The primary goal is to provide liquidity, not to bail out insolvent institutions.
*   **Emergency Nature:** It is typically invoked during periods of market stress when private markets are unable to provide sufficient funding.
*   **Relationship to Sovereign Debt:** The ability of a government to act as an LLR is often intertwined with its **Fiscal Policy** and its status as a **Reserve Currency** issuer, as the central bank must have the mandate and resources to act as the ultimate guarantor of last resort.
