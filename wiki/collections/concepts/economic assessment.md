# Economic assessment

**Economic Assessment** is a core pillar within the [[S&P Sovereign Rating Methodology]] used by [[S&P Global Ratings]] to evaluate the creditworthiness of sovereign governments. It serves as a fundamental component in determining the "indicative rating level" by analyzing a country's economic structure, growth potential, and capacity to withstand shocks. Alongside [[institutional assessment]], [[external assessment]], [[fiscal assessment]], and [[monetary assessment]], the economic assessment is scored on a scale of 1 to 6, where a lower score indicates stronger economic fundamentals and a higher score indicates greater economic risk.

The assessment primarily addresses questions regarding the stability of the tax base and the economy's ability to mitigate volatility through future growth. It reflects the **structural strength** of a sovereign's economy, distinguishing itself from fiscal metrics that can be managed through short-term austerity or borrowing, as economic fundamentals such as GDP per capita and diversity evolve over longer horizons.

## Core Components

According to the 2017 update of the methodology, the Economic Assessment primarily focuses on three key determinants:

1.  **GDP Per Capita**: This metric serves as a primary proxy for economic development and the absolute size of the economy. Higher GDP per capita generally correlates with greater economic resilience, a broader tax base, and stronger institutional development, contributing to a more favorable (lower) assessment score. It indicates the depth of the funding base available to the sovereign.
2.  **Growth Prospects**: Analysts evaluate the medium- and long-term growth potential of the economy. Sustainable growth enhances the ability of the government to service debt through expanding revenue bases. Factors influencing this include demographic trends, productivity growth, and investment levels. This component answers how future growth expands the tax base and mitigates volatility.
3.  **Economic Diversity**: This measures the resilience of the economy to external and internal shocks. Economies with diverse export bases, varied industrial sectors, and balanced regional development are considered less vulnerable to sector-specific downturns or commodity price fluctuations. A lack of diversity (e.g., heavy reliance on a single commodity export) increases the assessment score due to heightened volatility risk.

## Methodological Context and Integration

### Role in the Five-Pillar Framework
The Economic Assessment does not operate in isolation. It is integrated into a standardized framework that combines five pillars:
*   [[institutional assessment]]
*   **Economic Assessment**
*   [[external assessment]]
*   [[fiscal assessment]]
*   [[monetary assessment]]

Each pillar is scored independently, and these scores are combined in a matrix to derive an indicative rating. The Economic Assessment provides critical context for the [[fiscal assessment]] by establishing the underlying capacity of the economy to generate the revenues necessary for debt service. For instance, strong economic growth prospects may offset a higher current debt burden, whereas weak growth may exacerbate fiscal vulnerabilities.

### Interplay with Other Assessments
*   **Institutional Link**: Economic performance is often a function of institutional quality. Effective policymaking and transparency (elements of the [[institutional assessment]]) foster an environment conducive to sustainable economic growth. Conversely, poor economic outcomes can strain institutions and lead to political instability.
*   **External Link**: Economic structure influences the [[external assessment]]. Countries with economies dependent on exports of goods priced in foreign currencies face different external liquidity risks compared to those with service-based or reserve-currency economies.
*   **Monetary Link**: The credibility of monetary policy (part of the [[monetary assessment]]) is essential for maintaining price stability, which is a prerequisite for sustainable economic growth.

### Analyst Discretion and Replicability
While the Economic Assessment follows structured criteria based on quantitative and qualitative factors, the broader S&P methodology allows for significant **analyst discretion**. As highlighted in recent academic reviews (e.g., Stomper, 2024), the determination of growth prospects and the interpretation of economic diversity can involve subjective judgment. This discretion contributes to the argument that the rating process is not fully replicable, necessitating careful documentation of how economic fundamentals are weighted against other pillars.

## Significance for Sovereign Credit Ratings

The Economic Assessment is significant because it reflects the **structural strength** of a sovereign's economy.

*   **Risk Mitigation**: A strong Economic Assessment can provide a buffer against temporary fiscal or external setbacks, potentially limiting negative rating adjustments.
*   **Growth vs. Debt Sustainability**: The assessment helps analysts determine if a country's growth trajectory is sufficient to naturally reduce debt-to-GDP ratios over time, a key factor in [[debt sustainability]] analysis.
*   **Shock Absorption**: Economic diversity and high income levels indicate a greater capacity to absorb negative shocks without requiring external financial assistance or imposing severe austerity measures that could trigger social or political unrest.
*   **Tax Base Expansion**: By evaluating growth prospects and GDP per capita, the assessment directly informs the viability of the sovereign's tax base, which is the primary source of revenue for debt service.
