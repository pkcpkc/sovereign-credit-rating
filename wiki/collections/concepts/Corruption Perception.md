# Corruption Perception

Corruption perception refers to the assessment of the prevalence of corruption in a country, derived from official data and expert opinions, serving as a critical indicator for sovereign risk, institutional quality, and investment climate. In the context of the 2025 Rule of Law Report for Italy, corruption perception is evaluated through the efficacy of the **[[Anti-Corruption Framework]]**, specifically focusing on the National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC), judicial independence, and the integrity of public procurement.

## Core Components and Institutional Framework

The perception of corruption in Italy is shaped by the interplay between legislative actions, institutional capacity, and persistent structural vulnerabilities. Key components include:

### 1. National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC)
The **[[National Anti-Corruption Authority]]** plays a central role in shaping corruption perceptions. Under the leadership of **[[Giuseppe Busia]]**, ANAC has strengthened its human resources and issued critical guidelines on:
- **Revolving Doors:** Managing post-office employment restrictions for public officials to prevent conflicts of interest.
- **Public Procurement Risk:** Issuing guidelines to counter infiltration by organized crime in the public procurement sector.
- **National Anti-Corruption Plan:** Updated in early 2025, this plan outlines strategic actions to enhance transparency and integrity.

### 2. Judicial Independence and Accountability
Corruption perception is deeply linked to the efficiency and integrity of the judicial system. The **[[Judicial Independence]]** of Italy has been reinforced by comprehensive reforms implemented in 2024-2025, including:
- Directives on out-of-tenure appointments for magistrates.
- Performance appraisals and judicial management codes approved by the **[[High Council for the Judiciary]]**.
- High clearance rates across civil, criminal, administrative, and tax jurisdictions, indicating a reduced backlog and improved case resolution.

However, concerns persist regarding the high rate of convictions suspended in corruption cases, which negatively impacts the perception of judicial effectiveness.

### 3. Public Procurement and Organized Crime
Public procurement remains a high-risk sector for corruption perception due to the potential for infiltration by organized crime. While the digitalization of the public procurement platform has provided safeguards, the risk remains significant. ANAC’s active role in monitoring these risks is a key strength in mitigating negative perceptions.

### 4. Political Financing and Conflicts of Interest
A major gap in Italy’s anti-corruption framework, affecting corruption perception, is the lack of comprehensive rules on:
- **Conflicts of Interest:** No comprehensive rules exist for political office holders.
- **Political Party Financing:** Draft laws on lobbying and party financing have stalled in Parliament. The practice of channeling donations through political foundations continues to raise concerns about transparency and influence peddling.
- **Defamation Law:** Reform efforts have not advanced, leaving journalists vulnerable to legal harassment (SLAPPs) and potentially discouraging the exposure of corrupt practices.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Rating and Country Risk

Corruption perception is a direct input into sovereign credit rating factors, influencing Italy’s risk profile as analyzed in the 2025 Rule of Law Report.

### Threats (Negative Impact on Perception)
- **Persistent Staff Shortages:** Shortages in judicial and administrative staff across all court levels hinder the effective enforcement of anti-corruption laws.
- **Legal Uncertainty:** The lack of concrete measures to establish a **[[National Human Rights Institution]]** (in line with UN Paris Principles) signals institutional gaps.
- **Arbitrary Application of Law:** Stakeholder concerns over the precision of the Security Law suggest risks of arbitrary application, which can undermine the rule of law and increase perceived country risk.
- **Suspended Convictions:** The high rate of suspended convictions in corruption cases undermines public trust and the deterrent effect of anti-corruption measures.

### Strengths (Positive Impact on Perception)
- **Institutional Reform:** The comprehensive reform of the justice system, with implementing legislation and the active role of the **[[High Council for the Judiciary]]**, demonstrates a commitment to institutional integrity.
- **ANAC’s Expanded Mandate:** Strengthening ANAC’s resources and issuing guidelines on revolving doors and public procurement risks are viewed as positive steps by international stakeholders.
- **Digitalization:** The digitalized public procurement platform is cited as a strength in counteracting organized crime infiltration.
- **Media Regulation:** The independence and effectiveness of the media regulator (**[[AGCOM]]**) contribute to a more transparent information environment, indirectly supporting anti-corruption efforts by enabling media oversight.

## Conclusion

Corruption perception in Italy is characterized by a dichotomy between significant structural reforms and persistent institutional gaps. While the **[[National Anti-Corruption Authority]]** and judicial reforms have strengthened the framework for integrity, the lack of comprehensive rules on political financing and conflicts of interest, coupled with judicial staff shortages and suspended convictions, continues to pose risks. The stability of sovereign credit ratings depends on the continued implementation of these reforms and the closure of identified institutional gaps, particularly in political transparency and judicial efficiency.
