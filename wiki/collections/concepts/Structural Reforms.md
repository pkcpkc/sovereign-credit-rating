# Structural Reforms

Structural reforms refer to fundamental changes to the institutional, regulatory, and economic framework of a country designed to enhance long-term growth, improve productivity, and ensure macroeconomic stability. In the context of the [[IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Hungary]], these reforms are identified as critical for addressing stagnant output and overcoming systemic distortions.

## Key Pillars of Reform

### Productivity & Competition
A primary objective of structural reform is to close the productivity gap relative to regional peers. Key actions include:
- **Factor Mobility:** Removing barriers that prevent the efficient movement of labor and capital.
- **Firm Dynamism:** Fostering a competitive environment that encourages innovation and business growth.
- **Regulatory Alignment:** Addressing regulatory distortions caused by heavy state presence.

### Governance & Rule of Law
The IMF emphasizes that structural integrity is tied to institutional quality. Reforms in this area are essential for:
- **EU Funding:** Improving governance to unlock suspended European Union funds.
- **Business Environment:** Addressing corruption and rule-of-law concerns to create a predictable environment for private investment.

### Energy & Fiscal Rationalization
Structural shifts also involve transitioning the economic base and spending profile:
- **Energy Transition:** Diversifying energy supply and expanding renewables while coordinating the phase-out of fossil fuel subsidies to bolster competitiveness.
- **Subsidy Reform:** Phasing out retail energy subsidies and housing-related fiscal incentives to reduce market distortions.
- **Spending Rationalization:** Reducing administrative overhead and addressing long-term pressures in pension and healthcare spending.
