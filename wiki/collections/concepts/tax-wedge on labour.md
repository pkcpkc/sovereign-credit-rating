# tax-wedge on labour

The **tax-wedge on labour** refers to the difference between the total labour costs borne by employers (wages plus social security contributions) and the take-home pay received by employees (after income taxes and social security deductions). A high tax wedge represents a significant barrier to formal employment and economic dynamism, acting as a primary drag on competitiveness and labour market participation.

## Definition and Macroeconomic Impact

In the context of sovereign credit analysis and macroeconomic policy, the tax wedge is a critical metric for assessing:
- **Labour Market Efficiency**: High wedges create disincentives for both hiring (by increasing employer costs) and working (by reducing net returns on labour).
- **Fiscal Sustainability**: Heavy reliance on labour taxation constrains the tax base, particularly when combined with demographic ageing and low activity rates.
- **Competitiveness**: Elevated labour costs, relative to productivity, erode international competitiveness, especially in open economies.

## Case Study: Belgium (2025 Country Report)

According to the **European Commission's 2025 Country Report**, Belgium faces one of the highest tax burdens on labour in the European Union. This structural weakness is identified as a key threat to economic strength and growth prospects.

### Key Characteristics in Belgium
- **High Burden**: The tax wedge acts as a significant drag on formal economic activity. The burden is exacerbated by a complex tax structure and a wide range of non-cash benefits, which complicate the net return calculation for workers.
- **Disincentives to Work**: The high wedge, combined with generous and often unlimited unemployment benefits (prior to recent reforms), creates substantial traps that discourage labour force entry and retention. This contributes to Belgium's low activity rate (77.2%) compared to the EU average (83.3%), despite a relatively high employment rate among those already active (72.3%).
- **Competitiveness Erosion**: The high labour cost structure, coupled with automatic wage indexation (which links wage increases to inflation), leads to persistent competitiveness losses. The real effective exchange rate remains high, hindering export performance.

### Policy Responses and Reforms
The Belgian federal coalition agreement and the **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** submitted in 2025 outline specific measures to address the tax wedge:
1. **Labour Tax Cuts**: Planned reductions in income taxes and social security contributions for workers, aimed at improving work incentives and boosting participation.
2. **Financing Mechanism**: These labour tax cuts are intended to be financed by shifting the tax burden towards capital gains and property taxes, thereby broadening the revenue base while reducing the drag on labour.
3. **Benefit Reform**: Introduction of stricter conditions for unemployment benefits, such as a two-year limit for individuals under 55 and linking benefit amounts to prior income, to strengthen the link between work and income.

## Sovereign Credit Rating Implications

The tax-wedge on labour influences sovereign credit ratings through several channels:
- **Growth Potential**: Persistent high wedges suppress potential output by discouraging labour supply and formalization. Weak growth prospects constrain the denominator of debt-to-GDP ratios.
- **Fiscal Flexibility**: Over-reliance on labour income tax limits the government's ability to adjust fiscal policy during downturns without further discouraging work. Diversifying the tax base (e.g., towards property or capital) enhances fiscal resilience.
- **Structural Reform Credibility**: Successful implementation of tax shifts from labour to other bases demonstrates policy credibility and commitment to structural reforms, which is positively viewed by rating agencies. Conversely, failure to implement these reforms due to political fragmentation or institutional weaknesses (such as lack of coordination between federal and regional authorities) poses a downside risk to ratings.
