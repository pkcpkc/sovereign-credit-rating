# Circular Economy

The **Circular Economy** is an economic model aimed at minimizing waste and maximizing the lifecycle of resources by transitioning from a "take-make-dispose" linear system to one based on recycling, reusing, and refurbishing.

## Contextual Applications
In the context of European economic reports (e.g., Austria 2025, Belgium 2025), the Circular Economy is identified as a key pillar for achieving long-term sustainability and industrial resilience. It is closely linked to the following strategic goals:

*   **Decarbonisation:** Integrating circular principles is essential for reducing the carbon footprint of manufacturing and energy-intensive sectors.
*   **Innovation:** Transitioning to a circular model requires significant R&D investment to develop "breakthrough" technologies for material recovery, product longevity, and **eco-design**.
*   **Industrial Policy:** Addressing industrial decarbonisation—particularly in sectors contributing significantly to emissions—requires moving toward circular production cycles to mitigate environmental impact while maintaining competitiveness.

## Regional Performance & Challenges
Recent data highlights varying levels of implementation across the EU:
*   **Belgium:** Demonstrates a strong performance in circularity with a **19.7% rate**. However, significant investment is still required to close gaps in **waste management** and the integration of eco-design principles into production.
*   **Strategic Alignment:** Circularity is viewed as a primary lever for meeting 2030 climate targets and improving resource efficiency in the face of high energy costs.
