# GDP Growth

GDP Growth represents the annual percentage rate of increase in the inflation-adjusted value of all goods and services produced by an economy. It serves as a primary indicator of economic health, reflecting the expansion or contraction of economic activity.

## Current Context: Hungary (2024-2026)
Recent data from the **2025 Country Report - Hungary** highlights a period of slow economic recovery characterized by significant structural vulnerabilities. 

### Growth Projections
*   **2024 Performance:** GDP grew by a modest 0.5%.
*   **2025 Forecast:** Growth is projected to reach 0.8%.
*   **2026 Forecast:** A significant acceleration is expected, with growth forecasted to reach 2.5%.

### Influencing Factors & Constraints
The trajectory of [[GDP Growth]] in Hungary is currently influenced by several critical macroeconomic and structural factors:

*   **Inflationary Pressures:** High inflation remains a primary headwind, driven by underlying price pressures and rising food costs.
*   **Debt Sustainability:** Public debt reached 73.5% of GDP in 2024. High debt-servicing costs and an implicit interest rate near 6% pose risks to fiscal space for growth.
*   **Labour Productivity:** A shortage of skilled workers (particularly in STEM and the green transition) and low [[Labour Productivity]] are identified as major structural barriers to sustained expansion.
*   **Innovation Stagnation:** Low [[R&D Intensity]] (1.4% vs. the EU average of 2.2%) limits the economy's ability to transition into high-value, innovation-led growth.
*   **Regulatory Environment:** Market distortions and regulatory volatility create an unpredictable environment for long-term business planning and investment.
*   **Fiscal Policy:** An expansionary budgetary bias and a lack of transparent multiannual spending ceilings impact the reliability of long-term growth projections.
