# Automatic Wage Indexation

**Automatic Wage Indexation** is a labor market mechanism, primarily associated with the Belgian economic model, where wages are automatically adjusted in response to changes in the cost of living (typically inflation). 

## Overview and Impact
In the context of the Belgian economy, this system serves as a mechanism to preserve purchasing power for workers. However, recent analysis by the **IMF (2025 Article IV Consultation)** identifies it as a significant structural challenge with several negative externalities:

*   **Fiscal Deficits:** The system is cited as a primary driver of structural fiscal deficits, as it automatically increases the public wage bill during periods of high inflation.
*   **Labor Competitiveness:** It acts as a deterrent to international competitiveness by creating a "wage-push" effect that can make Belgian exports and services more expensive relative to peers.
*   **Productivity Concerns:** The automatic nature of these adjustments can decouple wage growth from productivity gains, contributing to a lag in **Total Factor Productivity (TFP)** compared to neighboring European economies.

## Policy Recommendations
The IMF suggests that to ensure long-term fiscal sustainability and economic dynamism, Belgium should consider:
*   **Abolition or Reform:** Evaluating the abolition of automatic indexation and the 1996 wage law.
*   **Structural Reform:** Combining wage reform with broader labor market changes, such as widening the income gap between work and non-work and reducing hiring/dismissal costs to increase labor force participation.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation:** Addressing the indexation-driven costs as part of a broader 7-year adjustment plan to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP.
