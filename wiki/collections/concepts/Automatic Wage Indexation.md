# Automatic Wage Indexation

**Automatic Wage Indexation** is a labor market mechanism, notably prominent in Belgium, where wages are automatically adjusted in response to changes in the cost of living (typically measured by inflation indices). 

### Key Characteristics and Context
*   **Mechanism:** The system is designed to preserve the purchasing power of workers by linking nominal wage growth to price levels.
*   **Regulatory Basis:** In Belgium, this system is governed by specific legislation, notably the **1996 wage law**.
*   **Economic Impact:** While providing a social safety net for workers, the IMF identifies this mechanism as a significant headwind for economic competitiveness.

### Critical Analysis & Challenges
According to the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Belgium**, automatic wage indexation presents several structural challenges for the Belgian economy:
*   **Labor-Cost Competitiveness:** The automatic nature of these increases can lead to rapid rises in labor costs, potentially making Belgian exports and services less competitive internationally.
*   **Inflationary Pressure:** By automatically pushing wages upward in response to inflation, the system can contribute to persistent core inflation.
*   **Labor Market Efficiency:** The IMF suggests that the current framework may hinder labor-market efficiency and recommends a review of the indexation rules to improve overall productivity and competitiveness.
