# Article IV Consultation

An **Article IV Consultation** is a standard surveillance mechanism conducted by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) where staff members visit member countries to assess their economic and financial health. These consultations serve as a periodic "health check" to provide policy advice, identify potential risks to global stability, and monitor compliance with international standards.

### Key Components & Objectives
*   **Economic Assessment:** Evaluation of macroeconomic indicators including GDP growth, inflation rates, and employment data.
*   **Policy Review:** Analysis of the country's [[Fiscal Policy]], including public debt levels, budget deficits, and the sustainability of public spending.
*   **Monetary Oversight:** Review of the central bank's [[Monetary Policy Stance]], interest rate trajectories, and inflation targeting mechanisms.
*   **Structural Analysis:** Identification of long-term structural issues such as [[Structural Reforms]], governance, labor market dynamics, and energy security.
*   **Stability Monitoring:** Assessment of the [[Sovereign-Bank Nexus]], financial sector vulnerabilities, and [[Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA)]].

### Recent Applications & Case Studies

#### Hungary (2025)
The 2025 consultation for Hungary highlighted several critical areas of focus:
*   **Fiscal Adjustment:** The IMF emphasized the need for a structural primary surplus (targeted at ~1.75% of GDP) to address elevated public debt and rebuild fiscal buffers.
*   **Inflation Management:** Recommendations focused on maintaining a restrictive monetary policy to anchor inflation toward the 3% target, specifically noting that price and margin caps are unsustainable for long-term disinflation.
*   **Geoeconomic Risks:** The consultation identified significant risks from [[Geoeconomic Fragmentation]], trade barriers, and external uncertainties affecting export-oriented growth.
*   **Productivity & Reform:** A core focus was placed on closing the productivity gap by reducing state aid distortions, improving factor mobility, and unlocking suspended EU funding through governance reforms.

#### Malta (2024/2025)
The 2024 Article IV Consultation for Malta (concluded January 15, 2025) provided a framework for transitioning to a productivity-driven growth model:
*   **Fiscal Consolidation & Subsidy Reform:** Recommendations included phasing out fixed energy price policies to redirect fiscal space toward public services (health, education) and innovation.
*   **Financial Stability & Macroprudential Policy:** The IMF identified significant [[Sovereign Risk]] and real estate exposure (70% of loan portfolios). It recommended raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB) and expanding its scope to include commercial real estate.
*   **Taxation & Corporate Governance:** A focus on [[Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Reform]] to align with the EU's Pillar II Minimum Tax and a need for judicial reforms to strengthen investor confidence.
*   **Labor & Sustainability:** Emphasis on closing the STEM skills gap and meeting greenhouse gas (GHG) reduction targets through green investment.
