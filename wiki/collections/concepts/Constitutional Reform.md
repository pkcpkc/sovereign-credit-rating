# Constitutional Reform

Constitutional Reform refers to the process of amending, restructuring, or modifying a nation's fundamental laws and institutional frameworks. In the context of Italy's current legal landscape, these reforms are primarily focused on the organization of the judiciary and the balance of powers.

## Current Developments in Italy (2024-2025)

### Judicial Career Separation
A significant focal point of recent constitutional reform in Italy is the legislative move to **separate the careers of judges and prosecutors**. 
- **Legislative Action:** The Chamber of Deputies approved a draft reform aimed at creating distinct career paths for these roles.
- **Institutional Friction:** The reform has met with significant reservations from the [[Judicial Independence]] oversight bodies, specifically the High Council for the Judiciary. Concerns have been raised regarding the authority and autonomy of separate councils for each career path.
- **Critical Opinions:** The High Council for the Judiciary issued a critical opinion on the reform in early 2025, highlighting potential risks to the independence of the magistracy.

### Institutional Checks and Balances
Broader constitutional concerns in Italy involve the preservation of the separation of powers:
- **Urgency Decrees:** There is ongoing debate regarding the high frequency of government urgency decrees (87 in the current legislature), which critics argue may undermine traditional constitutional checks.
- **Security Legislation:** The adoption of the new Security Law in June 2025 has been scrutinized for its potential impact on civic space and freedom of assembly, representing a shift in the constitutional balance between state security and individual liberties.
