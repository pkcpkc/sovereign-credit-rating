# Constitutional Reform

**Constitutional Reform** refers to the formal amendment or restructuring of a nation's fundamental legal framework, often aimed at enhancing governmental stability, clarifying institutional roles, and strengthening checks and balances. In the context of **Italy**'s 2025 Rule of Law assessment, constitutional reform represents a critical pillar for **political stability** and **institutional governance**, with significant implications for **sovereign credit ratings** and long-term **fiscal sustainability**.

## Key Developments in Italy (2025)

The Italian government has advanced a draft constitutional reform focused on two primary objectives: enhancing governmental stability and introducing mechanisms for the direct election of the Prime Minister. This initiative has been scrutinized by the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate, reflecting intense political debate regarding the balance between executive efficiency and democratic accountability.

### Judicial Career Separation
A central component of the current reform agenda is the separation of the careers of judges and prosecutors. In January 2025, the Chamber of Deputies approved this draft reform, aiming to strengthen judicial independence by preventing the overlap of investigative and adjudicative roles. However, this measure faces significant opposition from the **High Council for the Judiciary** and the **National Association of Magistrates**, who argue that such separation could undermine judicial autonomy and institutional cohesion. The **High Court of Cassation** has issued critical opinions on this matter, highlighting tensions between legislative intent and judicial self-governance.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Risk Analysis

From a sovereign credit rating perspective, constitutional reforms are evaluated based on their ability to reduce **political risk** and enhance **policy predictability**.

### Strengths for Credit Stability
- **Governmental Stability:** The reform's aim to stabilize government formation and potentially introduce direct popular mandate for the executive is viewed by stakeholders as a potential strength. It may reduce the frequency of government collapses and the associated economic uncertainty.
- **Stakeholder Support:** There is notable stakeholder support for measures that guarantee the impartiality of judges, which supports the **rule of law** and reduces legal uncertainty for investors.

### Threats and Risks
- **Judicial Opposition:** The strong resistance from judicial bodies (including the **High Council for the Judiciary** and the **National Association of Magistrates**) creates implementation risk. If the reform fails to pass or is significantly diluted, it may signal political fragmentation and institutional deadlock.
- **Civic Space Concerns:** The concurrent adoption of a **Security Law** (via urgency decree converted in June 2025) has raised concerns among the **Council of Europe** and **United Nations Special Rapporteurs** regarding its impact on fundamental freedoms. This has led to a rating of Italy's civic space as "narrowed," which can negatively affect **political and geopolitical risk** assessments.
- **Arbitrary Application Risks:** Stakeholders have expressed concern over the precision of the Security Law, fearing it may allow for arbitrary application, which undermines the **rule of law** and increases country risk.
