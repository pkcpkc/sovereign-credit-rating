# Reserve-Currency Flexibility (RCF)

**Reserve-Currency Flexibility (RCF)** is a critical analytical criterion used by Fitch Ratings within its sovereign credit assessment framework to determine the mapping of short-term ratings for foreign-currency (FC) denominated debt.

## Definition and Application
RCF serves as a qualitative and quantitative indicator of a sovereign's ability to manage its external obligations when debt is denominated in a foreign currency. It is specifically utilized during the **Short-Term Mapping** process, where long-term ratings (F1+ to D) are translated into short-term equivalents.

## Role in Rating Methodology
In the context of Fitch's Sovereign Rating Criteria (Sept 2025), RCF plays a decisive role in the following scenarios:

*   **Short-Term Mapping for Foreign-Currency (FC) IDRs:** While local-currency (LC) ratings typically default to the higher mapping option, the selection for FC ratings is contingent upon either the country's **Reserve-Currency Flexibility (RCF)** or a robust **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)**.
*   **Liquidity Thresholds:** If RCF is insufficient, a sovereign must maintain an **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)** greater than 100% to qualify for certain short-term rating mappings.
