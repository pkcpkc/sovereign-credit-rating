# European Public Prosecutor Office

The [[European Public Prosecutor Office]] (EPPO) is the independent prosecuting body of the European Union, established to investigate and prosecute crimes affecting the EU’s financial interests. Its operational status within member states serves as a critical indicator of institutional integrity, judicial independence, and compliance with EU rule of law mechanisms.

## Operational Status in Poland

As of January 31, 2025, the [[European Public Prosecutor Office]] became fully operational in Poland. This milestone represents a significant advancement in Poland's anti-corruption framework and its alignment with EU judicial standards. The full operationalization of the EPPO in Poland is cited as a key strength in the country's sovereign credit rating assessment, demonstrating active compliance with EU rule of law mechanisms.

The activation of the EPPO in Poland has direct implications for financial stability, fiscal sustainability, and country risk analysis:

### 2. Anti-Corruption Framework
The EPPO operates in tandem with domestic reforms, such as the establishment of a new Central Office for Combating Corruption within the Polish police. While systemic risks in public procurement remain, the EPPO provides a supranational layer of oversight that strengthens the overall integrity of the anti-corruption framework. ### 3. Judicial Independence and Rule of Law
The EPPO's operational success is intertwined with the broader state of judicial independence. While perceived judicial independence in Poland remains very low (26% among the general public), the functional independence of the prosecution service has seen significant progress. The government's legislative efforts to separate the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the Prosecutor General further support the EPPO's ability to operate without political interference. This alignment is crucial for maintaining the legitimacy of EU institutions and ensuring that Poland remains eligible for full access to EU financial instruments.
