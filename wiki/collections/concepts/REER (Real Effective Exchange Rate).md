# REER (Real Effective Exchange Rate)

## Definition
The **Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER)** is a measure of the relative value of a country's currency against a basket of other currencies, adjusted for inflation (price levels) in both the home country and its trading partners. Unlike a nominal exchange rate, which only shows the price of one currency in terms of another, the REER reflects the purchasing power and competitiveness of a nation's exports and imports.

## Key Characteristics and Indicators
*   **Competitiveness:** A rising REER indicates that a country's exports are becoming more expensive relative to its trading partners (appreciation), while a falling REER suggests that exports are becoming cheaper (depreciation).
*   **Inflation Adjustment:** By accounting for price differentials, the REER helps economists determine if a currency's value is changing due to actual economic shifts or merely due to domestic inflation.
*   **Trade Balance Impact:** Significant deviations in the REER can lead to trade imbalances. An overvalued REER often correlates with a widening **[[Current Account Deficit]]** as imports become cheaper and exports less competitive.

## Contextual Application: Romania (2025)
In the context of the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation**, the REER is highlighted as a critical metric for Romania's macroeconomic stability:
*   **Overvaluation:** The IMF identified a significant REER overvaluation in Romania, contributing to external imbalances.
*   **Current Account Deficit:** This overvaluation was a primary driver of the 8.2% current account deficit recorded in 2024, fueled by weak export performance against high import costs.
*   **Policy Recommendation:** To mitigate these risks and enhance economic resilience, the IMF suggested a transition toward gradual increases in two-way exchange rate flexibility. This would allow the currency to adjust more naturally to market pressures, potentially correcting the REER imbalance over time.

## Related Concepts
*   **[[External Imbalances]]**: The broader economic condition where a country's exports/imports or debt levels are out of sync with its economic capacity.
*   **[[Current Account Deficit]]**: The specific trade and investment imbalance affected by REER fluctuations.
*   **[[Fiscal Consolidation]]**: Often a complementary policy to manage the debt resulting from persistent trade deficits.
