# Corporate Income Tax (CIT)

Corporate Income Tax (CIT) is a tax levied by governments on the profits of corporations and other entities. It serves as a primary pillar of fiscal policy, influencing corporate behavior, investment flows, and national revenue streams.

## Current Context & Reforms
Recent developments in CIT are heavily influenced by international cooperation and regional fiscal frameworks, specifically within the European Union.

### OECD Pillar II & EU Alignment
A critical current focus for CIT reform is the alignment with the **[[OECD Pillar II]]** framework. This involves:
- **EU Minimum Tax Directive:** Implementing a roadmap for CIT reform to ensure compliance with the global minimum tax standards.
- **Regulatory Harmonization:** Ensuring that domestic tax structures do not conflict with international transparency and minimum taxation requirements.

### Fiscal Policy Implications
In the context of national economies (e.g., Malta), CIT reform is viewed as a tool for:
- **Fiscal Consolidation:** Balancing the budget while maintaining a competitive environment for businesses.
- **Interaction with Personal Income Tax:** Authorities are tasked with analyzing how CIT reforms interact with personal income tax structures to ensure a balanced fiscal approach.
- **Economic Sustainability:** Moving toward a productivity-driven growth model where tax structures support innovation and R&D rather than just labor-intensive expansion.
