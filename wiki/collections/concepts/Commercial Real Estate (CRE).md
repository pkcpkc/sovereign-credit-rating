# Commercial Real Estate (CRE)

Commercial Real Estate (CRE) refers to property used exclusively for business purposes, including office buildings, retail spaces, warehouses, and industrial facilities. In the context of broader economic stability, it represents a significant asset class that can influence [[Financial Stability]] and contribute to [[Systemic Risk]] if imbalances occur.

## Current Context and Risks
Recent analysis from the IMF (2025) highlights specific concerns regarding the CRE sector within the Austrian economy and the broader European landscape:

*   **Non-Performing Loans (NPLs):** There is a noted risk of rising NPLs specifically within the commercial real estate sector. This is a key metric for monitoring the health of the banking sector and potential credit stress.
*   **Banking Sector Resilience:** While the broader banking sector currently maintains healthy capital ratios (e.g., CET1 at 17.7%), the concentration of CRE exposure remains a focal point for regulatory vigilance.
*   **Macroeconomic Drivers:** The performance of CRE is currently influenced by high interest rates, shifting work patterns (affecting office demand), and the broader economic transition in the Eurozone.
