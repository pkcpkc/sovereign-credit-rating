# Energy Intensity

Energy intensity refers to the amount of energy consumed per unit of economic output (typically GDP). It serves as a critical metric for evaluating the energy efficiency of an economy and its progress toward [[Decarbonisation]].

## Contextual Analysis: Austria (2025)
In the context of the 2025 Country Report for Austria, energy intensity and related energy metrics are central to the nation's economic and environmental strategy:

*   **Industrial Decarbonisation:** Manufacturing is identified as a primary source of emissions (35%), making the reduction of energy intensity in these sectors a priority. The report emphasizes the need for faster implementation of measures to decarbonize energy-intensive industries.
*   **Renewable Leadership:** Austria has demonstrated high efficiency in electricity generation, achieving 87% renewable electricity in 2024. However, achieving further improvements in energy intensity is challenged by the need for significant grid expansion (estimated at EUR 53 billion by 2040).
*   **Economic Impact:** High energy costs are currently cited as a broad-based cost pressure impacting industrial competitiveness, highlighting the link between energy intensity, production costs, and overall economic dynamism.
