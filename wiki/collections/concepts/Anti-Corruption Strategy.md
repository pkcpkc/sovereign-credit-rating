# Anti-Corruption Strategy

The **Anti-Corruption Strategy** refers to the institutional framework, legislative measures, and oversight mechanisms designed to detect, prevent, and prosecute corrupt activities within a state's governance, judiciary, and public administration. 

In the context of the **2025 Rule of Law Report - Hungary**, the strategy is characterized by a significant gap between low-level regulatory progress and high-level systemic enforcement.

## Key Components and Current Status

### Oversight Bodies and Institutional Barriers
*   **Integrity Authority:** While established as a primary body, the authority faces critical operational hurdles, specifically regarding restricted access to state databases. This limitation hampers its ability to conduct comprehensive oversight.
*   **Anti-Corruption Task Force:** Reports indicate that the task force has had limited practical impact on high-level corruption cases to date.

### Judicial and Prosecutorial Mechanisms
*   **Prosecutorial Oversight:** A specific mechanism has been implemented allowing judges to review decisions made by prosecutors to decline investigations into corruption. Usage of this oversight mechanism has doubled since 2024, indicating an increased judicial involvement in vetting corruption leads.
*   **Judicial Independence:** The efficacy of any anti-corruption strategy is intrinsically linked to [[Judicial Independence]]. In Hungary, public perception of judicial fairness remains low (approx. 37-38%), which impacts the perceived legitimacy of corruption enforcement.

### Transparency and Accountability
*   **Asset Declarations:** A significant weakness in the current strategy is the lack of transparency and rigorous oversight regarding asset declarations for individuals in senior political functions.
*   **High-Level vs. Low-Level Corruption:** While some codes of conduct have been established for lower-level officials, the framework currently struggles to address high-level corruption and systemic political influence.
