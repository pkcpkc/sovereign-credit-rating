# Anti-Corruption Strategy

The **Anti-Corruption Strategy** refers to the institutional framework, legislative measures, and oversight mechanisms designed to detect, prevent, and prosecute corrupt activities within a state's governance, judiciary, and public administration. 

The efficacy of these strategies varies significantly by jurisdiction, often characterized by a gap between low-level regulatory progress and high-level systemic enforcement.

## Key Components and Regional Contexts

### Oversight Bodies and Institutional Barriers
*   **Integrity Authority:** In some contexts (e.g., Hungary), authorities face critical operational hurdles, specifically regarding restricted access to state databases, which hampers comprehensive oversight.
*   **Anti-Corruption Task Forces:** Reports indicate that specific task forces may have limited practical impact on high-level corruption cases despite their existence.
*   **Agency Performance:** In other regions (e.g., Romania), bodies like the National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA) may maintain high occupancy rates and continue to issue indictments despite heavy workloads.

### Judicial and Prosecutorial Mechanisms
*   **Prosecutorial Oversight:** Mechanisms have been implemented allowing judges to review decisions made by prosecutors to decline investigations. In some instances, the usage of these oversight mechanisms has doubled, indicating increased judicial vetting of corruption leads.
*   **Judicial Independence:** The efficacy of any anti-corruption strategy is intrinsically linked to [[Judicial Independence]]. Low public perception of judicial fairness directly impacts the perceived legitimacy of corruption enforcement.
*   **Statute of Limitations Crisis:** A major systemic risk involves court rulings (such as the *lex mitior* principle) that can lead to the closing of numerous corruption cases and the annulment of previous convictions.

### Transparency and Accountability
*   **Asset Declarations:** A recurring weakness is the lack of transparency regarding asset declarations for senior political functions. Recent legal developments in some jurisdictions have even declared it unconstitutional to require officials to declare the assets of spouses and children, creating significant transparency gaps.
*   **Lobbying Rules:** A lack of progress in introducing formal lobbying rules for Members of Parliament remains a critical gap in many anti-corruption frameworks.
*   **High-Level vs. Low-Level Corruption:** While codes of conduct for lower-level officials are common, frameworks frequently struggle to address systemic political influence and high-level corruption.

### Related Governance Frameworks
*   **Open Government:** Strategies for Open Government (e.g., 2025-2030 initiatives) are being adopted to address narrowing civic space and improve transparency.
*   **Media Pluralism:** Anti-corruption efforts are often undermined by a lack of [[Media Pluralism]], high risks in media ownership transparency, and the use of political funds to blur the lines between news and paid content.
*   **Legal Harassment:** The rise of [[SLAPPs]] and the harassment of journalists represent ongoing challenges to the free flow of information necessary for anti-corruption oversight.
