# Legal Professional Privilege

Legal Professional Privilege (LPP) is a fundamental legal principle and human right that protects the confidentiality of communications between a legal professional (such as a lawyer or solicitor) and their client. It is a cornerstone of the [[Rule of Law]] and the administration of justice, ensuring that individuals can seek legal advice with complete confidence.

## Core Functions and Significance
- **Confidentiality:** Ensures that communications, documents, and advice shared within the solicitor-client relationship remain protected from disclosure by third parties or government authorities.
- **Access to Justice:** By guaranteeing confidentiality, LPP allows individuals and entities to fully disclose facts to their counsel, enabling the provision of accurate legal advice and the preparation of a robust defense.
- **Judicial Independence:** LPP supports [[Judicial Independence]] by ensuring that the legal process is not undermined by external pressures or surveillance of private legal consultations.

## Contextual Observations (Belgium 2025)
In the context of the 2025 Rule of Law Report for Belgium, the protection of legal rights is situated within a broader framework of judicial reform and human rights protections:
- **Human Rights Framework:** LPP is intrinsically linked to the protection of [[Human Rights]] and the integrity of the justice system.
- **Transparency vs. Privilege:** While Belgium continues to move toward greater [[Digitalisation of Justice]] and administrative transparency, the preservation of legal privilege remains a critical boundary to ensure that privacy is not sacrificed for oversight.
- **Rule of Law Infrastructure:** The report highlights ongoing efforts to strengthen the justice system's structural integrity, which serves as the foundation for upholding professional privileges like LPP.
