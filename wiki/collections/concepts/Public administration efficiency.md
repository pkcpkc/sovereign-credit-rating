# Public administration efficiency

**Public administration efficiency** refers to the capacity of government institutions to design, implement, and enforce policies effectively, ensuring high-quality public service delivery, regulatory stability, and optimal resource allocation. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and macroeconomic stability, administrative efficiency is a critical determinant of **[[Fiscal sustainability]]**, as it influences tax collection effectiveness, the speed of reform implementation, and the credibility of policy commitments.

## Role in Macroeconomic Stability and Sovereign Credit

Inefficient public administration creates significant tail risks for sovereign borrowers. It manifests as:
*   **Regulatory Uncertainty:** Frequent changes in rules and short consultation periods undermine investor confidence, deterring long-term **[[Foreign direct investment]]** (FDI).
*   **Fiscal Leakage:** Weak oversight and administrative bottlenecks contribute to high tax gaps and informal economy activities, reducing the effective tax base.
*   **Reform Implementation Lag:** Administrative bottlenecks delay the absorption of structural funds (e.g., EU Recovery and Resilience Plans), postponing growth-enhancing effects and worsening short-term fiscal balances.

## Case Study: Romania (2025 Country Report Analysis)

According to the European Commission's 2025 Country Report, **public administration efficiency** is identified as a primary structural weakness in Romania, directly impacting its sovereign credit profile and macroeconomic outlook.

### Key Impacts on Credit Rating Factors

#### 1. Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength
*   **Threats:** Romania exhibits suboptimal administrative capacity and poor public service delivery. The **[[Ministry of Finance (Romania)]]** and other bodies face challenges in implementing complex reforms, such as the comprehensive tax system review. The frequent use of government emergency ordinances undermines legal certainty, signaling weak institutional planning.
*   **Impact:** These factors erode **policy credibility**, increasing the risk premium on sovereign debt. Investors perceive a higher likelihood of policy reversals or ineffective execution, particularly regarding **[[Fiscal consolidation]]** measures.

#### 2. Investment Climate and Business Environment
*   **Regulatory Burden:** Approximately 90% of Romanian firms cite regulatory uncertainty and legislative instability as the top barrier to long-term investment. The fiscal code has been amended hundreds of times since 2015, creating an unpredictable environment.
*   **Compliance Costs:** A high share of firms dedicates staff to regulatory compliance rather than productive activities. This inefficiency acts as a hidden tax on business, reducing productivity and potential tax revenues.
*   **Procurement Risks:** Public procurement remains a sector with high corruption risk, leading to inefficient allocation of public funds and delayed infrastructure projects.

#### 3. Fiscal Sustainability and Revenue Collection
*   **Tax Collection Inefficiencies:** Weak administrative capacity contributes to a high **VAT gap** (33.7% in 2024) and low overall tax revenue as a percentage of GDP (26.2%, compared to the EU average of 39%). The **[[National Agency for Fiscal Administration]]** faces challenges in combating the informal economy and ensuring compliance.
*   **Reform Delays:** The implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) is significantly delayed, with only 14% of milestones fulfilled. Administrative bottlenecks in procurement and decision-making prevent the timely deployment of EU funds, which are crucial for boosting productivity and fiscal space.

#### 4. Accountability and Corruption
*   **Perceived Corruption:** High perceived corruption and lack of transparency in lobbying (e.g., absence of a registry for MPs) weaken the legitimacy of public institutions.
*   **Enforcement Gaps:** Low enforcement of existing rules, such as SME tests and fiscal compacts, indicates a disconnect between legislative intent and administrative execution.

### Strategic Responses and Future Outlook

To address these inefficiencies, Romania has committed to the **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** (2025-2031), which includes measures to:
*   Strengthen the mandate of the **Romanian Fiscal Council** to enhance independent oversight of public finances.
*   Improve the quality of regulation through longer consultation periods and better dialogue between industry and government.
*   Enhance digitalization of public services to reduce bureaucracy and corruption opportunities.

However, the success of these measures hinges on overcoming political instability and building sustained administrative capacity. Without significant improvements in **public administration efficiency**, Romania risks remaining vulnerable to external shocks, with persistent fiscal deficits and slowed convergence with EU living standards.
