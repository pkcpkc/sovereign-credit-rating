# Fiscal Assessment

**Fiscal Assessment** is one of the five core pillars in the **S&P Global Sovereign Rating Methodology** used to evaluate a sovereign government's or monetary authority's ability and willingness to service its financial obligations to nonofficial commercial creditors. Introduced in the updated methodology of December 18, 2017, this assessment focuses specifically on the sustainability of fiscal policy, the burden of public debt, and the government's capacity to manage fiscal shocks. It operates alongside Institutional, Economic, External, and Monetary assessments to derive an indicative rating level.

## Core Components

The Fiscal Assessment is structurally divided into two primary dimensions: **Flows** and **Stocks**, supplemented by an evaluation of off-balance sheet risks.

### 1. Fiscal Flows (Performance and Flexibility)
This dimension evaluates the government's recent fiscal performance and its potential to adjust in the face of economic shocks. Key metrics include:
*   **Fiscal Balance:** The government's revenue and expenditure trends, including the primary deficit or surplus.
*   **Fiscal Flexibility:** The government's ability to raise revenues or cut expenditures in response to adverse economic conditions. This is influenced by the tax system's structure, political constraints on spending cuts, and social stability.
*   **Current Account Payments (CAP):** The 2017 methodology update specifies using current account payments rather than receipts in certain external assessment ratios, which indirectly influences the fiscal context by highlighting external financing needs.

### 2. Fiscal Stocks (Debt Burden)
This dimension assesses the stock of government debt and its sustainability. Key metrics include:
*   **Net General Government Debt:** The 2017 methodology shifted the baseline for initial fiscal assessments from gross debt to **net general government debt**. This change accounts for financial assets held by the government, providing a more accurate picture of net liability.
*   **Debt Trajectory:** The assessment examines whether debt levels are stable, declining, or rising, and the factors driving these changes (e.g., growth rates, interest costs).
*   **Debt Sustainability:** The long-term viability of the current debt level given projected economic growth and fiscal policy.

### 3. Contingent Liabilities
S&P explicitly evaluates **Contingent Liabilities**, which are potential obligations that may become actual liabilities depending on future events. These include:
*   Guarantees provided to financial institutions.
*   Potential support for nonfinancial public-sector enterprises.
*   Off-budget guarantees and public-private partnerships (PPPs) that pose risks to the general government balance sheet.

## Methodological Updates and Nuances

The 2017 update to the **S&P Sovereign Rating Methodology** introduced significant changes to how Fiscal Assessment interacts with other pillars:

*   **Net Debt Baseline:** Initial fiscal assessments are now based on changes in net general government debt rather than gross debt, allowing for a more nuanced view of assets that can offset liabilities.
*   **Six-Point Scale:** Each pillar, including Fiscal, is scored on a six-point numerical scale (from 1 to 6), combining quantitative data with qualitative judgments.
*   **Supplemental Adjustments:** Extremely high fiscal debt burdens can trigger supplemental adjustment factors. These adjustments can lower the indicative rating by more than one notch or impose absolute caps on the maximum achievable rating, reflecting the heightened risk associated with unsustainable debt levels.
*   **Integration with Other Pillars:** The fiscal profile is averaged with the External and Monetary profiles to form the "Flexibility/Performance Profile." This profile, along with the "Institutional/Economic Profile" (averaging Institutional and Economic assessments), determines the indicative rating level for foreign-currency **Sovereign Credit Rating**.

## Significance in Sovereign Credit Analysis

Fiscal Assessment is critical for determining **Debt Sustainability** and overall **Macroeconomic Stability**. A weak fiscal position, characterized by high debt burdens, poor fiscal flexibility, or significant contingent liabilities, directly impacts a country's creditworthiness. It affects the government's ability to respond to crises, maintain investor confidence, and service existing debt. Consequently, a strong Fiscal Assessment is often a prerequisite for achieving higher ratings, particularly in emerging markets where fiscal vulnerabilities are more pronounced. The assessment also interacts with **Monetary Policy Credibility**, as fiscal dominance (where monetary policy is subordinated to fiscal needs) can undermine central bank independence and price stability.
