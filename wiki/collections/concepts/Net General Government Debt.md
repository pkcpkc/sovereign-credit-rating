# Net General Government Debt

**Net General Government Debt** is a fundamental fiscal metric used to assess the creditworthiness of sovereign entities. Unlike gross debt, which measures the total amount of outstanding obligations, net debt provides a more accurate representation of a government's true financial position by accounting for the liquid assets available to service those obligations.

## Definition and Calculation
Net general government debt is calculated as the total general government debt minus **liquid financial assets**. By subtracting these assets, the metric reflects the net amount of funding a government requires from external and domestic creditors to meet its obligations.

## Role in Sovereign Rating Methodology
In the context of the S&P Global Ratings (2017) methodology, Net General Government Debt serves as a primary indicator for the **Fiscal Assessment** pillar. It is utilized in two distinct ways:

*   **Fiscal Performance and Flexibility:** This component evaluates the trajectory of a nation's finances based on the *change* in net general government debt over time.
*   **Debt Burden:** This component assesses the sustainability of the debt level relative to the interest costs incurred by the sovereign.

## Key Distinctions
*   **Net vs. Gross:** The shift toward focusing on net debt allows for a more nuanced evaluation of "Fiscal Flexibility," as it recognizes that significant liquid holdings can offset the burden of high gross debt figures.
*   **External Ratios:** While net debt is a core fiscal metric, it is complemented by other external indicators like **Current Account Payments (CAP)** to provide a holistic view of a country's ability to service its debt.
