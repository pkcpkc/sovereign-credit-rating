# Social Convergence

Social Convergence refers to the structural and economic processes aimed at reducing inequalities, ensuring social fairness, and fostering inclusive growth within a society. It involves balancing economic productivity with social protections to ensure that the benefits of growth are distributed equitably across different demographic groups.

## Contextual Application: Hungary (2025)
Based on the 2025 Country Report, challenges to social convergence in Hungary are multifaceted, characterized by rising inequality and structural barriers:

*   **Poverty & Social Exclusion:** There is a documented rise in poverty and social exclusion risks, specifically impacting vulnerable groups including children, the Roma population, and persons with disabilities.
*   **Housing Affordability:** A significant barrier to social convergence is the housing crisis. Hungary experienced a 230% increase in house prices between 2010 and 2024 (the highest in the EU), creating severe cost overburdens for low-income households.
*   **Labour Market Dynamics:** While nominal wages have seen rapid growth, the lack of corresponding [[Labour Productivity]] and a persistent [[Skills Shortage]] (particularly in STEM and the green transition) threaten long-term competitiveness and equitable wage distribution.
*   **Health & Education:** Low tertiary education attainment (32.3%) and a hospital-centric health system with high out-of-pocket costs further exacerbate social disparities.
