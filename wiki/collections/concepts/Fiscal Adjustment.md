# Fiscal Adjustment

Fiscal Adjustment refers to the strategic process of correcting a government's budgetary imbalance, typically characterized by reducing a high public deficit or national debt relative to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP). This process involves a combination of expenditure controls, revenue adjustments, and structural reforms aimed at ensuring long-term economic stability and sustainability.

## Contextual Application: Poland
In the context of the 2025 Country Report for Poland, fiscal adjustment is a critical priority driven by the following factors:

*   **Excessive Deficit Procedure:** Poland is currently subject to an [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]], necessitated by a 2023 deficit of 6.6% of GDP.
*   **Expenditure Path:** To comply with fiscal requirements, Poland has established a binding expenditure path. The objective is to reduce the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028.
*   **Public Spending Dynamics:** The adjustment process must navigate a landscape where net expenditure grew by 12.7% in 2024, primarily driven by public consumption and increased defense spending.
*   **Fiscal-Structural Plan:** The Council endorsed a medium-term fiscal-structural plan in January 2025, which serves as the roadmap for these adjustments.

## Key Objectives and Challenges
*   **Sustainability:** Balancing the need for immediate fiscal consolidation with the requirements for [[Public Investment Efficiency]] and long-term growth.
*   **Structural Reform:** Transitioning from emergency deficit reduction to a sustainable fiscal framework that addresses underlying issues like tax complexity and labor productivity.
*   **Economic Growth:** Ensuring that fiscal tightening does not stifle the projected GDP growth, which is expected to surpass 2.9% in 2025.
