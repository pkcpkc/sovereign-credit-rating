# Fiscal Adjustment

Fiscal adjustment refers to the set of policy actions and structural changes implemented by a government to reduce its budget deficit, manage public debt levels, and restore long-term fiscal sustainability. It involves balancing the need for immediate output growth with the necessity of rebuilding fiscal buffers.

## Context and Application: Hungary (2025)
Based on the [[IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Hungary]], high-quality fiscal adjustment is identified as a critical priority for the Hungarian economy to address several key challenges:

*   **Debt Sustainability:** With public debt projected to reach 79% of GDP by 2030, the IMF recommends a structural primary surplus of approximately 1.75% of GDP. This is intended to balance the requirements of debt sustainability with the need for continued output growth.
*   **Buffer Rebuilding:** Adjustment measures are necessary to rebuild fiscal buffers that have been depleted or strained, providing a cushion against external shocks and geoeconomic fragmentation.
*   **Structural Reform Integration:** Effective fiscal adjustment is linked to broader structural reforms, including:
    *   Phasing out fossil fuel subsidies.
    *   Reducing state aid distortions that hinder firm dynamism.
    *   Improving governance to unlock suspended EU funding.

## Key Related Concepts
*   [[Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA)]]: The framework used to evaluate the impact of fiscal adjustments on long-term debt levels.
*   [[Monetary Policy Stance]]: Works in tandem with fiscal adjustment; the IMF suggests a tight monetary policy to anchor inflation while fiscal measures address the deficit.
*   [[Structural Reforms]]: Essential for ensuring that fiscal adjustments do not stifle economic growth but instead improve productivity and the business environment.
*   [[Sovereign-Bank Nexus]]: Fiscal policies directly impact the stability of the banking sector through sovereign debt linkages.
