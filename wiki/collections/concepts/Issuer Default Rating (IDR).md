# Issuer Default Rating (IDR)

An **Issuer Default Rating (IDR)** is a credit rating assigned to an entity—in this context specifically sovereign and central bank issuers—that reflects the likelihood of that issuer defaulting on its financial obligations. It serves as a primary metric for assessing creditworthiness and determining the risk profile of sovereign debt.

## Framework and Methodology
According to the **Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Criteria (Sept 2025)**, IDRs are determined through a multi-pillared approach that synthesizes quantitative and qualitative data:

*   **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM):** A quantitative foundation using a multiple regression OLS model consisting of 18 variables. The model weights four primary pillars:
    *   **Structural Features:** 53.7% weight.
    *   **Public Finances:** 19.1% weight.
    *   **External Finances:** 17.3% weight.
    *   **Macroeconomic Performance:** 9.8% weight.
*   **Qualitative Overlay (QO):** A mechanism allowing analysts to adjust the baseline SRM score by up to +/- 3 notches. This accounts for nuances such as political stability, policy credibility, and fiscal sustainability.

## Key Rating Characteristics
*   **Currency Differentiation:** Fitch distinguishes between **Local-Currency (LC)** and **Foreign-Currency (FC)** IDRs. The distinction is based on the distribution of debt burden, the depth of domestic capital markets, and the issuer's access to foreign currency.
*   **Short-Term Mapping:** Short-term ratings (ranging from F1+ to D) are derived from long-term IDRs. For LC ratings, the higher option is typically selected. For FC ratings, selection depends on **Reserve-Currency Flexibility (RCF)** or an **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)** greater than 100%.
*   **Recovery Ratings (RR):** For sovereigns with lower credit quality (rated 'B+' or below), Fitch assigns Recovery Ratings (RR1-RR6) to provide transparency regarding potential creditor recoveries in the event of a default.

## Default and Distressed Scenarios
An issuer is moved to a **'RD' (Restructured/Default)** status under the following conditions:
*   Missed payments.
*   Forced redenomination of debt.
*   Execution of a **Distressed Debt Exchange (DDE)**.
*   *Note:* A 30-day grace period is generally applied for operational interruptions classified as Payment Force Majeure.

## Risk Screening
The IDR assessment incorporates modern risk signals, specifically the **Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)** framework. This screens for physical and transition risks, where a score of 50 or higher by the year 2035 triggers intensive additional analytical scrutiny.
