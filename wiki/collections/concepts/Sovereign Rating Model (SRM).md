# Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)

The **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)** is the core quantitative framework utilized by Fitch Ratings to assess and assign **Issuer Default Ratings (IDR)** for sovereign and central bank entities. It functions as a multiple regression Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) model designed to provide a transparent, data-driven baseline for creditworthiness.

## Core Methodology
The SRM synthesizes data across four primary analytical pillars to generate a baseline score calibrated to the Long-Term Foreign-Currency IDR scale. The weight distribution across these pillars is as follows:

*   **Structural Features (53.7%):** The most heavily weighted component of the model.
*   **Public Finances (19.1%):** Assessment of fiscal health and sustainability.
*   **External Finances (17.3%):** Evaluation of external debt and international obligations.
*   **Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%):** Analysis of broader economic indicators.

## Integration with Qualitative Analysis
While the SRM provides the quantitative foundation, it is complemented by a **Qualitative Overlay (QO)**. This allows analysts to make manual adjustments of up to +/- 2 notches per pillar (with a maximum total adjustment of +/- 3 notches) to account for nuances that the regression model may not fully capture, such as:
*   Geopolitical risks and political stability.
*   Policy credibility and fiscal flexibility.
*   Financial sector health.

## Related Frameworks and Metrics
The SRM operates in conjunction with several other specialized tools and metrics:
*   **Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS):** A screening tool for physical and transition risks; scores of 50 or more by the year 2035 trigger additional scrutiny.
*   **Reserve-Currency Flexibility (RCF) & International Liquidity Ratio (ILR):** Used to determine short-term rating mappings, particularly when the ILR is greater than 100%.
*   **Distressed Debt Exchange (DDE):** Protocols that trigger a move to 'RD' (Restructured/Default) status.
*   **Recovery Ratings (RR):** Assigned to sovereigns rated 'B+' or below to estimate potential creditor recoveries.
