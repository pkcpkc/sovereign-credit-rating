# Human rights protection

Human rights protection constitutes a critical component of the [[Rule of Law]] and institutional framework, serving as a primary indicator of [[Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength]] in sovereign credit assessments. The effectiveness of human rights protections is evaluated through the lens of institutional checks and balances, judicial independence, the functionality of National Human Rights Institutions (NHRIs), and the safeguarding of fundamental freedoms such as access to justice, freedom of information, and media pluralism.

While robust frameworks in countries like [[Austria]] contribute positively to sovereign credit ratings by reinforcing investor confidence, gaps in implementation—such as those observed in [[Italy]] regarding the absence of an NHRI and restrictions on civic space—pose risks to institutional integrity and long-term stability.

## Key Dimensions and Mechanisms

### Institutional Oversight and National Human Rights Institutions (NHRIs)
The existence and operational capacity of independent oversight bodies are central to human rights protection. The status of NHRIs varies significantly across jurisdictions, impacting their ability to provide effective grievance resolution and oversight of public administration.

*   **Austria (Model of Effectiveness):** The [[Ombudsman Board]] serves as the effective National Human Rights Institution. Its strong performance enhances [[Policymaking Stability]] and reinforces the [[Rule of Law]] by providing an independent channel for oversight. Key focus areas include strengthening nomination procedures to ensure continued independence from political influence.
*   **Italy (Institutional Gap):** Despite multiple draft laws remaining pending, no concrete measures have been undertaken to establish a National Human Rights Institution. This absence is cited as a threat to policy credibility and institutional strength, limiting the country's ability to independently monitor and address human rights violations.

### Access to Justice and Legal Frameworks
Access to justice is a fundamental human right that directly impacts the fairness of the [[Judiciary]] and the equitable application of laws. Recent developments highlight divergent trajectories in ensuring this right.

*   **Austria (Expansion of Rights):** The [[Austrian Constitutional Court]] ruled in October 2024 that previous restrictions on legal aid were unconstitutional, mandating legal aid for all administrative court proceedings. However, challenges remain, including a 23% average increase in court fees effective April 1, 2025, which balances fiscal sustainability against affordable access.
*   **Italy (Structural Challenges):** While the High Council for the Judiciary has implemented tasks under the comprehensive justice reform legislation (adopted March 2024) and mandated digital case management systems (APP) in criminal courts as of January 1, 2025, significant hurdles persist.
    *   **Staff Shortages:** Persistent shortages of administrative staff and magistrates (a 17% deficit) hinder the system's capacity.
    *   **Length of Proceedings:** Excessive length of judicial proceedings remains a serious issue, negatively affecting economic growth prospects and investment protection confidence.
    *   **Constitutional Reform Risks:** The draft constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors (approved by the Chamber of Deputies in January 2025) faces strong opposition from judicial bodies, who argue it undermines judicial autonomy and representation, posing a risk to judicial independence.

### Freedom of Information, Transparency, and Civic Space
Transparency is essential for protecting human rights, enabling citizens to hold authorities accountable. Restrictions on civic space and information access are critical monitoring points for [[Accountability]] metrics.

*   **Austria (Strengthening Transparency):** The implementation of the Freedom of Information Act, set to enter into force in September 2025, grants subjective rights to information. This strengthens transparency mechanisms and supports [[Media Pluralism]], reducing the risk of arbitrary governance.
*   **Italy (Concerns over Civic Space and Information Access):**
    *   **Security Law Risks:** The new Security Law (adopted June 2025) increases penalties for protest-related activities, raising stakeholder concerns over its impact on civic space and fundamental freedoms.
    *   **Judicial Information:** Rules regulating the flow of certain judicial information remain a source of concern, limiting journalists' and citizens' ability to hold the justice system accountable.
    *   **Lobbying and Financing:** Draft laws on lobbying and political party financing have not advanced. The lack of an operational lobbying register and persistent practices of channelling donations through political foundations limit transparency and increase corruption risks.

### Media Pluralism and Safety
Media freedom is integral to human rights protection, requiring independent regulation and the safety of journalists.

*   **Italy:** The media regulator ([[AGCOM]]) functions independently and is financially equipped. However, concerns persist regarding:
    *   **Public Service Broadcaster:** Governance independence of [[RAI]] remains under scrutiny, with legislative proposals for reform pending.
    *   **Journalist Safety:** Allegations of spyware use (e.g., Paragon) targeting journalists prompted investigations, and the Council of Europe registered 11 alerts concerning Italy in 2025.
    *   **Defamation Reform:** A draft defamation reform remains stalled, affecting the balance between freedom of expression and personality rights.

## Relevance to Sovereign Credit and Country Risk

The state of human rights protection is assessed under several sovereign credit rating factors, particularly within governance, institutional strength, and geopolitical stability categories.

1.  **Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength:**
    *   **Strengths (Austria):** High levels of perceived judicial independence (86% of the general population) and an effective NHRI indicate a stable democratic framework where rights are respected, supporting a high credit rating.
    *   **Weaknesses (Italy):** The absence of an NHRI and legislative stagnation on lobbying and political financing limit transparency. These gaps pose moderate risks to institutional integrity and investor confidence.

2.  **Accountability and Corruption Prevention:**
    *   **Positive Developments:** In Italy, the [[National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC)]] strengthened human resources and issued guidelines on "revolving doors" and whistleblowing in 2025. The [[Austrian Court of Audit]]'s extended mandates on party finance also contribute positively.
    *   **Risks:** In Italy, public procurement remains a high-risk area for corruption. The lack of progress on lobbying registers and political financing transparency undermines long-term institutional trust.

3.  **Geopolitical and Political Stability:**
    *   **Institutional Strain:** In Italy, the frequent use of urgency decrees (87 in the current legislature) and the new Security Law raise concerns about the separation of powers and the erosion of civic space. These factors can destabilize the political environment and deter foreign investment.
    *   **Legal Stability:** Delays in prosecutorial independence reforms and structural vulnerabilities in judicial appointments (as seen in Austria) or constitutional reforms (as seen in Italy) affect the perceived stability of the legal environment.

4.  **Economic Impact:**
    *   **Judicial Efficiency:** Lengthy judicial proceedings in Italy directly impact growth prospects and the enforcement of contracts. While there is a positive trend in reducing case backlogs, the efficiency gap remains a drag on economic performance.
    *   **Investment Protection:** Over half of companies in Italy express confidence in investment protection, but this is tempered by concerns over rule of law consistency and bureaucratic inefficiencies.
