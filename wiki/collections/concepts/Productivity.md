# Productivity

Productivity represents the efficiency with which inputs (such as labor, capital, and resources) are converted into economic output. In the context of macroeconomic stability and growth, it is a primary driver of long-term standard of living and sustainable GDP expansion.

## Current Context and Challenges
Recent analysis from the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Hungary** highlights significant productivity gaps that hinder economic recovery:

*   **Stagnant Output:** Productivity in certain regions has remained below those of regional peers, characterized by stagnant output over multi-year periods.
*   **Regulatory Distortions:** Heavy state presence and regulatory distortions are identified as primary barriers to firm dynamism and efficient factor mobility.
*   **Structural Barriers:** Key obstacles to enhancing productivity include:
    *   Barriers to factor mobility (the ease with which labor and capital move to where they are most productive).
    *   Governance issues that impede the flow of investment and funding.
    *   Price and margin caps, which are viewed as unsustainable distortions to market mechanisms.

## Strategic Reform Areas
To address these productivity gaps, the following structural reforms are prioritized:

*   **Competition & Governance:** Improving governance to unlock suspended funds and fostering a more competitive business environment.
*   **Factor Mobility:** Removing barriers that prevent resources from being allocated efficiently across the economy.
*   **Energy & Competitiveness:** Transitioning toward diversified energy supplies and phased-out fossil fuel subsidies to bolster long-term industrial competitiveness.
*   **Fiscal Alignment:** Linking productivity improvements with [[Fiscal Adjustment]] and [[Structural Reforms]] to ensure that growth is sustainable and not reliant on temporary subsidies.
