# Integrity Framework

The **Integrity Framework** refers to the systemic structures, legal mechanisms, and institutional oversight designed to ensure transparency, prevent corruption, and maintain the ethical standards of public administration and the judiciary.

## Core Components in the Belgian Context
Based on recent assessments, the integrity framework is characterized by several evolving pillars:

### 1. Anti-Corruption Structures
- **Financial Investigation:** Development of specialized services, such as the planned financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) and a dedicated prosecution service for financial crimes.
- **Police Integrity:** Efforts to standardize integrity policies across disparate local police zones, including the introduction of career-long integrity checks.
- **Asset Transparency:** Systems for mandatory asset declarations, though currently facing challenges regarding verification and public transparency (e.g., sealed envelope systems).

### 2. Institutional Oversight & Transparency
- **Lobbying and Revolving Doors:** Measures intended to prevent conflicts of interest when public officials move into the private sector. Currently, these remain largely non-binding circulars rather than formal legislation.
- **Administrative Transparency:** Frameworks governing access to documents and the transparency of federal actions, balanced against procedural gaps.

### 3. Judicial & Professional Integrity
- **Judicial Independence:** A core component of the integrity framework, involving the transfer of budgetary management from the executive to the judiciary to ensure autonomy.
- **Whistleblower Protection:** Recent legislative developments (e.g., the 2025 whistleblower protection law) provide legal safeguards for individuals reporting misconduct, a critical component of any integrity ecosystem.
