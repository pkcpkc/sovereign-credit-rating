# Wage Indexation

Wage indexation is a mechanism used in Belgium to automatically adjust wages, pensions, and social security benefits in response to changes in the cost of living (inflation). 

## Overview and Impact
In the Belgian economic model, wage indexation serves as a primary tool for maintaining purchasing power. However, it presents significant structural challenges for the national economy:

*   **Labour Costs:** Automatic wage indexation is identified as a primary driver of high labor costs in Belgium, which can impact international competitiveness.
*   **Fiscal Pressure:** The mechanism contributes to fiscal sustainability challenges, as it influences the broader cost of employment and public spending.
*   **Economic Dynamics:** While it protects workers from inflation, the resulting high labor costs are often cited as a barrier to business dynamism and a factor in the country's regulatory complexity.

## Contextual Relations
*   **Labour Market:** Directly influences employment costs and the overall attractiveness of the Belgian labor market.
*   **Fiscal Policy:** Linked to the [[Fiscal-Structural Plan]] and the management of public debt, as high labor costs can constrain government spending and investment.
*   **Inflation:** Serves as a reactive measure to headline inflation, which is projected to remain above the EU average in recent reports.
