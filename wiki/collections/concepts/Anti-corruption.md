# Anti-corruption

Anti-corruption measures and institutional frameworks are critical determinants of **[[Sovereign Credit Rating]]** stability, **[[Fiscal Sustainability]]**, and the overall health of the **[[Business Environment]]**. Effective anti-corruption mechanisms reduce transaction costs, improve **[[Institutional Strength]]**, and enhance investor confidence. Conversely, the erosion of these safeguards leads to regulatory unpredictability, increased **[[External Vulnerability]]**, and significant risks to **[[Macroeconomic Stability]]**.

## Concept Definition & Significance

In the context of country risk analysis and sovereign credit assessment, anti-corruption refers to the legal, administrative, and judicial capacity of a state to prevent, detect, and punish corrupt activities, particularly high-level political and economic corruption. It is a key component of **[[Rule of Law]]** and directly impacts **[[Policy Credibility]]**.

Strong anti-corruption frameworks:
- Ensure transparent **[[Public Procurement]]** processes, maximizing the efficiency of **[[Fiscal Policy]]** spending.
- Protect foreign direct investment (**[[FDI]]**) by guaranteeing a level playing field and legal certainty.
- Strengthen **[[Tax Base]]** integrity by reducing tax evasion and illicit financial flows.
- Enhance the independence of regulatory bodies and civil service, ensuring **[[Policymaking Stability]]**.

## Case Study: Slovakia (2025 Context)

The 2025 Country Report for Slovakia highlights a severe deterioration in anti-corruption standards, identified as a major threat to **[[Institutional Strength]]** and **[[Accountability]]**. This backsliding serves as a cautionary indicator for sovereign credit risk, demonstrating how the dismantling of enforcement agencies correlates with economic stagnation and increased systemic risk.

### Key Threats to Institutional Integrity
*   **Dismantling of Enforcement Agencies**: The dissolution of the **[[National Crime Agency]]** and the **[[Special Prosecution Office]]** has significantly disrupted the capacity to investigate and prosecute high-level corruption cases. This removal of specialized legal tools undermines deterrence and signals a lack of political will to uphold the rule of law.
*   **Legislative Weakening**: Recent legal changes have reduced prison sentences and limitation periods for corruption offenses. This softening of penalties diminishes the severity of repercussions for corrupt actors, further eroding the rule of law.
*   **Erosion of Civil Service Independence**: The dissolution of the **[[Civil Service Council]]** weakened institutional checks on the executive branch, increasing the risk of patronage and politicization within the public administration.

### Impact on Business Environment & Macroeconomic Stability
*   **High Perceived Corruption**: Approximately **85% of companies** in Slovakia perceive a high level of corruption in the business environment. This perception acts as a significant barrier to both domestic and foreign investment, contributing to Slovakia ranking last in the EU for doing business conditions.
*   **Regulatory Unpredictability**: The frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures, often bypassing impact assessments and stakeholder consultations, creates an unstable regulatory environment. This unpredictability increases compliance costs and deters long-term capital allocation.
*   **Public Procurement Risks**: Increased thresholds for low-value contracts have weakened transparency in **[[Public Procurement]]**, raising the risk of fund misallocation and inefficiency. This is particularly detrimental given the critical need for efficient absorption of **[[EU Funding]]** and the **[[Recovery and Resilience Plan]]** (RRP).

### Correlation with Economic Indicators
The degradation of anti-corruption frameworks in Slovakia is linked to several negative macroeconomic outcomes:
*   **Declining FDI**: The lack of legal certainty and high corruption perceptions contribute to the decline in Foreign Direct Investment, which fell to 46% of GDP in 2023.
*   **Reduced Competitiveness**: Administrative burdens and corruption-related inefficiencies exacerbate structural bottlenecks, hindering **[[Labour Productivity]]** growth.
*   **Fiscal Risks**: Weak oversight in public spending and procurement increases the risk of fiscal leakage, complicating efforts to address the high **[[Structural Deficit]]** (5.3% of GDP in 2024).

## Strategic Implications for Sovereign Credit Ratings

Anti-corruption performance is a non-economic factor that heavily influences the **[[Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength]]** pillar of sovereign credit ratings.

1.  **Risk Premium Increase**: Deteriorating anti-corruption standards lead rating agencies to assign higher risk premiums, increasing the cost of sovereign borrowing.
2.  **Investor Confidence**: Lack of transparency and rule of law violations reduce investor confidence, potentially leading to capital flight or reduced inflows, affecting **[[External Liquidity]]**.
3.  **EU Conditionality Risks**: Weak rule of law and anti-corruption mechanisms may trigger EU oversight mechanisms, including the suspension of funds under the **[[Rule of Law]]** conditionality mechanism, directly impacting **[[Fiscal Performance]]**.
