# checks and balances

**Checks and balances** refer to the system of mechanisms within a state's governance framework designed to prevent the concentration of power, ensure accountability, and uphold the rule of law. This concept is a critical component of **Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength** in sovereign credit rating analyses, as robust checks and balances mitigate political risk, enhance the predictability of the regulatory environment, and support long-term **fiscal sustainability** and **financial stability**.

In the context of national governance, effective checks and balances are operationalized through the independence of the judiciary, the autonomy of prosecutorial bodies, the integrity of anti-corruption frameworks, the pluralism of media, the transparency of lobbying and public administration, and the protection of **Civic Space**. A critical additional dimension involves the constraints on executive emergency powers, the transparency of administrative processes (such as case allocation in courts), the effectiveness of citizen-led oversight mechanisms, and the enforcement of international legal obligations (e.g., ECtHR judgments). Weaknesses in these areas can signal higher **geopolitical and political stability** risks, potentially leading to downgrades in credit ratings due to concerns over policy credibility, corruption risks, and the arbitrary application of laws. Furthermore, the erosion of checks and balances—such as through the extensive use of emergency decrees, accelerated legislative procedures without adequate consultation, narrowing of civic space, or constitutional crises regarding the legitimacy of key state institutions—can introduce legal uncertainty and reduce investor confidence.

Recent analysis of European Union member states (Austria, France, Hungary, Italy, Malta, Poland, and Romania) highlights that while legal frameworks may appear strong on paper, practical implementation gaps significantly impact sovereign risk profiles. Issues range from the frequent use of urgency decrees and stalled lobbying reforms in Italy and Austria, to executive overreach via emergency measures in France, Hungary, and Romania, limited progress on institutional accountability frameworks in Malta, a persistent constitutional crisis regarding the legitimacy of key state institutions in Poland, and systemic judicial unpredictability in Romania. These dynamics create divergent risk profiles where institutional erosion directly threatens macroeconomic stability by deterring foreign investment and increasing political risk premiums.

## Sovereign Credit Rating Implications

The efficacy of checks and balances directly impacts **Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength**, a key pillar in sovereign credit rating models:

*   **Institutional Strength & Rule of Law**: Strong judicial independence, transparent appointment processes for independent authorities, and independent oversight bodies (e.g., Constitutional Courts, State Audit Offices) signal a stable democratic framework, supporting high credit ratings. Conversely, politicization of appointments, lack of judicial involvement in key decisions, perceived lack of independence in oversight institutions, or political pressure on magistrates introduces uncertainty and weakens institutional trust. For instance, in Italy, the draft constitutional reform separating judges' and prosecutors' careers has faced strong opposition from judicial bodies, raising concerns about judicial autonomy. In Malta, while perceived judicial independence is high, the efficiency of the justice system is undermined by excessive length of proceedings. In **Poland**, a constitutional crisis persists regarding the legitimacy of the **Constitutional Tribunal**, with the government refusing to publish its judgments, while the appointment of judges by the post-2017 **National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ)** remains disputed, severely undermining institutional trust and legal certainty. In **Romania**, despite progress in implementing Venice Commission recommendations, perceived judicial independence has decreased, and the transparency of judicial promotions and disciplinary sanctions remains under scrutiny, signaling potential fragility in institutional checks.
*   **Executive Constraints & Emergency Powers**: The extent to which executive emergency powers are constrained by legislative and judicial review is a vital check on power. The frequent resort to urgency decrees by the Government, as seen in Italy (87 decrees in the current legislature) and France (Article 49-3), raises concerns about the separation of powers and reduces parliamentary scrutiny. In Hungary, the extensive use of emergency powers to override Acts of Parliament and interfere with merger control rules without judicial review significantly increases **Political Risk**. In **Romania**, the frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs) — with 199 adopted in 2024 — contributes to legislative unpredictability and fiscal uncertainty, particularly when used to amend fiscal codes or classification thresholds. These practices undermine legal certainty and signal weak institutional checks. In **Poland**, the government’s continued refusal to recognize the Constitutional Tribunal’s authority represents a fundamental breakdown in executive-judicial checks.
*   **Anti-Corruption & Fiscal Integrity**: Effective anti-corruption strategies, robust asset declaration systems, and transparent lobbying frameworks reduce the risk of resource misallocation and illicit financial flows. Weaknesses in these areas, such as the inability of integrity authorities to access data or a lack of convictions in high-level corruption cases, can lead to rent-seeking behavior, distorting **public procurement integrity** and undermining **fiscal performance**. In Italy, despite ANAC strengthening human resources, public procurement remains a high-risk area for corruption. In Malta, the Court of Justice of the EU ruled the investor citizenship scheme illegal in 2025, indicating past regulatory weaknesses. In **Poland**, public procurement remains highly vulnerable to corruption with significant single-bid rates, and broad immunities for top executives hinder criminal accountability. In **Romania**, a major threat to anti-corruption efficacy is the High Court of Cassation and Justice’s interpretation of *lex mitior*, which has led to the discontinuation of numerous corruption cases and annulment of convictions due to expired limitation periods. Additionally, a Constitutional Court ruling weakened the asset declaration system by deeming requirements for spouses’ and children’s assets unconstitutional, potentially creating impunity gaps and undermining **Policy Credibility**.
*   **Legislative Stability & Transparency**: The balance between legislative efficiency and democratic consultation impacts **Policymaking Stability**. Frequent late-stage substantive amendments that bypass public consultation, or the introduction of laws without adequate stakeholder engagement, signals higher political risk and policy unpredictability. In Italy, the use of law decrees and confidence motions may affect the separation of powers. In Hungary, late-stage amendments introduced by the Committee on Legislation bypass public consultation. In **Romania**, public consultations are often criticized as ineffective "box-ticking" exercises, and no progress has been made on introducing lobbying rules for Members of Parliament. In Malta, slow law-making processes and sudden legislative changes affect business confidence. In **Poland**, frequent changes in legislation affecting legal certainty pose threats, although the introduction of mandatory impact assessments for draft laws represents a positive step toward transparency.
*   **Accountability & Transparency**: Robust oversight bodies and freedom of information regimes enhance government accountability. Delays in implementing transparency reforms (e.g., lobbying registers, asset declarations for MPs) or the existence of weak sanctions for non-compliance pose risks to policy credibility. In Italy, no concrete measures have been undertaken to establish a National Human Rights Institution (NHRI), and draft laws on lobbying and political party financing have not advanced. In Malta, ministers' asset declarations remain unpublished. In **Poland**, the **Ombudsperson** continues to play a key role in checks and balances, and an operational whistleblower protection law has been enforced. However, limited transparency in political party financing remains a threat. In **Romania**, pending legislation to improve transparency of political party financing and insufficient information on media ownership beyond the audiovisual sector weaken accountability mechanisms. Furthermore, the accreditation process for National Human Rights Institutions (Romanian Institute for Human Rights and People's Advocate) is stalled due to questions regarding independence and resources, undermining external oversight.
*   **Legislative Stability & Civic Engagement**: The balance between legislative efficiency and democratic consultation impacts **Policymaking Stability**. Over-reliance on accelerated procedures or the exclusion of civil society from the law-making process can signal higher political risk. A "narrowed" civic space, characterized by budgetary restrictions on NGOs, intimidation of human rights defenders, or investigations into CSOs for alleged "foreign influence," weakens the societal feedback loop essential for long-term governance stability. In Italy, the new Security Law adopted in June 2025 raises stakeholder concerns over its impact on civic space. In Malta, civil society space is assessed as "narrowed." In **Poland**, the civic space was re-rated from "obstructed" to "narrowed" in March 2025. In **Romania**, civil society space is considered narrowed, with CSOs facing administrative barriers and attacks, although a Strategy for Open Government 2025-2030 was adopted to address these challenges.
*   **International Compliance & Pending Judgments**: The ability of a state to implement judgments from international bodies, such as the **European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR)**, is a key metric of institutional compliance. Delays signal weak institutional compliance with international obligations and can lead to reputational damage and financial penalties. In **Poland**, the number of pending leading judgments from the ECtHR increased to 54 as of June 2025, highlighting systemic delays in justice and compliance. In Malta, pending ECtHR judgments increased to 16 by mid-2025. In **Romania**, while the number of pending ECtHR judgments has decreased, the systemic issues regarding the statute of limitations and EU law compatibility remain under review by the Court of Justice of the European Union, posing ongoing legal risks.

## Case Study: Austria (2025 Rule of Law Report)

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Austria provides a detailed assessment of how checks and balances function within a high-income EU member state, highlighting both strengths and persistent vulnerabilities that influence its sovereign credit profile.

### Strengths in Institutional Checks and Balances

*   **High Judicial Independence Perception**: Austria maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence among the general public (86% in 2025, up from 82% in 2024). This strong public trust supports **Policy Credibility** and contributes to a stable investment environment.
*   **Effective Oversight Bodies**: The **Ombudsman Board** (National Human Rights Institution) functions effectively, providing a strong mechanism for administrative accountability. Similarly, the **Austrian Court of Audit** performs extended mandates on party finance, enhancing transparency in political financing.
*   **Independent Media Regulation**: The media regulator, **KommAustria**, operates with strong independence, supporting **Media Pluralism** and freedom. Reforms to the management bodies of the public service broadcaster (ORF) in March 2025, following a Constitutional Court ruling, further enhanced independence from political influence.
*   **Digital Justice Initiatives**: The advancement of "Justice 3.0" and the compulsory online publication of judgments (expanded to Higher Regional Courts in January 2025) improve transparency and efficiency in the justice system, supporting **Policymaking Stability**.

### Threats and Vulnerabilities

*   **Judicial Appointment Procedures**: A significant weakness is the lack of progress in introducing judicial involvement in the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents. The current varied procedures lack consistent judicial input, raising concerns against European standards. This gap undermines the perceived neutrality of the judiciary and poses a risk to **Institutional Strength**.
*   **Prosecutorial Autonomy**: The reform to establish an independent Federal Prosecution Office has seen limited progress. The Minister of Justice retains the power to issue instructions in individual cases (31 cases examined in 2024). An independent investigative commission confirmed past instances of political influence, highlighting urgent risks to **Rule of Law** and **Anti-Corruption** integrity. This vulnerability can deter foreign investment and increase political risk premiums.
*   **Weak Lobbying Transparency**: The lobbying framework remains weak, dating back to 2013, with no comprehensive transparency, supervision, or enforcement mechanisms. The lack of a robust transparency register and weak sanctions create opportunities for undue influence, threatening **Policy Credibility** and **Public Procurement Integrity**.
*   **Parliamentary Transparency Gaps**: There is limited progress on asset and interest declaration rules for Members of Parliament. Current obligations exclude a full range of assets and interests, and no monitoring or sanction mechanisms exist. This opacity weakens **Accountability** and increases the risk of conflicts of interest.
*   **State Advertising Distribution**: State advertising spending more than doubled in 2024 to EUR 417.9 million, raising concerns about political proximity and fair distribution. Lack of effective enforcement mechanisms for fair distribution threatens **Media Pluralism** and can be perceived as a tool for political favoritism, undermining **Policy Credibility**.

## Case Study: France (2025 Rule of Law Report)

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on France highlights a complex landscape where strong legal frameworks coexist with practical challenges in implementation and political pressure, influencing its sovereign credit profile through risks related to **Legislative Stability** and **Civic Space**.

### Strengths in Institutional Checks and Balances

*   **Independent Regulatory Bodies**: France maintains credible and independent regulatory institutions, including the media regulator **ARCOM**, the anti-corruption agency **AFA**, and the transparency authority **HATVP**. The **National Financial Prosecutor (PNF)** operates effectively in combating financial crimes.
*   **Judicial Independence Safeguards**: The implementation of the 2023 reform on the status of magistrates aims to enhance judicial independence. The **Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM)** actively defends judicial autonomy, and the President of the Republic has reaffirmed support for the judiciary.
*   **Transparency Initiatives**: Progress has been made in media ownership transparency via a national database. The HATVP has increased staffing to handle rising asset declarations, and the 2024-2029 National Anti-Corruption Plan is expected to strengthen integrity frameworks.
*   **Oversight Mechanisms**: The **Defender of Rights** and the **Court of Audit** provide effective oversight. Whistleblowing mechanisms are seeing increased usage, and ethical codes for Parliament, Government, and magistrates have been strengthened.

### Threats and Vulnerabilities

*   **Political Pressure on Judiciary**: Despite reforms, perceived judicial independence remains average. Magistrates face severe personal attacks and threats, particularly following rulings on national elections. This political polarization poses a risk to **Institutional Strength** and could undermine investor confidence in the rule of law.
*   **Legislative Stability Concerns**: The frequent use of accelerated legislative procedures and Article 49-3 of the Constitution to bypass parliamentary scrutiny raises concerns about **Legislative Stability** and policy predictability. While businesses welcome simplified regulations, the reduction in citizen consultation opportunities (e.g., interruption of the Agora application) signals a weakening of democratic feedback loops.
*   **Lobbying and Executive Transparency Gaps**: A significant vulnerability is that lobbying disclosure requirements do not apply to officials at the top-executive level. This gap creates opportunities for undue influence and threatens **Policy Credibility**.
*   **Narrowing Civic Space**: The civic space is rated as "narrowed." Civil society organizations face budgetary restrictions, and human rights defenders report intimidation and online harassment. The **National Consultative Commission on Human Rights (CNC DH)** lacks sufficient resources to fully execute its mandate. This constrains the ability of civil society to act as an external check on government power, increasing long-term **Political Risk**.
*   **Media Governance and Access**: Debates over public broadcasting reform (France Médias holding company) raise concerns about editorial autonomy and funding stability. Journalists face difficulties accessing administrative documents despite legal rights, and issues with police violence during protests remain. These factors threaten **Media Pluralism** and the flow of accurate information essential for market discipline.

## Case Study: Hungary (2025 Rule of Law Report)

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Hungary highlights a stagnation or deterioration in key rule of law areas, presenting a stark contrast to the high-income EU averages. The extensive erosion of checks and balances in Hungary introduces significant risks to **Institutional Strength**, **Policy Credibility**, and **Geopolitical Stability**, directly impacting its sovereign credit profile through heightened legal uncertainty and political risk.

### Threats and Vulnerabilities in Institutional Checks and Balances

*   **Erosion of Constitutional Oversight**: Institutional checks are weakened by the perceived lack of independence in key oversight bodies, including the **Constitutional Court**, the **State Audit Office**, and the **Competition Authority**. These bodies are increasingly viewed as applying rules selectively or lacking functional independence from the executive, undermining the system of checks necessary for **Policy Credibility**.
*   **Abuse of Emergency Powers**: The government continues to extensively use emergency powers to override Acts of Parliament, a practice that undermines legal certainty. Furthermore, the government can interfere with merger control rules via decrees of "strategic importance," which are not subject to judicial review. This concentration of power and the bypassing of legislative and judicial checks significantly increase **Political Risk** and deter foreign investment.
*   **Weak Anti-Corruption Framework & Accountability**: Hungary’s corruption perception remains high (CPI score 41/100). There is no robust track record of prosecuting high-level corruption cases. The **Integrity Authority** faces continued obstacles in accessing data and fulfilling oversight duties, while the asset declaration system lacks effective oversight and systematic follow-up for non-compliance. These weaknesses distort **Public Procurement Integrity** and undermine **Fiscal Integrity**.
*   **Deteriorating Judicial Independence & Case Allocation**: While the **Kúria** (Supreme Court) enforces uniform case law, the broader judiciary faces significant pressures. The **National Judicial Council** experienced severe political friction, culminating in the resignation of its President in December 2024 following protests against judicial reforms. A critical deficiency remains in the transparency of case allocation in lower-instance courts, where discretion allowed to court managers prevents verification of impartiality. This lack of transparency in administrative judicial processes weakens **Institutional Strength**.
*   **Legislative Instability & Bypassing Consultation**: The law-making process is characterized by frequent late-stage substantive amendments introduced by the Committee on Legislation, bypassing public consultation. Key legislative drafts, such as those regarding judicial age limits, were adopted without adequate consultation with judicial stakeholders, leading to the withdrawal of the National Judicial Council from reform agreements. This undermines **Policymaking Stability**.
*   **Narrowing Civic Space & Media Pluralism**: The environment for civil society organizations (CSOs) has deteriorated, with the **Sovereignty Protection Office** launching investigations into anti-corruption CSOs and those receiving EU funds. A proposed draft law on "transparency in public life" aims to restrict foreign funding for entities deemed to endanger sovereignty, threatening critical voices. In the media landscape, the **National Media and Infocommunications Authority (NMHH)** lacks functional independence, and state advertising is disproportionately channeled to pro-government outlets. Journalists face investigations for alleged "foreign interests," weakening the media's role as an external check on power.
*   **Regulatory Pressure on Foreign Investment**: Foreign companies, particularly from other EU Member States, face intensified regulatory pressure in strategic sectors through special taxes, export restrictions, and discriminatory practices. This regulatory capture, facilitated by weak checks on executive discretion, creates legal uncertainty that affects business operations and investment protection, posing risks to **Economic Strength** and **Growth Prospects**.

## Case Study: Italy (2025 Rule of Law Report)

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Italy outlines progress and persistent challenges across the justice system, anti-corruption framework, media pluralism, and institutional checks and balances. While Italy has made strides in digitalizing the justice system and strengthening anti-corruption resources, significant vulnerabilities in executive restraint, lobbying transparency, and civic space protections persist, impacting its sovereign credit profile through heightened political and institutional risks.

### Strengths in Institutional Checks and Balances

*   **Judicial Independence Perception**: Italy has seen an increase in high perceived judicial independence among the general public and companies. The **High Council for the Judiciary** has actively taken forward tasks under the implementing legislation for the comprehensive justice reform, signaling a commitment to institutional strengthening.
*   **Independent Media Regulation**: The media regulator, **AGCOM**, functions independently and is financially equipped, supporting **Media Pluralism**. The public service broadcaster, **RAI**, retained its license fee levels in 2025, providing financial stability, although governance reforms remain pending.
*   **Anti-Corruption Institutional Capacity**: The **National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC)** has strengthened its human resources and updated the National Anti-Corruption Plan with new guidelines for small municipalities, revolving doors, and whistleblowing. These actions enhance the operational capacity of anti-corruption frameworks, supporting **Fiscal Integrity**.
*   **Constitutional Court Oversight**: The **Constitutional Court** declared the law abrogating the offence of abuse of public office compliant with the Constitution in May 2025, providing legal clarity and reinforcing the court’s role as a final arbiter of legislative conformity.

### Threats and Vulnerabilities

*   **Executive Overreach via Urgency Decrees**: A major concern is the frequent resort to urgency decrees by the Government (87 decrees in the current legislature) and the use of confidence motions. These practices raise significant concerns about the **separation of powers**, as they bypass standard parliamentary scrutiny and legislative debate, undermining **Legislative Stability** and increasing **Political Risk**.
*   **Stalled Legislative Transparency Reforms**: Despite recommendations, no concrete measures have been undertaken to establish a **National Human Rights Institution (NHRI)**, with five draft laws remaining pending. Furthermore, draft laws on **lobbying regulation** and **political party financing** have not advanced. There is no operational lobbying register, and the practice of channelling donations through political foundations remains unaddressed, creating opportunities for undue influence and undermining **Policy Credibility**.
*   **Judicial Reform Controversies**: A draft constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors was approved by the Chamber of Deputies in January 2025 but faces strong opposition from the **High Council for the Judiciary** and magistrates’ associations. Critics argue it undermines judicial autonomy and representation. Additionally, persistent shortages of administrative staff in courts hinder judicial efficiency, and the length of judicial proceedings remains a serious issue, affecting **Growth Prospects** and investor confidence in dispute resolution.
*   **Narrowing Civic Space and Security Law**: The new **Security Law**, adopted in June 2025, increases penalties for protest-related activities and raises stakeholder concerns over its impact on **Civic Space** and fundamental freedoms. The High Court of Cassation issued a report on the law in June 2025, highlighting potential constitutional issues. This legislative move constrains the ability of civil society to act as an external check on government power.
*   **Media Safety and Information Access**: Although AGCOM is independent, concerns persist regarding **RAI’s** governance independence and access to judicial information. Journalists face ongoing challenges, including restrictions on accessing judicial information and threats; the Council of Europe registered 11 alerts concerning Italy in 2025. Allegations of spyware use targeting journalists prompted an investigation by the Parliamentary Committee for the Security of the Republic, threatening **Media Pluralism** and the free flow of information essential for market discipline.

## Case Study: Malta (2025 Rule of Law Report)

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Malta highlights mixed progress across the four key pillars of the rule of law: justice, anti-corruption, media pluralism, and checks and balances. While perceived judicial independence remains high, significant vulnerabilities in accountability mechanisms, anti-corruption enforcement, and civic space persist, impacting its sovereign credit profile through risks related to **Institutional Strength** and **Policy Credibility**.

### Strengths in Institutional Checks and Balances

*   **High Perceived Judicial Independence**: Malta maintains high perceived judicial independence among the general public (69%) and companies (63%). This supports a stable investment environment and confidence in legal protections.
*   **Prosecutorial Independence Reforms**: The transfer of prosecutorial powers for serious offenses to the Office of the Attorney General was completed in September 2024, enhancing prosecutorial independence and centralizing authority for non-summary cases.
*   **Constitutional Reform for Judicial Appointment**: The government tabled a comprehensive constitutional reform on May 13, 2025, concerning the justice sector, which includes involving the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice. This move strengthens checks on executive power over the judiciary.
*   **Access to Official Documents**: A reliable review mechanism for access to official documents is provided by the Data Protection and Information Commissioner, supporting transparency and accountability.

### Threats and Vulnerabilities

*   **Weak Anti-Corruption Enforcement**: Despite increased resources in investigative authorities, there is no robust track record of final judgments in high-level corruption cases. The Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC) has yet to demonstrate concrete results, citing insufficient resources and operational readiness, having never referred a case for prosecution. The Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) score dropped to 46/100, with 51% of companies perceiving corruption as a problem in doing business.
*   **Inadequate Integrity Measures for Politicians**: Integrity measures for high-level politicians remain limited. Ministers' asset declarations remain unpublished, and MPs are not covered by public administration conflict of interest rules. This opacity weakens **Accountability** and increases the risk of conflicts of interest, undermining **Policy Credibility**.
*   **Erosion of Citizen Oversight Mechanisms**: A controversial reform (Bill 125) approved in April 2025 increased the threshold for citizens to trigger magisterial inquiries. Stakeholders criticized this change as reducing accountability for high-level offenses and weakening a key citizen-led check on executive and judicial power.
*   **Pending Implementation of International Judgments**: The number of pending European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) leading judgments in Malta increased to 16 by June 2025. Delays in implementing these judgments undermine the rule of law and signal weak institutional compliance with international obligations.
*   **Narrowing Civic Space & Legislative Transparency**: Civil society space is assessed as "narrowed," with draft regulatory frameworks for NGOs under preparation. No formal framework for public participation in the legislative process has been introduced, and draft legislation for a National Human Rights Institution remains unbundled in Parliament. This limits the societal feedback loop essential for long-term governance stability.
*   **Justice System Efficiency Issues**: Excessive length of proceedings (e.g., 454 days for civil/commercial cases, 713 days for criminal cases in 2023) and backlogs negatively impact business confidence and legal certainty, posing risks to **Growth Prospects** and **Economic Strength**.
*   **Regulatory Weaknesses in Financial Sector**: The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) ruled Malta’s investor citizenship scheme illegal in April 2025, characterizing it as the "commercialisation" of Union citizenship. This highlights past regulatory weaknesses in financial sector oversight and checks on executive discretion regarding citizenship laws.

## Case Study: Poland (2025 Rule of Law Report)

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Poland highlights significant efforts by the Polish Government to address long-standing rule of law concerns, particularly regarding judicial independence, as outlined in the Action Plan on the Rule of Law. However, a deep constitutional crisis and persistent structural weaknesses continue to pose substantial risks to **Policy Credibility** and **Institutional Strength**.

### Strengths and Progress in Checks and Balances

*   **Legislative Transparency Improvements**: Poland strengthened rules for legislative consultations, introducing mandatory impact assessments for draft laws. This enhances the predictability of regulatory changes and supports **Policymaking Stability**.
*   **Ombudsperson Oversight**: The Ombudsperson continues to play a key role in checks and balances, providing an independent avenue for citizen grievance and administrative oversight.
*   **Operational Whistleblower Protection**: The whistleblower protection law has been largely enforced since September 2024, providing stronger mechanisms for internal accountability and reporting of misconduct.
*   **Progress on Prosecutorial Independence**: Significant progress has been made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General, a key step in ensuring the functional independence of the prosecution service from political executive control.
*   **EPPO Operationalization**: The **European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)** became fully operational in Poland in January 2025, adding a layer of supranational prosecutorial oversight for crimes against the EU budget.

### Threats and Vulnerabilities

*   **Constitutional Crisis & Judicial Legitimacy**: A major threat to **Institutional Strength** is the ongoing constitutional crisis regarding the legitimacy of the **Constitutional Tribunal**. The government continues to refuse to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, asserting the body lacks legitimacy. Legislation addressing the **National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ)** and the Constitutional Tribunal, adopted by Parliament, remains pending before the Constitutional Tribunal due to a presidential referral. This stalemate creates profound legal uncertainty and undermines the finality of constitutional review.
*   **Disputed Judicial Appointments**: The status of judges appointed by the post-2017 NCJ remains a critical vulnerability. While a draft law proposes a classification system to address the status of ~1,600 such judges, the dispute over their legitimacy continues to erode public trust in the judiciary. The proposed dismantling of the Supreme Court’s Chamber of Extraordinary Control is a positive step, but the broader independence of the judiciary remains compromised by past reforms.
*   **Weak Anti-Corruption Framework & Immunities**: Despite the establishment of a new Central Office for Combating Corruption within the police, Poland lacks a comprehensive anti-corruption strategy and lobbying rules. Broad immunities for top executives and impunity clauses in legislation hinder high-level corruption prosecutions, creating a risk of rent-seeking and undermining **Policy Credibility**.
*   **Narrowing Civic Space**: Although the civic space rating improved from "obstructed" to "narrowed" in March 2025, challenges persist. Negative rhetoric against NGOs, operational restrictions at the border, and the need for further legal safeguards limit the ability of civil society to act as an external check on government power.
*   **Pending ECtHR Judgments**: The number of pending leading judgments from the **European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR)** increased to 54 as of June 2025. This high backlog signals systemic delays in justice and weak institutional compliance with international human rights obligations, increasing legal and reputational risks for investors.
*   **Public Procurement Vulnerabilities**: Public procurement remains highly vulnerable to corruption, with significant single-bid rates and instances of bid-rigging. While investigations are ongoing, systemic transparency issues persist, posing risks to **Economic Strength** and the efficient use of public funds.

## Case Study: Romania (2025 Rule of Law Report)

The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Romania highlights significant progress in addressing judicial independence concerns, particularly through the completion of processes related to the Venice Commission’s recommendations on the Justice Laws. However, challenges persist in several key areas, including legislative unpredictability, weaknesses in anti-corruption enforcement due to judicial interpretations, and narrowing civic space, which collectively impact its sovereign credit profile.

### Strengths in Institutional Checks and Balances

*   **Judicial Independence Progress**: Significant progress was made in completing the process to implement Venice Commission recommendations on the Justice Laws. Legislative steps are being explored to extend the mandates of high-ranking prosecutors from three to five years, enhancing stability. The Ministry of Justice has shown openness to ensuring judicial independence in the reform of the IT system for courts, addressing concerns about centralized data access.
*   **Anti-Corruption Strategy Implementation**: The 2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy implementation is on track, with a new strategy under preparation. The National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA) maintains a stable occupancy rate and continues to secure convictions in high-level cases, demonstrating operational capacity in combating corruption.
*   **Improved Transparency in Judicial Sanctions**: There has been improved transparency of case-law on disciplinary sanctions in the judiciary, and the National Integrity Agency (ANI) maintains results in managing conflicts of interest, supporting **Accountability**.

### Threats and Vulnerabilities

*   **Legislative Unpredictability & Emergency Powers**: The frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs) — with 199 adopted in 2024 — contributes to legislative unpredictability and fiscal uncertainty. This is a primary concern for businesses and investors, undermining **Policymaking Stability**. GEOs have also been used to amend fiscal codes and classification thresholds, adding to economic uncertainty.
*   **Judicial Interpretation of Statute of Limitations**: A critical development involves the High Court of Cassation and Justice’s interpretation of *lex mitior*, which has led to the discontinuation of numerous corruption cases and annulment of convictions due to expired limitation periods. This case-law, extending beyond the 2018-2022 legal void, raises systemic risks regarding impunity and EU law compatibility, potentially weakening the deterrent effect of anti-corruption frameworks and impacting **Policy Credibility**.
*   **Weakened Asset Declaration System**: A Constitutional Court ruling deemed the requirement to declare spouses’ and children’s assets unconstitutional, weakening the transparency of the asset declaration system. This reduces the effectiveness of conflict of interest checks and accountability mechanisms for high-level politicians.
*   **Media Freedom & Pluralism Risks**: No progress was made in enhancing the independent governance and editorial independence of public service media. Untransparent political advertising and financing of private media by political parties have increased, affecting media independence. Journalists continue to face harassment, threats, and political pressure, weakening the media's role as an external check on power.
*   **Weak Public Consultations & Lobbying Rules**: Public consultations are often criticized as ineffective "box-ticking" exercises, limiting genuine civic engagement in the legislative process. No progress has been made on introducing lobbying rules for Members of Parliament, and legislation to improve transparency in political party financing remains pending, creating opportunities for undue influence.
*   **Narrowing Civic Space & NHRI Accreditation**: Civil society space is considered narrowed, with CSOs facing administrative barriers and attacks. The accreditation process for National Human Rights Institutions (Romanian Institute for Human Rights and People's Advocate) is stalled due to questions from GANHRI regarding independence and resources. The Romanian Institute for Human Rights faces severe budget cuts and staffing shortages, risking its functionality as an oversight body.
