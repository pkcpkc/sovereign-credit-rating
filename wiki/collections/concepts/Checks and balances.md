# Checks and Balances

**Checks and Balances** refers to the structural mechanisms within a state's governance framework designed to prevent the concentration of power, ensure mutual accountability among branches of government, and safeguard fundamental rights. In the context of **Belgium** and its sovereign credit rating factors, the effectiveness of these mechanisms is a critical determinant of institutional strength, policy predictability, and compliance with international legal obligations.

## Institutional Framework and Governance Strength

In Belgium, the integrity of checks and balances is evidenced by high perceived **Judicial Independence**, with 64% of the general public and 72% of businesses rating independence highly. This institutional trust supports a strong legal foundation for independent regulatory bodies, including media regulators (e.g., **VRM**, **CSA**, **Medienrat**) and a robust competition authority. These independent entities serve as crucial counterweights to executive overreach, particularly in sectors like digital services and market competition.

However, the system faces significant structural threats:
*   **Fragmentation**: The legal framework is fragmented between federal and regional levels, complicating unified enforcement of checks.
*   **Executive-Legislative Friction**: The absence of binding lobbying reforms and stalled political party financing updates indicate weak legislative oversight over executive influence. The new government's indecision on lobbying draft rules and "revolving doors" regulations highlights gaps in preventing conflict of interest.
*   **Integrity Gaps**: Monitoring of integrity policies for ministers and members of parliament is weak, lacking robust enforcement mechanisms. While asset declarations are submitted by 99% of officials, verification is largely limited to criminal investigations, reducing transparency and the deterrent effect of these checks.

## Judicial Oversight and Compliance

A core function of checks and balances is the enforcement of final rulings. In Belgium, compliance with final national court rulings and judgments from the **European Court of Human Rights** is identified as a weak spot. The government remains under ongoing supervision by the **Committee of Ministers** of the **Council of Europe** for implementing structural judgments, such as *Camara v. Belgium*.

The rate of implementing leading ECHR judgments stands at 70%, with some cases pending for over a decade. This non-compliance poses a direct threat to **institutional and governance strength** factors in sovereign credit analysis, signaling potential risks in policy predictability and the rule of law.

## Civic Space and Political Accountability

Checks and balances extend to the protection of **Civic Space** and the right to demonstrate. Recent developments indicate a "narrowing" of civic space, with concerns over disproportionate judicial measures against protesters. The **Constitutional Court** has annulled some restrictive provisions regarding urgent court orders challenging strikes, demonstrating a functional check on executive-imposed limitations. However, legislative obstacles to the right to demonstrate persist, and the use of Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation (SLAPPs) remains a concern, potentially chilling public discourse and media freedom.

## Role of the Federal Human Rights Institute

The **Federal Human Rights Institute** acts as a key institutional check, raising alerts regarding budget cuts to equality bodies (e.g., **Unia**). These cuts threaten the operational capacity of oversight mechanisms, potentially undermining broader human rights protection frameworks and the balance between state power and individual rights.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit and Risk Analysis

From a sovereign credit rating perspective, the efficacy of checks and balances directly influences **political and geopolitical risk** assessments:
*   **Strengths**: High judicial independence and independent regulatory authorities contribute to a stable investment climate and low conflict risk.
*   **Threats**: Fragmented legal frameworks, poor compliance with international court rulings, and weak integrity enforcement (lobbying, revolving doors) increase political risk. These factors can lead to policy uncertainty and undermine the credibility of fiscal and structural reforms, negatively impacting **policy predictability** and long-term fiscal sustainability.
