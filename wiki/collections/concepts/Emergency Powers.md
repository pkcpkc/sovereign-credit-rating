# Emergency Powers

Emergency powers refer to the legal framework and executive authority granted to a government to bypass or override standard legislative procedures, judicial oversight, or constitutional norms during periods of perceived crisis. 

## Current Context: Hungary
In the context of the 2025 Rule of Law Report for Hungary, the use of emergency powers is characterized by:

*   **State of Danger:** The government has maintained a prolonged "state of danger," which serves as the legal vehicle for exercising these powers.
*   **Legislative Overrides:** Under this status, the Government has the authority to override Acts of Parliament. This creates significant legal uncertainty for both private businesses and the integrity of the single market.
*   **Impact on Rule of Law:** The extensive and prolonged use of these powers is cited as a primary factor undermining legal certainty and the standard law-making process.
*   **Institutional Erosion:** The use of these powers is often linked to a broader trend of reduced public consultation on key legislative drafts and the introduction of substantive amendments at late stages of the legislative process.
