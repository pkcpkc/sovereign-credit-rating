# Emergency Powers

**Emergency Powers** refer to exceptional executive authorities granted to governments to address crises, often allowing for the bypassing of standard legislative procedures, judicial review, or constitutional checks and balances. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the extent, duration, and legal basis of emergency powers are critical indicators of **[[Geopolitical and Political Stability]]** and **[[Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength]]**.

## Role in Sovereign Credit Risk and Macroeconomic Stability

The use of emergency powers is a significant threat factor in sovereign credit rating models when it undermines **legal certainty** and weakens institutional oversight. According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report on Hungary, the extensive and prolonged use of emergency powers by the government serves as a primary geopolitical threat, directly impacting the country's risk profile through several mechanisms:

### 1. Erosion of Legal Certainty and Checks and Balances
The European Commission highlights that the Hungarian government continues to extensively use emergency powers to override Acts of Parliament. This practice:
- **Undermines Legal Certainty:** By allowing decrees to supersede legislative acts, the state creates an unpredictable regulatory environment. This lack of consistency increases the risk premium for investors and lenders.
- **Weakens Institutional Checks:** The bypassing of parliamentary scrutiny and public consultation reduces the effectiveness of institutional checks and balances, including the independence of the Constitutional Court and oversight bodies. This is categorized as a threat to **policy credibility**.

### 2. Impact on Foreign Investment and Business Environment
Emergency powers are frequently utilized to implement regulatory pressure on foreign companies and strategic sectors. Specific risks include:
- **Discriminatory Regulatory Pressure:** The government can use decrees of "strategic importance" to interfere with merger control rules, which are not subject to judicial review. This leads to de-investment by foreign companies and restricts export capabilities.
- **Asset Nationalization Risks:** The legal uncertainty associated with emergency decrees facilitates the nationalization of assets in strategic sectors, threatening the security of property rights and investment protection.

### 3. Policy Making Stability and Legislative Process
The reliance on emergency powers correlates with a broader deterioration in policymaking stability:
- **Bypassing Consultation:** Key legislative drafts are often implemented via emergency decrees or late-stage amendments without public consultation, reducing policy legitimacy and increasing the likelihood of abrupt, disruptive changes.
- **Override of Parliamentary Acts:** The use of government decrees to override parliamentary legislation diminishes the role of the legislature, concentrating power in the executive branch and reducing the transparency of decision-making processes.

## Case Study: Hungary (2025 Context)

In the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the European Commission identifies the use of emergency powers as a central component of the deterioration in Hungary’s rule of law framework. Key observations include:

- **Override of Legislation:** Emergency powers are used to override Acts of Parliament, creating a hierarchy where executive decrees hold superior practical authority to legislative statutes.
- **Lack of Judicial Review:** Decrees issued under the guise of strategic importance or emergency measures often fall outside the scope of judicial review, leaving businesses and individuals without legal recourse against state actions.
- **Correlation with Civic Space Restrictions:** The legal framework enabled by emergency powers facilitates increased scrutiny of civil society organizations (CSOs) and media outlets, particularly those receiving foreign funding, under the pretext of protecting sovereignty.

## Implications for Country Risk Analysis

For analysts and credit rating agencies, the persistent use of emergency powers signals:
- **High Political Risk:** Indicates a concentration of power and reduced accountability, increasing the potential for policy shocks.
- **Weak Contract Enforcement:** Suggests that legal frameworks may be applied selectively or overridden, undermining the enforceability of contracts and property rights.
- **Investment Deterrent:** Creates a hostile environment for foreign direct investment (FDI), particularly in strategic sectors, leading to capital flight and reduced economic growth prospects.
