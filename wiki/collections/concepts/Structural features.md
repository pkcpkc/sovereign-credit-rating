# Structural Features

**Structural Features** constitute the first and most heavily weighted pillar of the [[Fitch Ratings]] Sovereign Rating Model (SRM), carrying a weight of 53.7%. This pillar serves as a foundational assessment of a sovereign's institutional framework, political stability, and economic scale, capturing the "capacity" aspect of credit risk through quantitative proxies for governance and structural economic attributes.

## Core Components and Indicators

The SRM quantifies Structural Features using 18 variables, with this pillar specifically incorporating the following key indicators:

*   **Governance Indicators**: Derived from the [[World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators]], these metrics assess the quality of institutions, rule of law, and regulatory effectiveness. They are critical for evaluating political risk and institutional strength.
*   **GDP per Capita**: A proxy for the standard of living and fiscal capacity, influencing the government's ability to generate revenue and manage social obligations.
*   **Share in World GDP**: Reflects the country's economic size and global integration, providing context for systemic importance and diversification.
*   **Years Since Default/Restructuring**: A historical marker of credit events, measuring the distance from the last sovereign default or debt restructuring, which significantly impacts current risk perception.
*   **Broad Money Supply**: Used as a proxy for financial depth and the government's potential capacity to create domestic liquidity, though often adjusted via the Qualitative Overlay (QO) for more nuanced analysis.

## Analytical Framework and Impact

Structural Features act as the baseline for sovereign credit assessments, setting the initial rating range before adjustments for macroeconomic performance, public finances, and external finances. The high weight of this pillar underscores Fitch's view that strong institutions and political stability are primary determinants of long-term creditworthiness.

### Qualitative Overlay (QO) Adjustments
While the SRM provides a quantitative starting point, analysts apply a Qualitative Overlay to adjust for factors not fully captured by the model. For Structural Features, this includes:
*   **Political Stability**: Assessing risks related to leadership transitions, social unrest, or policy unpredictability.
*   **Institutional Strength**: Evaluating the independence of central banks, judicial systems, and regulatory bodies.
*   **Geopolitical Risks**: Considering external pressures that may impact governance or economic sovereignty.

Analysts can adjust the SRM output by up to +3/-3 notches based on these qualitative factors, allowing for a more granular assessment of political and institutional risks.

## Relevance to Credit Ratings

Structural Features are critical for differentiating between sovereigns with similar economic performance but varying levels of institutional resilience. Strong structural features can mitigate weaker macroeconomic indicators, while poor governance or political instability can lead to negative QO adjustments even with favorable economic data. This pillar is also integral to the assessment of [[Willingness and Capacity to Pay]], as governance quality directly influences a government's commitment to honoring debt obligations.

Furthermore, structural features interact with other pillars such as [[Fiscal Policy]] and [[Monetary Policy]], as institutional quality affects the credibility and effectiveness of these policies. The assessment of Structural Features is also increasingly linked to **Climate Risk**, where governance structures determine a country's ability to implement [[Structural Reforms]] related to climate adaptation and transition.
