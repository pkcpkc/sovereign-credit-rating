# National Council for the Judiciary

The **National Council for the Judiciary** (NCJ) is a constitutional body in Poland responsible for safeguarding the independence of courts and proposing appointments of judges. In the context of the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the NCJ represents a critical flashpoint in Poland's institutional framework, with its composition and legitimacy being central to the ongoing rule of law crisis. The report highlights that despite government efforts to address judicial independence through legislative reform, the NCJ remains a source of significant legal and political controversy, contributing to perceived low judicial independence among the general public (26%) and businesses (24%).

## Institutional Controversy and Composition

The core issue surrounding the NCJ stems from its composition following reforms implemented in 2017. The 2025 report notes that the body's legitimacy is contested, particularly regarding judges appointed by the post-2017 NCJ. This controversy has created a dual system of judiciary legitimacy, complicating the enforcement of judgments and the appointment process. The government's refusal to recognize the legitimacy of certain NCJ appointments has led to significant friction with European Union institutions, including ongoing infringement procedures before the **Court of Justice of the EU**.

## Legislative Reform Efforts (2025)

In response to EU recommendations and domestic pressure, the Polish government has initiated specific legislative measures aimed at addressing the NCJ's role:

*   **Draft Law on Judges Appointed by Post-2017 NCJ**: As of April 24, 2025, a draft law has been proposed to address the status of judges appointed by the controversial NCJ. This legislation aims to regularize their status or establish mechanisms for their removal, thereby resolving a major blockage in the judicial appointment process.
*   **Venice Commission Consultation**: Key amendments to the NCJ law have been referred to the **Venice Commission** for expert opinion. The outcome of these consultations is critical for determining whether the reforms will satisfy EU standards for judicial independence and allow for the normalization of Poland's relationship with EU judicial mechanisms.

## Impact on Judicial Independence and Sovereign Risk

The status of the NCJ is a primary driver of Poland's low **Judicial Independence** score. The report indicates that the controversy surrounding the NCJ contributes directly to:

1.  **Perceived Judicial Independence**: The ongoing crisis has resulted in very low perceived independence, undermining legal certainty for investors and businesses.
2.  **Rule of Law Mechanism**: The NCJ's contentious role is a key factor in the "Constitutional crisis with EU" threat identified in sovereign credit ratings. The inability to clearly define the legitimate judicial authority hampers the implementation of EU funds and increases **Legal Uncertainty**.
3.  **Institutional Checks and Balances**: The NCJ's conflict with other state powers, including the **Constitutional Tribunal** (whose legitimacy is also contested by the government) and the **Supreme Court**, creates a fragmented judicial landscape. This fragmentation affects the predictability of legal outcomes and the enforcement of contracts, impacting **Fiscal Sustainability** and **Policy Predictability**.
