# skills shortages

**Skills shortages** refer to the structural mismatch between the available workforce's competencies and the demands of the economy, acting as a critical constraint on productivity growth, labour market resilience, and long-term fiscal sustainability. In the context of sovereign credit rating analysis, persistent skills gaps—particularly in high-growth sectors like ICT, renewable energy, healthcare, and advanced manufacturing—undermine potential GDP growth, exacerbate wage inflation pressures, and increase the burden of public spending on social protection and retraining programs. These shortages are not merely sectoral issues but systemic bottlenecks that hinder the implementation of green and digital transitions, thereby affecting a country’s long-term growth trajectory and fiscal stability.

Across the EU, countries exhibit varying degrees of vulnerability. While **Austria** and **France** face structural rigidities and aging workforces, **Poland** presents a distinct profile: despite being one of the EU's fastest-growing economies, it suffers from critically low labor productivity (67% of the EU average) and a severe shortage of qualified labor in STEM and vocational fields, driven by rapid demographic decline and inadequate education outcomes.

## Economic and Macroeconomic Impact

### Productivity and Growth Constraints
Skills shortages are identified as a primary driver of declining **productivity growth** and low business dynamism across advanced and emerging EU economies.
*   **Total Factor Productivity (TFP):**
    *   **Austria:** TFP has experienced a structural decline since 2008. The lack of advanced technical and digital skills hampers the efficient adoption of new technologies, limiting the translation of high R&D expenditure (3.29% of GDP) into high-impact innovation.
    *   **France:** While labor productivity is recovering from recent losses, it remains hampered by skills shortages and low business R&D intensity.
    *   **Poland:** Labor productivity remains stubbornly low at 67% of the EU average. Despite strong GDP growth (2.9% in 2024), the economy is constrained by a complex tax framework and an inability to upskill the workforce sufficiently to support high-value manufacturing and clean tech sectors.
*   **Industrial Competitiveness:** Shortages in skilled labour contribute to lost cost competitiveness and declining exports.
    *   **Austria:** The automotive supplier industry faces vulnerability from the EV transition due to a lack of specialized technical skills required for new manufacturing processes.
    *   **France:** High taxes on production and skills shortages combine to hinder competitiveness. Corporate bankruptcies have reached record highs, reflecting the strain of high interest rates and the inability of firms to adapt due to skill gaps.
    *   **Poland:** Although Poland is emerging as a clean tech hub (producing 60% of Europe’s lithium batteries), high unit labor costs and skills gaps in advanced engineering threaten to erode this competitive advantage. The complexity of the regulatory environment further deters investment in sectors requiring high-skilled labor.
*   **Entrepreneurship:** Limited availability of skilled talent correlates with low enterprise birth rates. In France, stagnant business R&D intensity and a complex business environment further restrict the creation of new, high-value jobs that could absorb skilled graduates.

### Labour Market Dynamics
*   **Sectoral Imbalances:** Critical shortages exist in **ICT**, **care sectors** (healthcare and long-term care), **education**, and increasingly, **advanced manufacturing** and **vocational trades**.
    *   **Austria:** These shortages occur despite an overall stable employment rate (77.2% in 2025) and contained unemployment (5.3%), indicating a rigid labour market with poor match-making.
    *   **France:** The labour market shows signs of weakening, with employment growth turning negative in 2025 and unemployment rates expected to rise to 7.9%. Persistent skills mismatches are cited as a key structural challenge alongside educational inequalities.
    *   **Poland:** Unemployment remains at a historic low of 2.9% (2024), but this masks severe shortages in manufacturing, construction, and STEM fields. The tight labor market is exacerbated by a shrinking working-age population due to aging and low fertility rates. Additionally, the disability employment gap widened to 35.6%, one of the largest in the EU, representing a significant untapped labor resource.
*   **Aging Workforce:** The shrinking working-age population exacerbates skills shortages as experienced workers retire without adequate replacement.
    *   **Austria:** The working-age population has been declining since 2024, and a low effective retirement age accelerates the loss of institutional knowledge.
    *   **France:** Aging costs are a significant threat to fiscal sustainability, further strained by the inability to replace retiring skilled workers efficiently.
    *   **Poland:** Demographic decline poses a long-term risk to growth. The low statutory retirement age for women (60 years) and special pension schemes further reduce the effective labor supply, intensifying pressure on the pension system and exacerbating skills gaps in the healthcare and education sectors.
*   **Wage Pressure:** Scarcity of skilled labour in key sectors can lead to wage inflation that outpaces productivity growth, increasing unit labour costs and further eroding international competitiveness. This is particularly acute in **Poland**, where high unit labor costs threaten industrial competitiveness despite lower baseline wages than Western Europe.

## Sector-Specific Bottlenecks in Green and Digital Transitions

Skills shortages act as critical barriers to the execution of national strategic agendas, particularly the green and digital transitions, which are central to EU convergence funds and long-term growth models.

*   **Energy Transition and Decarbonization:**
    *   **France:** Despite achieving 95% low-carbon electricity production, the deployment of renewable energies is hindered by a lack of skilled labor for installation and maintenance, alongside complex permitting procedures. Building decarbonization is lagging significantly, with shortfalls in deep renovation targets driven partly by **skilled labor shortages** in the construction and retrofitting sectors.
    *   **Austria:** Inadequate preparation in green skills risks leaving the country lagging in key future industries such as renewable energy technologies, affecting long-term growth prospects.
    *   **Poland:** While making progress in battery manufacturing, Poland remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels (70% of electricity generation). The transition to renewables is hindered by a lack of specialized skills in grid modernization and renewable installation. High electricity prices, driven by taxation and generation costs, threaten industrial competitiveness, necessitating a rapid upskilling of the workforce to support the green transition.
*   **Digitalization and AI:**
    *   **France:** France lags in the digitalization of SMEs and the adoption of advanced technologies like AI. The efficiency of public support schemes (e.g., the research tax credit) is insufficient to bridge the digital skills gap in the private sector.
    *   **Austria:** A significant gap exists in digital and advanced technical skills among the current workforce. Digital infrastructure deficits, particularly broadband coverage in rural areas, further hinder the development and utilization of digital skills.
    *   **Poland:** Participation in adult learning is low (20.3%), hindering the green and digital transitions. The vocational education and training (VET) system lacks effectiveness in meeting labor market needs, particularly in digital skills required for modern manufacturing and services.

## Fiscal and Sovereign Credit Implications

### Fiscal Sustainability Risks
Skills shortages indirectly impact **fiscal sustainability** and **debt sustainability** through several channels:
1.  **Revenue Base Erosion:** Slower productivity growth limits GDP expansion, constraining the broad taxable income base needed to service rising debt levels.
    *   **Austria:** Debt is projected at 84.0% of GDP in 2025.
    *   **France:** Public debt rose to 113% of GDP in 2024, with deficits persisting at 5.8% of GDP. Sluggish tax revenues, partly due to lower economic dynamism driven by skills gaps, exacerbate the fiscal burden.
    *   **Poland:** Public debt is projected to rise to 58.0% of GDP in 2025, with a high budget deficit of 6.6% of GDP in 2024. The excessive deficit procedure (EDP) has been initiated. Low labor productivity and skills shortages constrain the tax base growth needed to service this debt, especially as the country faces rising defense spending (2.8% of GDP in 2025) and social expenditures.
2.  **Increased Public Expenditure:**
    *   **Social Protection:** Skills mismatches contribute to higher risks of poverty and social exclusion.
        *   **Austria:** The AROPE rate increased to 16.9% in 2024.
        *   **France:** Poverty risks have worsened, particularly among children and in outermost regions, increasing pressure on social safety nets.
        *   **Poland:** While poverty rates are below the EU average, regional disparities (particularly in eastern and rural areas) and a large disability employment gap create significant social inclusion challenges, requiring targeted fiscal support.
    *   **Healthcare and Care:** Shortages in the care sector, combined with an aging population, drive up costs.
        *   **Austria:** Healthcare and long-term care costs are projected to rise from 28% to 30% of GDP by 2070.
        *   **Poland:** The healthcare system remains hospital-centric with insufficient preventive care and high treatable mortality rates. Skills shortages in healthcare professionals exacerbate these inefficiencies, increasing long-term fiscal liabilities.
    *   **Education and Training:** States face increasing costs to address basic skill deficits and to provide adult training.
        *   **Poland:** Basic skills among 15-year-olds have declined significantly since 2018, with a high proportion of disadvantaged students failing to meet minimum competency levels. This foundational deficit necessitates increased public investment in education reform to prevent a long-term decline in human capital.
        *   **France:** The declining attractiveness of the teaching profession impacts long-term human capital development, necessitating further public intervention.

### Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength
*   **Reform Implementation:** The ability of governments to implement structural reforms is tested by the need to simultaneously address deep-seated skills gaps. Failure to address these gaps may lead to continued economic underperformance, undermining **policy credibility**.
    *   **France:** High administrative burden and regulatory complexity, compounded by skills gaps in the public and private sectors, hinder the efficient implementation of reforms under the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF).
    *   **Austria:** Simplifying regulations and improving access to risk capital is difficult when the workforce lacks the skills to leverage these opportunities.
    *   **Poland:** A complex and volatile regulatory environment, with frequent legislative changes (average preparation time of 31 days), deters investment and complicates the integration of skilled labor. The establishment of an independent Fiscal Council (operational 2026) aims to improve policy credibility, but its effectiveness depends on the underlying economic fundamentals, including skills adequacy.

## Educational and Structural Deficits

### Basic and Digital Skills
*   **Declining Basic Skills:** Underachievement rates in basic skills are rising, as evidenced by worsening PISA assessments in **Austria** and significant declines in basic skills among 15-year-olds in **Poland** since 2018. This foundational deficit limits the pipeline of future high-skilled workers.
*   **Digital Gap:** There is a significant gap in digital skills among the current workforce in all three countries. France specifically struggles with the adoption of AI and digitalization in SMEs, while Austria faces rural-urban divides in digital infrastructure and skills utilization. In Poland, low adult participation in learning (20.3%) hinders digital upskilling.

### Adult Training and Lifelong Learning
*   **Stagnant Participation:** Adult participation in training is stagnant in **Austria** and below national targets. In **Poland**, low participation rates hinder the green and digital transitions. This lack of upskilling and reskilling mechanisms prevents the workforce from adapting to changing technological landscapes.
*   **Teacher Shortage:** A critical shortage of teachers, many of whom are approaching retirement, threatens the quality of future education outputs.
    *   **Austria:** This creates a long-term feedback loop of declining skills.
    *   **France:** The declining attractiveness of the teaching profession exacerbates educational inequalities linked to socio-economic background, impacting long-term human capital development.
    *   **Poland:** The education system is under strain due to demographic changes and the need to improve outcomes for disadvantaged students.

### Vocational Education and Training (VET)
*   **Poland:** The VET system lacks effectiveness in meeting labor market needs. Misalignment between vocational curricula and the requirements of modern industries (particularly in manufacturing and construction) results in a persistent shortage of skilled technicians and tradespeople.

## Strategic Recommendations and Mitigation

To mitigate the impact of skills shortages on macroeconomic stability and creditworthiness, the following structural reforms are emphasized:

1.  **Enhance Education Quality:**
    *   **General:** Address basic skill deficits through targeted interventions in schools.
    *   **Poland:** Improve outcomes for disadvantaged students and reverse the decline in basic skills since 2018. Increase the attractiveness of the teaching profession to reduce educational inequalities.
    *   **France:** Reduce educational inequalities linked to socio-economic background.
2.  **Boost Adult Training and VET Reform:**
    *   **General:** Increase participation in lifelong learning programs, particularly in digital and green skills.
    *   **Poland:** Reform the VET system to better align with labor market needs, particularly in STEM, manufacturing, and construction. Increase adult learning participation to support the green and digital transitions.
    *   **France:** Improve the efficiency of R&D support schemes to boost private sector engagement in innovation.
3.  **Simplify Permitting and Regulatory Reform:**
    *   **Austria:** Simplify permitting procedures for industrial and renewable projects to reduce administrative burdens.
    *   **France:** Reduce high administrative burdens and regulatory complexity, particularly in services and retail, to encourage investment in sectors requiring skilled labour.
    *   **Poland:** Stabilize the regulatory framework and reduce legislative volatility to create a predictable environment for investment in human capital and high-value industries.
4.  **Support Innovation and Business Dynamism:**
    *   **General:** Improve access to risk capital for startups and foster an environment that supports enterprise creation.
    *   **France:** Enhance the efficiency of the research tax credit (CIR) to translate public support into private innovation output.
    *   **Poland:** Leverage EU funds (RRF and Cohesion Policy) to accelerate investment in clean tech and digital infrastructure, ensuring that workforce skills are aligned with these new industries.
5.  **Integration of Migrant and Disability Skills:**
    *   **Austria:** Better utilize the potential of migrants by improving recognition of foreign qualifications.
    *   **Poland:** Address the significant disability employment gap (35.6%) by implementing inclusive employment policies and accessibility measures, thereby expanding the effective labor supply.
6.  **Targeted Green Skills Development:**
    *   **France & Poland:** Implement specific training programs for the renewable energy sector, grid modernization, and building renovation to address the skilled labor shortages hindering decarbonization goals. In Poland, this is critical for maintaining its position as a clean tech hub.
