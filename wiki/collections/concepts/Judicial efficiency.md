# Judicial efficiency

**Judicial efficiency** refers to the capacity of a justice system to process cases within reasonable timeframes, manage backlogs, and maintain operational integrity through adequate resources, digital infrastructure, and effective case management. In the context of sovereign credit risk and institutional quality, it serves as a critical indicator of the rule of law, directly influencing investor confidence, the enforceability of contracts, and the overall stability of the legal framework protecting investment. Efficient judicial systems reduce legal uncertainty and litigation costs, thereby supporting productivity growth and foreign direct investment (FDI). Conversely, persistent inefficiencies, such as excessive length of proceedings and structural staff shortages, erode institutional credibility and can lead to violations of fundamental rights, negatively impacting country risk profiles.

## Core Dimensions and Indicators

### 1. Length of Proceedings and Backlogs
Efficiency is primarily measured by the duration of proceedings and the existence of case backlogs. Persistent delays undermine the certainty of law and increase litigation costs for businesses and individuals.
- **Critical Backlogs**: In Belgium, significant backlogs remain a structural concern, particularly in the **Court of Appeal of Brussels**. The **High Council for Justice** has published targeted reports to address these delays, indicating that specific jurisdictions require focused intervention.
- **Italian Context**: In Italy, the length of judicial proceedings remains a serious issue for growth prospects, despite a positive trend in reducing the case backlog across all jurisdictions. The reduction of backlogs is a key strength noted in recent rule of law assessments, contributing to improved confidence in the business environment.
- **Statistical Monitoring**: Improvements in data collection are essential for measuring efficiency. A **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF)**-funded project in Belgium aims to enhance statistical collection regarding criminal case durations, allowing for more precise identification of bottlenecks.

### 2. Digitalisation and Modernisation
The modernization of judicial processes through digital tools is a key driver of efficiency, yet it presents risks if poorly managed.
- **Strategic Shortcomings**: The **Belgian Court of Audit** identified serious shortcomings in the justice department’s digitalisation strategy, citing a lack of overarching approach and significant fraud risks.
- **Corrective Actions**: In response to audit criticisms, the Belgian Government submitted an initial action plan in late 2024 to address poor management and security vulnerabilities in judicial IT systems.
- **Italian Implementation**: Italy has made significant strides in digitalising criminal courts. The mandatory use of the digital case management system (**APP**) in criminal courts and prosecutors' offices began on January 1, 2025. However, technical challenges have led to derogations for some courts, highlighting the complexity of large-scale judicial digitalisation. Effective digitalisation is crucial for reducing administrative burdens and accelerating case processing in both nations.

### 3. Resource Allocation and Vacancies
Efficiency is intrinsically linked to the availability of qualified judicial personnel. Structural resource deficiencies and unattractive working conditions can lead to unfilled vacancies, exacerbating delays.
- **Recruitment Challenges in Belgium**: Despite high perceived judicial independence, structural shortages persist. Salaries and working conditions in Belgium are cited as primary barriers to filling judicial vacancies, which directly impacts the system's throughput.
- **Staff Shortages in Italy**: Persistent shortages of administrative staff in Italian courts remain a threat to policy credibility and institutional strength, despite ongoing recruitment efforts. Italy has also worked to close a 17% magistrate shortage through targeted recruitment initiatives.
- **Budgetary Management**: In Belgium, a significant reform initiative involves the transfer of budgetary management powers for justice administration from the executive branch to the judiciary. While intended to enhance institutional autonomy, this shift faces criticism from the **High Council for Justice** and the **Court of Cassation**, who oppose linking these reforms to disciplinary measures.

### 4. Structural Reforms and Legislative Measures
Governments often introduce specific legislative tools to mitigate inefficiencies, often amidst broader constitutional or institutional debates.
- **Amicable Settlements**: In late 2024, Belgium adopted an Act on amicable settlements of cases, a legislative measure designed to reduce court backlogs by encouraging out-of-court resolutions.
- **Italian Justice Reform**: Italy adopted legislative decrees in March 2024 implementing a comprehensive reform of the justice system. The **High Council for the Judiciary** has taken forward tasks under this legislation. Additionally, rules on mediation and assisted negotiation were revised in January 2025 to further support efficient dispute resolution.
- **Constitutional Tensions**: In Italy, a draft constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors was approved by the Chamber of Deputies in January 2025. This faces strong opposition from the **High Council for the Judiciary** and magistrates’ associations, who argue it undermines judicial autonomy. Such tensions between executive priorities and judicial independence can complicate long-term efficiency reforms.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit and Macroeconomic Stability

Judicial efficiency is a non-financial driver of sovereign credit ratings, influencing the "Institutional Quality" and "Political Stability" pillars of country risk analysis.

### 1. Investment Protection and Contract Enforcement
- **Investor Confidence**: Efficient courts are vital for enforcing contracts and protecting investment rights. In Belgium, 60% of companies express confidence in the effectiveness of investment protection by law and courts. Similarly, in Italy, over half of companies surveyed express confidence in the effectiveness of investment protection, a strength that supports the business environment.
- **Cost of Litigation**: Excessive length of proceedings increases the cost of doing business and creates legal uncertainty, which can deter foreign direct investment (FDI) and negatively impact productivity growth. In Italy, the persistent length of proceedings is explicitly cited as a threat to growth prospects.

### 2. Institutional Credibility and Rule of Law
- **Perceived Independence vs. Efficiency**: While both Belgium and Italy maintain high perceived judicial independence among the public and companies, persistent efficiency issues can erode trust in the system's overall effectiveness. The tension between resource constraints and the demand for timely justice is a key risk factor.
- **Compliance with International Standards**: Inefficiencies can lead to violations of fundamental rights, such as the right to a fair trial within a reasonable time (Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights). Belgium has **17 leading judgments of the ECtHR** pending implementation as of January 2025. Failure to address these structural inefficiencies can result in continued supervision by the **Council of Europe**, signaling institutional weakness to international investors.

### 3. Fiscal Implications
- **Budgetary Pressures**: Improving judicial efficiency often requires increased spending on digital infrastructure and staff recruitment. However, broader fiscal pressures highlight the trade-offs governments face. In Belgium, proposed budget cuts to interfederal bodies contrast with the need for institutional support. In Italy, the cost of maintaining digital systems and recruiting staff must be balanced against fiscal sustainability goals.
- **Fraud Risks**: Poorly managed digitalisation projects, as noted by the **Belgian Court of Audit**, pose direct fiscal risks through potential fraud and misallocation of public funds.

## Key Institutions and Actors
- **High Council for Justice (Belgium)**: Publishes data on case durations and backlogs; advises on judicial reforms.
- **High Council for the Judiciary (Italy)**: Implements comprehensive justice reforms; advises on judicial career structures and appointments.
- **Belgian Court of Audit**: Audits the efficiency and security of justice digitalisation strategies.
- **European Commission**: Monitors judicial efficiency through the Annual Rule of Law Report, providing critical assessments for EU member states like Belgium and Italy.
- **College of Courts and Tribunals (Belgium)**: Manages judicial resources and administration.
- **Council of Europe**: Supervises compliance with ECtHR judgments regarding procedural delays.
- **National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC, Italy)**: While primarily focused on corruption, ANAC’s guidelines on integrity impact the broader institutional credibility of the justice sector.
