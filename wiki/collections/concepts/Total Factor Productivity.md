# Total Factor Productivity

Total Factor Productivity (TFP) is a measure of economic efficiency that accounts for the portion of output not explained by the amount of inputs used (such as labor and capital). It represents the "solvability" of an economy through technological advancement, organizational improvements, and better utilization of existing resources.

## Current Context: France (2025)
In the context of the 2025 Country Report for France, TFP is a critical metric for assessing long-term competitiveness amidst structural hurdles.

*   **Productivity Trends:** While France has maintained high labor productivity, the growth of TFP is currently tempered by structural weaknesses.
*   **Barriers to Growth:** The primary inhibitors to enhancing TFP in the French economy include:
    *   **Skills Gap:** A shortage of skilled staff hinders the ability to implement modern technologies.
    *   **R&D Intensity:** Business R&D intensity (currently at 1.44%) lags significantly behind the 3% target, limiting the technological "leap" required for TFP gains.
    *   **Administrative Complexity:** Bureaucratic hurdles and complex licensing regulations act as a drag on the efficient deployment of capital and labor.
*   **Drivers of Improvement:** To improve TFP, the report emphasizes:
    *   **Digitalization:** Leveraging digital tools to streamline economic life.
    *   **Innovation:** Increasing equity financing for high-growth firms to move from the "unicorn" phase to scalable industrial output.
    *   **Administrative Simplification:** Implementing "tell us once" principles to reduce the non-productive costs of doing business.
