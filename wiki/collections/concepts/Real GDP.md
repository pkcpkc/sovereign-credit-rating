# Real GDP

Real Gross Domestic Product (Real GDP) is a fundamental macroeconomic metric used to measure the total value of all finished goods and services produced within a country's borders during a specific period, adjusted for inflation. Unlike nominal GDP, Real GDP provides a clearer picture of actual economic growth by removing the effects of price changes.

## Key Contextual Applications

### Growth Projections and Economic Trends
Real GDP serves as a primary indicator of economic health. For example, in the **2025 Country Report - France**, Real GDP growth is projected to decelerate to 0.6%. Such fluctuations are often influenced by:
- **Monetary Policy:** High interest rates and restrictive policies can dampen growth.
- **Investment Climate:** Subdued investment due to high capital costs directly impacts the components of Real GDP.
- **Net Exports:** Trade balances, affected by energy costs and international tariffs, contribute to the overall growth rate.

### Fiscal Sustainability
Real GDP is a critical denominator in calculating national debt and deficit ratios. A rising public debt-to-GDP ratio (projected at 116% for France in 2025) signals potential risks to [[Fiscal Sustainability]]. When the growth of Real GDP is slower than the accumulation of public debt, it necessitates aggressive fiscal consolidation and spending reviews.

### Productivity and Structural Dynamics
The quality of Real GDP growth is often assessed through [[Total Factor Productivity]] and labor metrics. While a country may maintain high employment, structural weaknesses—such as a "skills gap" or low R&D intensity—can limit the long-term expansion of Real GDP.
