# International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)

The **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)** is a critical metric used within the **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)** framework by Fitch Ratings to assess a sovereign's ability to meet its foreign-currency obligations. It serves as a key indicator of external financial health and currency stability.

## Role in Rating Methodology
The ILR plays a pivotal role in the differentiation between Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) **Issuer Default Ratings (IDR)**. Specifically, it is used to determine the mapping of short-term ratings from long-term ratings:

*   **Short-Term Mapping:** For sovereign entities with Foreign-Currency ratings, the selection of the short-term rating depends on either the **Reserve-Currency Flexibility (RCF)** or a robust **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR > 100%)**.

## Contextual Framework
The ILR is one of the core components evaluated under the **External Finances** pillar of the Sovereign Rating Model. While Structural Features carry the highest weight in the overall model, the ILR provides the quantitative basis for understanding a nation's liquidity buffer against external shocks and its capacity to service debt in international markets.
