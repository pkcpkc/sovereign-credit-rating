# Macro-prudential risks

**Macro-prudential risks** refer to the vulnerabilities within a financial system that can threaten its stability and, by extension, the sovereign's fiscal and economic outlook. In the context of sovereign credit analysis, these risks are critical determinants of financial sector health and contingent liabilities.

Within the **Fitch Ratings Sovereign Rating Criteria** (effective September 15, 2025), macro-prudential risks are explicitly integrated into the assessment framework, primarily under the **Structural Features** pillar, which carries the highest weight (53.7%) in the **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)**. Fitch monitors these risks to assess the probability of financial distress that could lead to government intervention, thereby impacting public finances through increased contingent liabilities.

## Assessment Methodology

Fitch evaluates macro-prudential risks through a combination of quantitative indicators and qualitative overlays:

1.  **Quantitative Indicators**:
    *   **Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)**: A specific metric used to gauge the breadth and depth of financial sector vulnerabilities.
    *   **Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)**: A key metric under the Structural Features pillar that specifically measures the health and systemic risk profile of the banking sector. The BSI is a direct input into the SRM's assessment of structural features.

2.  **Qualitative Overlay (QO)**:
    *   Macro-prudential risks are also subject to review under the **Qualitative Overlay** mechanism. While the SRM captures baseline financial sector health via the BSI, the QO allows for adjustments if there are emerging systemic risks, regulatory gaps, or financial sector fragilities not fully captured by the quantitative model.
    *   The QO assesses financial sector stability as a component of structural features, considering the policy credibility and effectiveness of macro-prudential frameworks in preventing asset bubbles or credit booms that could destabilize the economy.

## Impact on Sovereign Ratings

The significance of macro-prudential risks to sovereign ratings lies in the **contingent liability** channel:

*   **Fiscal Impact**: Instability in the banking sector or broader financial system increases the likelihood of government support (e.g., bailouts, guarantees). This crystallizes into direct fiscal costs or increased sovereign debt, negatively impacting the **Public Finances** pillar.
*   **Rating Adjustments**: Significant deterioration in macro-prudential metrics (such as a worsening BSI score) can lead to negative adjustments in the SRM score. If risks are acute or mismanaged, the QO may apply downward notch adjustments (up to -2 per pillar) to reflect the heightened risk of fiscal strain from financial sector rescue operations.
*   **Systemic Linkages**: Poor macro-prudential oversight can exacerbate external vulnerabilities (e.g., capital flight, currency depreciation), thereby impacting the **External Finances** pillar and overall sovereign risk profile.
