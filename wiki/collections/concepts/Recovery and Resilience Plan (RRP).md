# Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)

The **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)** is a key economic framework and investment vehicle designed by the European Commission to facilitate post-pandemic recovery and long-term structural reform within EU member states. In the context of Italy, the RRP serves as a primary mechanism for driving economic growth while addressing systemic challenges through targeted investments and policy shifts.

## Core Objectives and Implementation
The RRP is designed to align national recovery efforts with broader European goals, specifically focusing on:
*   **Digital Transition:** Accelerating the adoption of digital technologies across industries.
*   **Decarbonisation:** Supporting the transition to a low-carbon economy and meeting sustainability targets.
*   **Productivity Growth:** Addressing structural bottlenecks to improve the competitiveness of the national economy.

## Case Study: Italy
The 2025 Country Report highlights the RRP's role in Italy's economic strategy:
*   **Reform Endorsement:** The Council adopted a recommendation endorsing Italy's specific RRP, which includes vital reforms in justice and public administration.
*   **Investment Impact:** RRP investments have been instrumental in supporting moderate GDP growth (0.7% in 2024) despite high public debt and global economic uncertainties.
*   **Targeted Reforms:** The plan aims to address critical issues such as the [[Skills Mismatch]] in manufacturing and high-tech sectors, regional disparities (specifically the Mezzogiorno divide), and the need for improved social protection.
*   **Timeline:** The target for the completion of RRP-related reforms and investments is set for **2026**.

## Related Economic Dynamics
The success of the RRP is closely tied to several intersecting economic factors:
*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Balancing RRP-funded investments with the management of high [[Public Debt-to-GDP]] ratios.
*   **Innovation Gap:** Bridging the divide in R&D expenditure to improve Italy's standing relative to the EU average.
*   **Climate Resilience:** Funding infrastructure to mitigate high exposure to hydrogeological risks and extreme weather.
