# Fiscal Sustainability

Fiscal sustainability refers to the ability of a government to maintain its current spending, taxation, and debt policies in the long term without defaulting on obligations or experiencing uncontrollable inflation. It involves balancing the budget to ensure that public debt remains manageable relative to the country's economic output.

## Current Context: France (2025)
The 2025 Country Report highlights significant risks to fiscal sustainability in France, characterized by:

*   **Debt-to-GDP Projections:** Public debt is projected to reach **116% of GDP** by the end of 2025, posing a challenge to long-term economic stability.
*   **Deficit Management:** The general government deficit rose to **5.8% of GDP** in 2024, necessitating aggressive fiscal consolidation measures.
*   **Spending Reviews:** To address these sustainability risks, the report calls for a permanent spending review strategy. This is intended to rationalize **tax expenditures** and ensure that public spending aligns with long-term growth objectives.
*   **Economic Headwinds:** Sustainability efforts are complicated by a slowing **Real GDP** growth rate (forecast at 0.6% for 2025) and high political uncertainty, which can constrain the ability to implement necessary structural reforms.

## Key Drivers and Challenges
*   **Fiscal Consolidation:** The primary mechanism for restoring sustainability, involving a reduction in the budget deficit through both spending cuts and revenue management.
*   **Structural Hurdles:** High public debt levels can lead to higher capital costs, which in turn may stifle private investment and slow the transition to green and digital economies.
*   **Administrative Simplification:** Efforts to reduce bureaucracy (such as the "tell us once" principle) are viewed as complementary to fiscal health by improving economic efficiency and business competitiveness.
