# Issuer Credit Rating (ICR)

## Definition and Overview
The **Issuer Credit Rating (ICR)** is a credit risk assessment provided by rating agencies (such as S&P Global Ratings) to evaluate the ability and willingness of an entity—in this context, sovereign governments and monetary authorities—to service its financial obligations to nonofficial (commercial) creditors. 

For sovereign entities, the ICR is determined through a multi-dimensional framework that assesses various structural, economic, and fiscal pillars to determine the likelihood of default.

## Assessment Framework
The methodology for determining an ICR, particularly for sovereign issuers, is built upon five core pillars:

*   **Institutional Assessment:** Evaluates the effectiveness of policymaking, political stability, transparency, and data accountability. It also accounts for debt payment culture and external security risks.
*   **Economic Assessment:** Analyzes income levels (GDP per capita), growth prospects, and economic diversity, while penalizing high volatility or concentration.
*   **External Assessment:** Focuses on the status of the currency (e.g., **Reserve Currency** vs. Actively Traded), external liquidity, and external indebtedness.
*   **Fiscal Assessment:** Evaluates "Fiscal Performance and Flexibility" (based on changes in **Net General Government Debt**) and "Debt Burden" (the ratio of debt levels to interest costs).
*   **Monetary Assessment:** Reviews the exchange rate regime and the credibility of monetary policy, including independence, price stability, and the ability to act as a **Lender of Last Resort**.

## Key Technical Components
The calculation of an ICR involves several specific technical metrics and adjustments:

### Net Debt and External Ratios
Modern ICR methodologies focus on **Net General Government Debt** (total debt minus liquid financial assets) rather than gross figures. Furthermore, external assessment ratios have shifted to utilize **Current Account Payments (CAP)** in the denominator to more accurately reflect external obligations.

### Contingent Liabilities and BICRA
ICRs are adjusted based on "Contingent Liabilities"—risks that may manifest as debt but are not currently on the balance sheet. These are quantified using:
*   **BICRA (Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment):** A ranking system (1-10) used to quantify risks stemming from the banking sector.
*   **Systemic Risk Adjustments:** Assessments of non-deposit-taking institutions and nonfinancial public-sector enterprises (NFPEs) that pose systemic risks.

### Currency and Notching
The ICR distinguishes between foreign-currency and local-currency ratings:
*   **Local-Currency Uplift:** A sovereign may receive a one-notch uplift in its local-currency rating if it possesses an independent monetary policy, deep capital markets, and sufficient fiscal flexibility.
*   **Notching:** Ratings are subject to "notched down" adjustments for extreme risks (e.g., imminent event risk, weak external liquidity) or "notched up" for exceptional buffers (e.g., liquid assets exceeding 100% of GDP).
