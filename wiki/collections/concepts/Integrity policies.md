# Integrity policies

**Integrity policies** refer to the frameworks, regulations, and monitoring mechanisms designed to prevent corruption, manage conflicts of interest, and ensure ethical conduct among public officials, politicians, and state institutions. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, robust integrity policies are critical for maintaining **policy credibility**, **institutional strength**, and **investment protection**. Weaknesses in these frameworks can lead to political instability, inefficient resource allocation, and diminished investor confidence.

## Definition and Scope

Integrity policies encompass a wide range of regulatory instruments, including:
- **Asset and Interest Declarations:** Systems requiring public officials to disclose financial assets and interests to detect potential conflicts of interest.
- **Lobbying Transparency:** Rules governing interactions between private interests and public decision-makers.
- **Revolving Door Regulations:** Cooling-off periods and restrictions on former public officials taking up roles in private sectors where they previously held influence.
- **Codes of Conduct:** Ethical guidelines for public servants, police forces, and legislators.
- **Whistleblower Protection:** Mechanisms to protect individuals reporting corruption or misconduct.

## Country Case Study: Belgium (2025 Context)

Based on the **2025 Rule of Law Report** by the European Commission, Belgium presents a mixed picture regarding the effectiveness of its integrity policies. While the country maintains high perceived judicial independence, significant structural gaps in integrity frameworks pose risks to **policy credibility** and **accountability**.

### Key Weaknesses and Risks

1.  **Ineffective Asset and Interest Declarations:**
    - The system for asset and interest declarations for politicians and Members of Parliament (MPs) suffers from a **lack of verification and transparency**.
    - Declarations are sealed and accessible only during criminal investigations, leading to criticism from civil society.
    - Compliance relies solely on individual responsibility without adequate institutional oversight, creating a risk of hidden conflicts of interest.

2.  **Fragmented Police Integrity:**
    - Integrity policies across the **181 local police zones** are disparate, lacking a unified **Code of Conduct**.
    - This fragmentation undermines the effectiveness of anti-corruption measures within law enforcement, potentially facilitating local graft and reducing public trust.

3.  **Stagnant Lobbying and Revolving Door Rules:**
    - The new Government (formed in February 2025) had not yet taken a decision on draft lobbying rules by mid-2025.
    - Progress on "revolving door" regulations is limited to non-binding circulars; binding rules on cooling-off periods remain absent, allowing potential conflicts of interest between public service and private sector roles.

4.  **Political Interference and Information Leaks:**
    - Allegations of undue political influence and information leaks in sensitive corruption investigations (handled by the **Central Office for the Repression of Corruption**, CDBC-OCRC) suggest vulnerabilities in the independence of anti-corruption bodies.
    - Public criticism of the judiciary by politicians risks undermining the role of independent institutions, a key component of integrity.

5.  **Lack of Monitoring for Public Office Holders:**
    - There is a notable absence of a monitoring mechanism for the **Code of Conduct for federal public office holders**, reducing accountability and enforcement of ethical standards.

### Strengths and Recent Developments

- **Whistleblower Protection:** On March 27, 2025, the King sanctioned a law strengthening whistleblower protection for the House of Representatives and the Senate, improving mechanisms for reporting misconduct.
- **New Investigative Structures:** The government intends to create a new **Financial Investigation Service (FIOD-SRFF)** and a specialized prosecution service for financial crime, aiming to enhance the detection and prosecution of complex corruption cases.
- **Judicial Independence Perception:** Despite structural issues, public perception of judicial independence remains relatively high (64% of the public, 72% of companies), providing a foundation for institutional trust.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Macroeconomic Stability

### Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength
Weak integrity policies directly impact the **policy credibility and institutional strength** pillar of sovereign credit ratings:
- **Investor Confidence:** A lack of transparency in asset declarations and lobbying can deter foreign direct investment (FDI), as investors perceive higher risks of hidden conflicts of interest and unfair competition.
- **Governance Costs:** Fragmented police integrity and ineffective anti-corruption frameworks increase the cost of governance and may lead to misallocation of public resources.
- **Political Stability:** While Belgium has high perceived judicial independence, the lack of progress on lobbying rules and revolving doors creates political friction and uncertainty, particularly in a fragmented political landscape.

### Fiscal Sustainability and Public Administration Efficiency
- **Resource Allocation:** Corruption and lack of oversight can lead to inefficient public spending. The Belgian Court of Audit has highlighted shortcomings in justice digitalisation, partly due to poor management and fraud risks, which can strain public finances.
- **Institutional Capacity:** Dispersed powers concerning integrity policy across various bodies reduce the overall efficiency of the public administration, potentially slowing down reforms and service delivery.

### Country Risk Analysis
- **Legal and Regulatory Risk:** The absence of binding revolving door rules and the non-binding nature of some appeal bodies for access to administrative documents increase legal ambiguity for stakeholders.
- **Reputational Risk:** Allegations of political interference in corruption investigations can damage Belgium's international reputation, affecting its standing in organizations like the OECD and the European Union.
