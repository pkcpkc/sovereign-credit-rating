# Digital Transformation

Digital Transformation refers to the integration of digital technology into all areas of a country's economy and public administration, fundamentally changing how operations are conducted and value is delivered. It involves moving toward a productivity-driven growth model that leverages innovation to overcome structural limitations.

In modern economic landscapes, digital transformation is characterized by both specific infrastructure milestones and broader structural hurdles:

## Key Developments & Initiatives
*   **E-Receipt Systems:** The planned introduction of an eReceipt system (scheduled for 2025 in Hungary) represents a move toward digitizing fiscal transactions and improving transparency in the retail and service sectors.
*   **Database Infrastructure:** Plans for a centralized database for private equity funds are intended to modernize financial tracking and reporting.
*   **Cybersecurity & Resilience:** Implementation of frameworks like the EU's Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) is prioritized to ensure the security of digital infrastructures.
*   **Vision 2050:** Strategic initiatives (e.g., Malta's Vision 2050) aim to reconcile development goals with social and environmental sustainability through innovation and digital skills.

## Structural Challenges & Policy Drivers
*   **Innovation Stagnation:** Digital transformation is often hindered by low [[R&D Intensity]], which limits the development of homegrown technological solutions.
*   **Skills Shortage:** A critical lack of skilled workers, particularly in STEM fields, creates a bottleneck for the adoption and maintenance of advanced digital technologies.
*   **Investment Gaps:** Declines in venture capital and underdeveloped stock markets can limit the private funding necessary to fuel digital innovation.
*   **Fiscal & Energy Realignment:** Transitioning away from fixed energy price models is identified as a critical step to reallocate funds toward R&D, public services, and green investment, which are foundational for a digital economy.
*   **Regulatory Alignment:** Aligning Corporate Income Tax (CIT) with the [[OECD Pillar II]] and ensuring robust [[AML/CFT Framework]]s are essential for maintaining the investor confidence required for digital growth.
