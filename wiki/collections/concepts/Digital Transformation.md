# Digital Transformation

Digital Transformation refers to the integration of digital technology into all areas of a country's economy and public administration, fundamentally changing how operations are conducted and value is delivered.

In the context of Hungary's 2025 economic landscape, digital transformation is characterized by both infrastructure milestones and structural hurdles:

## Key Developments & Initiatives
*   **E-Receipt Systems:** A planned introduction of an eReceipt system (scheduled for 2025) represents a move toward digitizing fiscal transactions and improving transparency in the retail and service sectors.
*   **Database Infrastructure:** Plans for a centralized database for private equity funds are intended to modernize financial tracking and reporting.

## Structural Challenges
*   **Innovation Stagnation:** Digital transformation is currently hindered by low [[R&D Intensity]], which remains significantly below the EU average (1.4% vs 2.2%). This limits the development of homegrown technological solutions.
*   **Skills Shortage:** A critical lack of skilled workers, particularly in STEM fields, creates a bottleneck for the adoption and maintenance of advanced digital technologies.
*   **Investment Gaps:** The decline in venture capital investment and an underdeveloped stock market limit the availability of private funding necessary to fuel digital innovation and startup ecosystems.
