# Separation of powers

**Separation of powers** is a foundational principle of constitutional governance that divides the responsibilities of the state into distinct branches—typically legislative, executive, and judicial—to prevent the concentration of power and ensure mutual accountability. In the context of sovereign credit analysis and country risk, the integrity of this separation is a critical determinant of **[[Institutional Checks and Balances]]**, policy credibility, and long-term economic stability.

## Concept Definition and Scope

The separation of powers ensures that no single branch of government can exercise unchecked authority. Effective implementation requires:
- **Legislative Independence**: The ability of parliament to draft, amend, and pass laws without undue executive coercion.
- **Judicial Autonomy**: The capacity of the judiciary to interpret laws and adjudicate disputes free from political interference, ensuring the **[[Rule of Law]]**.
- **Executive Accountability**: Mechanisms for the government to be held answerable to both the legislature and the judiciary.

Violations or erosions of these boundaries, such as the excessive use of executive decrees to bypass legislative scrutiny, signal weak institutional frameworks and elevated political risk.

## Relevance to Sovereign Credit and Macroeconomic Stability

The adherence to separation of powers directly impacts sovereign credit ratings through several transmission channels:

### 1. Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength
Strong separation of powers enhances **[[Policy Credibility]]** by ensuring that laws are enacted through transparent, deliberative processes. Conversely, when the executive branch overrides legislative procedures, it introduces uncertainty regarding the stability and reversibility of policy frameworks.
- **Risk Indicator**: Frequent resort to urgency decrees by the government raises concerns about the erosion of legislative oversight, potentially leading to abrupt regulatory shifts that disrupt business planning and investment flows.

### 2. Judicial Independence and Investment Protection
The separation between the executive/judiciary is vital for enforcing contracts and protecting property rights. If the judiciary is perceived as subordinate to political interests, investor confidence declines.
- **Credit Impact**: High perceived judicial independence is a strength that supports investor protection. However, reforms that blur the lines between prosecutorial and judicial functions (e.g., separating careers without adequate safeguards for autonomy) can trigger resistance from judicial bodies, creating institutional friction and legal uncertainty.

### 3. Fiscal Sustainability and Anti-Corruption Frameworks
Effective checks and balances are necessary for robust **[[Anti-Corruption]]** frameworks. Weak separation of powers often correlates with higher corruption risks, as oversight mechanisms fail to constrain executive discretion in public procurement and resource allocation.
- **Fiscal Risk**: Corruption in public procurement, a high-risk area in countries with weak oversight, directly erodes the tax base and fiscal efficiency.

## Case Study: Italy (2025 Rule of Law Report Context)

The European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report on Italy highlights specific tensions regarding the separation of powers, illustrating its direct relevance to country risk analysis.

### Threats to Separation of Powers
- **Executive Overreach via Decrees**: The Italian Government’s frequent use of urgency decrees (87 decrees in the current legislature) is cited as a significant threat to the separation of powers. This practice allows the executive to enact laws with minimal immediate legislative debate, weakening the **[[Chamber of Deputies]]** and Senate's role as check on government power.
- **Legislative Bypass**: The use of confidence motions and law decrees to fast-track legislation limits the opportunity for thorough parliamentary scrutiny, potentially affecting the quality and legitimacy of laws.

### Institutional Friction and Reform
- **Judicial-Career Separation Debate**: A draft constitutional reform aimed at separating the careers of judges and prosecutors was approved by the Chamber of Deputies in January 2025. However, this initiative faces strong opposition from the **[[High Council for the Judiciary]]** and magistrates’ associations. Critics argue that without careful design, such reforms could undermine judicial autonomy and the internal balance of power within the justice system, potentially allowing executive influence over prosecutorial decisions.
- **Security Law Concerns**: The new Security Law adopted in June 2025 has raised stakeholder concerns regarding its impact on civic space and fundamental freedoms. While primarily a civil liberties issue, the manner of its adoption and its implications for state power versus individual rights reflect broader checks-and-balances dynamics.

### Strengths in Institutional Checks
- **Independent Regulators**: Certain regulatory bodies, such as the Communication Regulatory Authority (**[[AGCOM]]**), function independently and are financially equipped, demonstrating pockets of effective separation of powers in the media sector.
- **Constitutional Court Oversight**: The **[[Constitutional Court]]** maintains its role as a key check on legislative and executive action, as evidenced by its validation of the law abrogating the offence of abuse of public office in May 2025. This reinforces the judiciary's capacity to review the constitutionality of government actions.

## Key Institutions and Actors

- **[[European Commission]]**: Monitors rule of law developments, including separation of powers, as part of the annual Rule of Law Report, influencing EU funding and political dialogue.
- **[[High Council for the Judiciary]] (Italy)**: Key actor in safeguarding judicial independence and resisting reforms perceived as undermining judicial autonomy.
- **[[Constitutional Court]] (Italy)**: Ultimate arbiter of constitutional disputes, ensuring that executive and legislative actions remain within constitutional bounds.
- **[[Chamber of Deputies]]**: The lower house of parliament, whose effective legislative oversight is challenged by the executive's use of decree powers.
