# Anti-Corruption Strategy

The **Anti-Corruption Strategy** refers to a comprehensive, state-level framework designed to prevent, detect, and prosecute corrupt activities within public institutions and the private sector. In the context of Romania’s 2025 Rule of Law Report, this strategy is evaluated not merely as a legislative instrument but as a critical determinant of sovereign credit quality, institutional resilience, and macroeconomic stability. The strategy's implementation status directly influences investor confidence, fiscal sustainability, and the country's risk profile within the European Union framework.

## Strategic Implementation and Legal Framework

The core component of Romania’s current approach is the **2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy**, which has been identified as being implemented on track. This strategy serves as the primary vehicle for aligning national legislative actions with European Union standards and Venice Commission recommendations. Key legislative milestones supporting this strategy include the adoption of laws on conflicts of interest, gifts, and the broader **Strategy for Open Government 2025-2030**.

However, the efficacy of this strategy is heavily contingent upon judicial enforcement and legal certainty. While high-level prosecutions continue, the strategy faces systemic risks due to rulings by the **High Court of Cassation and Justice** regarding the statute of limitations. These rulings have led to the annulment of numerous corruption convictions and the discontinuation of active cases, creating significant legal uncertainty. This unpredictability undermines the deterrent effect of the strategy and exposes the state to reputational damage regarding its commitment to the **Rule of Law**.

## Institutional Actors and Operational Challenges

The execution of the Anti-Corruption Strategy relies on a network of specialized state institutions, each facing distinct operational challenges that impact overall strategic effectiveness:

*   **National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA):** As the primary law enforcement agency, the DNA continues to conduct high-level prosecutions. However, its effectiveness is occasionally compromised by strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs) and allegations of harassment against investigative journalists, which can deter transparency.
*   **National Integrity Agency (ANI):** The ANI plays a crucial role in preventive anti-corruption measures through asset declaration systems. Despite maintaining operational effectiveness, the ANI suffers from inadequate resource allocations. Furthermore, a recent Constitutional Court decision has weakened the asset declaration system by ruling aspects of it unconstitutional, thereby creating gaps in transparency and accountability that the Anti-Corruption Strategy must now address through legislative amendments.
*   **Judicial System:** The judiciary's independence and efficiency are central to the strategy's success. While progress has been made in completing Venice Commission recommendations on Justice Laws, including steps to extend the mandates of high-ranking prosecutors, the perception of judicial independence remains average. Increased length of proceedings acts as a barrier to justice, allowing corruption cases to languish or expire, thereby eroding public trust.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit and Economic Stability

The performance of the Anti-Corruption Strategy has direct implications for sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis. High perceived corruption in the public sector is cited as a significant threat to Romania’s economic strength and tax base. Conversely, strong implementation of the strategy correlates with higher confidence in investment protection among companies.

Key risk factors associated with the Anti-Corruption Strategy include:
1.  **Regulatory Burden:** Inconsistent application of anti-corruption laws increases the regulatory burden on businesses, potentially deterring foreign direct investment.
2.  **Legal Uncertainty:** The closure of corruption cases due to procedural rulings creates a volatile legal environment, which is a negative factor for sovereign credit ratings.
3.  **Fiscal Risks:** Corruption in public procurement remains a leakage point for state resources. However, the digitalization of public procurement processes, supported by the strategy, helps mitigate fraud risk and improves budgetary efficiency.

Conversely, strengths in the strategy—such as the implementation of the 2021-2025 framework and the adoption of open government initiatives—contribute to a stable macroeconomic environment. These strengths support Romania’s trajectory toward Eurozone accession and its ability to access **EU Recovery and Resilience Plan** funds, which are often contingent upon adherence to strict rule-of-law and anti-corruption benchmarks.

## Media Freedom and Transparency

A robust Anti-Corruption Strategy must also encompass media freedom and pluralism, as a free press is essential for exposing corrupt practices. The 2025 Report highlights significant threats in this domain, including opaque financing of private media by political parties and insufficient transparency in media ownership. The lack of progress in strengthening the independent governance of public service media further complicates the anti-corruption landscape. Harassment, surveillance, and intimidation of journalists represent a direct attack on the informational ecosystem necessary for holding public officials accountable under the Anti-Corruption Strategy.

## Conclusion

The **Anti-Corruption Strategy** in Romania is a dynamic and critical component of the nation's institutional integrity. While legislative implementation is progressing, significant challenges remain regarding judicial enforcement, resource allocation for oversight bodies like the **National Integrity Agency**, and the protection of media freedom. Addressing these issues is paramount for improving Romania's sovereign credit rating, ensuring fiscal sustainability, and maintaining long-term economic stability. Future efforts must focus on stabilizing case law regarding statutes of limitations, adequately funding anti-corruption agencies, and ensuring the transparency of media ownership to fully realize the strategy's potential.
