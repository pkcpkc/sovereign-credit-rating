# Labour Productivity

Labour productivity is a key economic indicator reflecting the efficiency with which a workforce generates output. It serves as a critical determinant of long-term economic growth, international competitiveness, and real wage sustainability.

## Current Context: Hungary
The 2025 Country Report identifies low labour productivity as a persistent structural vulnerability for the Hungarian economy. While the country has experienced rapid nominal wage growth between 2022 and 2024, this trend faces significant risks if not supported by corresponding gains in productivity.

### Key Drivers and Challenges
*   **Unit Labour Costs (ULCs):** Rising ULCs pose a threat to Hungary's international competitiveness. To maintain a balance, these costs must be offset by improvements in productivity.
*   **Skills Shortage:** A significant barrier to productivity is the shortage of skilled workers, particularly in STEM fields and the green transition. This is compounded by one of the lowest tertiary education attainment rates in the EU (32.3%).
*   **Innovation Stagnation:** Low [[R&D Intensity]] (1.4% vs. the EU average of 2.2%) limits the technological advancement necessary to drive productivity gains.
*   **Business Environment:** Regulatory volatility and market distortions created by state intervention can hinder the investment in processes and technologies that typically enhance labour efficiency.
