# Civic space

**Civic space** refers to the institutional, legal, and physical environment in which civil society organizations (CSOs), trade unions, media, and individuals can organize, express themselves, and participate in public life without undue interference or restriction. In the context of sovereign credit rating analysis and country risk, the status of civic space is a critical indicator of **geopolitical and political stability** and **institutional strength**. A "narrowed" civic space often signals underlying tensions between the state and civil actors, potential legal ambiguities, and risks to the effective functioning of democratic checks and balances.

Recent assessments by the **European Commission** in its 2025 Rule of Law Reports highlight that civic space is currently characterized as **"narrowed"** in both **Belgium** and **France**, albeit through different mechanisms ranging from legislative restrictions to budgetary constraints and social fragmentation.

## Status in Belgium (2025)

According to the **2025 Rule of Law Report** by the European Commission, the civic space in Belgium is currently characterized as **"narrowed."** This assessment reflects specific legislative and judicial developments that pose threats to fundamental freedoms, particularly the right to demonstrate and access to information.

### Key Threats and Developments

1.  **Restrictions on Demonstrations**:
    *   There are significant concerns regarding new obstacles to the right to demonstrate.
    *   Proposals have been introduced for **judicial demonstration bans** targeting individuals previously convicted of acts committed during protests.
    *   These measures are viewed by stakeholders as potentially disproportionate and restrictive to the fundamental right of peaceful assembly.

2.  **Legal Challenges and Constitutional Review**:
    *   Trade unions and CSOs lodged a constitutional complaint on **2024-10-07** regarding the "malicious attack on government authority," highlighting tensions between state authority and civic expression.
    *   The **Constitutional Court** issued relevant judgments in late 2024 (September and November) concerning subsidy eligibility for organizations and urgent court orders related to strikes, impacting the operational capacity of civic actors.

3.  **Access to Public Documents**:
    *   While federal legislation adopted in **2024-07** aimed to improve the administrative transparency framework, procedural shortcomings persist.
    *   Appeal bodies regarding access to documents remain **non-binding**, limiting the effectiveness of transparency mechanisms.
    *   Belgium has signed but not yet ratified the Council of Europe Convention on Access to Official Documents, leaving a gap in international compliance.

## Status in France (2025)

The 2025 Rule of Law Report identifies France’s civic space as **"narrowed"** due to a combination of legislative simplification efforts that reduce citizen consultation, budgetary restrictions on civil society, and social unrest. Unlike Belgium, where the focus is on demonstration bans, France’s challenges are linked to **policy credibility**, **legislative stability**, and the **operational capacity** of oversight bodies.

### Key Threats and Developments

1.  **Legislative Simplification vs. Democratic Consultation**:
    *   The government’s drive to "simplify economic life" has been welcomed by businesses but criticized by civil society for reducing opportunities for citizen consultation.
    *   The frequent use of accelerated legislative procedures and **Article 49-3** of the Constitution (bypassing parliamentary vote) has raised concerns about legislative stability and democratic engagement.
    *   Citizen consultation platforms, such as the Agora application, have faced interruptions, undermining trust in participatory governance.

2.  **Budgetary Constraints and Institutional Capacity**:
    *   Civil society organizations face significant budgetary restrictions, limiting their ability to monitor government actions.
    *   The **National Consultative Commission on Human Rights (CNC DH)** lacks sufficient resources to fully execute its mandate, weakening a key pillar of civic oversight.
    *   Public service media (e.g., France Médias) faces funding instability, with debates over shifting from license fees to VAT shares, raising concerns about editorial independence and job security.

3.  **Social Unrest and Intimidation**:
    *   Journalists and human rights defenders report instances of intimidation, online harassment, and police violence during protests.
    *   Magistrates face severe personal attacks and threats, particularly following rulings on national elections, prompting the **Superior Council of the Magistracy** to reaffirm judicial independence.

4.  **Access to Administrative Documents**:
    *   Despite strong legal guarantees, journalists and citizens face practical difficulties in accessing administrative documents.
    *   Lobbying transparency remains incomplete, as disclosure rules do not apply to top-executive officials, although new rules for foreign lobbyists are expected to launch in mid-2025.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Rating & Country Risk

The condition of civic space directly influences the **Geopolitical and Political Stability** and **Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength** factors in sovereign credit assessments for both Belgium and France.

### 1. Geopolitical and Political Stability
*   **Political Fragmentation and Polarization**:
    *   **Belgium**: Restrictions on civic space can exacerbate social tensions and political fragmentation. The period of caretaker government (June 2024–February 2025) demonstrated fragility; further erosion of trust through restricted civic freedoms may deepen political divides.
    *   **France**: Political polarization is evident in attacks on magistrates and the use of emergency legislative powers. Social unrest, reflected in strikes by public broadcasters and journalists, indicates underlying dissatisfaction that could destabilize the political environment.
*   **Social Cohesion**: Limitations on the right to demonstrate and strike (Belgium) or intimidation of civil society (France) may lead to increased social unrest or institutional conflict, destabilizing the political environment.

### 2. Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength
*   **Rule of Law Perception**:
    *   A narrowed civic space contradicts high perceived judicial independence and weakens the overall rule of law framework. Investors and rating agencies view consistent adherence to international human rights standards as a marker of institutional quality.
    *   In **France**, the perceived judicial independence remains average due to pressure on magistrates, while in **Belgium**, the issue lies more with legislative encroachment on assembly rights.
*   **International Compliance**:
    *   **Belgium**: The failure to fully implement ECtHR judgments (17 pending as of Jan 2025) and delays in ratifying transparency conventions signal weak enforcement mechanisms.
    *   **France**: While pending ECHR leading judgments decreased from 26 to 22 by June 2025, historical backlogs remain. The lack of lobbying transparency for top executives in France highlights gaps in accountability.

### 3. Macroeconomic and Fiscal Implications
*   **Investor Confidence**:
    *   While a majority of companies in both countries express confidence in investment protection, persistent issues with civic space and political transparency can deter long-term foreign direct investment (FDI) sensitive to political risk.
    *   In **France**, SMEs perceive high regulatory burdens and legal uncertainty due to rapid legislative changes, which can negatively impact growth prospects.
*   **Fiscal Sustainability**:
    *   **Belgium**: Proposed budget cuts to the Interfederal Centre for Equal Opportunities (**Unia**, a 25% cut) and public service media (e.g., **RTBF** facing a €78 million shortfall) indicate fiscal pressure on institutions that support civic engagement and pluralism.
    *   **France**: Budgetary constraints limit resources for justice and anti-corruption bodies. The shifting funding model for public broadcasters poses a long-term fiscal and stability risk.
*   **Operational Efficiency**:
    *   In **France**, the lack of resources for the CNC DH and delays in digitalizing justice procedures (e.g., Portalis technical issues) reduce the efficiency of civic oversight and legal recourse, indirectly affecting the business environment.

## Related Concepts and Institutions

*   **Judicial Independence**: High perceived independence acts as a counterbalance to political encroachments on civic space, though the link to disciplinary reforms remains controversial in Belgium, and magistrates face direct threats in France.
*   **Media Pluralism**: Media freedom is integral to civic space. While legal protections for journalists have improved in Belgium (decriminalization of defamation), budgetary pressures on public broadcasters and harassment risks remain in both countries.
*   **Whistleblower Protection**:
    *   **Belgium**: New laws sanctioned in **March 2025** aim to strengthen protection, supporting transparency.
    *   **France**: Whistleblowing mechanisms are seeing increased usage, but resource constraints for protection persist, and lobbying transparency is incomplete for top executives.
*   **Constitutional/Constitutional Bodies**:
    *   **Belgium**: The **Constitutional Court** plays a pivotal role in adjudicating conflicts between state authority and civic rights.
    *   **France**: The **Constitutional Council** and **Council of State** provide checks and balances, but their efficacy is tested by accelerated legislative procedures.
*   **European Commission**: Monitors rule of law developments, including civic space, through the annual Rule of Law Report.
*   **Council of Europe / ECtHR**: Provides international oversight. Both countries face ongoing challenges with pending judgments, though France has shown recent progress in reducing the backlog.
