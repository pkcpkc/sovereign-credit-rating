# Current Account Payments (CAP)

## Definition and Overview
Current Account Payments (CAP) represent a key metric used in the assessment of a sovereign nation's external position and creditworthiness. In the context of sovereign rating methodologies, CAP serves as a critical component in calculating external assessment ratios to determine a country's ability to service its external obligations.

## Role in Sovereign Rating Methodology
The use of CAP is a specific methodological shift in the S&P Global Ratings: Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017). 

### Key Methodological Shifts
*   **Denominator Integration:** The methodology shifted to using Current Account Payments (CAP) in the denominator of external assessment ratios. This replaces previous metrics (such as current account receipts) to provide a more accurate reflection of the external liquidity and debt burden.
*   **External Assessment Pillar:** CAP is a primary factor in the **External Assessment** pillar, which analyzes:
    *   Currency status (Reserve vs. Actively Traded).
    *   External liquidity.
    *   External indebtedness (narrow net external debt).

## Relationship to Other Concepts
*   **[[Net General Government Debt]]**: While CAP focuses on the external flow and position, Net General Government Debt is used to assess the internal fiscal performance and debt burden.
*   **[[Issuer Credit Rating (ICR)]]**: CAP directly influences the final ICR by determining the strength of the external assessment pillar, which contributes to the overall sovereign creditworthiness.
*   **[[Contingent Liabilities]]**: While CAP measures active flows, contingent liabilities (often measured via [[BICRA]]) represent potential future obligations that could impact the sustainability of the current account position.

## Contextual Significance
The integration of CAP into the 2017 methodology reflects a move toward more sophisticated modeling of how sovereign states manage their external balances. By focusing on payments, the methodology better captures the ongoing pressure on a nation's foreign exchange reserves and its capacity to meet nonofficial (commercial) creditor obligations.
