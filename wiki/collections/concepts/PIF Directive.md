# PIF Directive

The **PIF Directive** (Protection of the European Union's Financial Interests) is a legislative framework established by the European Union to ensure the integrity of the EU budget and prevent fraud, corruption, and other illegal activities that could jeopardize the Union's financial interests.

## Implementation Context (Slovakia)
In the context of Slovakia's 2025 Rule of Law Report, the PIF Directive serves as a primary driver for domestic legislative alignment. Significant developments include:

*   **Criminal Code Alignment:** In December 2024, Slovakia adopted additional amendments to its Criminal Codes specifically to align with the requirements of the PIF Directive.
*   **Judicial Impact:** While these amendments are intended to strengthen the anti-corruption framework, they have sparked concerns regarding the "chilling effect" on the judiciary. Critics argue that without sufficient safeguards, judges may face undue criminal liability for their independent decisions, potentially undermining [[Judicial Independence]].
*   **Legislative Process:** The alignment with these directives has occasionally been facilitated through [[Fast-track Procedure]]s, which some stakeholders argue bypasses necessary consultation and scrutiny.

## Related Concepts
- **[[Judicial Independence]]**: The balance between enforcing PIF Directive compliance and protecting judges from political or criminal harassment.
- **[[Fast-track Procedure]]**: The legislative mechanism used to expedite the adoption of amendments related to the directive.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework**: The broader institutional goal of the PIF Directive, which in Slovakia is currently facing challenges due to the dissolution of agencies like NAKA and SPO.
