# Renewable Energy

Renewable energy refers to energy derived from natural sources that are replenished at a rate equal to or faster than the rate at which they are consumed. It is a cornerstone of the global transition toward a low-carbon economy and is central to meeting international climate targets.

## Context in France and the EU
As of 2025, France has demonstrated significant progress in its energy transition, achieving a milestone where **95% of its electricity** is generated from low-carbon sources. Despite this success, the transition faces ongoing structural and logistical challenges:

*   **Sectoral Challenges:** While the electricity mix is largely decarbonized, significant hurdles remain in decarbonizing the **transport** and **building** sectors. These difficulties are often exacerbated by administrative complexity and a shortage of skilled renovation workers.
*   **Infrastructure & Investment:** Maintaining long-term competitiveness requires enhanced investment in renewable energy infrastructure. This is viewed as a critical component for both economic growth and environmental sustainability.
*   **Targets:** Key milestones include the planned phase-out of coal use by 2027 and a target for a 50% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030.

## Related Economic and Structural Factors
The advancement of renewable energy is inextricably linked to several macroeconomic and structural factors:

*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** The transition requires significant capital; however, high public debt-to-GDP ratios (projected at 116% in France for 2025) and the need for aggressive fiscal consolidation can impact the scale of public investment.
*   **Skills Gap:** A primary barrier to the "green transition" is the shortage of skilled staff and laborers capable of implementing large-scale renewable projects and building renovations.
*   **Digitalization:** The integration of digital technologies is seen as a complementary force to the green transition, aiding in the optimization of energy grids and resource management.
*   **Innovation:** Increasing business R&D intensity is necessary to drive technological breakthroughs in energy storage, generation efficiency, and carbon removal technologies.
