# R&D Intensity

**R&D Intensity** refers to the proportion of a country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) spent on Research and Development. It serves as a critical indicator of a nation's commitment to innovation, technological advancement, and long-term economic competitiveness.

## Regional Context and Performance Gaps
Analysis of recent Country Reports reveals significant disparities in R&D intensity across the European Union:

*   **Hungary**: Records an R&D intensity of **1.4%**, significantly below the EU average of **2.2%**. This is identified as a structural hurdle contributing to overall innovation stagnation.
*   **Malta**: Exhibits an even more acute deficit, with an R&D intensity of only **0.64% of GDP**, ranking as the second lowest in the EU.

## Structural Challenges and Barriers
The development of R&D intensity is hindered by several systemic factors identified across different member states:

*   **Innovation Stagnation**: Low investment levels correlate with a lack of technological advancement and a struggle to keep pace with the **[[Digital Decade]]** goals.
*   **Concentration of Investment**: In certain markets, research activities are not broadly distributed but are heavily concentrated within a small number of large, foreign-owned firms.
*   **Skills Shortage**: A lack of skilled researchers and workers, particularly in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics), limits the human capital necessary for high-intensity R&D.
*   **Capital Availability**: Underdeveloped **[[Venture Capital]]** ecosystems can stifle the early-stage funding required for breakthrough innovations.

## Related Economic Factors
The growth of R&D intensity is influenced by these interconnected economic pillars:
*   **[[Skills Shortage]]**: Directly impacts the availability of human capital for high-intensity research and the green transition.
*   **[[Business Environment]]**: Regulatory volatility, high administrative burdens, and judicial delays (e.g., long durations for commercial cases) can deter the long-term investment required for sustained R&D growth.
*   **[[GDP Growth]]**: While R&D is a primary driver of long-term growth, current fiscal pressures—including high inflation and debt-servicing costs—impact the available fiscal space for innovation investments.
