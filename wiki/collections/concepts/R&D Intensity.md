# R&D Intensity

**R&D Intensity** refers to the proportion of a country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) spent on Research and Development. It serves as a critical indicator of a nation's commitment to innovation, technological advancement, and long-term economic competitiveness.

## Current Status and Context
Based on the **2025 Country Report - Hungary**, the following observations characterize the R&D landscape:

*   **Performance Gap:** Hungary's R&D intensity remains significantly below the European Union average. Specifically, Hungary records an intensity of **1.4%**, compared to the EU average of **2.2%**.
*   **Innovation Stagnation:** The low R&D intensity is identified as a structural hurdle, contributing to overall innovation stagnation within the country.
*   **Concentration of Investment:** Research and development activities are not broadly distributed across the economy; instead, business R&D is heavily concentrated within a small number of large, foreign-owned firms.

## Related Economic Factors
The development of R&D intensity is influenced by several interconnected factors highlighted in the regional economic analysis:
*   **[[Skills Shortage]]**: A lack of skilled workers, particularly in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) and the green transition, limits the human capital available for high-intensity R&D.
*   **[[Business Environment]]**: Regulatory volatility and market distortions can deter the long-term investment required for sustained R&D growth.
*   **[[GDP Growth]]**: While R&D is a driver of long-term growth, current economic pressures—including high inflation and debt-servicing costs—impact the fiscal space available for innovation investments.
