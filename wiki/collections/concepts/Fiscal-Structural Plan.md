# Fiscal-Structural Plan

The **Fiscal-Structural Plan** refers to the strategic framework utilized by member states, specifically highlighted in the context of Belgium's 2025 Country Report, to address the dual challenges of fiscal sustainability and structural economic reform. 

## Core Objectives
The plan serves as a roadmap to stabilize national finances while simultaneously addressing deep-seated structural imbalances. Key pillars of this framework include:

*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Managing the headline deficit (projected at 5.4% of GDP for Belgium in 2025) and controlling public debt, which is pressured by aging-related costs and defense spending.
*   **Labor Market Reform:** Addressing high labor costs driven by [[Wage Indexation]] and creating incentives to increase employment rates among migrants, older workers, and low-skilled individuals.
*   **Demographic Adaptation:** Preparing for the long-term fiscal impact of an aging population, specifically regarding [[Long-term Care]] spending and pension system reforms (e.g., targets for the legal pension age).
*   **Productivity & Education:** Bridging the skills mismatch by promoting [[STEM Education]] to improve business dynamism and reduce job vacancy rates.
*   **Green Transition:** Integrating the [[Energy Transition]] and [[Circular Economy]] targets into the national budget to meet EU climate goals while managing high energy costs.

## Related Context
The Fiscal-Structural Plan is heavily influenced by:
- **Net Expenditure:** Monitoring the actual outflow of public funds against GDP.
- **Regulatory Complexity:** Reducing the tax and regulatory burdens that hinder private investment and business dynamism.
- **Regional Disparities:** Ensuring that fiscal measures address uneven poverty risks and employment rates across different regions.
