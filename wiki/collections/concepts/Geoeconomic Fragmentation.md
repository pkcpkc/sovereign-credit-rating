# Geoeconomic Fragmentation

Geoeconomic fragmentation refers to the increasing division of the global economy into distinct blocs or regional spheres, characterized by a breakdown in integrated trade, investment, and technological cooperation. It represents a shift away from globalized economic integration toward a more fragmented landscape driven by geopolitical tensions, national security concerns, and the weaponization of economic dependencies.

## Key Characteristics and Drivers
*   **Trade Barriers:** The emergence of trade policy shocks, including potential new barriers (notably from the US), which disrupt established global supply chains and market access.
*   **External Uncertainty:** High levels of geopolitical instability that create significant downside risks for national economies, particularly those dependent on international trade and investment.
*   **Economic Blocs:** The tendency for countries to align with specific economic spheres, leading to "friend-shoring" or "de-risking" strategies that prioritize geopolitical alignment over pure economic efficiency.

## Impact on National Economies
The phenomenon poses significant risks to developing and transitioning economies, as evidenced in the context of:
*   **Hungary:** The economy faces significant downside risks due to geoeconomic fragmentation, which complicates the maintenance of stable output and influences the trajectory of inflation and debt sustainability.
*   **Investment Climate:** Fragmentation can lead to a potential loss of investor confidence and create hurdles for firms seeking to navigate complex, bifurcated regulatory environments.
*   **Structural Vulnerability:** Countries with high external uncertainty must navigate a landscape where traditional economic growth drivers are increasingly influenced by non-economic, geopolitical variables.
