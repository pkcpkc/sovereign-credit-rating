# public expenditure

**Public expenditure** refers to the total spending by the general government, including central, state, and local governments, as well as social security funds. In the context of sovereign credit analysis and macroeconomic stability, the level, composition, and trajectory of public expenditure are critical determinants of [[Fiscal Sustainability]], [[Debt Burden]], and [[Macroeconomic Stability]].

## Strategic Context and Fiscal Dynamics

In the specific case of **France**, public expenditure is characterized by high rigidity and substantial volume, significantly impacting the nation's [[Fiscal Performance]] and [[Debt Payment Culture]]. According to the **2025 Country Report - France** issued by the **European Commission**, France's public expenditure stood at **57.1% of GDP** in 2024. This high level of spending is a primary driver of the country's persistently high general government deficits (5.8% of GDP in 2024) and rising public debt (113% of GDP).

### Key Characteristics and Challenges

*   **Expenditure Rigidity:** A significant portion of public expenditure is deemed "rigid," meaning it is difficult to reduce in the short term without major structural reforms. This rigidity limits the government's [[Policy Credibility]] and flexibility in responding to economic shocks.
*   **Interest Payments:** The high debt stock necessitates substantial public expenditure on interest payments. These are projected to rise to **2.9% of GDP by 2026**, crowding out other potentially growth-enhancing expenditures such as [[Green Transition]] investments or [[Business Environment]] improvements.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation Strategy:** Under the reformed **EU Economic Governance Framework**, France is required to implement a medium-term fiscal-structural plan. The focus of public expenditure management has shifted towards **net expenditure growth** targets. The **Council of the European Union** endorsed France's plan in January 2025, emphasizing the need for strict spending reviews to ensure the deficit reduction path is credible.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit and Economic Indicators

The management of public expenditure directly influences several key sovereign credit rating factors and macroeconomic indicators:

1.  **[[Fiscal Sustainability]] Risks:** High and rigid public expenditure exacerbates long-term fiscal risks, particularly concerning aging costs and the sustainability of the [[Social Fairness]] safety net. The **2023 pension reform** was aimed at mitigating these long-term expenditure pressures.
2.  **[[Competitiveness]] and Growth:** High public expenditure is often linked to high taxes on production to fund these services. The report notes that high taxes and complex administrative burdens hinder [[Labor Productivity]] and business competitiveness. Furthermore, inefficient allocation of public funds (e.g., in R&D support schemes like the CIR) can limit [[Economic Diversity]] and innovation output.
3.  **[[Macroeconomic Stability]]:** Political fragmentation following the 2024 elections has led to delays in budget adoption, creating uncertainty around public expenditure levels. This unpredictability can deter private investment, as evidenced by the subdued investment and record corporate bankruptcies in 2024-2025.
4.  **[[Energy Security]] and [[Green Transition]]:** While France has a low-carbon electricity mix, public expenditure is crucial for overcoming barriers in renewable energy deployment and building decarbonization. However, budget constraints and skilled labor shortages are limiting the effectiveness of these expenditures.

## Institutional and Policy Framework

*   **Reformed EU Economic Governance:** France is subject to the new EU fiscal rules, which require a binding medium-term fiscal-structural plan. The **High Council of Public Finance** plays a key role in monitoring these commitments.
*   **Spending Reviews:** The Commission emphasizes that effective spending reviews are essential to identify savings within the high baseline of public expenditure. This involves distinguishing between necessary investments (e.g., [[Green Transition]], digitalization) and rigid, less efficient spending.
*   **Contingent Liabilities:** Public expenditure risks also extend to [[Contingent Liabilities]], such as the state's support for state-owned enterprises like **EDF** in the energy sector, which can impact the overall fiscal position.

## Conclusion

For France, the challenge lies in reducing the high level of public expenditure (57.1% of GDP) without undermining [[Social Fairness]] or essential investments in the [[Green Transition]] and [[Digitalization]]. The success of the medium-term fiscal-structural plan depends on the government's ability to reform rigid expenditure structures, improve the efficiency of public spending, and maintain political stability to ensure timely implementation of fiscal decisions. Failure to control the growth of public expenditure relative to GDP will continue to pose significant threats to [[Fiscal Sustainability]] and increase the [[Debt Burden]] in the medium term.
