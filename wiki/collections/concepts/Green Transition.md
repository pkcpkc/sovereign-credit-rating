# Green Transition

The **Green Transition** refers to the structural shift toward a low-carbon, sustainable economy. In the context of macroeconomic policy and national fiscal planning, it involves the systemic decarbonization of industries, energy systems, and transport networks.

## Key Components and Policy Drivers
Based on international assessments (such as the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation), a successful green transition requires:

*   **Coherent National Strategy:** A unified framework to meet specific climate targets and transition the economy away from carbon-intensive practices.
*   **Fiscal Policy Integration:** Balancing the costs of transition with **Fiscal Consolidation**. This includes rationalizing public spending while specifically protecting and prioritizing public investment in green infrastructure.
*   **Carbon Pricing:** Implementation of carbon taxation as a primary mechanism to internalize environmental costs.
*   **Subsidy Reform:** The systematic phasing out of fossil fuel subsidies to redirect capital toward renewable energy and sustainable technologies.

## Regional Context: Belgium
In Belgium, the green transition is identified as a critical priority for public investment. The IMF highlights that while fiscal consolidation is necessary, spending rationalization should be balanced to ensure that the transition remains funded. The goal is to achieve climate targets without compromising the country's long-term **Sovereign Risk** profile or its ability to invest in essential public services.
