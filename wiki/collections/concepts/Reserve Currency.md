# Reserve Currency

A **Reserve Currency** is a primary currency used by central banks and other financial institutions to conduct international trade, settle debts, and accumulate foreign exchange reserves. In the context of sovereign credit analysis, the status of a country's currency as a reserve currency significantly influences its external assessment and creditworthiness.

## Role in Sovereign Credit Assessment
According to the *S&P Global Ratings: Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)*, a country's currency status is a critical component of the **External Assessment** pillar. The methodology distinguishes between currencies that are "Reserve" status versus those that are merely "Actively Traded."

### Key Implications for Credit Ratings:
*   **External Liquidity:** Reserve currencies typically provide a country with higher levels of external liquidity and lower costs for international transactions.
*   **External Indebtedness:** The status of a currency affects the calculation of narrow net external debt.
*   **Monetary Union Impact:** For members of a monetary union, the initial credit assessment is often derived from the currency status of the union's common currency.
*   **Rating Uplifts:** A country's ability to maintain a stable reserve currency, combined with an independent monetary policy and deep capital markets, can be a factor in determining the spread between foreign-currency and local-currency ratings.
