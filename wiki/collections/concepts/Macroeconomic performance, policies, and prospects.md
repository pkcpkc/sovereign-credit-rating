# Macroeconomic performance, policies, and prospects

**Macroeconomic performance, policies, and prospects** is one of the four core analytical pillars used by **Fitch Ratings** in its **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)** to assess the capacity and willingness of sovereign issuers to honor their debt obligations. Effective as of September 15, 2025, this pillar evaluates the stability, growth trajectory, and policy credibility of a sovereign economy. While it carries a lower weight (9.8%) in the quantitative SRM compared to [[Structural features]] (53.7%), [[Public finances]] (19.1%), and [[External finances]] (17.3%), it is critical for assessing short-to-medium term vulnerabilities and the effectiveness of government responses to economic shocks.

## Role in the Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)

In the quantitative phase of the rating methodology, **Macroeconomic performance, policies, and prospects** serves as a baseline indicator of economic health. The SRM utilizes an **Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)** regression of 18 key variables, with the following specific metrics contributing to this pillar:

*   **Real GDP Growth:** Measures the absolute expansion or contraction of the economy.
*   **Real GDP Growth Volatility:** Assesses the stability of economic growth; higher volatility indicates greater risk and lower predictability for fiscal planning.
*   **Consumer Price Inflation:** Evaluates price stability, a key component of monetary policy credibility and real debt burden.

The SRM generates an initial score based on these inputs, calibrated to the Long-Term Foreign-Currency Issuer Default Rating (FC IDR) scale. However, because quantitative data cannot capture all nuances of economic management, the qualitative overlay is essential.

## Qualitative Overlay (QO) Assessment

The **Qualitative Overlay (QO)** allows analysts to adjust the SRM-derived rating by up to ±2 notches for this pillar (within a total cap of ±3 notches across all pillars). The QO focuses on factors that are difficult to quantify but significantly impact creditworthiness:

### 1. Policy Credibility and Flexibility
*   **Exchange Rate Regimes:** Special attention is paid to fixed exchange rate regimes and dollarized economies. In these contexts, policy flexibility is constrained, and the cost of maintaining the peg or managing a single currency can heavily impact fiscal and external balances.
*   **Monetary and Fiscal Policy:** The effectiveness of central bank independence and the government's ability to implement counter-cyclical policies is evaluated.

### 2. Medium-Term Growth Outlook
*   Beyond historical growth figures, analysts assess the **medium-term GDP growth outlook**. This includes analyzing structural drivers of growth, such as demographics, productivity trends, and investment levels.
*   A weak long-term growth prospect can undermine [[Debt sustainability]] even if current debt levels appear manageable, as the denominator (GDP) grows slowly relative to the numerator (debt).

### 3. Macroeconomic Stability
*   **Unemployment:** High or rising unemployment is assessed as a drag on growth and a source of social/political instability, which can indirectly affect [[Governance indicators]].
*   **Sectoral Imbalances:** The QO identifies vulnerabilities within specific sectors (e.g., over-reliance on tourism, commodities, or real estate) that could trigger broader macroeconomic distress.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Financial Stability

The assessment of **Macroeconomic performance, policies, and prospects** directly influences sovereign credit ratings through several mechanisms:

*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Strong and stable macroeconomic performance provides the revenue base necessary to service [[Public finances]]. Conversely, high inflation or volatile growth erodes tax receipts and increases the real cost of debt, threatening [[Debt sustainability]].
*   **External Vulnerability:** Macroeconomic misalignments (e.g., overvalued exchange rates) can exacerbate [[External finances]] pressures, leading to capital flight or reserve depletion. The **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)** and [[External debt sustainability]] are closely linked to the macroeconomic environment.
*   **Financial Sector Health:** Macroeconomic shocks often translate into [[Macro-prudential risks]] within the banking sector. The **Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)** and **Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)** are monitored to ensure that macroeconomic downturns do not trigger banking crises, which would create significant [[Contingent liabilities]] for the sovereign.
*   **Climate Risk Interaction:** While **Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)** are primarily assessed under structural features, their impact on macroeconomic performance (e.g., through supply chain disruptions or agricultural losses) is evaluated within this pillar. Sovereigns with high Climate.VS scores may face heightened macroeconomic volatility, requiring additional analytical scrutiny.

## Key Metrics and Indicators

| Metric | Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Real GDP Growth | Quantitative (SRM) | Annual change in inflation-adjusted gross domestic product. |
| GDP Growth Volatility | Quantitative (SRM) | Standard deviation of GDP growth over a historical period. |
| Consumer Price Inflation | Quantitative (SRM) | Year-over-year change in the price level of a basket of consumer goods. |
| Policy Credibility | Qualitative (QO) | Assessment of government and central bank commitment to stability. |
| Growth Outlook | Qualitative (QO) | Medium-term projections based on structural economic drivers. |
| Sectoral Balance | Qualitative (QO) | Diversity and resilience of key economic sectors. |
