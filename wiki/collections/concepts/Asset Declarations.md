# Asset Declarations

Asset declarations are a fundamental transparency mechanism used to monitor the financial holdings, interests, and assets of public officials to prevent corruption, conflicts of interest, and illicit enrichment.

## Current Status in Hungary
Based on the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the framework for asset declarations in Hungary faces significant systemic challenges:

*   **Transparency Shortcomings:** There are significant deficiencies in the transparency and oversight of asset declarations specifically for individuals holding senior political functions.
*   **Anti-Corruption Context:** While the country has established some low-level codes of conduct, the lack of robust oversight on high-level asset disclosures contributes to the perception that high-level corruption remains largely unaddressed.
*   **Institutional Obstacles:** The effectiveness of monitoring these declarations is hindered by the limitations faced by the Integrity Authority, which struggles with accessing necessary state databases to verify reported assets.

## Contextual Developments in Poland
The 2025 Rule of Law Report for Poland provides additional context regarding the broader anti-corruption landscape, which informs the systemic requirements for asset declaration frameworks:

*   **Anti-Corruption Strategy Gaps:** While Poland has seen institutional shifts (such as the liquidation of the CBA and the operationalization of the EPPO), the report notes a lack of a comprehensive national anti-corruption strategy. This highlights a systemic need for standardized asset declaration protocols to complement new enforcement bodies.
*   **High-Level Impunity:** A critical hurdle identified in the Polish context is the persistence of "impunity clauses" for top executives. Effective asset declaration systems are considered a primary tool to mitigate these legal shields and ensure accountability for high-level officials.
*   **Institutional Coordination:** The transition of competencies to the police, Internal Security Agency (ABW), and National Revenue Administration (KAS) suggests a move toward multi-agency oversight, which is essential for verifying the veracity of declared assets.
