# public finance deterioration

**Public finance deterioration** refers to the progressive worsening of a government's fiscal position, characterized by widening budget deficits, rising public debt-to-GDP ratios, and increasing structural imbalances between expenditures and revenues. In the context of sovereign credit analysis, this phenomenon signals a degradation of **[[fiscal sustainability]]** and an elevated risk of **[[debt sustainability]]** stress.

The 2025 Country Report for Belgium provides a critical case study of public finance deterioration driven by demographic pressures, rigid expenditure structures, and weak institutional coordination. The deterioration is not merely cyclical but structural, rooted in high **[[ageing costs]]** and limited fiscal flexibility.

## Key Drivers and Characteristics

### 1. Deficit Widening and Debt Trajectory
A primary indicator of public finance deterioration is the expansion of the general government deficit beyond sustainable limits, leading to an upward trajectory of public debt.
*   **Deficit Expansion**: In Belgium, the general government deficit widened from 4.5% of GDP in 2024 to a projected 5.4% of GDP in 2025. This expansion is driven by net expenditure growth (forecast at 5.0% for 2025) that outpaces revenue growth.
*   **Debt Accumulation**: With public debt already high at 104.7% of GDP in 2024, the lack of primary surpluses pushes the debt-to-GDP ratio higher. Without policy intervention, projections indicate a surge to 126.4% of GDP by 2035, severely limiting future **fiscal performance and flexibility**.

### 2. Structural Expenditure Pressures
Deterioration is often fueled by rigid expenditure components that are difficult to adjust in the short term.
*   **Ageing-Related Costs**: Belgium faces one of the steepest increases in age-related spending in the EU. By 2070, ageing expenditure is projected to rise by 5.1 percentage points of GDP (compared to the EU average of 1.4). This is driven by:
    *   Pension spending increasing by 3.5% of GDP.
    *   Long-term care spending increasing by 1.7% of GDP.
*   **Rigid Expenditure Structure**: The expenditure profile is dominated by these pension and long-term care costs, leaving little room for counter-cyclical policy or new investments.
*   **Defence Spending**: Recent geopolitical shifts have necessitated increased defence spending (projected to reach 1.7% of GDP in 2025), further straining the budget. While accommodated via the **National Escape Clause**, this contributes to the immediate fiscal pressure.

### 3. Revenue Constraints and Fiscal Framework Weaknesses
Public finance deterioration is exacerbated when revenue bases are constrained or fiscal governance is ineffective.
*   **Stable but Insufficient Revenue**: Belgium maintains a high tax burden (tax revenue ~42.5% of GDP), yet this stable base is insufficient to cover rising expenditures, leading to primary deficits.
*   **Institutional Fragmentation**: Weak budgetary coordination between federal and regional entities in Belgium prevents effective monitoring of compliance with fiscal rules. The **High Council of Finance** lacks sufficient autonomy and resources to enforce constraints, contributing to the deterioration.
*   **Absence of Multiannual Planning**: Historically, the lack of a fully developed multiannual fiscal planning framework at the national level has led to discrepancies between government projections and independent forecasts (e.g., from the **Federal Planning Bureau** or the **European Commission**).

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Country Risk

Public finance deterioration directly impacts sovereign credit ratings through several channels:

*   **Debt Payment Culture and Flexibility**: While Belgium has a history of timely debt servicing, the rising debt trajectory (126.4% by 2035) limits fiscal flexibility. In times of economic shock, the high debt burden reduces the government's ability to implement stimulus measures, increasing vulnerability.
*   **Policy Credibility**: The activation of the **National Escape Clause** and the reliance on long adjustment periods (7-year MTP) may raise concerns among rating agencies about the political feasibility of sustained fiscal consolidation. Weak enforcement mechanisms (e.g., under-resourced fiscal councils) further erode policy credibility.
*   **Economic Strength and Tax Base**: Fiscal deterioration is both a cause and consequence of weak economic performance. High tax burdens on labour and low **productivity growth** constrain the expansion of the tax base, creating a vicious cycle where deterioration begets further economic stagnation.
*   **Contingent Liabilities**: Opaque off-budget obligations due to the complex federal structure and state-owned enterprises increase the risk of unexpected fiscal shocks, a key factor in sovereign risk assessment.

## Mitigation and Policy Responses

To arrest public finance deterioration, comprehensive structural reforms are required, as outlined in Belgium's **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)**:
*   **Pension Reform**: Introduction of a bonus-malus system and reduction of assimilated periods to improve long-term sustainability.
*   **Labour Market Activation**: Limiting unemployment benefits and linking them to income to boost activity rates and broaden the tax base.
*   **Fiscal Framework Strengthening**: Enhancing the autonomy and resources of fiscal councils and improving coordination between federal and regional budgets.
*   **Revenue Mobilization**: Implementing tax reforms, such as labour tax cuts financed by capital gains and property taxes, to improve competitiveness while maintaining revenue levels.
