# Legal Certainty

**Legal Certainty** is a fundamental principle of the **European Union** legal order, requiring that laws be clear, precise, and predictable so that individuals and businesses can ascertain their rights and obligations. In the context of **Hungary**, the 2025 **Rule of Law Report** identifies a systemic deterioration in legal certainty, driven by frequent legislative changes, the extensive use of emergency powers, and selective enforcement. This erosion directly impacts **sovereign credit ratings**, **fiscal sustainability**, and **country risk analysis** by undermining investor confidence, distorting market competition, and creating unpredictable regulatory environments.

## Core Drivers of Legal Uncertainty in Hungary

The report highlights several structural and legislative factors that compromise legal certainty:

### 1. Erosion via Emergency Powers
The Hungarian government’s repeated extension of the **"state of danger"** until November 2025 has been used to override parliamentary acts and adopt measures unrelated to the initial humanitarian crisis, such as price caps and VAT exemptions. While the **Fifteenth Amendment** (effective 2026) introduces a requirement for parliamentary authorization to extend this state, the current framework allows executive dominance over legislative processes. This practice creates significant legal uncertainty, particularly regarding its impact on the **single market** and cross-border trade.

### 2. Legislative Instability and Discriminatory Regulation
Legal certainty is further eroded by:
*   **Frequent Legislative Changes:** Constant amendments to laws undermine the stability of the legal framework.
*   **Late-Stage Substantive Amendments:** These often bypass public consultation, preventing stakeholders from adapting to new requirements.
*   **Discriminatory Regulations:** Specific taxes and export restrictions target foreign investors in strategic sectors (e.g., financial, retail, construction). These "tailor-made" measures have led to de-investment patterns and ongoing **infringement procedures** by the **European Commission** for violating EU law on the freedom of establishment.

### 3. Judicial Independence and Enforcement
The lack of functional independence in oversight bodies contributes to selective enforcement:
*   **Judicial Independence Erosion:** The **National Judicial Council**’s powers have been centralized, and undue pressure on judges persists, including public criticism from the **Kúria President**. The **Fourteenth Amendment** raised the minimum age for judges to 35 and extended retirement to 70, changes which judicial associations argue compromise independence.
*   **Prosecutor General Appointment:** The **Fifteenth Amendment** removed the requirement for the **Prosecutor General** to be selected from among prosecutors, lowering eligibility criteria and increasing the risk of political influence.
*   **Merger Control Interference:** The government retains the power to declare mergers of "strategic importance," exempting them from **Competition Authority** control and judicial review. This facilitates nationalization strategies in energy, media, and telecommunications, creating significant legal uncertainty for investors regarding asset protection and market entry.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Country Risk

The degradation of legal certainty has direct negative implications for **Hungary**’s sovereign credit profile and macroeconomic stability:

*   **Investment Protection Confidence:** Legal uncertainty hinders the ability of foreign companies to protect their investments, leading to structural barriers for foreign firms in strategic sectors.
*   **De-investment and Market Concentration:** Regulatory pressure has led to de-investment by foreign firms and increased market concentration due to framework agreements with single bidders, raising **corruption risks** in **public procurement** and construction.
*   **Fiscal Risks:** The use of emergency decrees for multiple budget amendments and disproportionate retail taxes on larger foreign companies creates fiscal unpredictability. Additionally, opaque public interest trusts linked to the **National Bank** introduce contingent liabilities and fiscal sustainability concerns.
*   **EU Fund Suspension:** The suspension of **EU funds** due to rule of law breaches and infringement procedures directly impacts market access, investment flows, and the state's ability to finance its budget deficits.
