# Prosecutor General

The **Prosecutor General** is a key constitutional office in Poland whose functional independence is critical to the integrity of the judicial system, anti-corruption efforts, and overall rule of law compliance. As of 2025, the role has undergone significant structural reforms aimed at separating it from the **Minister of Justice**, a move designed to mitigate political interference and enhance institutional credibility. This separation is a central pillar of Poland's **Action Plan on Rule of Law implementation**, directly impacting sovereign credit assessments by reducing political risk and improving institutional governance strength.

## Institutional Reform and Separation of Powers

A landmark development in 2024–2025 was the legislative effort to separate the functions of the **Minister of Justice** from those of the **Prosecutor General**. The Polish Cabinet adopted a draft law to effect this separation, aiming to ensure the functional independence of the prosecution service. Key components of this reform include:

- **Discontinuation of Criminal Investigations**: The state ceased criminal investigations against **Supreme Court** judges for their judicial decisions, a practice previously used to exert pressure on the judiciary.
- **End of Disciplinary Secondments**: The practice of seconding prosecutors to other roles as a disciplinary sanction was discontinued, removing a tool for indirect political control over prosecutorial staff.
- **Legislative Timeline**: The draft law was adopted by the Cabinet in late 2024, with implementation scheduled to take effect in 2025. This reform is part of broader efforts to align Poland's institutional framework with **European Union** standards and requirements for accessing structural funds.

## Impact on Anti-Corruption and Financial Stability

The independence of the **Prosecutor General** is a prerequisite for effective anti-corruption measures, which are directly linked to **sovereign credit ratings** and **fiscal sustainability**.

- **European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO)**: As of January 31, 2025, the **European Public Prosecutor Office** became fully operational in Poland. The independence of the national prosecution service is vital for the EPPO's effectiveness in investigating fraud affecting the EU budget.
- **Corruption Risks**: While large-scale corruption cases involving public officials are being investigated, systemic risks remain in **public procurement** and state-owned enterprises. The independence of the **Prosecutor General** is seen as a key strength indicator by credit rating agencies, contributing to "compliance with EU rule of law mechanisms" and "EU fund management" strengths.
- **Sovereign Credit Factors**: The reform contributes to "Institutional and Governance Strength" by addressing "Judicial politicization." It reduces "Political and Geopolitical Risk" threats related to "Constitutional crisis with EU" and improves "Policy Predictability and Reform Continuity."

## Current Challenges and Risks

Despite progress, significant challenges to the **Prosecutor General**'s independence persist:

- **Politicization**: Perceived judicial independence remains very low (26% among the general public). The legacy of pre-2025 reforms continues to impact trust in prosecutorial decisions.
- **Impunity and Lobbying**: No progress has been made on introducing comprehensive lobbying regulations, and impunity clauses for top executives remain in force, potentially undermining the **Prosecutor General**'s ability to prosecute high-level corruption effectively.
- **Implementation Gaps**: While the legislative framework for separation exists, full operational independence requires sustained political will and consistent application by the government led by **Prime Minister Donald Tusk**.
