# Digitalisation of Justice

Digitalisation of Justice refers to the integration of digital technologies and overarching digital strategies into the judicial system to improve efficiency, transparency, and accessibility. This involves moving beyond isolated IT projects toward a cohesive digital infrastructure that supports the rule of law and judicial administration.

## Current Status and Challenges (Belgium)
Recent assessments of the Belgian justice system highlight several key points regarding the digital transformation of its judiciary:

- **Strategic Oversight:** The Belgian Government is currently addressing criticisms from the Court of Audit regarding the lack of a cohesive, overarching approach to digitalisation.
- **Consultancy Dependency:** There is a noted concern regarding an excessive reliance on external consultants to drive digital initiatives rather than internal structural development.
- **Efficiency and Backlogs:** Digitalisation is being leveraged as a tool to combat long-standing backlogs, particularly in high-volume areas like the Brussels Court of Appeal. 
- **Data Management:** Projects funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility are specifically targeting file management systems to improve the accuracy and availability of judicial statistics.

## Case Study: France (2025 Report)
France has demonstrated significant progress in digitalising judicial procedures, characterized by the following developments:

- **The Portalis Tool:** A key instrument for expanding digital access, currently being rolled out to all labor and local courts.
- **Public Accessibility:** By late 2024, online access to over one million decisions was achieved. The government has set a target for the end of 2025 to have all criminal court decisions uploaded online.
- **AI Integration:** The Government and the Court of Cassation are actively studying safeguards for the use of Artificial Intelligence in drafting administrative decisions and performing other judicial tasks.
