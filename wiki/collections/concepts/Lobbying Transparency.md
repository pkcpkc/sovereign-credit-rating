# Lobbying Transparency

Lobbying transparency refers to the systems, regulations, and public disclosure requirements designed to monitor and provide visibility into the influence efforts of interest groups, corporations, and individuals on public policy and legislative processes.

## Current Status and Developments (France)
Based on the 2025 Rule of Law Report, France has made significant strides in establishing a framework for lobbying transparency, though several key areas remain under development or refinement:

*   **Legislative Progress:** A notable milestone was reached in October 2024 with the passage of a law dedicated to recording the declarations of lobbyists representing foreign entities.
*   **Institutional Oversight:** The High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP) plays a central role in oversight, notably seeing a 48% increase in the examination of asset declarations in 2024.
*   **Ethical Frameworks:** New ethics codes have been adopted for Members of Parliament (MPs) and the Presidency to further standardize conduct.
*   **Remaining Gaps:** Despite improvements in general transparency, current disclosure requirements do not yet apply to officials at the top-executive level.

## Related Concepts
*   [[Anti-Corruption]]: Lobbying transparency serves as a primary pillar in the broader anti-corruption framework, aimed at preventing undue influence and bribery.
*   [[Judicial Independence]]: Ensuring that policy-making and judicial oversight are not compromised by opaque lobbying interests.
*   [[Media Pluralism]]: Transparency in lobbying is often linked to media freedom, as it ensures that the public and journalists can monitor who is influencing the narrative of governance.
