# Qualitative Overlay (QO)

## Definition and Core Function

**Qualitative Overlay (QO)** is a critical adjustment mechanism within the [[Fitch Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] that accounts for qualitative factors, structural nuances, and risks not captured by the quantitative Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression. While the SRM provides a baseline score based on 18 key variables across four analytical pillars, the QO allows analysts to refine this output to reflect the specific context of a sovereign issuer, such as geopolitical risk, policy credibility, financial sector stability, and emerging climate vulnerabilities.

The QO serves as the bridge between raw statistical modeling and expert judgment, ensuring that the final [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] output aligns with the comprehensive assessment of a country's capacity and willingness to honor its debt obligations.

## Mechanism and Adjustment Rules

The QO operates by applying notch adjustments to the initial score generated by the SRM. The methodology defines specific constraints and allowances for these adjustments:

*   **Per-Pillar Adjustment Range:** Analysts can apply a notch adjustment of **±2** for each of the four analytical pillars:
    1.  [[Structural features]]
    2.  [[Macroeconomic performance, policies, and prospects]]
    3.  [[Public finances]]
    4.  [[External finances]]
*   **Overall Cap:** The total QO adjustment is generally capped at **±3 notches** from the SRM output.
*   **Exceptions:** Wider adjustments beyond the ±3 notch cap are permitted in specific scenarios, including:
    *   Countries currently in crisis or recovering from a crisis.
    *   Sovereigns with a history of default within the last five years.
    *   Situations where material risks are not adequately reflected in the model's variables.

## Pillar-Specific Qualitative Assessments

The QO is not applied as a single blanket adjustment but is synthesized from assessments across the four core pillars of the [[Fitch Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]]:

### 1. Structural Features
This pillar carries the highest weight in the SRM (53.7%). The QO here addresses factors such as:
*   **Political Stability and Geopolitical Risks:** Assessment of governance quality beyond the standard [[Governance indicators]] (e.g., World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators), including political fragmentation or external geopolitical pressures.
*   **Financial Sector Health:** Evaluation of the banking system's resilience using the [[Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)]] and [[Macro-prudential risks]] via the [[Macro-prudential Indicator Model (MPI)]].
*   **Business Environment:** Quality of the institutional framework and rule of law.

### 2. Macroeconomic Performance, Policies, and Prospects
With a lower SRM weight (9.8%), this pillar's QO focuses on:
*   **Policy Credibility:** The track record and flexibility of policymakers, particularly in economies with fixed exchange rate regimes or high dollarization.
*   **Medium-Term Outlook:** Assessment of long-term growth potential and macroeconomic stability, including unemployment trends and sectoral imbalances.

### 3. Public Finances
Accounting for 19.1% of the SRM weight, the QO evaluates:
*   **Fiscal Financing Flexibility:** Access to domestic and international markets, and the structure of the debt portfolio.
*   **Debt Sustainability:** Insights from the [[Fitch Debt Dynamics Model (DDM)]] regarding long-term fiscal trajectories.
*   **Fiscal Structure:** The breadth of the revenue base and budgetary rigidity.
*   **Contingent Liabilities:** Potential risks from [[Contingent liabilities]] such as [[Debt sustainability]] challenges in state-owned enterprises (SOEs) or required bailouts for the banking sector.

### 4. External Finances
Representing 17.3% of the SRM weight, the QO assesses:
*   **External Financing Flexibility:** Resilience of market access and reliance on short-term borrowing.
*   **Liquidity Risk:** Use of the [[International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)]] to gauge short-term external liquidity positions.
*   **Balance Sheet Vulnerability:** Exposure to external shocks, remittance flows, and [[External finances]] structure.
*   **Reserve-Currency Flexibility:** The ability to issue debt in sovereign currency, which mitigates external default risk.

## Integration of Climate Risk (Climate.VS)

A significant evolution in the QO framework (effective September 2025) is the integration of [[Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)]]. This screening tool identifies sovereigns with high exposure to physical and transition risks.

*   **Screening Threshold:** Sovereigns with a Climate.VS score of **50 or higher** in 2035 are subject to additional analytical scrutiny.
*   **Risk Categories:**
    *   **Physical Risks:** Exposure to heatwaves, droughts, storms, floods, and sea-level rise.
    *   **Transition Risks:** Dependence on fossil fuels and costs associated with green energy transition.
*   **QO Application:** If climate risks are deemed material and are not sufficiently captured by the existing SRM variables or other QO factors, analysts may apply a notch adjustment under the "Other structural factors" category within the Structural Features pillar. This ensures that long-term sustainability risks are reflected in the credit rating.

## Significance in Sovereign Credit Analysis

The Qualitative Overlay is essential for distinguishing between sovereigns that may appear similar statistically but have divergent risk profiles due to institutional, political, or environmental factors. It ensures that the [[Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)]] remains dynamic and responsive to:

1.  **Non-Linear Risks:** Events such as political shocks or natural disasters that do not follow historical statistical patterns.
2.  **Policy Nuances:** The effectiveness of fiscal and monetary policies, which cannot be fully quantified by standard macroeconomic variables.
3.  **Emerging Threats:** Such as [[Climate Risk]], which requires forward-looking assessment beyond historical financial data.

By capping adjustments at ±3 notches under normal conditions, Fitch maintains the discipline of its quantitative model while allowing sufficient flexibility to adjust for material qualitative weaknesses or strengths. This balance ensures that ratings reflect both historical performance and forward-looking risk assessments.
