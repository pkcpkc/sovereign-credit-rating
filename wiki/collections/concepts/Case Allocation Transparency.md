# Case Allocation Transparency

**Case Allocation Transparency** refers to the procedural mechanisms and systemic safeguards ensuring that the distribution of legal cases to specific judges or judicial panels is conducted impartially, predictably, and free from discretionary interference by administrative officials or political actors. High transparency in this domain is a critical indicator of **[[Judicial Independence]]** and the overall integrity of the justice system.

## Definition and Core Principles

In a rule-of-law compliant system, case allocation should be automated or governed by strict, publicly verifiable algorithms that prevent "forum shopping" or the strategic assignment of sensitive cases to judges perceived as favorable to specific political or economic interests. Key principles include:

*   **Impartiality:** Cases must be assigned without regard to the identity of the parties, the nature of the dispute, or potential political implications.
*   **Verifiability:** The criteria and mechanisms for allocation must be transparent to the public and legal professionals, allowing for the verification of whether allocation rules were followed.
*   **Limitation of Discretion:** Administrative officials (e.g., court managers) should have minimal to no discretionary power to override allocation protocols, preventing manipulation of judicial outcomes through procedural means.

## Current Status and Challenges: The Hungarian Context

Based on the **2025 Rule of Law Report** issued by the **[[European Commission]]**, the state of case allocation transparency in Hungary presents a dichotomy between higher and lower courts, highlighting significant systemic deficiencies in lower-instance courts.

### Lower Courts: Critical Deficiencies
*   **Lack of Progress:** The report highlights that no progress has been made in improving the transparency of case allocation systems in lower-instance courts, despite it being a long-standing recommendation from EU bodies.
*   **Discretionary Abuse:** Significant discretion remains in the hands of court managers. This discretion, combined with frequent changes to allocation rules, prevents effective verification of impartiality.
*   **Impact on Independence:** The opacity in lower courts undermines confidence in the **[[Judicial Independence]]** of the broader system, as it raises concerns that cases may be steered toward judges sympathetic to government positions or specific interests.

### Higher Courts: Functioning Mechanisms
*   **Kúria Performance:** In contrast to lower courts, case allocation at the **[[Kúria]]** (the Supreme Court of Hungary) is reported to be functioning well with high transparency. This suggests that while top-level judicial processes may adhere to international standards, the foundational transparency required for a robust rule of law is absent at the level where the majority of legal disputes are resolved.
