# Budgetary Framework

The **Budgetary Framework** refers to the set of rules, ceilings, and transparency mechanisms that govern a country's public spending, revenue generation, and debt management. It serves as the foundational structure for a nation's [[Fiscal Policy]], ensuring that expenditures remain sustainable and aligned with long-term economic objectives.

## Current Context: Hungary
Based on the 2025 Country Report, Hungary's current budgetary framework is characterized by several structural and operational challenges:

*   **Transparency and Reliability:** The framework currently lacks transparency and reliability. This is primarily due to frequent revisions of fiscal plans and a notable absence of **multiannual spending ceilings**, which are standard for predictable economic planning.
*   **Expenditure Dynamics:** There is an observed **expansionary budgetary bias**. For instance, net expenditure growth in 2025 is projected at 6.1%, which exceeds specific Council recommendations.
*   **Fiscal Risks & Debt:** The framework is under pressure from high **Public Debt** (reaching 73.5% of GDP in 2024) and elevated debt-servicing costs. The implicit interest rate on government debt remains high (near 6% in 2025), necessitating a robust and stable budgetary structure to maintain fiscal sustainability.
*   **Policy Interventions:** The framework's execution is often influenced by national specificities, such as "escape clauses" for defense spending, which can affect the cumulative limits of public expenditure.
