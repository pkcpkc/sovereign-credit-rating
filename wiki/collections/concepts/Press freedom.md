# Press freedom

**Press freedom** refers to the right and ability of individuals and organizations to gather, produce, and disseminate information and ideas without undue interference from state or non-state actors. In the context of sovereign credit rating and country risk analysis, press freedom serves as a critical proxy for institutional quality, transparency, and the rule of law. Strong protections for journalists and a pluralistic media landscape enhance policy credibility, deter corruption, and foster the political stability necessary for sustainable economic growth.

## Concept Synthesis: Belgium Case Study (2025 Rule of Law Report)

Based on the European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report regarding Belgium, press freedom is characterized by a strong legal framework that is currently under pressure from budgetary constraints, civic space narrowing, and emerging digital threats.

### 1. Legal Framework and Journalist Protections
Belgium has recently strengthened the statutory protections for press freedom through legislative reforms aimed at aligning with European standards:
*   **Decriminalization of Defamation:** Defamation has been decriminalized, shifting such disputes to civil courts, which reduces the chilling effect of criminal penalties on journalistic inquiry.
*   **Enhanced Penalties for Crimes Against Journalists:** New laws introduce harsher penalties for attacks on journalists, recognizing the specific vulnerability of media professionals.
*   **EU Compliance:** Efforts are underway to transpose the **EU anti-SLAPP Directive** (Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation), which aims to protect journalists from abusive litigation intended to silence critical reporting.

Despite these legal advancements, practical challenges persist. Journalists report increasing levels of harassment, legal intimidation, and online threats. The gap between statutory protection and on-the-ground safety remains a point of concern for institutional observers.

### 2. Media Pluralism and Regulatory Independence
The structural independence of media regulators is a key component of press freedom in Belgium:
*   **Regulatory Strength:** Regional media regulators—the **Flemish Regulator for the Media (VRM)**, the **Conseil Supérieur de l’Audiovisuel (CSA)**, and the **Medienrat**—have strengthened their legal and operational independence. This independence was bolstered by increased powers under the **Digital Services Act (DSA)**.
*   **Risk Assessment:** The **Media Pluralism Monitor** reports a "low risk" for political independence in the Belgian media landscape, although this risk level has slightly increased compared to 2024, indicating a need for continued vigilance.
*   **Public Service Media (PSM) Governance:** Public broadcasters (**VRT**, **RTBF**, **BRF**) operate under governance structures that are subject to scrutiny. While they remain central to media pluralism, they face significant budgetary and political pressures.

### 3. Threats to Press Freedom and Civic Space
Press freedom is intrinsically linked to the broader **civic space**, which the European Commission rates as "narrowed" in Belgium. Restrictions on related freedoms directly impact the ability of the press to operate freely and report on public affairs:
*   **Restrictions on Demonstrations:** Proposed measures, including judicial bans for individuals convicted of acts during protests, pose obstacles to the right to demonstrate. This limits the public’s ability to express dissent, a key subject of journalistic coverage.
*   **Access to Public Documents:** While federal legislation has partially improved the transparency framework, procedural shortcomings remain. The appeal bodies for access to administrative documents are non-binding, and the **Council of Europe Convention on Access to Official Documents** has been signed but not ratified. Limited access to information hampers investigative journalism.
*   **Political Interference Risks:** There are broader concerns regarding public criticism of the judiciary by politicians and allegations of information leaks in sensitive corruption investigations. Such an environment can indirectly threaten press freedom by fostering a climate of intimidation or undue influence over media narratives.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Country Risk

The state of press freedom in Belgium has direct and indirect implications for sovereign credit rating factors:

### Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength
*   **Transparency and Anti-Corruption:** A free press acts as a watchdog against corruption. Belgium’s relatively low score on corruption perceptions is supported by independent media oversight. However, gaps in lobbying transparency and asset declaration verification weaken this check. Stronger press freedom enhances the accountability of public officials, reducing the risk of hidden fiscal liabilities or policy mismanagement.
*   **Institutional Checks:** The independence of media regulators and the decriminalization of defamation reflect a commitment to **institutional checks** and balances. This contributes to a stable regulatory environment, which is favorable for long-term investment confidence.

### Geopolitical and Political Stability
*   **Civic Space and Social Cohesion:** Narrowing civic space, including restrictions on demonstrations and access to information, can lead to social unrest and erode public trust in institutions. For credit rating agencies, political fragmentation and social instability are negative factors. Ensuring robust press freedom helps mitigate these risks by allowing for open discourse and the peaceful resolution of grievances.
*   **Rule of Law Adherence:** Belgium’s compliance with European norms (e.g., anti-SLAPP directives) and the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) standards is monitored through media coverage. Persistent structural issues, such as the slow implementation of ECtHR judgments (17 leading judgments pending as of Jan 1, 2025), are highlighted by the press, keeping pressure on the government to reform.

### Fiscal Sustainability
*   **Budgetary Pressures on PSM:** The financial health of public service media is linked to state subsidies. **RTBF** faces a projected EUR 78 million shortfall, and there are proposed budget cuts to bodies like **Unia** (Interfederal Centre for Equal Opportunities). While not a direct fiscal burden on the state budget in the short term, the degradation of PSM quality can signal broader fiscal mismanagement or political neglect of institutional infrastructure, affecting long-term institutional quality scores.

## Key Institutions and Stakeholders

*   **European Commission:** Issuer of the Rule of Law Report, monitoring media pluralism and freedom.
*   **Council of Europe:** Oversees compliance with human rights standards, including freedom of expression.
*   **Media Regulators:** **VRM**, **CSA**, **Medienrat** ensure compliance with broadcasting and digital content regulations.
*   **Public Service Media:** **VRT**, **RTBF**, **BRF** serve as key sources of impartial news and pluralistic content.
*   **Civil Society Organizations (CSOs):** Monitor access to documents and advocate for journalist protections.
