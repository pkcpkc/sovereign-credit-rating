# Demographics

**Demographics** refers to the statistical characteristics of a population, including age distribution, workforce size, and dependency ratios. In the context of sovereign credit analysis and [[Macroeconomic Stability]], demographic trends are critical structural factors that influence long-term fiscal sustainability, labour market dynamics, and economic growth prospects.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit and Fiscal Policy

Demographic shifts, particularly ageing populations and shrinking working-age cohorts, pose significant threats to sovereign credit ratings by increasing fiscal burdens on [[Pension System]]s and public healthcare while constraining potential economic growth.

### Labour Market Implications
*   **Shrinking Workforce:** Poland faces a shrinking working-age population, leading to labour market tightness and persistent skills shortages. This demographic pressure exacerbates competitiveness challenges, particularly in [[Clean Technology]] and manufacturing sectors.
*   **Employment Dynamics:** While Poland’s employment rate (78.4% in 2024) has surpassed EU 2030 targets, the demographic structure creates risks for integrating underrepresented groups, including older workers, persons with disabilities, and women.
*   **Productivity Risks:** Low labour productivity, partly driven by demographic constraints and skill mismatches (e.g., declining basic skills among youth and low STEM enrolment), threatens long-term economic diversity and growth prospects.

### Fiscal Sustainability Pressures
*   **Pension System Strain:** An ageing society places increasing pressure on the sustainability of the pension system. With statutory retirement ages set at 60 for women and 65 for men, the annuity factor is projected to lead to a substantial decline in pension benefits relative to rising life expectancy. This dynamic potentially increases government spending, contributing to the rise in public debt.
*   **Healthcare and Long-Term Care:** Poor health outcomes among working-age adults and a lack of formal long-term care capacity shift the burden onto informal carers, creating hidden fiscal liabilities and social costs.
*   **Fiscal Deficit Risks:** Demographic pressures are a contributing factor to the general government deficit (reaching 5.3% of GDP in 2023), which triggered an [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]] initiated by the Council in January 2025.

## Structural Reforms and Policy Responses

To mitigate demographic risks, Poland is pursuing structural reforms aimed at enhancing [[Fiscal Sustainability]] and labour market efficiency.

*   **Pension Reforms:** There is ongoing discussion regarding the adjustment of retirement ages and the promotion of private pension schemes (PPE, IKZE). However, uptake remains limited, with only about 30% of the eligible population participating in private schemes.
*   **Labour Market Integration:** Policies are being developed to address the disability employment gap (35.6% in 2024) and reduce the gender employment gap (11.6 pps in 2024) through improved childcare and long-term care services.
*   **Skills Development:** Addressing skill shortages is critical for maintaining Poland’s status as a clean tech hub. Reforms focus on improving STEM enrolment, adult learning participation, and science-business collaboration to counteract demographic-driven labour shortages.
