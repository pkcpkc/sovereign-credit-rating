# Industrial Policy

Industrial policy refers to the strategic use of government action to shape economic development, influence the structure of production, and foster specific industrial sectors. In the context of modern economies, it involves a mix of fiscal incentives, regulatory frameworks, and structural reforms aimed at enhancing competitiveness and addressing systemic economic gaps.

## Contextual Application: Hungary
Based on the [[IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Hungary]], industrial policy in Hungary is currently evaluated through the lens of structural reform and productivity enhancement. Key elements include:

*   **Productivity and Firm Dynamism:** A primary objective is closing the productivity gap by eliminating barriers to factor mobility and reducing state aid distortions that may hinder market competition.
*   **Energy Transition:** Industrial strategy is increasingly tied to energy security, specifically the phasing out of fossil fuel subsidies and the transition toward renewable energy sources.
*   **Innovation and Capital:** Policy focus is directed toward expanding access to risk capital for high-growth startups to foster a more dynamic private sector.
*   **Governance and Funding:** Improving the business environment through governance reforms is identified as a critical lever to unlock [[Structural Reforms]] and access EU Cohesion funds.
