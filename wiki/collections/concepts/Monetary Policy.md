# Monetary Policy

Monetary policy refers to the actions undertaken by a central bank—such as the **Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB)** in Hungary—to manage the money supply, interest rates, and other monetary factors to achieve macroeconomic objectives like price stability, full employment, and sustainable economic growth.

## Current Context: Hungary (2025)
Based on the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation**, the current monetary landscape for Hungary is characterized by a "tight monetary stance" necessitated by persistent inflationary pressures.

### Key Components and Strategies
*   **Interest Rate Management:** The MNB is advised to maintain a restrictive base rate (currently at 6.5%). This is intended to anchor inflation expectations while navigating a period where core inflation remains elevated (projected at 4.5% in Q4:2025).
*   **Data-Dependent Approach:** Policy decisions are increasingly tied to real-time economic data to balance the need for disinflation against the risks of stagnant output and high refinancing costs.
*   **Inflation Targeting:** A primary objective is to return headline inflation to a target of 3.0% by 2027.

### Risks and Interdependencies
*   **Sovereign-Bank Nexus:** A critical area of concern is the linkage between sovereign debt and the banking sector. While the banking sector remains well-capitalized, vulnerabilities exist regarding corporate FX exposures and the potential for sovereign stress to impact financial stability.
*   **Fiscal-Monetary Interaction:** The effectiveness of monetary policy is intertwined with [[Fiscal Adjustment]]. The IMF emphasizes that maintaining a tight stance requires complementary fiscal measures, such as rebuilding fiscal buffers and reducing headline deficits.
*   **Market Distortions:** The presence of price and margin caps, along with housing-related fiscal incentives and voluntary APR caps, are identified as unsustainable distortions that can complicate the transmission of monetary policy.
