# Public investment efficiency

Public investment efficiency refers to the ability of government expenditure on capital assets (infrastructure, technology, human capital) to generate sustained economic growth, productivity gains, and long-term fiscal sustainability. It is a critical determinant of [[Sovereign Credit Rating]] factors, particularly regarding growth prospects and fiscal flexibility. Inefficient public investment—characterized by cost overruns, delays, or projects with low economic returns—exacerbates fiscal deficits and debt burdens without delivering corresponding improvements in competitiveness or potential GDP.

Conversely, high-efficiency public investment serves as a multiplier for potential growth, allowing governments to pursue fiscal consolidation without sacrificing long-term economic capacity. International institutions, including the IMF and European Commission, increasingly emphasize that fiscal adjustment strategies must prioritize rationalizing current spending rather than cutting productive investment. Enhancing efficiency in sectors such as healthcare, education, and digital infrastructure is viewed as essential for maintaining debt sustainability while addressing structural challenges like demographic decline and the green transition.

## Context: Comparative Challenges in Poland and Belgium

While both Poland and Belgium are subject to [[EU Economic Governance]] frameworks requiring fiscal consolidation, their approaches to public investment efficiency are shaped by distinct structural and institutional contexts.

### Poland: Implementation Capacity and Structural Gaps
In Poland, public investment efficiency is a pivotal variable in the medium-term fiscal consolidation strategy. The European Commission’s 2025 Country Report highlights that while private consumption is strong, public finances face strain, with a general government deficit of 6.6% of GDP in 2024.

*   **EU Fund Dependence and Absorption:** A significant portion of Poland’s investment is funded by EU sources, including the [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] (RRF) and [[Cohesion Policy]]. As of mid-2025, Poland had fulfilled only 25% of milestones under its RRF plan, indicating severe bottlenecks in administrative implementation capacity.
*   **Infrastructure and Energy Transition:** Efficient investment is critical for modernizing the electricity grid and supporting the energy transition. Despite emerging as a clean tech hub (producing 60% of Europe’s lithium batteries), Poland’s grid remains reliant on fossil fuels. High electricity prices threaten [[Competitiveness]], necessitating efficient public investment in grid connectivity to lower costs.
*   **Regional Disparities:** Public investment must address significant regional inequalities. Western and capital regions (e.g., Warsaw) attract the majority of [[Foreign Direct Investment]] and exhibit higher productivity, while eastern regions suffer from infrastructure deficits. Efficient targeting of cohesion funds is necessary to reduce these disparities.

### Belgium: Growth-Enhancing Reforms and Debt Sustainability
In Belgium, the IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation underscores the need to balance fiscal consolidation with growth-enhancing public investment. With public debt at 104.1% of GDP in 2024 and projected to rise to 123% by 2030 under unchanged policies, the IMF supports a 7-year fiscal adjustment plan.

*   **Rationalizing Current Spending vs. Investment:** The IMF explicitly recommends that fiscal adjustment should prioritize rationalizing current spending (such as social benefits and public wages) rather than cutting public investment. The goal is to preserve or increase investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education to boost potential growth.
*   **Education and Healthcare Efficiency:** Specific efficiency gains are targeted in human capital sectors. For education, reforms should align curricula with market needs and improve teacher time usage. The IMF estimates that such measures could save 1.4 percentage points of GDP while maintaining educational outcomes.
*   **Climate and Regional Coordination:** Belgium faces challenges in meeting 2030 climate targets due to weak coordination between federal and regional governments. The IMF calls for a coherent national strategy to consolidate efforts, emphasizing that efficient public investment in the green transition is necessary to close the gap with EU Effort Sharing Regulation goals.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Risk and Fiscal Sustainability

The efficiency of public investment directly influences several sovereign credit rating factors across different economic contexts:

1.  **Growth Prospects and Productivity:**
    *   **Poland:** With labor productivity at 67% of the EU average, inefficient investment fails to bridge this gap. Efficient investment in digital infrastructure, skills (via [[Vocational Education and Training]]), and innovation is required to support forecasted GDP growth acceleration.
    *   **Belgium:** Sluggish total factor productivity growth and declining labor-cost competitiveness (exacerbated by automatic wage indexation) pose risks. Efficient public investment in R&D and infrastructure is seen as a lever to offset these headwinds and support the return to potential growth (1.3%) by 2027.

2.  **Fiscal Performance and Debt Burden:**
    *   **Poland:** High budget deficits and rising defense spending (projected to reach 2.8% of GDP in 2025) limit fiscal space. If public investment is inefficient, it may require further cuts to current spending or higher borrowing, worsening the [[Debt Burden]]. The "national escape clause" flexibility depends on the growth-generating capacity of these investments.
    *   **Belgium:** The structural primary balance must be reduced by approximately 0.6% of GDP annually until 2031. Efficient investment ensures that this adjustment does not erode the tax base or growth potential, facilitating the projected debt reduction to ~78% of GDP.

3.  **Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength:**
    *   **Poland:** Delays in EU-funded projects undermine [[Policy Credibility]]. The establishment of the independent [[Fiscal Council]] (operational from 2026) is expected to improve ex post evaluation of fiscal measures, enhancing scrutiny of public spending.
    *   **Belgium:** Complex fiscal federalism challenges accountability. The IMF recommends revitalizing the fiscal council and enforcing binding burden-sharing rules to improve coordination and ensure that public investment decisions are aligned with national fiscal and climate targets.

4.  **Macroeconomic Stability:**
    *   **Poland:** Inefficient investment can lead to inflationary pressures if it strains labor and material markets without boosting supply capacity, a concern given Poland’s history with [[Inflation Concerns]].
    *   **Belgium:** Sticky core inflation, driven by indexed wages and rents, remains a concern. Efficient investment in supply-side resilience (energy, infrastructure) is critical for price stability and reducing vulnerability to external shocks.

## Key Drivers of Inefficiency

*   **Regulatory Volatility (Poland):** Frequent legislative changes with short preparation times and a complex tax framework deter investment and complicate project implementation.
*   **Administrative Capacity (Poland):** Bottlenecks in absorbing EU funds suggest gaps in coordination between institutions and execution capabilities.
*   **Skills Mismatch (Poland):** The education system’s failure to meet labor market needs reduces the return on investment in physical capital.
*   **Structural Wage Rigidities (Belgium):** Automatic wage indexation and the 1996 wage law drive up unit labor costs, reducing the competitiveness of the economy and potentially diluting the returns on investment.
*   **Federal-Regional Fragmentation (Belgium):** Weak coordination among federal and regional governments regarding climate and fiscal targets leads to fragmented and less efficient public spending.

## Strategic Recommendations for Enhanced Efficiency

To improve public investment efficiency and support fiscal sustainability, the following measures are critical:

*   **Accelerate Implementation of EU Funds:** Enhance administrative capacity to absorb [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] and Cohesion Policy funds quickly and effectively (Poland).
*   **Focus on Growth-Enhancing Sectors:** Prioritize investment in digital infrastructure, green energy grids, and education/training systems that directly address [[Skills Shortages]] and improve [[Labor Productivity]].
*   **Strengthen Ex Post Evaluation:** Utilize independent fiscal councils (Poland’s new Fiscal Council; Belgium’s revitalized High Council of Finance) to rigorously evaluate the economic returns of public investment projects, ensuring accountability.
*   **Regulatory and Institutional Stability:** Reduce legislative volatility (Poland) and improve inter-governmental coordination (Belgium) to provide a predictable environment for public and private investment partnerships.
*   **Integrate Climate and Fiscal Goals:** Develop coherent national strategies that align public investment with climate targets, ensuring that green transition spending yields both environmental and economic returns.
