# Artificial Intelligence in Justice

Artificial Intelligence in Justice refers to the integration of automated systems, machine learning, and algorithmic processing into judicial procedures, administrative decision-making, and legal research.

## Current Status and Implementation
As of 2025, the application of AI in the French justice system is characterized by a cautious but active exploration of technological integration. Key developments include:

*   **Drafting and Administrative Tasks:** The French Government and the Court of Cassation are actively studying safeguards for the use of AI in drafting administrative decisions and performing various judicial tasks.
*   **Regulatory Oversight:** Efforts are focused on establishing robust ethical and legal safeguards to ensure that AI deployment does not undermine [[Judicial Independence]] or the right to a fair trial.
*   **Digitalisation Context:** AI initiatives are part of a broader push for the [[Digitalisation of Justice]], which includes tools like *Portalis* and the goal of providing online access to millions of criminal court decisions.

## Key Considerations
*   **Safeguards:** A primary focus of current policy is the creation of frameworks to prevent bias, ensure transparency, and maintain human oversight in automated judicial outputs.
*   **Efficiency vs. Ethics:** While AI offers potential to address "efficiency challenges"—such as the high resolution times for bribery cases—the primary concern remains the preservation of human rights and the integrity of the rule of law.
