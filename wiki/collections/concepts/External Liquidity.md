# External Liquidity

External Liquidity is a critical component of the **External Assessment** pillar within the S&P sovereign credit rating methodology. It measures a country's ability to meet its international obligations and manage its position in global transactions.

### Role in Credit Assessment
In the context of sovereign creditworthiness, external liquidity is evaluated to determine how effectively a nation can navigate its international financial commitments. It serves as a key metric for assessing the country's stability in the face of external shocks and its ability to access foreign exchange or other liquid assets when necessary.

### Relationship to Other Concepts
External Liquidity is analyzed in conjunction with several other factors to form a comprehensive external profile:
- **Exchange Rate Regime:** The framework for how a country manages its currency value relative to others.
- **Resident Assets and Liabilities:** The underlying stock of international holdings and debts that dictate liquidity needs.
- **Fiscal Assessment:** While external liquidity focuses on international transactions, it complements the "flows" and "stocks" analysis of a country's internal fiscal health.

### Methodology Context
Within the S&P framework, external liquidity is one of the multidimensional variables used to determine a sovereign's credit rating. It is specifically grouped under the **External Assessment** category, which aims to quantify a nation's exposure and resilience in the global economy. Analysts may also apply "supplemental adjustment factors" to the rating if a country exhibits extremely weak liquidity, which can override indicative ratings.
