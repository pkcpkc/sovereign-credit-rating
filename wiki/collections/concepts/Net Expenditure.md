# Net Expenditure

Net Expenditure refers to the net cost or spending incurred by a government or entity after accounting for specific offsets, often analyzed within the context of fiscal sustainability and public debt management. 

## Contextual Analysis: Belgium (2025)
In the context of the **2025 Country Report - Belgium**, net expenditure is heavily influenced by demographic shifts and structural economic pressures:

*   **Demographic Pressures:** Belgium faces significant rising costs due to an aging population. Pension spending is projected to increase by 3.5% of GDP between 2022 and 2070, which is significantly higher than the EU average.
*   **Long-term Care:** By 2070, Belgium is projected to have some of the highest long-term care spending in the EU. This necessitates a shift toward more cost-effective, community-based services to manage net expenditure in the healthcare sector.
*   **Fiscal Deficit:** The headline deficit is forecast to rise to 5.4% of GDP in 2025, with public debt projected to reach 107.1%. These figures are driven by a combination of defense spending and aging-related costs.
*   **Structural Drivers:** The sustainability of net expenditure is tied to the [[Fiscal-Structural Plan]], which aims to address high labor costs driven by [[Wage Indexation]] and the need for a more efficient tax-and-benefit system.
