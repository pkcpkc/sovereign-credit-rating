# Public Broadcasting Reform

Public Broadcasting Reform in France refers to the structural and governance overhaul of the national public media sector, specifically targeting the creation of a unified holding company, **France Médias**, to oversee major public broadcasters including **France Télévisions**, **Radio France**, and the **Institut National de l'Audiovisuel (INA)**. This reform, scheduled for legislative debate in mid-2025, aims to pool resources and streamline investments through a common CEO for subsidiaries. However, the initiative has sparked significant controversy, resulting in strikes and intense debate regarding its impact on editorial independence and job security.

## Governance and Structural Changes

The core of the reform involves transitioning from a fragmented governance structure to a centralized holding model. Proponents argue this will enhance operational efficiency and investment capacity. Conversely, critics and media unions highlight the risk of centralizing decision-making power in a way that could compromise the autonomy of individual broadcasters. The proposed change represents a fundamental shift in how public service media is managed, moving away from distinct organizational identities toward a consolidated corporate structure.

## Impact on Editorial Independence and Media Pluralism

A primary concern associated with this reform is the potential erosion of **Editorial Independence**. The consolidation of leadership under a single holding company raises fears that political or economic pressures could more easily influence content across all platforms (television, radio, and digital archives). This tension is situated within the broader context of **Media Ownership Transparency** in France, where efforts to create a national database for beneficial ownership have recently improved transparency. The public broadcasting reform is viewed by many stakeholders as a counter-weight to these transparency gains, potentially introducing new opaque layers of governance.
