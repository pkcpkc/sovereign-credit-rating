# Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds

The **Next Generation EU (NGEU)** funds represent a temporary instrument of the European Union designed to support the recovery and structural transformation of member states following economic shocks, particularly those related to the pandemic and geopolitical instability. In the context of sovereign credit risk and macroeconomic analysis, NGEU funds function as a critical source of grant and loan financing that significantly influences near-term GDP growth, public investment cycles, and fiscal sustainability metrics.

## Macroeconomic Impact and Growth Prospects

For economies like Poland, the absorption of NGEU funds is a primary driver of near-term economic recovery and growth acceleration.

*   **Growth Driver**: The IMF's 2024 Article IV Consultation for Poland highlights that NGEU fund absorption is a key factor supporting the projected GDP growth acceleration to 3.5% in 2025 and 3.3% in 2026. The funds fuel public investment and support domestic demand.
*   **Temporary Boost vs. Medium-Term Reality**: While NGEU funds provide a significant temporary boost to GDP, the medium-term growth outlook (post-2026) is expected to moderate to slightly below 3%. This moderation reflects the unwinding of temporary EU fund boosts, combined with structural headwinds such as population aging and the need for productivity-enhancing reforms.
*   **External Sector Effects**: The influx of EU funds contributes to higher imports associated with investment spending, contributing to the projected movement of Poland's current account balance from a surplus into a deficit (projected at -1.5% of GDP) over the medium term.

## Fiscal Sustainability and Debt Dynamics

The integration of NGEU funds into national budgets presents a complex interplay between fiscal consolidation efforts and debt sustainability, particularly for countries entering the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP).

*   **Fiscal Space and Consolidation**: While NGEU funds support investment, the underlying fiscal position remains under pressure. In Poland's case, the general government deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, driven by wage increases, social benefits, and defense spending, despite the presence of EU funds. The IMF notes that while NGEU funds support growth, they do not eliminate the need for structural fiscal adjustment.
*   **Debt Sustainability Assessment**: The IMF recommends that fiscal consolidation plans should preserve public investment, which includes NGEU-funded projects. The Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) aims to reduce the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028. The sustainability of this path relies on balancing the phase-out of NGEU grants/loans with sustainable domestic revenue generation and expenditure control.
*   **Debt Structure**: NGEU resources, particularly grants, help mitigate the immediate debt burden compared to pure borrowing. However, the overall public debt trajectory (projected to rise from ~50% to 65% of GDP by 2029 in Poland) requires careful management to ensure that the exit from NGEU support does not trigger a sharp fiscal adjustment that jeopardizes growth.

## Structural Reforms and Investment

NGEU funds are conditional upon the implementation of specific structural reforms and investment priorities, linking financial support to long-term economic resilience.

*   **Climate Transition**: A significant portion of NGEU funding is directed toward the green transition. For coal-dependent economies like Poland, this financing is crucial for mitigating climate transition costs and decarbonizing the energy sector, addressing a key long-term growth threat.
*   **Productivity and Innovation**: The funds support investments aimed at boosting productivity, which is essential for countering the long-term drag of population aging on potential growth.
*   **Reform Implementation**: The successful absorption of funds requires strong institutional capacity and transparency. Improvements in the oversight of funds, such as those managed by the Polish Development Bank (BGK), are critical to ensuring that NGEU resources are efficiently deployed and to maintain policy credibility.

## Relevance to Sovereign Credit Rating

In sovereign credit analysis, the role of NGEU funds is assessed through several lenses:

1.  **Growth Support**: Positive for near-term ratings due to the stimulation of GDP growth and private sector confidence.
2.  **Fiscal Credibility**: Neutral to Negative if the reliance on EU funds masks underlying fiscal imbalances (e.g., high domestic deficits unrelated to NGEU-eligible spending). The entry into the EDP despite access to NGEU signals underlying fiscal pressures.
3.  **External Vulnerability**: NGEU funds improve the net international investment position (NIIP) and external liquidity in the short term, but the subsequent rise in imports and current account deficits requires monitoring for external balance sustainability.
4.  **Institutional Strength**: The administrative capacity to absorb and manage large-scale EU funds is a proxy for institutional quality and policy implementation strength.

## Key Relationships

*   **[[Fiscal Consolidation]]**: NGEU funds provide a buffer for public investment, allowing fiscal consolidation to focus on current expenditures, but the phase-out of funds necessitates careful sequencing to avoid pro-cyclical tightening.
*   **[[Debt Sustainability]]**: Grants from NGEU reduce the need for market borrowing for specific projects, improving the debt-to-GDP trajectory compared to scenarios without such support.
*   **[[Structural Reforms]]**: Fund disbursement is often linked to reform milestones, creating a mechanism for enforcing structural changes in labor markets, energy, and governance.
*   **[[Monetary Policy]]**: By supporting growth and inflationary pressures through demand-side injections, NGEU absorption can influence inflation dynamics, indirectly affecting central bank policy decisions (e.g., NBP's rate path).
