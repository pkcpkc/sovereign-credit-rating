# rule of law safeguards

**Rule of law safeguards** refer to the institutional, legal, and procedural mechanisms designed to ensure the independence of the judiciary, the effectiveness of anti-corruption frameworks, the protection of fundamental rights, and the adherence to constitutional and international obligations. In the context of sovereign credit rating analysis and country risk assessment, the robustness of these safeguards is a critical determinant of policy credibility, institutional strength, and long-term economic stability. Weaknesses in these safeguards directly correlate with increased geopolitical and political stability risks, reduced investment protection, and higher sovereign risk premiums.

The 2025 Rule of Law Report on Slovakia, issued by the European Commission, provides a comprehensive case study illustrating how the erosion or stagnation of rule of law safeguards can negatively impact a nation's fiscal sustainability and macroeconomic environment. Despite an intensive dialogue with the EU, Slovakia demonstrated limited to no progress in addressing key concerns, highlighting specific vulnerabilities in judicial governance, anti-corruption enforcement, and media freedom.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Country Risk

The integrity of rule of law safeguards is a primary driver of **policy credibility and institutional strength**, which are key factors in sovereign credit rating assessments. In Slovakia, the following threats have been identified as directly impacting credit outlooks:

*   **Geopolitical and Political Stability**: Political polarization and attacks on non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and media by politicians create an unstable political environment. Additionally, potential constitutional amendments that challenge the primacy of EU law pose significant legal and geopolitical risks, threatening the country's alignment with European standards and access to EU funds.
*   **Accountability and Institutional Strength**: The broad powers held by the Prosecutor General to annul final decisions and interfere in high-level corruption cases undermine judicial finality and independence. The lack of dismissal safeguards for members of the Judicial Council further weakens institutional checks and balances. These factors contribute to low public and corporate perception of judicial independence (32% and 34%, respectively), signaling high institutional risk to investors.
*   **Policymaking Stability**: The frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures (adopted for 28% of laws in 2024) bypasses necessary consultation, leading to unpredictable administrative conduct. This unpredictability affects investment protection and business climate perception, thereby hindering growth prospects.
*   **Economic Strength and Tax Base**: Poor business climate perception due to rule of law risks, combined with lengthy administrative proceedings (e.g., 1,040 days for administrative case resolution in 2023), hinders business permits and overall economic efficiency.

## Key Dimensions of Rule of Law Safeguards

### 1. Judicial Independence and Governance
Safeguards for judicial independence are essential to ensure impartial dispute resolution and contract enforcement. In Slovakia, these safeguards face significant challenges:
*   **Judicial Council Independence**: No progress was made in establishing sufficient safeguards for the dismissal of Judicial Council members, particularly those not elected by judges. This raises concerns about political influence over judicial appointments and discipline.
*   **Disciplinary Proceedings**: The expanded role of the Judicial Council in disciplinary proceedings poses risks to judicial independence. While amendments to judges' laws introduced some efficiency measures and clarified procedural aspects of the "abuse of law" crime, sufficient safeguards against misuse and chilling effects remain lacking.
*   **Perception of Independence**: The low percentage of the public and businesses perceiving the judiciary as independent reflects a broader crisis of confidence, which can deter foreign direct investment and increase the cost of capital.

### 2. Anti-Corruption Framework and Enforcement
Effective anti-corruption safeguards require specialized, independent institutions capable of investigating high-level corruption. Slovakia's framework has been weakened by:
*   **Dissolution of Specialized Entities**: The dissolution of specialized anti-corruption entities, such as the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Specialized Anti-Corruption Prosecution Office (SPO), has led to operational inefficiencies, delays, and a significant drop in the number of investigated corruption cases. The lack of specialized investigators further compromises the state's ability to combat high-level graft.
*   **Prosecutorial Interference**: The Prosecutor General continues to use powers to annul final decisions, including in high-profile corruption cases, undermining the finality of judicial outcomes and the principle of *ne bis in idem*.
*   **Strategic Gaps**: While a new National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2024-2029 is in preparation, no progress was made in regulating lobbying or strengthening asset declaration transparency. Conversely, restrictions on public access to asset disclosures are being considered, which would reduce transparency and accountability.

### 3. Media Freedom and Pluralism
A free and pluralistic media landscape is a crucial safeguard against abuse of power and corruption. In Slovakia:
*   **Public Service Media**: The governance structure of the new Slovak Television and Radio (STVR), which entered into force in July 2024, raises concerns about politicization. Safeguards for the independence of public service media remain inadequate.
*   **Access to Information**: Amendments to the Freedom of Information Act, which entered into force in March 2025, introduced fees and longer response times, facing constitutional challenge. These changes restrict public access to information and hinder journalistic scrutiny.
*   **Journalist Safety**: The environment for journalists has become increasingly hostile, with no legislative progress on defamation reform. This chills investigative journalism and reduces the transparency necessary for effective governance.

### 4. Civil Society and Checks and Balances
Robust civil society and stakeholder involvement are essential for democratic accountability. In Slovakia:
*   **Legislative Process**: The frequent use of fast-track procedures limits stakeholder involvement in law-making, reducing the quality and legitimacy of legislation.
*   **NGO Environment**: Civil society faces increased pressure through new reporting obligations and reduced funding for some human rights advocates. The adoption of laws amending NGO functioning acts in April 2024 has further constrained the operational space for independent watchdogs.
*   **Constitutional Integrity**: Proposed constitutional amendments, which raised concerns about the primacy of EU law, were postponed after their first reading in April 2025. However, the mere proposal of such amendments signals a potential erosion of constitutional safeguards and alignment with international legal frameworks.

### 5. Implementation of International Obligations
Adherence to international judicial decisions is a key indicator of a country's commitment to the rule of law. Slovakia has shown weak enforcement of international judicial decisions:
*   **ECtHR Judgments**: As of January 2025, there were 31 leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) pending implementation, rising to 33 by June 2025. This backlog indicates systemic issues in enforcing human rights obligations and undermines the country's international legal standing.

## Conclusion

The state of **rule of law safeguards** in Slovakia represents a significant risk factor for sovereign credit ratings and macroeconomic stability. The lack of progress in strengthening judicial independence, the weakening of anti-corruption institutions, and the erosion of media and civil society space contribute to a poor business climate, low investment protection, and reduced policy credibility. Addressing these structural weaknesses is essential for restoring institutional trust, ensuring fiscal sustainability, and mitigating country risk. The European Commission's ongoing dialogue and potential enforcement mechanisms serve as external safeguards, but domestic political will remains the critical determinant for future improvements.
