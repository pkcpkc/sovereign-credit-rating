# Product Market Regulation

Product Market Regulation (PMR) refers to the framework of rules, standards, and administrative procedures that govern how businesses operate, compete, and interact within a specific market. It encompasses the legal environment that determines the ease of doing business, including licensing requirements, compliance costs, and the removal of barriers to entry.

## Current Status and Challenges
Based on recent assessments (e.g., the 2025 Country Report for Malta), certain jurisdictions face significant hurdles regarding Product Market Regulation:

- **Regulatory Burden**: High administrative burdens can hinder economic competitiveness. In some regions, these regulations are identified as significant bottlenecks, ranking those areas among the weakest performers in the EU.
- **Business Uncertainty**: Inefficient regulatory frameworks, often compounded by judicial delays in civil and commercial cases, create uncertainty for private investment.
- **Impact on Innovation**: Excessive regulatory friction can contribute to a lower [[R&D Intensity]] by diverting resources from research and development toward compliance and administrative overhead.

## Key Dimensions
- **Administrative Burdens**: The non-productive costs incurred by businesses to comply with government regulations.
- **Market Access**: The ease with which new entrants can participate in a market without facing unfair competition or excessive bureaucratic hurdles.
- **Judicial Efficiency**: The speed and reliability of the legal system in resolving commercial disputes, which serves as a cornerstone of a stable product market.
