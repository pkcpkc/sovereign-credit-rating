# Primary Surplus

A **Primary Surplus** is a fiscal metric representing the amount of government revenue in excess of government spending, excluding the payment of interest on public debt. It serves as a key indicator of a country's fiscal health and its ability to service its debt without relying on new borrowing.

## Key Characteristics
*   **Debt Servicing Exclusion:** Unlike a "budget surplus," which includes interest payments, the primary surplus focuses strictly on the core government operations.
*   **Debt Sustainability:** A consistent primary surplus is essential for stabilizing or reducing a high debt-to-GDP ratio. It indicates that the government is generating enough internal revenue to cover its expenditures, leaving a portion of the funds available to pay down the principal or interest on existing debt.
*   **Fiscal Discipline:** Achieving a primary surplus is often used by international organizations (such as the [[IMF]]) as a benchmark for fiscal discipline and commitment to long-term economic stability.

## Contextual Application: Italy (2024-2025)
In the context of recent economic assessments:
*   **Recent Performance:** Italy achieved a primary surplus of **0.4% of GDP** in 2024. This was driven by improved tax compliance and the phasing out of specific housing-related tax credits.
*   **Strategic Targets:** To address high public debt (135.3% of GDP), the IMF has recommended a more aggressive primary surplus target of **3% by 2027**.
*   **Policy Drivers:** Achieving these targets involves rationalizing tax expenditures (which currently account for 6% of GDP) and transitioning from hiring subsidies to measures that enhance productivity.
