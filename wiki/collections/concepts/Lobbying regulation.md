# Lobbying regulation

**Lobbying regulation** refers to the legal and institutional frameworks designed to govern interactions between government officials, public agencies, and private interest groups (lobbies). Its primary objectives are to ensure transparency in political decision-making, prevent undue influence and corruption, and safeguard the integrity of democratic processes. Effective lobbying regulation typically involves the establishment of mandatory registers for lobbyists and interest representatives, disclosure requirements for meetings with officials, and clear codes of conduct for public servants regarding conflicts of interest.

### Regulatory Gaps and Stalled Reform
Despite lobbying regulation being identified as a key concept in Italy’s Rule of Law framework, significant legislative inertia persists:
*   **Absence of Operational Register:** As of mid-2025, there has been **no progress** in establishing an operational national lobbying register. This lack of transparency obscures the flow of influence between private entities and public decision-makers.
*   **Stalled Legislation:** Draft laws specifically aimed at regulating lobbying activities have **not advanced** in Parliament. This stagnation limits overall transparency in the political sphere and hinders the ability of external monitors and the public to track interest group activities.
*   **Link to Political Financing:** The lack of progress in lobbying regulation is closely tied to broader issues in [[Political financing transparency]]. The report highlights that the practice of channelling donations through political foundations remains unaddressed, exacerbating concerns about opaque funding sources influencing policy outcomes.
