# Public Debt

Public Debt refers to the total amount of money owed by a government to creditors, including both domestic and foreign lenders. It is a critical indicator of a nation's fiscal health and its ability to fund public services, infrastructure, and economic stimulus.

## Current Context: Hungary (2025 Report)
The 2025 Country Report highlights significant challenges regarding Hungary's public debt profile:

*   **Debt-to-GDP Ratio:** In 2024, Hungary's public debt reached **73.5% of GDP**.
*   **Debt-Servicing Costs:** Hungary faces some of the highest debt-servicing costs in the European Union.
*   **Interest Rates:** The implicit interest rate on government debt is notably high, nearing **6% in 2025**.
*   **Fiscal Framework Issues:** The sustainability of this debt is complicated by a lack of transparency in the **[[Budgetary Framework]]**, characterized by frequent revisions and an absence of multiannual spending ceilings.
*   **Expenditure Bias:** There is a noted expansionary budgetary bias; net expenditure growth in 2025 was forecast at 6.1%, which exceeded initial Council recommendations.

## Related Concepts
*   **[[GDP Growth]]**: Used as the primary denominator to measure debt sustainability.
*   **[[Budgetary Framework]]**: The rules and limits governing government spending and debt management.
*   **Fiscal Policy**: The broader economic policy used to manage government revenue and spending, directly impacting debt levels.
