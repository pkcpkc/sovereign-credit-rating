# Decarbonisation

Decarbonisation refers to the process of reducing or eliminating carbon dioxide emissions from economic activities, industrial processes, and energy production to mitigate climate change. It is a core pillar of modern sustainability policy and fiscal planning, involving the transition away from carbon-intensive systems toward low-carbon alternatives.

## Contextual Applications
Based on recent economic reports and regional analyses, decarbonisation involves several critical dimensions:

### Energy Transition & Security
- **Renewable Leadership:** A primary driver of decarbonisation is the shift toward renewable electricity. For example, Austria achieved a record 87% renewable electricity generation in 2024.
- **Infrastructure & Flexibility:** Achieving high levels of decarbonisation requires massive investment in grid expansion (e.g., estimated at EUR 53 billion by 2040) to accommodate decentralized sources. Furthermore, high adoption of specific technologies, such as solar production (e.g., Hungary's 25% EU share), must be balanced with sufficient grid flexibility and storage infrastructure.
- **Energy Security:** Decarbonisation is intrinsically linked to [[Energy Security]], as nations seek to reduce heavy dependence on imported Russian fossil fuels (which can exceed 70-80% of gas and oil supplies in certain regions).
- **Clean Tech & Adoption:** While some nations lead in manufacturing [[Clean Tech]] (e.g., solar PV and wind), decarbonisation progress is often hindered by uneven adoption rates of end-user technologies like electric vehicles (EVs), where regional lags can persist despite manufacturing leadership.

### Industrial & Economic Frameworks
- **High-Emission Sectors:** Manufacturing remains a significant source of emissions (accounting for approximately 35% of emissions in certain contexts). Effective decarbonisation requires the rapid implementation of specific measures to transition energy-intensive industrial sectors away from carbon-heavy inputs.
- **Fossil Fuel Subsidies:** A significant barrier to decarbonisation is the persistence of fossil fuel subsidies, which can reach high percentages of GDP (e.g., 1.01% in some contexts) and require phased-out policies to align with climate goals.
- **Circular Economy:** Decarbonisation is supported by [[Circular Economy]] practices, aiming to improve circular material use rates to mitigate environmental degradation and resource scarcity.

### Climate Risks & Economic Impact
- **Climate Vulnerability:** The necessity of decarbonisation is underscored by increasing climate risks. Regions highly exposed to hydrogeological risks and extreme weather face significant annual infrastructure costs (e.g., approx. €2 billion by 2030 in certain contexts), necessitating a faster transition to low-carbon models.
- **Energy Pricing:** High gas dependence and carbon-intensive energy structures contribute to significantly higher wholesale electricity prices in certain regions compared to peer countries.

### Strategic Targets
- **Climate Neutrality:** National and regional policies often set specific deadlines for climate neutrality (e.g., 2040 targets).
- **Renewable Targets:** Specific milestones, such as 100% renewable electricity targets, serve as benchmarks for successful decarbonisation efforts.
- **Green Skills:** The transition requires addressing the "skills shortage" in STEM and green technologies to facilitate the workforce shift necessary for a low-carbon economy.
