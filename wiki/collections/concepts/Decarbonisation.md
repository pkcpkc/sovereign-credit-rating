# Decarbonisation

Decarbonisation refers to the process of reducing or eliminating carbon dioxide emissions from economic activities, industrial processes, and energy production to mitigate climate change. It is a core pillar of modern sustainability policy and fiscal planning.

## Contextual Applications
Based on recent economic reports, decarbonisation involves several critical dimensions:

### Energy Transition
- **Renewable Leadership:** A primary driver of decarbonisation is the shift toward renewable electricity. For example, Austria achieved a record 87% renewable electricity generation in 2024.
- **Infrastructure Requirements:** Achieving high levels of decarbonisation requires massive investment in grid expansion (e.g., estimated at EUR 53 billion by 2040) to accommodate decentralized and renewable sources.

### Industrial Decarbonisation
- **High-Emission Sectors:** Manufacturing remains a significant source of emissions (accounting for approximately 35% of emissions in certain contexts).
- **Targeted Measures:** Effective decarbonisation requires the rapid implementation of specific measures to transition energy-intensive industrial sectors away from carbon-heavy inputs.

### Strategic Targets
- **Climate Neutrality:** National and regional policies often set specific deadlines for climate neutrality (e.g., 2040 targets).
- **Renewable Targets:** Specific milestones, such as 100% renewable electricity targets, serve as benchmarks for successful decarbonisation efforts.
