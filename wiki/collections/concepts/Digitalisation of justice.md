# Digitalisation of justice

Digitalisation of justice refers to the integration of digital technologies into the administration of justice to enhance efficiency, accessibility, and transparency. In the context of sovereign credit rating analysis, effective digitalisation is a critical component of institutional quality and policy credibility. Failures in this domain signal potential weaknesses in administrative capacity, increased risk of fraud or corruption, and structural inefficiencies that can prolong legal disputes and deter foreign investment.

## Institutional Quality and Efficiency Impact

The digitalisation strategy of a nation's justice system directly impacts **judicial efficiency**, a key metric for assessing the rule of law and economic stability.

*   **Strategic Shortcomings**: In Belgium, the 2025 Rule of Law Report identified "serious shortcomings" in the justice department's digitalisation strategy. Key issues include the lack of an overarching approach and significant **fraud risks**. These deficiencies were formally highlighted by the **Belgian Court of Audit** in December 2024.
*   **Efficiency and Backlogs**: Poor digital infrastructure exacerbates existing structural problems, such as excessive length of proceedings and case backlogs (notably in the Court of Appeal of Brussels). While digitalisation aims to streamline processes, inadequate implementation can hinder these goals, leading to delayed dispute resolution. This uncertainty negatively affects **economic strength** and **investment protection**, as evidenced by the correlation between judicial efficiency and business confidence.
*   **Government Response**: In response to audit findings, the Belgian Government submitted an initial action plan in September 2024. However, the persistence of these issues represents a threat to **policy credibility**, suggesting gaps in the executive's ability to manage complex administrative reforms effectively.

## Sovereign Credit Rating Implications

Deficiencies in the digitalisation of justice present specific risks to sovereign credit ratings through several channels:

1.  **Fraud and Integrity Risks**: The identified "fraud risks" within the digital justice framework pose a direct threat to **fiscal sustainability** and **institutional integrity**. Weak digital controls can facilitate embezzlement or mismanagement of public funds allocated to the justice sector, undermining trust in public institutions.
2.  **Administrative Capacity**: The lack of a cohesive digital strategy reflects broader weaknesses in **policy credibility and institutional strength**. For rating agencies, this signals potential rigidity in adapting to modern administrative standards, which can increase operational costs and reduce the overall effectiveness of the state.
3.  **Investment Climate**: Judicial efficiency is a prerequisite for a stable investment environment. Delays and inefficiencies driven by poor digitalisation can lead to a deterioration in the **economic strength and tax base**, as businesses may perceive higher legal risks and costs when operating in the jurisdiction.
