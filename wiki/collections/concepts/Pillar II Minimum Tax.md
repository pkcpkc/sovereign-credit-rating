# Pillar II Minimum Tax

**Pillar II** refers to a global framework established by the OECD/G20 to ensure that large multinational enterprises (MNEs) pay a minimum level of tax regardless of where they operate. It aims to curb "base erosion and profit shifting" (BEPS) by establishing a global minimum corporate tax rate.

## Context and Implementation
In the context of European Union policy, Pillar II is implemented through the **EU Minimum Tax Directive**. This directive requires member states to align their domestic tax regimes with the global standard to prevent revenue leakage to low-tax jurisdictions.

## Key Implications for Malta
According to the **IMF (2025) Malta Country Report**, the implementation of Pillar II is a critical component of the country's fiscal and structural reform agenda:

*   **Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Reform:** Malta is urged to develop a clear roadmap for **[[Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Reform]]** specifically to align with the EU’s Pillar II requirements.
*   **Revenue Protection:** The primary objective of this alignment is to prevent the leakage of corporate tax revenue to other jurisdictions, ensuring that the fiscal benefits of Malta's service-oriented economy are retained.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation:** This reform is part of a broader effort toward **[[Fiscal Consolidation]]**, which includes shifting fiscal space away from energy subsidies and toward public services and innovation.
