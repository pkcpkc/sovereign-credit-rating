# Investment climate

The **investment climate** refers to the set of economic, institutional, and regulatory conditions that determine the attractiveness of a country for both domestic and foreign investment. It encompasses the stability of legal frameworks, the efficiency of public administration, the depth of financial markets, and the macroeconomic environment. A robust investment climate is critical for sovereign credit ratings, as it drives productivity, tax revenue base expansion, and sustainable economic growth, thereby enhancing debt sustainability and fiscal resilience.

## Key Determinants and Structural Factors

Based on the 2025 Country Report for Romania, the investment climate is significantly influenced by the following structural and institutional factors:

### 1. Regulatory Stability and Legal Certainty
Regulatory uncertainty is a primary deterrent to long-term investment.
- **Legislative Instability**: In Romania, frequent changes in tax rules and legislative amendments (e.g., hundreds of changes to the Fiscal Code since 2015) create an unpredictable environment. This is cited by 90% of firms as the top barrier to long-term investment.
- **Policy Credibility**: The use of government emergency ordinances undermines legal certainty. Short public consultation periods and a lack of dialogue between industry and government further erode trust.
- **Impact on Credit Risk**: Volatility in the tax burden and frequent policy shifts increase the perceived risk premium for investors, potentially raising the cost of capital and hindering the tax base expansion necessary for fiscal consolidation.

### 2. Institutional Strength and Corruption
The efficiency and integrity of public institutions directly impact the cost of doing business.
- **Perceived Corruption**: High levels of perceived corruption, particularly in public procurement, remain a significant risk. Low trust in public institutions and the lack of a transparent lobby register for MPs contribute to a challenging environment for businesses.
- **Administrative Capacity**: Weak administrative capacity in local and regional authorities, combined with suboptimal public services, delays the implementation of reforms and EU-funded projects (e.g., only 14% of Recovery and Resilience Plan milestones fulfilled).
- **Compliance Burden**: A high share of firms dedicate staff resources to regulatory compliance, indicating an over-regulated environment that reduces productive efficiency.

### 3. Financial Market Depth and Access to Finance
A mature investment climate requires diversified and deep financial markets.
- **Underdeveloped Capital Markets**: Romania relies heavily on bank loans and internal financing due to low listed equity and bond volumes. This limits the ability of firms to raise long-term capital for innovation and infrastructure.
- **Banking Sector Concentration**: While the banking sector is well-capitalized (CET1 ratio of 20%), its heavy exposure to government bonds creates concentration risk. The small size of the banking sector relative to GDP (52.8%) constrains credit availability for the private sector.
- **Investment Funding**: The reliance on foreign capital inflows to finance domestic investment, coupled with a negative net international investment position (-41.5% of GDP), highlights external vulnerabilities in the investment landscape.

### 4. Infrastructure and Innovation Capacity
Physical and digital infrastructure, along with innovation ecosystems, are foundational to modern investment.
- **Infrastructure Deficits**: Outdated transport infrastructure (short motorway network, aging railways) and energy grid limitations act as bottlenecks. Despite Schengen accession facilitating trade, internal logistics remain inefficient.
- **Low R&D Intensity**: With R&D intensity at 0.52% of GDP (lowest in the EU), the innovation system is fragmented. Weak public-private collaboration and an underdeveloped venture capital market hinder the transition to a knowledge-based economy.
- **Skills Shortages**: Mismatches between the education system and labor market needs, particularly in STEM and digital skills, reduce the productivity of investments.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Fiscal Sustainability

The state of the investment climate has direct implications for sovereign creditworthiness and fiscal health:

- **Fiscal Multiplier and Tax Base**: A poor investment climate stifles private sector growth, limiting the expansion of the tax base. Romania’s low tax revenue as a percentage of GDP (26.2% vs. EU average of 39%) and high VAT gap (33.7%) reflect inefficiencies that constrain fiscal space.
- **Debt Sustainability**: Weak domestic investment forces reliance on foreign borrowing, exacerbating external imbalances (current account deficit of 8.4% of GDP in 2024). This increases vulnerability to external shocks and shifts in investor sentiment, which can trigger currency volatility and higher borrowing costs.
- **Reform Implementation**: Delays in structural reforms, driven by administrative bottlenecks and political instability, threaten the credibility of fiscal consolidation plans (e.g., the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan). Successful implementation is essential to restore investor confidence and secure favorable credit ratings.
- **FDI Flows**: Declining Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) flows signal reduced confidence in the long-term stability of the economy. Conversely, positive developments such as Schengen accession (January 2025) are expected to facilitate trade and attract new foreign investment, provided regulatory stability improves.

## Strategic Recommendations for Improvement

To enhance the investment climate and support fiscal sustainability, the following measures are critical:
1. **Enhance Regulatory Stability**: Reduce the frequency of legislative changes, ensure longer consultation periods, and guarantee legal certainty to boost investor confidence.
2. **Strengthen Institutions**: Combat corruption, particularly in public procurement, and improve administrative capacity to accelerate the implementation of reforms and EU funds.
3. **Deepen Financial Markets**: Promote capital market development to reduce reliance on bank lending and provide diverse funding sources for private investment.
4. **Invest in Infrastructure and Skills**: Prioritize transport and digital infrastructure upgrades and align education systems with labor market needs to improve productivity and innovation capacity.
5. **Improve Dialogue**: Establish effective channels for dialogue between government and industry to align policies with business needs and reduce compliance burdens.
