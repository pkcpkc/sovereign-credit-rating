# Monetary Policy Stance

**Monetary Policy Stance** refers to the prevailing posture of a central bank's policy framework, typically characterized by the level of interest rates and the regulatory environment used to manage macroeconomic variables such as inflation, employment, and financial stability.

### Key Characteristics and Current Context
Based on recent economic assessments (notably the IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation for Hungary), the monetary policy stance is defined by several critical components:

*   **Restrictive Stance:** A "tight" or restrictive monetary policy is employed when a central bank maintains high interest rates to anchor inflation expectations and cool an overheating economy. In the context of Hungary, the **Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB)** is advised to maintain restrictive rates into 2026 to bring inflation down toward the 3% target.
*   **Disinflationary Mechanisms:** Effective monetary policy relies on market-based mechanisms. The IMF notes that non-market interventions, such as **price and margin caps**, are viewed as unsustainable for long-term disinflation and do not replace the necessity of a robust monetary stance.
*   **Inflation Targeting:** The primary objective of a restrictive stance is to stabilize price levels. Current projections suggest that while inflation is decelerating, the policy must remain firm until targets are consistently met.
*   **Interaction with Fiscal Policy:** The monetary policy stance does not operate in isolation. It is closely linked to **Fiscal Adjustment**; high public debt and fiscal expansion can necessitate a more aggressive monetary stance to maintain currency stability and manage **Debt Sustainability**.
