# STEM Education

STEM Education refers to the integrated approach to teaching and learning in the fields of Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics. It is a critical driver for economic productivity, technological advancement, and the transition to a green and digital economy.

## Current Status and Challenges
Based on recent economic assessments (e.g., the 2025 Country Report for Belgium), STEM education faces several critical hurdles:

*   **Skills Mismatch:** There is a significant gap between the skills provided by the current educational system and the requirements of the labor market. This is evidenced by high job vacancy rates, particularly in the STEM and healthcare sectors.
*   **Educational Decline:** There is a documented long-term decline in basic skills (as measured by PISA/TIMSS) and a notably low share of STEM graduates.
*   **Impact on Productivity:** The shortage of qualified STEM professionals is identified as a primary factor hampering productivity growth and hindering the ability of nations to meet modern industrial demands.

## Economic and Strategic Importance
*   **Innovation Leadership:** While high R&D intensity (e.g., 3.3% of GDP in Belgium) indicates a strong innovative foundation, the lack of a sufficient pipeline of STEM-educated workers can limit the dynamism of the business environment.
*   **Labor Market Dynamics:** Addressing the STEM education gap is essential for reducing job vacancy rates and improving employment activity among low-skilled individuals and older workers.
*   **Digital & Green Transition:** STEM education is the backbone of achieving targets related to the [[Circular Economy]] and the [[Energy Transition]], as these fields require advanced technical literacy and engineering capabilities.
