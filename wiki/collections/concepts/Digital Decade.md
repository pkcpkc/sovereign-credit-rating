# Digital Decade

The **Digital Decade** refers to the European Union's strategic framework and initiative aimed at accelerating digital transformation across all sectors of the economy and society. It serves as a cornerstone for modernizing infrastructure, fostering innovation, and ensuring that the EU remains competitive in a globalized digital landscape.

## Context and Implementation
The Digital Decade is closely linked to the **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)**, which provides the funding mechanism for large-scale transitions. In specific national contexts, such as Malta, Romania, and Slovakia, the initiative focuses on:

- **Digital Transformation**: Utilizing RRP grants to bolster digital infrastructure and services. In Romania, this is a critical priority to address "Digital Lag," where current adoption rates—particularly in AI (just over 3% in businesses)—remain significantly below EU averages. In Slovakia, efforts are needed to increase high-capacity network penetration, which currently stands at 69.12% of households.
- **Innovation and R&D**: Addressing "Innovation Deficits" by increasing **R&D Intensity**. For example, Romania faces a severe deficit with an R&D intensity of 0.52% of GDP, while Slovakia’s R&D intensity is at 1%, both significantly below the EU average and the 2% target for 2027. The initiative seeks to develop venture capital ecosystems and move economies from low-cost labor models to innovation-driven ones.
- **Skills Development and Human Capital**: Bridging education gaps to ensure the workforce can navigate a digital-first economy. This specifically targets high-demand sectors like ICT, addressing massive shortages of skilled workers and low tertiary education attainment. It also addresses the need to mitigate "brain drain" and improve underachievement in core subjects like mathematics and science.

## Key Objectives
- **Economic Growth**: Leveraging digital tools to improve productivity, address labor shortages in critical service sectors, and mitigate **Macroeconomic Imbalance**. This includes transitioning from low-cost manufacturing toward a high-value economy.
- **Sustainability and Green Transition**: Integrating digital technologies to optimize energy mix, waste management, and water exploitation monitoring. This includes supporting the **Circular Economy**, promoting **Decarbonisation** (particularly in manufacturing), and improving **Energy Affordability** by reducing fiscal burdens on electricity and addressing high costs for energy-intensive industries.
- **Regulatory Reform**: Streamlining administrative burdens, improving **Public Procurement** processes, and enhancing the business environment by simplifying uncoordinated legislative changes to facilitate digital entrepreneurship.
- **Social Integration and Fairness**: Ensuring digital literacy is accessible to all, aiming to improve the 27.7% of the population currently possessing basic digital skills. This includes addressing the **Tax Wedge** to improve social fairness and providing targeted active labor market policies for marginalized communities.
