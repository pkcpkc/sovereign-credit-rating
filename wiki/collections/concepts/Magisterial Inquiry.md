# Magisterial Inquiry

## Definition and Context
A **Magisterial Inquiry** is a specific legal mechanism within the Maltese judicial system that allows for the investigation of matters of public interest or serious misconduct. It serves as a critical tool for ensuring accountability, particularly regarding high-level offences and corruption. In the context of the 2025 **Rule of Law** assessment for **Malta**, this mechanism has become a focal point for debate regarding judicial independence, political accountability, and institutional checks and balances.

## Key Developments: Bill No. 125 (2025)
In April 2025, the **Maltese** Parliament approved Bill No. 125, which introduces significant reforms to the framework governing magisterial inquiries. The legislation has drawn strong criticism from various stakeholders, including legal experts and civil society organizations, due to its potential impact on transparency and accountability.

### Legislative Changes and Criticisms
*   **Increased Thresholds:** The reform increases the threshold required to initiate a citizen-prompted magisterial inquiry. Critics argue this change effectively limits the scope for holding public officials accountable, particularly in cases involving high-level political figures.
*   **Accountability Concerns:** The European Commission’s 2025 **Rule of Law** Report highlights these concerns, noting that the reform may weaken the mechanism's ability to address corruption and abuse of power. The report identifies "limited scope for citizen-prompted magisterial inquiries" as a specific weakness in the integrity measures for high-level politicians.
*   **Stakeholder Reaction:** The changes have been viewed by many as a step backward for judicial transparency, potentially shielding powerful actors from scrutiny and undermining public trust in the justice system.
