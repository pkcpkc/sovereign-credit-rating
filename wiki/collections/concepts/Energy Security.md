# Energy Security

Energy security refers to the uninterrupted availability of energy sources at an affordable price. It encompasses the reliability of supply, the diversity of energy sources, and the infrastructure required to transport and distribute energy.

## Current Context: Hungary
The 2025 Country Report identifies significant vulnerabilities and structural challenges regarding energy security in Hungary:

### $\text{⚓}$ Dependency & Risks
*   **Fossil Fuel Reliance:** Hungary maintains a high level of dependence on Russian fossil fuels, accounting for over 70% of natural gas and 80% of crude oil consumption as of 2024.
*   **Subsidies:** The country maintains one of the highest figures in the EU for fossil fuel subsidies (1.01% of GDP), with no current plans to phase these out before 2030.

### $\text{⚓}$ Transition & Infrastructure
*   **Solar Leadership:** Hungary leads the EU in solar production share (25%), representing a core component of its [[Decarbonisation]] strategy.
*   **Grid Constraints:** Despite high solar production, energy security is hindered by a lack of sufficient grid flexibility and storage infrastructure to manage intermittent supply.
*   **Environmental Interdependencies:** Energy security is increasingly linked to water resilience, as Hungary faces severe water scarcity and pollution issues that impact industrial and energy processes.
