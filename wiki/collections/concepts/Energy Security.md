# Energy Security

Energy security refers to the uninterrupted availability of energy sources at an affordable price. It encompasses the reliability of supply, the diversity of energy sources, and the infrastructure required to transport and distribute energy. Modern energy security is increasingly intertwined with [[Decarbonisation]] goals, fiscal policy, and regional geopolitical stability.

## Regional Context & Case Studies

### Hungary
The 2025 Country Report identifies significant vulnerabilities and structural challenges regarding energy security in Hungary:

*   **$\text{⚓}$ Dependency & Risks:** Hungary maintains a high level of dependence on Russian fossil fuels, accounting for over 70% of natural gas and 80% of crude oil consumption as of 2024.
*   **Subsidies:** The country maintains one of the highest figures in the EU for fossil fuel subsidies (1.01% of GDP), with no current plans to phase these out before 2030.
*   **$\text{⚓}$ Transition & Infrastructure:** Hungary leads the EU in solar production share (25%), representing a core component of its [[Decarbonisation]] strategy. However, energy security is hindered by a lack of sufficient grid flexibility and storage infrastructure to manage intermittent supply.
*   **Environmental Interdependencies:** Energy security is increasingly linked to water resilience, as Hungary faces severe water scarcity and pollution issues that impact industrial and energy processes.

### Slovakia
Recent IMF assessments (2025) highlight a different trajectory for energy security in the Slovak Republic:

*   **$\text{⚓}$ Diversification:** Slovakia has successfully reduced its reliance on Russian natural gas, marking a significant shift in regional energy security.
*   **$\text{⚓}$ Climate Targets & Subsidies:** To meet 2030 climate targets, recommendations include the faster implementation of the ETS II scheme and a concerted effort to phase out fossil fuel subsidies.
*   **Fiscal Interplay:** Energy security measures are linked to [[Fiscal Consolidation]]. The IMF suggests that reducing subsidies is a key lever for both meeting long-term fiscal targets and advancing climate objectives.
