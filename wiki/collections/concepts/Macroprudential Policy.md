# Macroprudential Policy

Macroprudential policy refers to the regulatory framework and measures implemented by authorities to ensure the stability of the financial system as a whole. Unlike microprudential policy, which focuses on the safety and soundness of individual institutions, macroprudential policy aims to mitigate systemic risks—threats that could trigger a broad collapse or significant instability across the entire economy.

## Key Objectives and Mechanisms
The primary goal of macroprudential policy is to prevent "systemic risk," which can arise from interconnectedness, excessive leverage, and asset bubbles. Key mechanisms include:

*   **Capital Buffers:** Requiring banks to hold specific amounts of capital against risk-weighted assets to absorb losses during economic downturns.
*   **Lending Limits:** Imposing prudential limits on specific types of credit, such as mortgage loans, to prevent overheating in the housing market.
*   **Liquidity Requirements:** Ensuring that financial institutions maintain sufficient liquid assets to meet short-term obligations during periods of market stress.
*   **Sectoral Systemic Risk Buffers (sSyRB):** Specific tools used to address concentrated risks in particular sectors, such as real estate, by requiring higher capital requirements for exposures in those areas.
*   **Debt Service-to-Income (DSTI) Ratios:** Monitoring and potentially recalibrating limits on consumer credit to prevent rapid acceleration of household debt.

## Current Context and Application
In the context of European economic monitoring, macroprudential policy is vital for managing the stability of national and regional economies.

*   **Risk Assessment:** Systemic risks are currently assessed as "moderate" in certain jurisdictions, requiring a balance between fostering credit flow and maintaining stability.
*   **Real Estate Exposure Management:** A critical application of macroprudential policy involves managing high concentration risks. For example, in Malta, banks face significant exposure to the real estate sector (comprising 70% of loan portfolios). Recommendations include raising the **sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB)** and broadening its scope to encompass commercial real estate.
*   **Banking Resilience and Surveillance (Romania):** As highlighted in the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Romania**, macroprudential policy is essential for monitoring specific vulnerabilities such as the "sovereign-bank nexus" (where a high percentage of bank assets are held in sovereign bonds) and the volume of unhedged FX loans. In this context, regulators must remain ready to recalibrate policies like the DSTI ratio if consumer credit growth becomes excessive.
*   **Slovak Republic Application:** In Slovakia, while the banking system is characterized by high capital ratios and ample liquidity, macroprudential policy remains a key tool for refining the management of vulnerabilities in the residential and commercial real estate markets. Authorities are advised to continue refining these tools to ensure sector-specific stability.
*   **Regulatory Compliance:** Implementation often follows international standards, such as the **FSAP (Financial Sector Assessment Program)** recommendations.
*   **Interplay with Fiscal Policy:** While macroprudential policy manages financial stability, it works in tandem with [[Fiscal Consolidation]] and [[Sovereign Risk]] management to ensure that high public debt levels do not destabilize the broader financial infrastructure.
