# Macroprudential Policy

Macroprudential policy refers to the regulatory framework and set of measures designed to ensure the stability of the financial system as a whole. Unlike microprudential policy, which focuses on the safety and soundness of individual financial institutions, macroprudential policy aims to mitigate [[Systemic Risk]]—the risk that the failure of one institution or a localized shock could trigger a widespread collapse of the financial infrastructure.

## Key Objectives and Applications
The primary goal is to maintain [[Financial Stability]] by monitoring and regulating systemic vulnerabilities. This includes:
- **Countercyclical Measures:** Implementing regulations that act as buffers during periods of excessive credit growth.
- **Systemic Risk Mitigation:** Identifying and addressing "too big to fail" institutions and interconnected risks.
- **Sectoral Oversight:** Monitoring specific high-risk sectors that could impact the broader economy.

## Contextual Application: Austria (2025)
In the context of the Austrian economy, macroprudential vigilance is currently focused on:
- **Commercial Real Estate (CRE):** The IMF highlights the need for continued vigilance over the CRE sector, specifically regarding rising Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) which pose a risk to the banking sector's health.
- **Banking Resilience:** While Austrian banks maintain strong capital ratios (CET1 at 17.7%), macroprudential oversight ensures these buffers are sufficient to withstand shocks from the real estate market and broader economic contractions.
- **Policy Synergy:** Macroprudential measures work in tandem with [[Fiscal Consolidation]] and monetary policy to stabilize the economy during periods of high inflation and low growth.
