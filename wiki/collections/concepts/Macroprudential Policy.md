# macroprudential policy

**Macroprudential policy** refers to the regulatory and supervisory measures designed to safeguard the stability of the entire financial system, rather than just individual institutions. In the context of sovereign credit analysis and macroeconomic stability, effective macroprudential frameworks mitigate systemic risks, prevent financial cycles from destabilizing the real economy, and support fiscal sustainability by reducing the likelihood of costly banking crises.

Strong macroprudential oversight is increasingly cited by international institutions like the **IMF** as a key institutional strength that enhances **sovereign credit ratings**. By ensuring financial sector resilience, these policies reduce contingent liabilities (such as bank bailouts) and preserve fiscal space for governments facing high debt burdens.

## Role in Financial Stability and Sovereign Risk

According to recent **IMF Article IV Consultations** (2025) for both **Belgium** and **France**, macroprudential policy is a critical component of maintaining financial sector resilience, which directly influences the assessment of a country's sovereign credit risk and debt sustainability. The effectiveness of these policies is often linked to the autonomy and coordination capabilities of national central banks and dedicated financial stability councils.

### Implementation and Institutional Frameworks

#### Belgium: National Bank of Belgium (NBB)
In Belgium, the **National Bank of Belgium (NBB)** has assumed increasing autonomy in macroprudential oversight. This institutional strength is cited as a key factor in maintaining moderate overall systemic risks despite specific sectoral vulnerabilities. The effectiveness of this oversight is reinforced by the broader monetary stability provided by **Eurozone** membership and the **European Central Bank (ECB)**, which anchors inflation expectations and ensures access to deep local-currency markets. The increasing autonomy of the NBB is viewed as a strength in policy credibility, contrasting with threats such as weak coordination among federal and regional governments in other areas.

#### France: High Council for Financial Stability (HCSF) and Banque de France
In France, macroprudential oversight is characterized by a "strong financial oversight and macroprudential policy toolkit." The **High Council for Financial Stability (HCSF)** plays a pivotal role in coordinating stability efforts, working alongside the **Banque de France**. The IMF highlights this robust framework as a significant strength in France's **policy credibility and institutional strength**, helping to mitigate risks despite challenges in domestic political consensus and fiscal slippages. The Banque de France operates within the strong ECB framework, ensuring that macroprudential measures are aligned with broader Eurozone stability goals.

## Key Policy Measures and Sectoral Risks

The IMF staff reports for 2025 highlight specific macroprudential tools and emerging risks that require vigilant monitoring in both jurisdictions.

### Belgium: Mitigating Systemic Risks
The banking sector in Belgium is characterized by low non-performing loans (2.1%) and strong capitalization. However, macroprudential surveillance is increasingly focused on:
*   **Commercial Real Estate (CRE)**: Rising vacancies and price contractions in the CRE sector pose increasing risks to financial stability.
*   **Household Indebtedness**: With household debt at 58% of GDP, the IMF advises maintaining current mortgage lending limits and monitoring credit growth to ensure affordability.
*   **Corporate Bankruptcies**: Rising corporate bankruptcies require vigilance to prevent spillovers into the banking sector.
*   **Capital Buffers**: The IMF advises maintaining current capital buffer requirements to ensure banks remain well-capitalized.

### France: Banking Resilience and Emerging Vulnerabilities
The 2025 **Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP)** for France confirms the banking sector's resilience, with high capital ratios (19.8% regulatory capital ratio) and strong liquidity coverage. However, profitability is weak due to net interest margin compression. Key macroprudential focus areas include:
*   **Non-Bank Financial Intermediaries (NBFIs)**: Close monitoring of NBFIs is required to address risks that may not be fully captured by traditional banking supervision.
*   **Corporate Indebtedness**: High corporate indebtedness and rising bankruptcies among SMEs present significant risks, necessitating strict credit standards.
*   **Cyber and Climate Risks**: The framework includes robust provisions for managing cyber risk and the financial implications of climate change.
*   **Sovereign-Financial Sector Nexus**: While manageable, the link between sovereign debt levels and banking sector health remains a key area of monitoring.

## Impact on Sovereign Credit Ratings and Fiscal Sustainability

Effective macroprudential policy indirectly supports **fiscal sustainability** and enhances **sovereign credit ratings** by reducing the probability of financial crises that would require government bailouts or fiscal support.

*   **Risk Mitigation and Spreads**: In **Belgium**, the "moderate" assessment of systemic risks, underpinned by strong macroprudential oversight, contributes to a "moderate" overall risk of sovereign stress. This helps keep sovereign spreads low, despite high public debt levels (104.1% of GDP in 2024).
*   **Institutional Credibility**: In **France**, the presence of a strong macroprudential toolkit is listed as a key strength in institutional analysis. This credibility helps offset threats related to recent fiscal slippages and political uncertainty, contributing to a lower short-term risk of sovereign debt distress.
*   **Fiscal Space Preservation**: By preventing financial instability, macroprudential policies help preserve fiscal space. This is particularly important for both Belgium and France, which face significant pressure from aging population costs and the need for fiscal consolidation under the new **EU Economic Governance Framework (EGF)**.
*   **Contingent Liabilities**: Strong macroprudential regimes reduce the size of potential contingent liabilities arising from bank bailouts. In France, this is further supported by a strong deposit insurance fund and limited sovereign exposure in banking sector assets.
