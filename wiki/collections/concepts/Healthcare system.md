# Healthcare system

The **healthcare system** represents a critical structural component in sovereign credit analysis, particularly regarding long-term fiscal sustainability and public service efficiency. In the context of Poland, as highlighted in the European Commission’s 2025 Country Report, the healthcare sector is identified as a significant area requiring reform to address demographic pressures and improve health outcomes.

## Structural Characteristics and Challenges

The Polish healthcare system is currently characterized as **hospital-centric**, with a structural imbalance that prioritizes curative care over preventive measures. Key structural deficiencies include:

*   **Low Preventive Care Spending:** Insufficient allocation of resources towards prevention contributes to higher long-term costs and poorer population health metrics.
*   **High Treatable Mortality:** Poland exhibits treatable mortality rates that remain higher than the EU average, indicating inefficiencies in care delivery, access, or quality within the existing system.
*   **Demographic Pressure:** The system faces intensifying strain due to Poland’s aging population and demographic decline, which increases the demand for healthcare services while simultaneously shrinking the working-age tax base required to fund them.

## Fiscal and Macroeconomic Implications

The sustainability of the healthcare system is directly linked to sovereign credit rating factors, specifically **fiscal performance** and **fiscal sustainability**:

*   **Fiscal Sustainability Risks:** The aging population exerts upward pressure on public spending, creating a dual threat to the pension system and the healthcare system. Without structural reforms, healthcare expenditures risk becoming unsustainable relative to GDP and fiscal capacity.
*   **Impact on Fiscal Consolidation:** As Poland undertakes fiscal consolidation to reduce its high budget deficit (6.6% of GDP in 2024) under the excessive deficit procedure, the healthcare sector must be optimized. Inefficient spending in healthcare exacerbates the difficulty of meeting medium-term fiscal targets.
*   **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF):** Healthcare reform is a designated priority area within Poland’s RRP implementation. The success of these reforms is monitored as part of the broader effort to absorb EU funds and deliver structural improvements.

## Policy Direction and Reform

The European Commission recommends targeted interventions to enhance the resilience and efficiency of the healthcare system:

1.  **Shift to Preventive Care:** Rebalancing the system to increase spending on prevention to reduce the long-term burden of treatable diseases.
2.  **Integration with Labor Market Policies:** Addressing healthcare accessibility and workforce health supports the broader goal of increasing labor participation, particularly among the aging population and those with disabilities.
3.  **Alignment with Green and Digital Transitions:** Modernizing healthcare infrastructure through digitalization and efficient resource use, supported by EU cohesion and recovery funds.
