# Judicial Independence

Judicial independence refers to the principle that the judiciary should be kept separate from other branches of government. It ensures that judges can make decisions based solely on the law and facts, free from external pressures, political influence, or interference from the executive or legislative branches.

## Current Status and Context (Austria 2025)
As of the 2025 EU Commission Rule of Law Report, Austria maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence. Public and corporate perception remains strong, with 86% of the general public and 71% of companies viewing judicial independence as "fairly or very good."

## Key Challenges and Reform Areas
Despite high perception scores, several structural areas are identified as requiring reform to meet European standards:

*   **Administrative Appointments:** There is a noted lack of progress in involving the judiciary in the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents. Integrating judicial input into these appointments is considered a critical step for ensuring structural independence.
*   **Prosecutorial Autonomy:** A primary area of concern is the ongoing right of the Minister of Justice to instruct prosecutors in individual cases. To mitigate this, the government has committed to establishing an independent [[Federal Prosecution Office]] as a collegiate body.
*   **Access to Justice:** Recent rulings by the Constitutional Court have expanded access to justice by declaring certain restrictions on legal aid in the Administrative Procedural Code as unconstitutional.
*   **Digitalization:** The "Justice 3.0" initiative is currently working to modernize the system, aiming for 90% digital case management to improve efficiency and resource allocation.
