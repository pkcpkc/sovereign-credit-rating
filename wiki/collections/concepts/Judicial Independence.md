# Judicial Independence

Judicial independence refers to the principle that the judiciary should be kept separate from other branches of government. It ensures that judges can make decisions based solely on the law and facts, free from external pressures, political influence, or interference from the executive or legislative branches.

## Current Status and Context
Perception of judicial independence varies across European jurisdictions, reflecting different structural progress:

*   **Austria (2025):** Maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence. Public and corporate perception remains strong, with 86% of the general public and 71% of companies viewing judicial independence as "fairly or very good."
*   **Belgium (2025):** Shows a high level of perceived independence, with 64% of the general public and 72% of companies perceiving it as "fairly or very good."

## Key Challenges and Reform Areas

### Structural & Administrative Reforms
*   **Administrative Appointments:** In Austria, there is a noted lack of progress in involving the judiciary in the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents. Integrating judicial input into these appointments is considered a critical step for ensuring structural independence.
*   **Budgetary Autonomy:** In Belgium, the Government intends to transfer budgetary management from the executive to the judiciary, though this remains linked to pending reforms of the evaluation and disciplinary systems.
*   **Resource Deficiencies:** Belgium faces challenges where the budget and number of judges per 100,000 inhabitants remain below EU averages, contributing to long-standing backlogs (notably in the Brussels Court of Appeal).

### Prosecutorial and Integrity Frameworks
*   **Prosecutorial Autonomy:** A primary area of concern in Austria is the ongoing right of the Minister of Justice to instruct prosecutors in individual cases. To mitigate this, the government has committed to establishing an independent [[Federal Prosecution Office]] as a collegiate body.
*   **Integrity Frameworks:** Belgium is addressing integrity through plans for voluntary mergers of local police zones and the introduction of career-long integrity checks to standardize policies across various zones.

### Modernization and Access
*   **Digitalisation:** Both nations are prioritizing modernization. Austria's "Justice 3.0" initiative aims for 90% digital case management. Belgium is responding to Court of Audit criticisms by seeking an overarching approach to [[Digitalisation of Justice]] to reduce reliance on external consultants.
*   **Access to Justice:** Recent rulings by the Austrian Constitutional Court have expanded access to justice by declaring certain restrictions on legal aid in the Administrative Procedural Code as unconstitutional.
