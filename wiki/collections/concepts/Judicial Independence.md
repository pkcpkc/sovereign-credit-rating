# Judicial Independence

Judicial independence refers to the principle that the judiciary should be kept separate from other branches of government. It ensures that judges can make decisions based solely on the law and facts, free from external pressures, political influence, or interference from the executive or legislative branches.

## Current Status and Context
Perception of judicial independence varies significantly across European jurisdictions, reflecting different structural progress and political climates:

*   **Austria (2025):** Maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence. Public and corporate perception remains strong, with 86% of the general public and 71% of companies viewing judicial independence as "fairly or very good."
*   **Belgium (2025):** Shows a high level of perceived independence, with 64% of the general public and 72% of companies perceiving it as "fairly or very good."
*   **France (2025):** Perceived independence remains "average" (51% public, 53% companies). The Superior Council of Magistracy (CSM) has actively defended judges against personal attacks following high-profile decisions.
*   **Hungary (2025):** Perceived independence remains notably low, with only 37% of the public and 38% of companies viewing it as "fairly or very good." The report highlights a lack of consultation between the government and the National Judicial Council (NJC) regarding legislative amendments, such as raising the minimum age for judges to 35 and extending service until age 70.
*   **Italy (2025):** Perceived independence among the general public rose to 46% in 2025, a significant increase from 34% in 2021. However, tensions persist regarding the proposed separation of careers between judges and prosecutors.
*   **Malta (2025):** Perceived judicial independence remains high, with 69% of the general public and 63% of companies viewing it as favorable.
*   **Poland (2025):** Perceived judicial independence remains very low (26% for the general public, 24% for companies). While there has been a slight increase in business confidence, profound concerns remain regarding the independence of the Constitutional Tribunal and the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ).
*   **Romania (2025):** Public perception of judicial independence saw a decline, dropping to 44% (from 52% in 2024), while company perception fell to 51%. Significant progress was noted in aligning Justice Laws with [[Venice Commission]] recommendations.
*   **Slovakia (2025):** Public and corporate trust in judicial independence remains low, with only 32% of the general population perceiving it as "fairly or very good."

## Key Challenges and Reform Areas

### Structural & Administrative Reforms
*   **Administrative Appointments:** In Austria, there is a noted lack of progress in involving the judiciary in the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents. Integrating judicial input into these appointments is considered a critical step for ensuring structural independence.
*   **Magistracy Reform (France):** Implementation of the 2023 law is underway, removing age limits for admissions and creating a 360° evaluation process for court presidents.
*   **Budgetary Autonomy:** In Belgium, the Government intends to transfer budgetary management from the executive to the judiciary, though this remains linked to pending reforms of the evaluation and disciplinary systems.
*   **Remuneration & Quality (Hungary):** Significant progress was made in increasing salaries for judges and prosecutors (aiming for a 48% increase by 2027), though structural indexation remains absent.
*   **Constitutional Reform (Italy):** The Chamber of Deputies approved a reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors. However, the High Council for the Judiciary and various magistrates' associations expressed reservations regarding the authority of separate councils.
*   **Constitutional Reform (Malta):** A comprehensive constitutional reform was tabled in May 2025 to involve the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice. Additionally, the government proposed removing executive involvement in the appointment of members of specialized tribunals.
*   **Judicial Reform (Poland):** Poland is consulting the [[Venice Commission]] on reforms for the NCJ and Constitutional Tribunal. A draft law aims to remove approximately 1,600 judges appointed via irregular procedures as part of an [[Action Plan on the Rule of Law]].
*   **HR and Vacancies (Romania):** While occupancy rates for judge positions reached 83% in early 2025, vacancies remain a continuous concern due to high workloads.
*   **Judicial Oversight and Liability (Slovakia):** Concerns persist regarding the Judicial Council's expanded role in disciplinary proceedings and the lack of independent guarantees regarding the dismissal of its members. Furthermore, stakeholders warn that a lack of sufficient safeguards regarding "abuse of law" crimes creates a chilling effect on independent decision-making for judges.
*   **Court Efficiency:** Administrative courts in Slovakia have seen a sharp deterioration in efficiency, with resolution times rising to 1,040 days in 2023—one of the highest in the EU.

### Prosecutorial and Judicial Efficiency
*   **Prosecutorial Autonomy:** A primary area of concern in Austria is the ongoing right of the Minister of Justice to instruct prosecutors in individual cases. To mitigate this, the government has committed to establishing an independent [[Federal Prosecution Office]] as a collegiate body.
*   **Prosecutorial Oversight (Hungary):** A new mechanism allows judges to review prosecutorial decisions not to investigate corruption, which has seen a doubling in usage since 2024.
*   **Prosecutorial Transfer (Malta):** The transfer of non-summary case prosecutions from the Police to the Office of the Attorney General was completed in September 2024.
*   **Prosecution Reform (Poland):** Significant progress was achieved in separating the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General. The National Prosecution Office audited 2016–2023 cases, identifying numerous irregularities.
*   **Prosecutorial Safeguards (Romania):** The Ministry of Justice is drafting a law to extend the mandate of high-ranking prosecutors from three to five years.
*   **Prosecutorial Power (Slovakia):** The Prosecutor General retains broad powers to annul final decisions of lower-ranking prosecutors, raising concerns about undue interference in high-level corruption cases.
*   **Resource Deficiencies:** Belgium faces challenges where the budget and number of judges per 100,000 inhabitants remain below EU averages, contributing to long-standing backlogs. Malta similarly faces a judge-to-inhabitant ratio among the lowest in the EU, leading to high disposition times and significant backlogs in civil, criminal, and administrative cases.

### Integrity Frameworks
*   **Integrity Frameworks:** Belgium is addressing integrity through plans for voluntary mergers of local police zones and the introduction of career-long integrity checks to standardize policies across various zones.
*   **Anti-Corruption (France):** France is expanding its anti-corruption frameworks, including the reorganization of the Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) and increased resources for the National Financial Prosecutor (PNF).
*   **Lobbying & Conflicts of Interest (Italy):** Progress remains limited; while some ministries have voluntary registers, a comprehensive national lobbying register and rules for political office holders remain pending.
*   **Anti-Corruption Strategy (Malta):** While high-level corruption investigations have progressed, a "robust track record of final judgments" is still lacking. The Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC) remains under-resourced.
*   **Anti-Corruption Framework (Poland):** The Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (CBA) is being liquidated, with resources transferred to the police, Internal Security Agency (ABW), and National Revenue Administration (KAS). The European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) is now fully operational in Poland.
*   **Strategy and Risks (Romania):** The 2021-2025 [[Anti-Corruption Strategy]] remains on track, with a new strategy being drafted. However, a systemic risk has emerged regarding the *lex mitior* principle, where courts have closed numerous corruption cases and annulled convictions.
*   **Lobbying and Transparency (Romania):** While some rules on gifts were adopted in March 2025, there has been no progress on introducing lobbying rules for Members of Parliament.
*   **Whistleblower Protection (Slovakia):** Despite existing frameworks, reporting remains low, and the Ministry of Interior faced a EUR 90,000 fine for improperly suspending protected whistleblowers.

### Modernization and Access
*   **Digitalisation:** Both nations are prioritizing modernization. Austria's "Justice 3.0" initiative aims for 90% digital case management. Belgium is seeking an overarching approach to [[Digitalisation of Justice]] to reduce reliance on external consultants. France is expanding the *Portalis* tool to all labor and local courts, aiming for full criminal court decision uploads by the end of 2025.
*   **Digitalisation Trends (Italy):** While tax, civil, and administrative justice are fully digitalized, the criminal justice system is struggling with the *APP* platform due to technical issues and obsolete equipment.
*   **Artificial Intelligence:** The Government and the Court of Cassation (France) are actively studying safeguards for [[Artificial Intelligence in Justice]] regarding its use in drafting administrative decisions and judicial tasks.
*   **Access to Justice:** Recent rulings by the Austrian Constitutional Court have expanded access to justice by declaring certain restrictions on legal aid in the Administrative Procedural Code as unconstitutional.
*   **Legal Aid (Romania):** Expansion of legal aid to non-profit legal persons and groups in special situations was noted, though data transparency remains lacking.

### Systemic Risks and Rights
*   **Emergency Powers:** The prolonged use of "state of danger" (as seen in Hungary) allows the government to override Acts of Parliament, creating legal uncertainty and undermining the rule of law.
*   **Urgency Decrees (Italy):** Stakeholders expressed concern over the frequent use of government decrees (87 in the current legislature), which may impact the separation of powers.
*   **Legislative Unpredictability (Romania):** The frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (156 GEOs in 2024) remains a primary concern for businesses and civil society.
*   **Fast-Track Legislation (Slovakia):** The frequent use of fast-track procedures (e.g., 28 of 58 laws in 2024) continues to bypass meaningful stakeholder consultation.
*   **Media Pluralism:** The independence of the judiciary is often linked to [[Media Pluralism]]. In some regions, state-dominated advertising and pressure on journalists by agencies like the Sovereignty Protection Office can impact the public's ability to monitor judicial integrity. In Italy, the *Costa amendment* has been noted for creating a "chilling effect" on journalists regarding pre-trial custody orders.
*   **Public Service Media (Slovakia):** The establishment of the new Slovak Television and Radio (STVR) entity is viewed as a regression in editorial independence due to the potential for political influence over its Council.
*   **Journalists' Rights and SLAPPs:** While Malta enacted a law on cross-border strategic lawsuits (anti-SLAPP) in July 2024, further safeguards for journalists' working conditions and improvements to public service media independence remain areas for progress. In Poland, the number of [[SLAPPs]] has decreased, but journalists still face risks from defamation and insult laws.
*   **Media Landscape (Romania):** Characterized by high risks regarding ownership transparency (72%) and political influence, with significant difficulties in identifying ownership of online news sites. New alerts regarding harassment and intimidation of journalists were recorded in 2025.
*   **Civic Space (Poland):** The assessment of civic space in Poland was updated from "obstructed" to "narrowed" in March 2025, highlighting the ongoing struggle for checks and balances.
*   **Open Government (Romania):** A new Strategy for [[Open Government]] (2025-2030) was adopted in April 2025 to address the narrowing civic space.
*   **Asset Declarations (Romania):** A Constitutional Court ruling on May 29, 2025, declared it unconstitutional to require officials to declare assets of spouses and children, creating a potential transparency gap.
*   **NGO Pressures (Slovakia):** A new legal framework imposes heavy reporting and disclosure obligations on NGOs, which stakeholders describe as an increased administrative burden.
