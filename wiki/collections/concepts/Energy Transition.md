# Energy Transition

The **Energy Transition** refers to the structural shift from fossil-based energy systems toward renewable, low-carbon, and sustainable energy sources. This process involves fundamental changes in infrastructure, technology, and economic policy to meet climate targets and ensure long-term energy security.

## Contextual Overview: Belgium
In the context of the 2025 Country Report for Belgium, the energy transition is characterized by several key factors and challenges:

### Key Challenges
*   **Climate Targets:** Belgium is currently projected to fall short of its 2030 effort-sharing targets, aiming for a 42.6% reduction in emissions against a target of 47%.
*   **Economic Barriers:** High energy costs remain a primary obstacle to private investment. Specifically, the electricity-to-gas price ratio in Belgium is among the highest in the EU, which creates a significant hurdle for the adoption of heat pumps and other electrified heating solutions.
*   **Regulatory & Infrastructure Needs:** Successful transition requires addressing high regulatory complexity and investing in the rollout of renewable energy sources.

### Related Concepts
*   **[[Circular Economy]]**: Complementary to the energy transition, Belgium shows strong performance in circularity (19.7% rate) but requires further investment in waste management and eco-design to support a sustainable economy.
*   **[[STEM Education]]**: The transition requires a skilled workforce; however, Belgium faces a skills mismatch and a low share of STEM graduates, which may impact the development of green technologies.
*   **Fiscal Policy**: The transition is influenced by the broader [[Fiscal-Structural Plan]], as aging populations and high public debt (projected at 107.1% by 2025) create competing demands for public spending.
