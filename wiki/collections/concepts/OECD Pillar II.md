# OECD Pillar II

OECD Pillar II refers to the international tax framework designed to ensure that large multinational enterprises (MNEs) pay a minimum level of tax regardless of where they operate. It is a core component of the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework on Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS).

## Key Context and Implementation
In the context of European fiscal policy, Pillar II is closely linked to the **EU Minimum Tax Directive**. The framework aims to:
- **Standardize Corporate Income Tax (CIT):** Establish a global minimum tax rate to prevent "races to the bottom" where countries lower tax rates to attract corporate investment.
- **Fiscal Sustainability:** Support countries in maintaining robust revenue streams while ensuring a level playing field for domestic and international businesses.
- **Reform Roadmap:** For countries like Malta, implementing Pillar II requires a strategic roadmap to align existing Corporate Income Tax (CIT) structures with these international standards while managing the interactions with personal income tax systems.

## Relationship to Fiscal Policy
The adoption of Pillar II is a critical element of broader **Fiscal Consolidation** efforts. By aligning with international tax minimums, jurisdictions can better manage their fiscal deficits and ensure that corporate tax revenues remain stable and predictable. This is particularly important as nations move away from heavy energy subsidies and toward productivity-driven growth models.
