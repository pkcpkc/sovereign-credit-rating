# Poland

## Geopolitical and Political Stability

### Strengths

- Stable government with increasing trust indices in 2024.
- Stable democratic framework with regular elections.
- Active engagement with Venice Commission and EU institutions.
- Strong domestic political consensus on defense spending and security.
- Successful integration of Ukrainian refugees into the labor market has mitigated some labor supply shocks.

### Threats

- Geopolitical tensions affecting defense spending and macroeconomic stability.
- Regional instability due to proximity to the war in Ukraine.
- Constitutional crisis regarding the legitimacy of the Constitutional Tribunal.
- Political polarization and distrust in judicial institutions.
- Tensions between domestic constitutional authorities and EU institutions.
- Russia's war in Ukraine poses significant external security and geopolitical risks, including potential spillovers and refugee flows.
- Geoeconomic fragmentation and conflicts present headwinds to trade and FDI.

## Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength

### Strengths

- Establishment of an independent Fiscal Council (operational 2026).
- Action Plan on Rule of Law.
- Improved regulatory governance and digitalisation.
- Adoption of amendments to NCJ and Constitutional Tribunal laws (pending review).
- Progress in separating Minister of Justice from Prosecutor General.
- Strengthened rules for legislative consultation.
- Strong institutional framework, including an independent National Bank of Poland (NBP) with a credible inflation-targeting record.
- Recent amendments to the stabilizing expenditure rule and plans for an independent fiscal council enhance governance.

### Threats

- Complex and fast-changing regulatory framework deterring investment.
- Perceived erosion of judicial independence.
- Weak ex post evaluation of legislation.
- Refusal to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments.
- Disputed appointment of judges by the National Council for the Judiciary.
- Weaknesses in anti-corruption strategy and lobbying rules.
- Fiscal credibility is challenged by the widening deficit and entry into the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP).
- Implementation of identified fiscal consolidation measures remains partially unspecified.

## Policymaking Stability

### Strengths

- Measures to limit use of fast-track procedures.
- Increased use of impact assessments.
- Introduction of mandatory impact assessments for draft laws.
- Digitalization of court procedures.
- Clear medium-term fiscal structural plan (MTFSP) aimed at aligning with EU frameworks.
- Stable monetary policy anchor provided by the NBP.

### Threats

- Frequent changes in laws with short preparation times (avg. 31 days).
- Legislative volatility.
- Frequent changes in legislation affecting legal certainty.
- Slow pace of media licensing decisions.
- Uncertainty regarding the pace and composition of medium-term fiscal adjustment.
- Potential populist pressure on pension and social benefit reforms.

## Accountability

### Strengths

- Increased transparency in decision-making.
- Digitalisation of public services.
- Operational whistleblower protection law.
- Investigations into large-scale corruption cases involving politicians.
- Active Ombudsperson fulfilling checks and balances role.
- Improved oversight of the Polish Development Bank (BGK) funds.
- Plans to establish an independent fiscal council to strengthen accountability.

### Threats

- Concerns over lobbying transparency and enforcement against high-level corruption.
- Broad immunities for top executives hindering criminal accountability.
- Impunity clauses in legislation.
- Limited transparency in political party financing.
- Limited transparency on certain extrabudgetary funds, though improving.

## Debt Payment Culture

### Strengths

- Commitment to fiscal consolidation under new EU framework.
- Strong record of honoring sovereign obligations.
- Poland maintains an investment-grade sovereign rating with low perceived default risk.
- Strong track record of meeting debt obligations.

### Threats

- Rising public debt levels.

## Economic Strength and Tax Base

### Strengths

- Fast-growing economy in the EU (2.9% GDP growth in 2024).
- Strong private consumption.
- High level of independence of national competition authority.
- High GDP per capita convergence, now at 80% of the EU27 average.
- Robust private consumption driven by real wage growth.
- Strong education outcomes and low income inequality.

### Threats

- Complex tax framework hampering innovation.
- Low tax-to-GDP ratio.
- Corruption risks in public procurement affecting investment.
- Diminishing cost-competitiveness due to real effective exchange rate appreciation.
- Low personal income tax (PIT) revenues compared to EU peers.

## Growth Prospects

### Strengths

- Accelerating growth forecast for 2025.
- Strong investment funded by EU.
- Clean tech manufacturing hub.
- Deregulation efforts to reduce administrative burden.
- Near-term growth supported by NGEU fund absorption and domestic demand.
- Potential for productivity gains through structural reforms and innovation.

### Threats

- Demographic decline (aging population).
- Skills shortages.
- Low labor productivity (67% of EU average).
- Low confidence in investment protection among companies due to legislative instability.
- Population aging poses a long-term drag on potential growth.
- Subdued growth outlook in main trading partners (Euro Area).
- Climate transition costs and energy dependency on coal.

## Economic Diversity

### Strengths

- Diversified manufacturing sector.
- Growing clean technology and battery production.
- Diversified economy with strong domestic demand.
- Diversified industrial base and strong export orientation.
- Growing services sector.

### Threats

- Regional disparities in competitiveness and development.
- High carbon intensity of the economy relies heavily on coal.

## External Liquidity

### Strengths

- Current account surplus.
- Improved external sustainability.
- Resilient external position.
- Ample foreign exchange reserves (approx. 160% of IMF adequacy metric).
- Strong access to international capital markets.

### Threats

- Reliance on external funding for infrastructure.
- Current account projected to move into deficit.

## Currency Status

### Strengths

- Flexible exchange rate.
- Stable currency within EU monetary framework.
- Free-floating exchange rate allows for automatic adjustment to external shocks.

### Threats

- Appreciation of the złoty posing risks to cost competitiveness.
- Zloty is not a reserve currency, limiting monetary flexibility compared to USD/EUR.

## External Asset/Liability Position

### Strengths

- Reducing net debtor position.
- Moderate external debt levels.
- Declining gross external debt ratio (approx. 45% of GDP).
- Stable funding sources dominated by FDI and long-term debt.

### Threats

- Net international investment position deficit (-30.8% of GDP).
- Reliance on foreign direct investment.
- Negative Net International Investment Position (NIIP), though improving.

## Financial Sector Health

### Strengths

- Resilient banking sector with high capitalization.
- Declining non-performing loan ratios.
- Strong liquidity.
- Well-capitalized banking sector.
- Banking sector is well-capitalized and liquid, with buffers above regulatory requirements.
- Low non-performing loan ratios.
- Strong bank profitability.

### Threats

- Legal risks from legacy forex mortgages.
- Litigation risks from CHF mortgages and potential WIBOR disputes.
- Distortions in private credit due to tax incentives for public debt.
- Slow recovery in corporate credit.

## Fiscal Performance and Flexibility

### Strengths

- Endorsed medium-term fiscal-structural plan.
- Fiscal adjustment measures increasing revenues.
- Prudent fiscal management within EU framework.
- Some fiscal space available due to low sovereign spreads.
- Capacity for fiscal adjustment demonstrated in previous crises.

### Threats

- High budget deficit (6.6% of GDP in 2024).
- Rising public spending.
- High and rising defense spending.
- Potential fiscal risks from contingent liabilities (SOEs).
- High fiscal deficit (5.9% of GDP in 2024) driven by wage increases, social benefits, and defense spending.
- Limited fiscal space for near-term tightening due to political constraints.

## Debt Burden

### Strengths

- Debt affordability remains manageable given growth and inflation.
- Local-currency debt.
- Manageable public debt levels.
- Debt levels remain below EU average.
- Favorable debt structure with long maturities and local currency dominance.

### Threats

- Rising public debt (projected 58.0% of GDP in 2025).
- High interest costs.
- Public debt rising from ~50% to projected 65% of GDP by 2029 under baseline.
- Increasing interest burden as debt stock grows.

## Fiscal Sustainability

### Strengths

- Pension system reform discussions.
- Efforts to increase effective retirement age.
- Long-term fiscal sustainability supported by structural reforms.
- Medium-term consolidation plan aims to stabilize debt.
- Strong revenue base potential through tax reforms (VAT, property tax).

### Threats

- Aging population pressures on pension and healthcare systems.
- Special pension schemes.
- Long-term risks from aging population (pension and healthcare costs).
- Climate mitigation investment needs.

## Contingent Liabilities

### Strengths

- Strong banking capital buffers.
- Audit controls by Supreme Audit Office.
- Improved transparency of BGK activities.
- Banks have established significant provisions for litigation risks.

### Threats

- Potential costs from banking sector disputes.
- Risks from state-owned enterprises and clientelist networks.
- Off-budget risks associated with BGK and extrabudgetary funds.
- Potential costs from unresolved financial litigation (CHF/WIBOR).

## Price Stability

### Strengths

- Inflation easing (3.7% in 2024).
- Credible inflation targeting by central bank.
- Inflation has declined significantly from 2023 peaks.
- Anchored inflation expectations.

### Threats

- High unit labour costs.
- Inflation concerns.
- Inflation remains above target (approx. 5% y/y), with sticky core inflation.
- Wage-price spiral risks in a tight labor market.

## Central Bank Independence

### Strengths

- Independent monetary policy.
- NBP has demonstrated independence by maintaining tight policy despite political headwinds.
- Strong institutional autonomy.

### Threats

- Political pressure to cut rates early to support growth.

## Exchange Rate Regime

### Strengths

- Flexible management.
- Floating exchange rate within EU context.
- Flexible exchange rate regime absorbs external shocks.

### Threats

- Real effective exchange rate appreciation affecting competitiveness.
