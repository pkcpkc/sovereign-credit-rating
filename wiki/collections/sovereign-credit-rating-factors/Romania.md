# Romania

## Geopolitical and Political Stability

### Strengths

- **Schengen Accession**: Accession to the Schengen Area on 1 January 2025 is expected to facilitate trade and foreign investment, enhancing economic integration.
- **EU Membership Anchor**: Stable macroeconomic environment is generally supported by membership in the European Union, providing a framework for policy coordination.
- **Open Government Strategy**: Adoption of the Strategy for Open Government 2025-2030 addresses challenges for civil society and aims to improve transparency.
- **Venice Commission Alignment**: Completion of the process to take into account Venice Commission recommendations on Justice Laws demonstrates alignment with European democratic standards.

### Threats

- **Political Instability**: Frequent changes in top political decision-makers and strategic planning create uncertainty.
- **Policy Uncertainty**: Political instability and uncertainty over fiscal policy negatively affect investor confidence.
- **Populist Measures**: Proposals for populist measures, such as price controls by the government, introduce volatility.
- **Administrative Weakness**: Weak administrative capacity in local and regional authorities hampers effective governance.
- **Electoral Irregularities**: Annulment of the first round of the 2024 Presidential election due to irregularities, leading to repeated elections, highlights vulnerabilities in electoral administration.
- **Media Pressure**: Political pressure on editorial independence and harassment of journalists affect the information environment and political discourse.

## Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength

### Strengths

- **Independent Banking Sector**: The banking sector operates independently with robust capitalization.
- **Justice System Efficiency**: The justice system is efficient overall, with decreasing length of civil and commercial cases.
- **Judicial Independence Reforms**: Ministry of Justice shown openness to adapt IT system reforms to guarantee judicial independence and address concerns about centralized data access.
- **Anti-Corruption Strategy**: Implementation of the 2021-2025 National Anti-Corruption Strategy is on track, with a positive record in prosecuting high-level corruption.
- **Prosecutorial Stability**: Stable occupancy rate in prosecution services despite vacancies ensures continuity in legal enforcement.
- **International Cooperation**: Law strengthening criminalisation of foreign bribery entered into force in late 2024, enhancing compliance with international anti-corruption standards.

### Threats

- **SOE Management**: Weak management and oversight of state-owned enterprises pose governance risks.
- **Administrative Capacity**: Suboptimal administrative capacity and public services hinder effective policy implementation.
- **Reform Implementation**: Poor implementation of reforms, including the tax system review, undermines policy credibility.
- **Legal Certainty**: Frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances (GEOs) undermines legal certainty and rule of law. 199 ordinances were adopted in 2024, contributing to legislative unpredictability.
- **Corruption Perception**: Low trust in public institutions and perceived corruption among businesses remain significant issues.
- **Regulatory Burden**: A high share of firms dedicating staff to regulatory compliance indicates an inefficient business environment.
- **Asset Declaration Transparency**: Constitutional Court ruling in May 2025 deemed requirements to declare spouses’ and children’s assets unconstitutional, weakening the transparency of the asset declaration system managed by the National Integrity Agency.
- **Judicial Independence Perception**: Perceived judicial independence has decreased among the general public and companies despite institutional reforms.

## Policymaking Stability

### Strengths

- **Medium-Term Planning**: Submission of the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP) extending adjustment to 2031 provides a clearer long-term fiscal trajectory.
- **New Anti-Corruption Strategy**: Preparation of a new Anti-Corruption Strategy based on ex-post evaluation of the previous framework supports continuous improvement.

### Threats

- **Investment Barrier**: Fiscal and legislative uncertainty is cited as the top barrier to long-term investment.
- **Consultation Deficits**: Short public consultation periods and lack of dialogue between industries and government reduce policy quality. Consultations are often criticized as ineffective "box-ticking" exercises.
- **Regulatory Volatility**: Volatility in tax burden and frequent changes in rules create an unpredictable environment. Legislative unpredictability is a primary concern for businesses.
- **Short-Termism**: Absence of a medium-to-long-term dimension in government finances, with a focus on short-term gains.
- **Lobbying Transparency**: No progress made on introducing lobbying rules for Members of Parliament, reducing transparency in political decision-making.

## Accountability

### Strengths

- **Fiscal Council**: The Romanian Fiscal Council has a broad mandate to monitor fiscal policy, though recruitment issues persist.
- **Anti-Corruption Track Record**: Positive track record in combating high-level corruption cases exists, with the National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA) maintaining stable operations.
- **Judicial Transparency**: Improved transparency of case-law on disciplinary sanctions in the judiciary.
- **Conflict of Interest Management**: National Integrity Agency maintains results in managing conflicts of interest, despite recent legislative challenges.
- **Human Rights Compliance**: Number of pending European Court of Human Rights judgments has decreased.

### Threats

- **Perceived Corruption**: High perceived corruption and lack of a lobby register for MPs undermine transparency.
- **Procurement Risks**: Public procurement remains a sector with high corruption risk, although digitalization of the process is ongoing.
- **Judicial Loopholes**: Many corruption cases are closed due to statute of limitations rulings. Recent High Court of Cassation and Justice decisions on *lex mitior* led to discontinuation of numerous corruption cases and annulment of convictions, raising risks of impunity.
- **Enforcement Issues**: Low enforcement of existing rules such as SME tests and fiscal compacts.
- **Media Ownership Transparency**: Insufficient information on media ownership beyond the audiovisual sector. Untransparent political advertising and financing of private media by political parties increase.
- **Whistleblower Protection**: Weak protection for whistleblowers in public bodies.
- **Political Financing**: Pending legislation to improve transparency of political party financing.
- **Human Rights Institutions**: Accreditation process for National Human Rights Institutions (Romanian Institute for Human Rights and People's Advocate) is stalled due to questions regarding independence and resources.

## Debt Payment Culture

### Strengths

- **Market Access**: Timely issuance of sovereign green bonds indicates active market access and willingness to engage with international investors.
- **Below-Average Debt**: Government debt remains significantly below the EU average (81.6%), providing a buffer against default risks.

### Threats

- **Rapid Debt Accumulation**: Public debt increased rapidly to 54.8% of GDP in 2024, signaling deteriorating fiscal discipline.
- **Banking Exposure**: High share of banking sector claims on government (25% of assets) creates interconnected risk.
- **Funding Risks**: Risk of funding shortfalls forcing reliance on national budget rather than external markets.

## Economic Strength and Tax Base

### Strengths

- **Convergence**: GDP per capita converged to ~80% of the EU average in 2024, indicating strong catch-up growth.
- **Consumption Resilience**: Resilient private consumption supports economic activity and domestic demand.
- **Investment Confidence**: Over half of companies express confidence in the effectiveness of investment protection.

### Threats

- **Low Tax Revenue**: Low tax revenue as a percentage of GDP (26.2%) compared to the EU average (39%) limits fiscal space.
- **Informal Economy**: High VAT gap (33.7%) indicates a large informal economy and inefficiencies in tax collection.
- **Weak Capital Markets**: Large reliance on internal financing for firms due to weak capital markets constrains business growth.
- **Regulatory Burden**: Regulatory burden is seen as a major competitive drawback by companies.
- **Fiscal Code Instability**: Changes in the fiscal code via GEOs affecting micro-enterprise classification thresholds create uncertainty for small businesses.

## Growth Prospects

### Strengths

- **Growth Recovery**: Growth is projected to pick up to 1.4% in 2025 and 2.2% in 2026.
- **EU Funding**: Substantial EU funding available (over EUR 90 billion) for infrastructure and reforms supports future investment.
- **Industrial Base**: The automotive sector serves as a crucial, high-value industrial base.
- **Digitalization**: Ongoing digitalization of public procurement process improves efficiency and transparency.

### Threats

- **Growth Deceleration**: Real GDP growth decelerated to 0.8% in 2024 due to weak net exports and investment slowdown.
- **Infrastructure and R&D**: Outdated infrastructure and low R&D capacity hinder productivity improvements.
- **Skills Shortages**: Skills shortages and low educational attainment limit labor productivity.
- **External Shocks**: Potential negative impact from US tariffs through trade and investment channels.
- **Civil Society Constraints**: Civil society space is considered narrowed, with CSOs facing administrative barriers, which may impede bottom-up innovation and accountability.
- **Legislative Unpredictability**: Remains a concern for investors, potentially dampening long-term growth.

## Economic Diversity

### Strengths

- **Export Diversification**: Diversified export sectors including automotive, agriculture, and tech services.
- **Energy Transition**: Renewable energy generation is reaching 48% of the electricity mix, reducing fossil fuel dependence.

### Threats

- **Energy Mix**: Heavily reliant on fossil fuels in the broader energy mix, posing transition risks.
- **Industrial Narrowness**: Narrow industrial base in some regions with low innovation intensity.
- **Regional Disparities**: High regional disparities in competitiveness and development across the country.

## External Liquidity

### Strengths

- **Reserve Buffer**: Official foreign reserves at 20.8% of GDP provide a significant buffer against external shocks.
- **Low External Debt**: Low net external debt position despite a negative Net International Investment Position (NIIP).

### Threats

- **Current Account Deficit**: Current account deficit widened to 8.4% of GDP in 2024, reflecting high import demand and weak competitiveness.
- **Negative NIIP**: Negative net international investment position (-41.5% of GDP) indicates Romania is a net borrower from the rest of the world.
- **Foreign Capital Dependence**: High reliance on foreign capital inflows to finance domestic investment creates vulnerability to capital flow reversals.

## Currency Status

### Strengths

- **Monetary Autonomy**: Flexible exchange rate regime provides some monetary policy autonomy.

### Threats

- **Limited International Use**: The Lei is not a reserve currency and has limited international use.
- **Managed Exchange Rate**: Exchange rate is managed by the National Bank to remain relatively steady against the Euro, which may limit flexibility during external shocks.

## External Asset/Liability Position

### Strengths

- **Asset Base**: Assets on foreigners stand at 39% of GDP.
- **Reserve Strength**: Strong official reserve assets provide a cushion.

### Threats

- **High Liabilities**: Liabilities to foreigners amount to 83% of GDP.
- **Negative Investment Flows**: Net portfolio investments and direct investments are negative, indicating outflows of ownership stakes.

## Financial Sector Health

### Strengths

- **Strong Capitalization**: Banking sector is well-capitalized with a CET1 ratio of 20% (above EU average).
- **Asset Quality**: Low non-performing loan ratio (2.7%) and strong profitability.
- **Liquidity**: Robust liquidity positions in banks.

### Threats

- **Underdeveloped Markets**: Underdeveloped capital markets with low listed equity and bond volumes.
- **Banking Dominance**: Heavy reliance on bank loans and internal financing; small banking sector size relative to GDP (52.8%).
- **Concentration Risk**: High exposure of banks to government bonds creates concentration risk and potential contagion from fiscal stress.

## Fiscal Performance and Flexibility

### Strengths

- **Consolidation Commitment**: Commitment to the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan with net expenditure caps.
- **Emergency Measures**: Adoption of emergency measures to save 2.0% of GDP in 2025 (wage freezes, removal of tax exemptions).

### Threats

- **High Deficit**: Government deficit rose to 9.3% of GDP in 2024, far exceeding EU recommendations.
- **Expenditure Growth**: Net expenditure growth of 19.9% in 2024, driven by generous income policies and public investment.
- **Adjustment Capacity**: Low capacity for fiscal adjustment without growth-enhancing reforms.
- **Fiscal Unpredictability**: Frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances in the fiscal area adds to unpredictability and complicates budget credibility.

## Debt Burden

### Strengths

- **Below-EU Average**: Debt ratio remains below the EU average.
- **Debt Structure**: Favorable debt structure with significant local currency debt implied by central bank management.

### Threats

- **Rapid Accumulation**: Rapid debt accumulation to 54.8% of GDP in 2024.
- **Projected Increase**: Debt is projected to increase to 59.4% in 2025 and 63.3% in 2026.
- **Interest Costs**: High interest expenditure burden as debt levels rise.

## Fiscal Sustainability

### Strengths

- **Gradual Consolidation**: Extended adjustment period (2025-2031) allows for gradual fiscal consolidation.
- **Revenue Reforms**: Reforms aimed at increasing recurrent revenues by 1.7% of GDP.

### Threats

- **Persistent Deficits**: Persistent deficits risk long-term fiscal sustainability.
- **Demographic Pressure**: Aging population increasing pressure on pensions and healthcare costs.
- **Tax Reform Lag**: Limited progress in tax framework reform and VAT gap reduction.

## Contingent Liabilities

### Strengths

- **Low Explicit Obligations**: Low explicit off-budget obligations mentioned.
- **Banking Solvency**: Banking sector support costs are unlikely due to strong capitalization.

### Threats

- **SOE Governance**: Weak governance of state-owned enterprises poses contingent liability risks.
- **Climate Risks**: High exposure to climate-related disasters impacting public finances.

## Price Stability

### Strengths

- **Inflation Trend**: Inflation is falling and anchored by National Bank policies.
- **Real Wages**: Real wages are rising, supporting domestic demand.

### Threats

- **High Inflation**: High inflation at 5.8% in 2024, though falling from 10% in 2023.
- **Sticky Inflation**: Inflation is projected to hover around 5% in 2025.
- **Wage-Price Dynamics**: In-work poverty is high, indicating wage inflation is not fully translating to productivity.

## Central Bank Independence

### Strengths

- **Credible Framework**: National Bank of Romania maintains a credible inflation-targeting framework.
- **Institutional Autonomy**: Strong institutional autonomy is generally respected.

### Threats

- **Fiscal Pressure**: Pressure for monetary financing implied by high fiscal deficits.
- **Exchange Rate Constraint**: Central bank managing exchange rate steady against Euro, limiting policy flexibility.

## Exchange Rate Regime

### Strengths

- **Managed Flexibility**: Managed float provides flexibility while maintaining stability.
- **No Unsustainable Pegs**: No unsustainable pegs are in place.

### Threats

- **Devaluation Risk**: Potential devaluation risk if current account deficits persist.
- **Limited Use**: Limited international use of the Lei reduces its resilience as a store of value internationally.
