# Malta

## Geopolitical and Political Stability

### Strengths

- Stable government and political consensus within the EU framework.
- Strong integration into the Single Market and EU governance structures.
- Comfortable parliamentary majority (Labor Party) with the next election not until 2027, providing political stability.
- High perceived judicial independence among the general public and companies.
- Stable democratic institutions and ongoing constitutional reforms to enhance justice sector independence.

### Threats

- Criticism regarding legislative reforms limiting citizen-led magisterial inquiries, raising concerns about accountability.
- Pending implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments.
- Civil society space is assessed as "narrowed," with draft regulatory frameworks for NGOs under preparation.
- Spillovers from regional conflicts (Russia-Ukraine, Israel-Gaza).
- Deepening geoeconomic fragmentation.
- Cyberattacks and misuse of AI technologies pose emerging security risks.

## Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength

### Strengths

- Highly developed digital public services (EU leader in e-government).
- Implementation of reforms via the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF), including the establishment of [[Science Malta]] (Xjenza Malta).
- Timely transposition of EU directives (among best in EU).
- Completion of the transfer of prosecutorial powers to the Office of the Attorney General.
- Increased human resources in investigative and prosecution authorities.
- Strong perception of investment protection among businesses.
- Enhanced anti-money laundering enforcement capabilities in the Attorney General's office.
- Commitment to fiscal consolidation under the EU framework.
- Strong AML/CFT supervision and judicial reform efforts.
- High data adequacy rating (SDDS Plus).

### Threats

- Significant concerns regarding corruption perceptions (CPI score 46/100 vs EU 62).
- Lengthy judicial proceedings (454 days for civil/commercial cases, 1,350 days for administrative) hamper business confidence and enforcement.
- Limited independence and effectiveness of the [[Malta Fiscal Advisory Council]] (MFAC).
- Weak enforcement of anti-money laundering and aggressive tax planning regulations.
- Weak capacity of the Permanent Commission Against Corruption to achieve concrete results.
- Lack of transparency in the implementation of the National Anti-Fraud and Corruption Strategy.
- Data gaps in the commercial real estate sector hinder effective risk assessment.
- Challenges in implementing energy price reform due to social and political sensitivity.

## Policymaking Stability

### Strengths

- Endorsed Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan provides a clear fiscal trajectory.
- Strong commitment to EU strategic priorities (green/digital transition) via Cohesion and RRF funds.
- Ongoing constitutional reform involving the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice.
- Preparation of legislation to align with the European Media Freedom Act.
- Policy direction broadly aligned with past staff recommendations (IMF/EC).
- Launch of 'Malta Vision 2050' for long-term strategic direction.

### Threats

- Regulatory burden and administrative complexity remain high (OECD PMR indicator second weakest in EU).
- Policy reversals or inconsistencies in social dialogue, characterized by short consultation timelines.
- Slow law-making processes and sudden changes in legislation affecting business confidence.
- Limited progress on establishing a formal framework for public participation in legislative processes.
- Disagreements over energy price reforms create policy friction.
- Risks from abrupt policy reversals if external shocks hit.

## Accountability

### Strengths

- Strong digital infrastructure for transparency in public services.
- Participation in EU anti-corruption and rule of law monitoring mechanisms.
- Reliable review mechanism for access to official documents provided by the Data Protection and Information Commissioner.
- High perceived independence of the national competition authority.
- Effective oversight by [[Malta Financial Services Authority]] (MFSA) and [[Central Bank of Malta]] (CBM).
- Implementation of Digital Justice Strategy.

### Threats

- Perceived widespread corruption in business and public procurement.
- Low number of final decisions in corruption cases; lack of robust track record in judicial enforcement of integrity.
- Transparency issues in social dialogue platforms (MCESD).
- Inadequate integrity measures for high-level politicians and officials.
- Ministers' asset declarations remain unpublished.
- Limited oversight mechanisms for anti-corruption agencies.
- Need to further strengthen the chief justice appointment process.
- Monitoring emerging threats like trade-based money laundering.

- Low overall risk of sovereign stress.
- Debt below EU debt ceiling reinforces creditor confidence.

## Economic Strength and Tax Base

### Strengths

- High GDP per capita (107% of EU average in existing data; converged with EU average in IMF data) and broad-based economic growth.
- Robust export performance in services (tourism, iGaming, financial services).
- Over two-thirds of companies express confidence in the effectiveness of investment protection.
- Strong revenue from corporate income tax.
- Robust service exports.

### Threats

- Tax revenue is low (26.6% of GDP vs EU 39%) and heavily reliant on corporate income tax, creating vulnerability to shocks and aggressive planning.
- Large VAT compliance gap (25.9% vs EU 7%) indicates significant revenue leakage.
- Low effective carbon rate and absence of recurrent property taxes limit environmental and stable revenue streams.
- Delays in judicial proceedings undermine legal certainty and hamper economic activities.
- Reliance on export-oriented services (tourism, gaming) exposes the tax base to sector-specific cycles.
- Strain on infrastructure and public services from population density affects economic efficiency.

## Growth Prospects

### Strengths

- Strong short-to-medium term growth forecasts (4.1% in 2025, 4.0% in 2026).
- High productivity growth rates (3.2% annualized 2020-2023).
- Diversified economy with strong business confidence in legal protections.
- Projected to outperform other European countries (4% medium-term growth).
- Strong domestic demand recovery.
- Innovation and digitalization initiatives.

### Threats

- Severe labor shortages and skills mismatches constrain productivity and growth potential.
- Very low R&D intensity (0.64% of GDP) hinders long-term innovation-led growth.
- Infrastructure congestion and housing affordability issues impact business competitiveness.
- Corruption perceived as a problem in doing business by 51% of companies.
- 38% of companies report corruption prevented them from winning public tenders.
- Structural constraints (labor shortages, infrastructure bottlenecks).
- Maturing of gaming sector.
- Tight labor markets limiting potential growth.

## Economic Diversity

### Strengths

- Diversified service exports including IT, financial, and professional services.
- Strong performance in digital intensity among SMEs.
- Diverse service exports (professional services, ICT).
- Developing venture capital and startup ecosystem.

### Threats

- High dependence on tourism and service sectors, making the economy vulnerable to external shocks.
- Limited industrial base; manufacturing accounts for only ~2% of GHG emissions and small share of GVA.
- Concentration in tourism and online gaming.
- Vulnerability to external shocks due to small open economy structure.

## External Liquidity

- Strong net international investment position (100.3% of GDP in Q3 2024; ~95% of GDP in IMF data).
- Adequate foreign exchange reserves and access to EU funding instruments.
- Current account surplus.
- Euro as reserve currency eliminates external financing constraints for sovereign.

## External Asset/Liability Position

### Strengths

- Net creditor position ensures strong external solvency.
- Resilient balance of payments with consistent current account surpluses.
- Sizable gross asset position.
- Net external creditor position.

### Threats

- High gross external assets and liabilities (approx. 3000% of GDP) indicate high financialization and exposure to global capital flows.
- Sizable gross external liabilities (~30x GDP).
- Volatility of financial flows.

## Financial Sector Health

### Strengths

- Banking sector exhibits high capital adequacy (CET1 ratio 20.2% vs EU 16.6%; Tier 1 ratio ~21% in IMF data).
- Strong liquidity (LCR 358%; LCR >350%).
- Low non-performing loans (1.8% overall, 3.3% corporate; ~2% in IMF data).
- Enhanced anti-money laundering enforcement capabilities in the Attorney General's office.
- High profitability.

### Threats

- Underdeveloped domestic capital markets limit funding alternatives for businesses.
- High concentration in banking sector (top 5 MFIs control >75% of assets).
- EU Court ruled investor citizenship scheme illegal, indicating past regulatory weaknesses.
- Substantial exposure to real estate (70% of loan portfolio).
- Cybersecurity risks.
- Limited pass-through of ECB rates to mortgages may mask risks.

## Fiscal Performance and Flexibility

### Strengths

- Deficit is projected to decline significantly (3.7% in 2024 to 2.8% in 2026).
- Strong revenue growth expected from improved tax collection efforts.
- Increased personnel in National Audit Office and Internal Audit and Investigations Department.
- Commitment to deficit reduction (target 2.6% of GDP by 2027).
- Revenue administration reforms improving collection efficiency.

### Threats

- High net expenditure growth (13.9% in 2024) driven by energy subsidies and capital expenditures.
- Cumulative net expenditure deviation exceeds Council recommendations.
- Auditing institutions identified weaknesses in the use of public funds and bypassing of procurement regulations.
- Reliance on broad spending restraint.
- Sizable energy subsidies (20% of fiscal deficit) threaten fiscal space.
- Risks to fiscal space if energy prices spike.

## Debt Burden

### Strengths

- Low government debt ratio (47.4% of GDP in 2024), well below the EU average (~81%) and the 60% treaty reference value.
- Debt ratio is projected to stabilize below 50% by 2026.
- Favorable debt structure with low interest burdens relative to GDP.
- Debt well below EU 60% ceiling.
- Low interest rates relative to growth.
- Local currency debt eliminates rollover risk.

### Threats

- Debt rising marginally to ~50% of GDP.
- Higher energy consumption and constant prices could increase debt further.

## Fiscal Sustainability

### Strengths

- Current low debt levels provide fiscal space for adjustment.
- Implementation of medium-term fiscal-structural plan under EU scrutiny.
- Debt stabilization in baseline scenario.
- Low long-term risk of sovereign stress.

### Threats

- Significant long-term pressure from aging population: pension spending projected to rise from 6% to 10.5% of GDP by 2070.
- Healthcare spending projected to surge from 5.1% to 7.2% of GDP by 2070.
- Total age-related expenditure projected to rise from 17% to 26% of GDP by 2070 (second largest increase in EU).
- Aging-related spending pressures (health, pensions).
- Climate transition costs.
- Need for long-term fiscal framework to address future spending needs.

## Contingent Liabilities

### Strengths

- No immediate large-scale banking sector recapitalization needs due to strong bank capitalization.
- Strong bank capital buffers mitigate systemic risk.
- Administrative bank insolvency regime under development.

### Threats

- Potential costs associated with state-owned enterprise (Enemalta) subsidies and energy sector interventions.
- Risks from unmet healthcare and long-term care needs.
- Risk of public funds mismanagement in local government and public procurement.
- Real estate market downturn potential impact on banks.
- Government guarantees.

## Price Stability

### Strengths

- Low and stable inflation (2.4% in 2024, projected 2.2% in 2025), aligned with ECB targets.
- Headline inflation fallen to ~2%.
- Core inflation stable.

### Threats

- Inflationary pressures in services sector (transport, tourism).
- Risk of higher wage growth leading to inflation.
