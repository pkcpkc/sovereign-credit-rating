# Malta

## Political and Geopolitical Risk

### Strengths
- Stable political environment with a comfortable parliamentary majority
- Strong alignment with European Union policies and institutions
- Low exposure to direct geopolitical conflict or regional instability
- High public perception of judicial independence among citizens and businesses

### Threats
- Vulnerability to spillovers from Russia’s war in Ukraine
- Geoeconomic fragmentation and supply chain disruptions
- Risks from Israel-Gaza conflict affecting regional stability
- Potential cyberattacks and misuse of artificial intelligence

## Institutional and Governance Strength

### Strengths
- High perceived judicial independence among the general public (69%) and companies (63%)
- Completion of the transfer of non-summary case prosecutions to the [[Attorney General]]
- Strong digital public services infrastructure and high adoption of e-government
- Establishment of independent bodies such as the [[Malta Fiscal Advisory Council]] and [[Science Malta]] (Xjenza Malta)
- Ongoing judicial reforms, including improvements to the chief justice appointment process
- Strengthened Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) framework

### Threats
- Lengthy judicial proceedings (e.g., 454 days for civil cases), impacting business confidence
- Weaknesses in the independence of specialized tribunals (e.g., Immigration Appeals Board)
- Inadequate integrity measures and codes of conduct for high-level politicians
- Lack of a robust track record of final judgments in high-level corruption cases
- Limited scope for citizen-prompted magisterial inquiries following recent legislative reforms
- Administrative burdens and barriers to trade/investment
- Emerging trade-based money laundering risks

## Policy Predictability and Reform Continuity

### Strengths
- Commitment to implementing the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)
- Medium-term fiscal-structural planning aligned with EU frameworks
- Launch of 'Malta Vision 2050' for long-term strategic direction
- Broad alignment with past International Monetary Fund staff recommendations

### Threats
- Inconsistent implementation of structural reforms
- Slow progress on climate and energy transition targets
- Pending legislative reforms for media regulator and civil society oversight
- Risk of revenue cession to other jurisdictions due to delayed Pillar II implementation
- Disagreements over the gradual exit from energy price reforms

## Debt Payment Culture

### Strengths
- Strong history of honoring sovereign obligations and fiscal compliance
- Adherence to European Union fiscal framework rules
- Low risk of default or willingness-to-pay issues

### Threats
- None identified in current assessments

## Economic Strength and Tax Base

### Strengths
- High GDP per capita with convergence toward the European Union average
- Robust real GDP growth (6.0% in 2024) driven by exports and domestic demand
- High employment rate and high female labor force participation
- Strong service sector performance, including tourism, financial services, iGaming, and ICT
- High tax-to-GDP ratio trends increasing, supported by improved revenue administration

### Threats
- Narrow tax base with heavy reliance on corporate income taxation
- Low labor taxation share relative to GDP
- Significant VAT compliance gap
- Strain on public services and infrastructure from rapid population density growth
- Low investment in Research and Development (0.64% of GDP, second lowest in the EU)

## Growth Prospects

### Strengths
- Growth rates remain among the highest in Europe
- Strong domestic demand and export performance in services
- Productivity-driven growth strategy via innovation, digitalization, and STEM enrollment
- Convergence towards European Union average productivity levels

### Threats
- Severe skills shortages and mismatches in key sectors (ICT, construction, healthcare)
- Structural constraints from a labor-intensive growth model
- Maturing gaming sector dynamics
- Infrastructure strain from tourism and migration inflows

## Economic Diversity

### Strengths
- Diversified service sector exports (professional services, science, tech, insurance)
- Strong financial and insurance services industries
- Resilient domestic demand

### Threats
- High dependency on tourism and real estate sectors
- Vulnerability to external shocks in goods trade
- Climate-sensitive output and high reliance on fossil fuels for energy

## External Position and Liquidity

### Strengths
- Positive net international investment position (NIIP); net creditor status to the rest of the world
- Substantially stronger external position than fundamentals imply, driven by robust service exports
- Strong foreign exchange reserves relative to liabilities

### Threats
- High volatility in net international investment position
- Dependence on foreign direct investment flows
- Expected decline in current account surplus over the medium term

## Currency and Exchange Rate Flexibility

### Strengths
- Eurozone membership providing exchange rate stability and integration with the European monetary framework
- Use of the euro as a reserve currency reduces FX mismatch risk

### Threats
- Limited national control over monetary policy as an EU member state

## Financial Sector Health

### Strengths
- Highly capitalized and sound banking sector with strong liquidity coverage ratios
- Low non-performing loan (NPL) ratios
- Strong insurance sector solvency

### Threats
- Substantial exposure of banks to real estate (residential, commercial, and construction)
- High banking sector concentration
- Cybersecurity risks and data gaps in commercial real estate markets
- Large size of non-bank financial intermediaries with limited domestic economic linkages

## Fiscal Performance and Flexibility

### Strengths
- Fiscal consolidation underway, with general government deficit projected to decrease (e.g., to 2.8% of GDP in 2026)
- Increasing tax revenues as a share of GDP
- Strong revenue administration reform progress

### Threats
- High net expenditure growth and deviation from recommended net expenditure paths
- Large energy subsidies consuming a significant portion of the fiscal deficit (approx. 20%)
- Need for substantial infrastructure investment to support population growth

## Debt Burden

### Strengths
- Low government debt-to-GDP ratio, consistently below 50% (well under the 60% EU debt ceiling)
- Projected stabilization of the debt ratio
- Low risk of sovereign stress and favorable debt structure

### Threats
- Public debt projected to rise marginally to 50% of GDP, though remaining manageable

## Fiscal Sustainability

### Strengths
- Current low public pension expenditure relative to GDP
- Reformed fiscal governance framework and medium-term structural plans

### Threats
- Rising age-related expenditure pressures (pensions, healthcare, long-term care)
- High projected increase in pension spending by 2070
- Inflationary pressures from wage growth in tight labor markets

## Contingent Liabilities

### Strengths
- Limited explicit contingent liabilities identified in baseline scenarios

### Threats
- Potential restructuring costs associated with Air Malta
- Potential banking sector support costs if real estate exposures deteriorate

## Monetary Policy and Price Stability

### Strengths
- Inflation has fallen to around 2%, aligning with Euro Area averages
- Stable monetary policy framework via the European Central Bank

### Threats
- Inflationary pressures in the service sector due to strong tourism demand
- Tight labor markets driving wage growth and potential inflationary persistence
