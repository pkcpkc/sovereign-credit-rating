# Hungary

## Geopolitical and Political Stability

### Strengths

- High public trust in certain institutions compared to some regional peers.
- Stable overall government control.
- Stable government has demonstrated ability to implement significant fiscal adjustment recently.

### Threats

- Dependence on Russian fossil fuels and nuclear energy creates supply security risks amidst regional conflict and sanctions.
- Persistent use of "state of danger" emergency powers limits parliamentary oversight and increases political unpredictability.
- Concerns over democratic backsliding and rule of law issues.
- Extensive and prolonged use of government emergency powers undermines legal certainty.
- Deteriorating environment for civil society organizations and civic space.
- Intensified regulatory pressure on foreign companies and strategic sectors.
- Political fragmentation and populist pressure affecting institutional stability.
- Deepening geoeconomic fragmentation and escalation in trade measures pose significant downside risks.
- Intensification of regional conflicts increases energy security risks and inflationary pressure.
- National elections in early 2026 create political economy challenges for reform implementation.
- High domestic and external uncertainty weigh on investment and growth outlook.

## Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength

### Strengths

- Strong nominal wage growth.
- Implementation of digital public services is progressing.
- High overall efficiency of the judiciary in resolving cases.
- Ongoing digitalization of the justice system.
- Central Bank maintains independent monetary policy stance focused on price stability.
- IMF and authorities engage in constructive dialogue on policy reforms.

### Threats

- Weak anti-corruption framework; high perceived corruption among businesses.
- Limited independence and capacity of the [[Hungarian Fiscal Council]].
- Frequent regulatory changes and use of emergency decrees reduce policy predictability.
- Opaque public fund management structures lacking effective oversight.
- Weak institutional checks and balances, including limited independence of the Constitutional Court and oversight bodies.
- Absence of procedural safeguards and effective oversight in secret surveillance measures.
- Frequent legislative changes weakening legal certainty.
- Limited accountability of the prosecution service and potential political influence.
- Governance reforms lagging, resulting in suspension of €19 billion in EU funds due to rule of law concerns.
- Regulatory measures such as price, interest, and margin caps distort market signals and add uncertainty.
- Heavy state presence in key sectors impedes competition and productivity.

## Policymaking Stability

### Strengths

- Medium-term fiscal-structural plan submitted and endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]].
- Authorities committed to medium-term fiscal structural plan (MTFSP) and deficit reduction targets.

### Threats

- High regulatory volatility and frequent legislative amendments hinder long-term business planning.
- Discretionary state intervention in business transactions and public procurement.
- Budgets adopted early in the year with frequent revisions, reducing reliability.
- Law-making process bypassing public consultation for key legislative drafts.
- Introduction of substantive amendments at late stages of parliamentary procedure.
- Extensive use of government decrees to override Acts of Parliament.
- Risk of expansionary fiscal measures triggered by upcoming elections in 2026.
- Frequent regulatory changes create an unpredictable business environment.
- Delay in needed fiscal adjustment could heighten market concerns about debt sustainability.

## Accountability

### Strengths

- Justice system performs efficiently in terms of case disposition time.
- Existence of a National Anti-Corruption Strategy and Action Plan.
- Implementation of integrity tests and lifestyle checks.
- Fiscal Council exists to monitor fiscal risks, though oversight capacity varies.

### Threats

- Weak checks and balances due to emergency powers.
- Lack of transparency in public procurement and beneficial ownership of private equity funds.
- Limited judicial independence concerns monitored by the Commission.
- High perception of corruption in the public sector (CPI score 41/100).
- Limited track record of convictions in high-level corruption cases.
- Obstacles faced by the [[Integrity Authority]] in fulfilling oversight tasks.
- Lack of transparency in political party financing and electoral campaigns.
- Broad governance, rule of law, and corruption concerns persist.
- Limited transparency in off-budget financing vehicles and state-owned enterprises (SOEs).

## Debt Payment Culture

### Strengths

- No history of default; strong commitment to honoring obligations.
- Banks incentivized to hold government debt.
- Sovereign wealth assets and reserves provide buffer against immediate liquidity stress.
- No history of sovereign default; strong record of honoring obligations.

### Threats

- High implicit interest rates on government debt.
- High risk premia and widening bond spreads indicate market concerns regarding debt affordability.

## Economic Strength and Tax Base

### Strengths

- High employment rate (81.1% in 2024).
- Low corporate income tax rate (9%) attracts FDI.
- Developed transport infrastructure.
- Broad taxable income base implied by functioning public finance system.
- High GDP per capita relative to regional peers, though productivity lags.
- Strong labor market participation and favorable wage dynamics support consumption.

### Threats

- Low labour productivity, stagnant for 15 years.
- High energy import dependence.
- Narrow tax base with heavy reliance on consumption taxes.
- Regulatory pressure leading to de-investment by foreign companies.
- Discriminatory taxes and administrative fines targeting specific foreign investors.
- Output stagnation over the past three years contrasts with CEE peers.
- Heavy reliance on consumption-based taxes insulates revenues less from investment weakness.

## Growth Prospects

### Strengths

- Growth projected to pick up to 2.5% in 2026 driven by EV battery manufacturing capacities.
- High investment levels in manufacturing (though dropping from peak).
- Modest recovery expected in 2025-2026 driven by consumption and potential investment pickup.
- Operationalization of past investments in EV and battery production may boost medium-term growth.

### Threats

- Subdued GDP growth (0.5% in 2024, forecast 0.8% in 2025).
- Weak external demand and business sentiment.
- Skills shortages hampering high-value investment.
- High inflation eroding real wages and competitiveness.
- Legal uncertainty affecting business operations and investment protection.
- Restrictions on export and nationalization of assets in strategic sectors.
- Productivity has fallen behind peers amid heavy state presence and regulatory barriers.
- Investment contribution has been the most negative in the region.
- Downside risks include trade tensions, particularly exposure to US tariffs and EU supply chains.

## Economic Diversity

### Strengths

- Emerging leadership in electric vehicle battery manufacturing.
- Diversified export base within manufacturing.
- Diversified export sectors including pharmaceuticals, automotive, and electronics.

### Threats

- Heavy reliance on automotive industry exports (vulnerable to global shifts and tariffs).
- Dependence on foreign direct investment (FDI) for innovation.
- Low participation in high-value added services.
- High integration into global value chains centered in Germany exposes economy to German fiscal and industrial shocks.
- Vulnerability to sector-specific shocks in automotive and pharmaceuticals.

## External Liquidity

### Strengths

- Significant foreign exchange reserves (22.4% of GDP).
- Trade balance improved in 2024.
- Adequate foreign exchange reserves (approx. 110-112% of IMF ARA metric).
- Current account surplus provides some external buffer.

### Threats

- High energy import bills impact the trade balance.
- Net external liabilities remain high.
- Potential loss of EU funding access if rule of law reforms are not completed.
- High gross financing needs (GFN) averaging 17% of GDP through 2030.

## Currency Status

### Strengths

- Monetary autonomy.
- Flexible exchange rate regime helps absorb external shocks.

### Threats

- Non-euro member; vulnerable to exchange rate fluctuations.
- Forint depreciation pressure amid global risk sentiment shifts.
- Currency is not a major international reserve currency, limiting monetary flexibility.

## External Asset/Liability Position

### Strengths

- Declining net external liabilities trend in recent years.
- Liabilities are predominantly FDI, which carries lower rollover risk compared to portfolio debt.

### Threats

- Negative Net International Investment Position (NIIP) at -31.8% of GDP.
- High foreign direct investment stock relative to GDP.
- Net International Investment Position (NIIP) remains negative (-36.3% of GDP in 2024).
- Gross external debt is elevated, though declining from peak levels.
- Rapid growth in FX debt (over 30% of total debt) heightens exchange rate risk.

## Financial Sector Health

### Strengths

- Banking sector is well-capitalized (CET1 ratio 18.8%) and highly profitable.
- Low non-performing loan (NPL) ratios.
- Strong liquidity coverage ratios.
- Banking sector is well-capitalized (CAR 20.5%), liquid, and profitable.
- Non-performing loan (NPL) ratios remain historically low.

### Threats

- High concentration of government bonds in bank assets creates systemic risk.
- Subsidized lending schemes distort credit allocation.
- Underdeveloped capital markets.
- Banks' growing exposure to sovereign debt amplifies macro-financial risks (sovereign-bank nexus).
- Corporate sector risks amid heightened external uncertainty and elevated interest rates.
- Significant rise in banks' aggregate short FX positions.
- Buoyant housing prices and potential overvaluation in commercial real estate.

## Fiscal Performance and Flexibility

### Strengths

- Deficit decreased from 6.7% in 2023 to 4.9% in 2024.
- Access to EU funds (RRF and Cohesion Policy).
- Recent fiscal adjustments have reduced the headline deficit significantly.
- Primary balance has improved, nearing surplus in recent years.

### Threats

- High government deficit (4.9% of GDP in 2024).
- High net expenditure growth projected for 2025 (6.1%).
- Limited fiscal policy flexibility due to rigid spending commitments and tax incentives.
- Current announced policies fall short of authorities' budget targets and debt sustainability needs.
- High administrative expenditure and energy subsidies weigh on fiscal balance.
- Fiscal deficit projected to remain around 4.5% of GDP through medium term under current policies.

## Debt Burden

### Strengths

- Debt projected to decrease gradually in the medium term.
- Low short-term debt maturity pressure.
- Debt is predominantly denominated in local currency.
- Long average maturity of sovereign debt mitigates immediate rollover risk.

### Threats

- Public debt rose to 73.5% of GDP in 2024.
- High debt-servicing costs (implicit interest rate close to 6%).
- High gross financing needs.
- Public debt-to-GDP ratio is elevated (73.5% in 2024) and projected to rise to ~79% by 2030 under baseline.
- High financing costs and interest burden due to risk premia.
- Large stock of redeemable inflation-linked retail debt poses refinancing risks.

## Fiscal Sustainability

### Strengths

- Low short-term sustainability risks.
- IMF assesses risk of sovereign stress as 'moderate', indicating debt is currently manageable with policy action.

### Threats

- High medium-term and medium-long-term sustainability risks per Commission framework.
- Aging population increasing pension spending pressures (projected to rise 4.3 pps by 2070).
- Persistent primary deficits.
- Long-term pressures from aging population on pension and healthcare spending (estimated +3.5% GDP by 2050).
- Persistent deficits and rising debt ratios without further adjustment threaten long-term solvency.
- Contingent liabilities from SOEs and guarantees are significant (14% of GDP in guarantees).

## Contingent Liabilities

### Strengths

- Banking sector resilience reduces immediate need for public recapitalization.
- Authorities have begun monitoring SOE performance, though transparency remains limited.

### Threats

- Off-budget public interest trusts and funds operate with limited oversight.
- Potential risks from state-owned enterprises and public-private partnerships.
- Risks related to public interest trusts and state-owned enterprises.
- Potential liabilities from complex investment structures involving national assets.
- Rapid growth of SOE liabilities and related government guarantees.
- Off-balance sheet financing vehicles (foundations, SPVs) pose implicit fiscal risks.
- Bank recapitalization risk is low currently but sovereign-bank linkages remain a vulnerability.

## Price Stability

### Strengths

- Inflation declined significantly from 17.0% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2024.
- Inflation has decelerated from multi-decade highs.
- Central bank maintains restrictive stance to anchor expectations.

### Threats

- Inflation picked up in Q1 2025 due to underlying price pressures and rising food prices.
- High construction prices.
- Inflation remains well above the 3% target (4.4-4.6% in mid-2025).
- Core services inflation remains elevated at 7.6% due to wage pressures.
- Price and margin caps may defer rather than eliminate inflationary pressures.

## Central Bank Independence

### Strengths

- Central bank maintains operational autonomy in many technical aspects.
- MNB maintains data-dependent, tight monetary stance consistent with staff recommendations.
- Legal framework supports operational independence, though political pressure exists.

### Threats

- Subsidized loan schemes and administratively controlled mortgage rates limit monetary policy effectiveness.
- Political pressure on financial market pricing.
- Conflicts of interest identified in audits of [[Central Bank of Hungary (MNB)]] trusts.
- Potential political influence over central bank governance.
- Political interference via regulatory caps (prices, fees) undermines monetary policy transmission.
- MNB's secondary goal of environmental sustainability and non-core functions distract from price stability mandate.

## Exchange Rate Regime

### Strengths

- Flexible exchange rate allows for adjustment.
- Floating exchange rate allows for automatic adjustment to external shocks.

### Threats

- Exchange rate volatility risks due to inflation differentials with the Eurozone.
- Forint volatility linked to global risk sentiment and fiscal uncertainty.
- FX swap facility adjustments indicate need for central bank intervention to stabilize rates.
