# Belgium

## Geopolitical and Political Stability

### Strengths

- Stable democratic institutions with high perceived quality of government.
- Strong international integration and peaceful regional relations.
- High level of perceived judicial independence among the general public (64%) and companies (72%).
- New Government exempted the judiciary from budget cuts, showing commitment to institutional stability.
- Resilient economy amidst multiple shocks.

### Threats

- Political fragmentation due to federal structure and complex coordination between federal and regional governments.
- Slow legislative processes hampering rapid policy implementation.
- Political fragmentation and delays in forming government, evidenced by the caretaker Government period between June 2024 and February 2025.
- Public criticism of the judiciary and lawyers by politicians, potentially undermining the role of independent institutions.
- Deepening geoeconomic and trade fragmentation.
- Intensification of regional conflicts affecting energy, trade, and financial spillovers.

## Policy Credibility and Institutional Strength

### Strengths

- Submission and positive assessment of a medium-term fiscal-structural plan aligned with new EU governance rules.
- Strong independent macroeconomic forecasting capacity via the [[Federal Planning Bureau]].
- Government intends to transfer budgetary management powers for justice administration from executive to judiciary.
- Creation of new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) and specialised prosecution service for financial crime.
- Decriminalisation of defamation and introduction of harsher penalties for crimes against journalists.
- Some progress on access to official documents and whistleblower protection in Parliament.
- Commitment to a 7-year fiscal adjustment under the new EU economic governance framework.
- Strong institutional capacity and high-quality debt management.

### Threats

- Weak budgetary coordination between federal and federated entities, preventing effective monitoring of compliance.
- High Council of Finance lacks sufficient autonomy, staff, and resources to effectively monitor fiscal rules.
- Absence of a fully developed multiannual fiscal planning framework at the national level.
- Serious shortcomings in the digitalisation strategy of the justice department, including lack of overarching approach and fraud risks.
- Shortcomings in monitoring and enforcement of integrity policies for Ministers and MPs.
- Lack of adequate verification and transparency in the system of asset and interest declarations.
- Limited progress on lobbying legislative reform and revolving door rules.
- Insufficient action taken by the Government to address structural issues identified by the European Court of Human Rights (*Camara v. Belgium*).
- Budget cuts proposed for the interfederal equality body (Unia), raising concerns about human rights protection.
- High and rising public debt increasing vulnerability to market sentiment changes.
- Complex fiscal federalism challenging accountability across federal and regional entities.

## Policymaking Stability

### Strengths

- Coalition agreement includes clear commitments to tax reform, pension reform, and labor market activation.
- Government agreement outlines intentions for judicial reform and integrity improvements.
- High Council for Justice published reports to address backlogs in specific courts.
- New government formed in February 2025 with a clear agenda for structural reforms and fiscal consolidation.

### Threats

- Discrepancies between government projections and Commission forecasts on net expenditure.
- Delays in implementing recovery and resilience plan reforms.
- New Government has not yet taken a decision on draft lobbying rules.
- Dispersed powers concerning integrity policy across various bodies.
- Disparate integrity policies across 181 local police zones.
- Protracted government formation periods creating uncertainty.
- Automatic wage indexation driving structural deficits despite policy intentions.

## Accountability

### Strengths

- High level of transparency in public institutions compared to EU average.
- Effective use of digital public services enhances administrative accountability.
- Joint memorandum by highest courts requiring Government to respect court judgments.
- Publication of all open positions for magistrates and court staff.
- Increased powers for media regulators under Digital Services Act.
- Plans to revitalize the fiscal council and enforce binding burden-sharing rules.

### Threats

- Loose lobbying regulations and lack of a compulsory public register for government members.
- Justice system efficiency challenged by structural resource deficiencies.
- Allegations of information leaks and undue political influence in sensitive corruption investigations.
- Lack of monitoring mechanism for the Code of Conduct for federal public office holders.
- Non-binding nature of appeal bodies for access to administrative documents.
- Obstacles to the right to demonstrate, with proposals for judicial demonstration bans.
- Weak coordination among federal and regional governments regarding climate and fiscal targets.
- Limited effectiveness of the High Council of Finance due to lack of hierarchy among entities.

## Debt Payment Culture

### Strengths

- History of timely debt servicing and strong investor confidence in Belgian sovereign bonds.
- Strong commitment to honoring obligations.
- Low default risk with debt predominantly in local currency and long maturity.
- Strong record of timely debt repayment and low sovereign spreads.

### Threats

- Rising public debt trajectory (projected 126.4% of GDP by 2035) limits fiscal flexibility.

## Economic Strength and Tax Base

### Strengths

- High GDP per capita and broad revenue base (tax revenue ~42.5% of GDP).
- Strong position in pharmaceuticals, chemicals, and financial services.
- 60% of companies express confidence in the effectiveness of investment protection by law and courts.
- Broad taxable income base.
- Innovative economy with high R&D spending.

### Threats

- High tax burden on labor (one of the highest in EU) acts as a drag on formal economic activity.
- Low business dynamism and presence of 'zombie firms' trap resources.
- Sluggish labor productivity growth.
- Declining labor-cost competitiveness.

## Growth Prospects

### Strengths

- Robust R&D intensity (3.3% of GDP) and world-class research institutions.
- Resilient private consumption in 2024.
- Potential for higher growth through structural reforms in labor and product markets.

### Threats

- Weak labour productivity growth over the past decade.
- Low innovation diffusion from R&D to broader economy.
- Demographic aging and low activity rates constrain potential output.
- Growth slowing to 1.0% in 2024, projected to return to potential (1.3%) only by 2027.
- Total factor productivity growth lagging peers.
- Headwinds from disinflation and fiscal consolidation.

## Economic Diversity

### Strengths

- Diversified export base including chemicals, metals, pharmaceuticals, and services.
- Diversified industrial base with strong presence in chemicals, pharmaceuticals, and transport equipment.

### Threats

- Dependence on energy-intensive industries vulnerable to price shocks.
- High exposure to global value chains and trade fragmentation risks.

## External Liquidity

### Strengths

- Strong access to international capital markets.
- Belgian banks are well-capitalized with low reliance on ECB funding.
- Net international investment position remains significantly positive (57.3% of GDP).
- Large positive net portfolio investment positions.

### Threats

- High import dependency for fossil fuels and critical raw materials.
- Current account deficit, though narrowing.

## Currency Status

### Strengths

- Access to Eurozone deep and liquid capital markets provides funding stability.
- Eurozone membership provides access to deep local-currency markets and monetary stability.

### Threats

- Loss of monetary policy autonomy as a Eurozone member limits response to asymmetric shocks.

## External Asset/Liability Position

### Strengths

- Net international investment position (NIIP) is positive (~56% of GDP), indicating net creditor status.
- Large net external asset position mitigates external vulnerabilities.

### Threats

- High external liabilities relative to GDP.
- External position weaker than implied by medium-term fundamentals.

## Financial Sector Health

### Strengths

- Banking sector is sound, well-capitalized (CAR 19.1%), and profitable.
- Low non-performing loan ratios (1.7%).
- Well-capitalized banking sector with robust liquidity buffers.
- Low non-performing loans (2.1%).
- Moderate overall systemic risks.

### Threats

- Banks face margin pressure from high competition and rising interest rates.
- Rising corporate bankruptcies.
- Increased risks in the commercial real estate sector.
- Household indebtedness remains elevated (58% of GDP).

## Fiscal Performance and Flexibility

### Strengths

- Stable revenue collection at around 50% of GDP.
- Plan to rationalize current spending and increase public investment efficiency.
- Intended tax reforms to shift burden from labor to capital.

### Threats

- Deteriorating public finances with deficit rising to 4.5% (2024) and projected 5.4% (2025).
- Rigid expenditure structure dominated by pension and long-term care costs.
- Limited fiscal space for new investments due to high debt and spending pressure.
- Budgetary pressures on public service media, with RTBF facing a projected EUR 78 million shortfall.
- Proposed 25% budget cut to the Interfederal Centre for Equal Opportunities (Unia).
- General government deficit widened to 4.4% of GDP in 2024.
- Structural fiscal deficits increased due to pandemic and energy crisis support.
- High labor tax wedge (52.7%).

## Debt Burden

### Strengths

- Long average maturity of government debt provides short-term refinancing cushion.
- Long average residual maturity of debt (11.1 years).
- Significant share of debt held by domestic investors.

### Threats

- High public debt-to-GDP ratio (104.7% in 2024), projected to rise to 107.1% in 2025.
- Rising interest payments and ageing-related costs drive primary deficits.
- High public debt at 104.1% of GDP in 2024.
- Debt projected to rise to 123% of GDP by 2030 under unchanged policies.
- Rising interest costs as low-interest debt matures.

## Fiscal Sustainability

### Strengths

- Planned pension reforms (bonus-malus system, reduced assimilated periods) aim to improve long-term sustainability.
- Potential for debt reduction to ~78% of GDP under the recommended 7-year adjustment path.

### Threats

- Ageing report projects pension spending to increase by 3.5% of GDP by 2070.
- Long-term care spending projected to increase by 1.7% of GDP by 2070.
- Aging population costs projected to increase fiscal deficit by 4.2 ppt of GDP by 2070.
- Pressures from green transition and defense spending.

## Contingent Liabilities

### Strengths

- Strong banking sector reduces immediate risk of sovereign-bank bailouts.
- Low explicit off-budget risks noted in general assessment.

### Threats

- Risks from state-owned enterprises and complex federal structure create opaque off-budget obligations.
- Potential costs related to long-term care and infrastructure investment gaps.
- Potential liabilities from state-owned enterprises and regional governments.

## Price Stability

### Strengths

- Inflation expected to ease to 2.8% in 2025 as industrial goods and energy prices stabilize.
- Headline inflation declining from peaks; expectations anchored.

### Threats

- Headline inflation remained high in 2024 (4.3%), highest in Euro area, driven by phasing out of energy measures.
- Core inflation remains persistent and sticky, supported by indexed wages and rents.
- Headline inflation at 4.4% in Jan 2025.

## Central Bank Independence

### Strengths

- [[ECB]] maintains price stability mandate; [[National Bank of Belgium]] (NBB) effectively implements macroprudential policy.
- ECB sets monetary policy with high credibility.
- National Bank of Belgium has increasing autonomy in macroprudential oversight.

### Threats

- Limited direct relevance as Belgium is part of the [[ECB]] system.

## Exchange Rate Regime

### Strengths

- Stable currency framework provided by the Euro.
- Flexible exchange rate mechanism within the Eurozone.

### Threats

- Competitiveness losses risk if real effective exchange rate remains high due to index-linked wages.
- Real effective exchange rate overvaluation estimated at 4.9-17.4%.
