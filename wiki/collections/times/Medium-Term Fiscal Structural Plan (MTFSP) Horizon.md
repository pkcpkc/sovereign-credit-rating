# Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) Horizon

## Event Details

The **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)** represents Poland's strategic framework for fiscal consolidation, adopted in response to widening budget deficits and the initiation of the **EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)** in 2024. As detailed in the **IMF's 2024 Article IV Consultation** (released January 2025), the plan aims to reduce the general government deficit from 5.9% of GDP in 2024 to approximately 2.9% of GDP by 2028. This trajectory is designed to stabilize public debt, which is projected to rise from ~50% of GDP in 2023 to 65% by 2029 under baseline scenarios, and to align with new EU fiscal governance standards.

Key components and IMF recommendations regarding the MTFSP horizon (2025–2028) include:

- **Fiscal Consolidation Targets**: The authorities have committed to a structural adjustment aimed at bringing the deficit below the 3% of GDP threshold by 2028. The IMF supports this direction but recommends **frontloading consolidation** with a modest additional adjustment of ~0.5% of GDP in 2025 to rebuild fiscal buffers, support disinflation, and create space for earlier monetary easing.
- **Medium-Term Adjustment**: Beyond identified measures, the IMF advises an additional structural adjustment of ~1.5% of GDP over the medium term to stabilize debt around 60% of GDP while preserving public investment needs.
- **Revenue and Expenditure Measures**: Consolidation strategies involve addressing permanent increases in public sector wages and social benefits that drove the 2024 deficit. Long-term sustainability hinges on structural reforms, including potential changes to VAT, property tax, and pension systems (e.g., equalizing retirement ages) to mitigate the drag from population aging.
- **Institutional Framework**: The MTFSP operates within an amended stabilizing expenditure rule aligned with EU frameworks. A key accountability measure is the planned establishment of an **Independent Fiscal Council** with operational start projected for 2026, aimed at enhancing transparency and oversight, particularly regarding extrabudgetary funds managed by the **Polish Development Bank (BGK)**.
- **Macroeconomic Context**: The consolidation effort occurs amidst a cyclical economic recovery (GDP growth projected at 3.5% in 2025) driven by **Next Generation EU (NGEU)** fund absorption and strong domestic demand. However, fiscal credibility remains challenged by the need to balance defense spending, social commitments, and the imperative to reduce inflation (sticky at ~5% y/y in late 2024) without derailing growth.

The successful implementation of the MTFSP is critical for maintaining Poland’s investment-grade sovereign rating, ensuring access to international capital markets, and facilitating a credible transition to a low-carbon economy.
