# Formation of New Federal Government

## Event Details

Following the **June 2024 Elections**, Belgium experienced protracted coalition negotiations that significantly stalled policymaking and legislative progress. The political fragmentation resulting from these extended talks highlighted the challenges of **Complex fiscal federalism** and the lack of hierarchy among government entities, posing a threat to **Policy predictability and reform continuity**.

A new federal government was successfully formed in **February 2025**, marking a critical turning point in the country's political landscape. This formation initiated a renewed policy agenda aimed at addressing structural and fiscal challenges, including a commitment to a seven-year fiscal adjustment under the **EU Economic Governance Framework**. The new administration, led by Prime Minister **Alexander De Croo** and including Deputy Prime Ministers **Jan Jambon** and **Simon Van Peteghem**, moved to restart stalled reforms. The government's agenda focuses on fiscal consolidation, structural productivity improvements, and labor market reforms, aiming to reduce the general government deficit below 3 percent of GDP and place public debt on a sustainable downward path.
