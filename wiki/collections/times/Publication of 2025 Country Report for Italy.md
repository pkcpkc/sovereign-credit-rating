# Publication of 2025 Country Report for Italy

## Event Details

The economic trajectory of Italy over the 2024-2026 period is defined by a transition toward fiscal stabilization and structural reform under the [[Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)]]. 

**2024: Stabilization and Fiscal Correction**
At the start of 2024, Italy's real GDP growth stabilized at 0.7%. Throughout the year, the country made significant strides in fiscal management, with the government deficit dropping sharply to 3.4% of GDP by year-end. This period also marked a milestone in primary balance, achieving the first surplus since 2019 (0.4% of GDP).

**Early 2025: Policy Endorsement**
On January 21, 2025, the Council adopted a Recommendation endorsing Italy's specific plans for economic and social reform. This paved the way for the comprehensive analysis released later in the year.

**June 2025: The 2025 Country Report**
On June 4, 2025, the European Commission published the **2025 Country Report for Italy**. The report highlights a dual reality: while RRP investments have bolstered growth, Italy faces systemic hurdles including:
*   **Debt Dynamics:** A projected rise in the debt-to-GDP ratio to 138.2% by 2026 due to high interest costs and past tax credits.
*   **Productivity & Innovation:** A significant R&D expenditure gap (1.31% of GDP vs. 2.24% EU average) and a fragmented venture capital market.
*   **Labour & Social Issues:** Record high employment rates tempered by a severe macroeconomic skills mismatch and gender participation gaps in the South.
*   **Sustainability:** Leadership in solar and wind manufacturing contrasted with a slow adoption of electric vehicles and high exposure to hydrogeological risks.

**2026 and Beyond: Reform Completion**
The roadmap concludes with a 2026 target for the completion of major RRP reforms and investments, aimed at narrowing the "Mezzogiorno Divide" and fostering a more sustainable, high-tech industrial strategy.
