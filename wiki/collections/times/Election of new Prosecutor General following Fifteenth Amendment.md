# Election of new Prosecutor General following Fifteenth Amendment

## Event Details

In June 2025, the **Prosecutor General** was elected following the adoption of the Fifteenth Amendment to Hungary's Fundamental Law. This appointment process occurred within a context where the European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report identified a systemic deterioration in the rule of law framework. The Fifteenth Amendment specifically removed the statutory requirement that the **Prosecutor General** must be selected from among existing prosecutors, thereby lowering eligibility criteria and potentially increasing the risk of political influence over the office.

The election took place while the hierarchical structure of the Prosecution Service remained intact, raising concerns regarding potential political pressure on individual cases. This event was part of a broader series of judicial reforms in 2025, including the implementation of new judicial appointment criteria and retirement age extensions in March, and legislative changes strengthening the **Kúria** (Supreme Court) in June. The European Commission highlighted these developments as contributing to the erosion of **Judicial Independence**, noting that the removal of previous safeguards for the Prosecutor General's selection contrasts with ongoing concerns about the politicization of key judicial and prosecutorial roles in Hungary.
