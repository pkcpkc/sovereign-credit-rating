# Belgium submits Annual Progress Report; Data cut-off for Country Reports

## Event Details

On April 30, 2025, Belgium submitted its Annual Progress Report to the European Commission, marking the critical data cut-off date for the compilation of the **2025 Country Report - Belgium**. This submission occurred within the framework of the reformed EU Economic Governance Framework (**Regulation (EU) 2024/1263**) and coincided with Belgium’s formal request to activate the **National Escape Clause** to accommodate increased defence spending.

### Key Context and Economic Indicators (as of April 30, 2025 Cut-off)
The data captured at this cut-off period reflects a significant deterioration in Belgium's fiscal position and a slowdown in economic momentum:
*   **Fiscal Deterioration**: The general government deficit widened to **4.5% of GDP** in 2024 and was projected to rise further to **5.4% of GDP** in 2025. Public debt stood at **104.7% of GDP** in 2024, with projections indicating an unsustainable trajectory towards **126.4% by 2035** without structural reforms.
*   **Economic Growth**: GDP growth decelerated from **1% in 2024** (driven by resilient private consumption) to a projected **0.8% in 2025**, hampered by weakening exports (due to external shocks such as US tariffs) and cooling domestic investment.
*   **Inflation Pressure**: Headline inflation remained high at **4.3% in 2024** (the highest in the Euro area), driven by the phasing out of energy support measures, though expected to ease to 2.8% in 2025.
*   **Labour Market**: Despite a record employment rate of **72.3%**, the activity rate remained below the EU average at **77.2%**, constrained by high tax wedges, complex benefit systems, and demographic aging.

### Strategic Policy Actions
Simultaneously with the progress report, Belgium utilized the **National Escape Clause** mechanism to legally exceed net expenditure limits for **defence spending**, projected to rise to **1.7% of GDP** in 2025. This deviation was conditioned upon the submission of a comprehensive **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** for 2025–2029, which the Commission endorsed on May 21, 2025. The MTP commits to a seven-year fiscal adjustment path supported by structural reforms in pensions (introducing a bonus-malus system), labour market activation, and tax restructuring, aimed at addressing the severe long-term fiscal pressures driven by aging costs.

### Institutional and Governance Implications
The submission highlighted ongoing challenges within Belgium’s federal structure, including weak budgetary coordination between federal and regional entities and the limited resources of the **High Council of Finance**. However, the timely submission and the endorsement of the MTP demonstrated policy credibility, leveraging strong independent forecasting capacity from the **Federal Planning Bureau** and maintaining access to deep Eurozone capital markets. The event serves as the baseline for the European Commission’s subsequent publication of the final Country Report on June 4, 2025.
