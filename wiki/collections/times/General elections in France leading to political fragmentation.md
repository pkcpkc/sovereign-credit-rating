# General elections in France leading to political fragmentation

## Event Details

The **2024 General elections in France** resulted in a fragmented political landscape, marking a significant shift in the country's institutional dynamics. This political instability had profound macroeconomic and fiscal repercussions, which were highlighted in the **2025 Country Report - France** published by the European Commission.

### Key Consequences and Developments

*   **Legislative Gridlock and Budgetary Delays**: The lack of a clear political majority in Parliament following the elections led to unprecedented delays in the adoption of the national budget. This political instability complicated the timely implementation of fiscal decisions and reduced the overall fiscal ambitions of the government, creating heightened uncertainty for businesses and investors.
*   **Impact on Fiscal Trajectory**: The political fragmentation contributed to persistently high general government deficits, which reached **5.8% of GDP in 2024**. Public debt rose to **113% of GDP**, driven by sluggish tax revenues, high interest payments, and rigid expenditures. The Commission noted that political uncertainty hampered the efficiency of necessary structural reforms.
*   **EU Governance and Fiscal Adjustment**: In response to the deteriorating fiscal position, the European Commission and the Council of the European Union engaged in structured oversight:
    *   **January 21, 2025**: The Council endorsed France's medium-term fiscal-structural plan and issued a recommendation to end the excessive deficit situation.
    *   **October 31, 2024**: France submitted its medium-term fiscal-structural plan.
    *   **April 30, 2025**: A deadline extension was granted for the implementation of reforms underpinning fiscal adjustment, reflecting the challenges posed by the political environment.
*   **Macroeconomic Uncertainty**: The fragmented political landscape exacerbated economic slowdown, with real GDP growth forecast to drop from 1.2% in 2024 to **0.6% in 2025**. This slowdown was attributed partly to subdued investment and high uncertainty stemming from the post-election political climate.
*   **Institutional Responses**: Despite the political challenges, France maintained a commitment to the reformed EU Economic Governance Framework. The government pursued a seven-year fiscal adjustment period, emphasizing the need for spending reviews to ensure fiscal sustainability amidst the constraints imposed by the divided parliament.
