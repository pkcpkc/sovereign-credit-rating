# Extension of 'state of danger' until November 2025

## Event Details

In November 2025, the Hungarian government extended the 'state of danger' until November 2025. This extension allows the executive to override parliamentary acts and adopt emergency measures unrelated to the initial humanitarian crisis that triggered the state, such as price caps and value-added tax (VAT) exemptions.

The European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report criticizes this practice, noting that it undermines legal certainty and affects the single market. The report highlights that such extensive use of emergency powers is a key factor contributing to Hungary's political and geopolitical risks, including tensions with EU institutions and ongoing infringement procedures regarding rule of law breaches. While the upcoming Fifteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law is scheduled to require parliamentary authorization for any future extensions of the state of danger, the current extension remains in force without such legislative check, maintaining the government's ability to rule by decree.
