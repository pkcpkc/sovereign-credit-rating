# Implementation of new judicial appointment criteria and retirement age extensions

## Event Details

In March 2025, Hungary implemented new criteria for judicial appointments alongside extensions to the retirement age for judges. These measures were introduced following the adoption of the Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law in December 2024, which raised the minimum age for judges to 35 and extended the mandatory retirement age to 70.

The European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report identified these changes as contributing to the erosion of judicial independence. The report noted that while judicial salaries were increased, structural reforms failed to ensure independent appointment processes. Judicial associations argued that the new criteria compromised the independence of the judiciary by allowing for increased political influence over case allocation and appointments. The National Judicial Council continued to exercise powers acquired through prior reforms, but consultation processes were frequently deemed ineffective. Additionally, undue pressure on judges persisted, including public criticism from the President of the [[Kúria]] and smear campaigns against service courts.
