# Entry into force of emergency measures to save 2.0% of GDP (wage/pension freeze, tax reforms)

## Event Details

On December 1, 2024, Romania implemented a set of emergency fiscal measures designed to consolidate public finances and save an estimated 2.0% of GDP in 2025. These measures were a critical component of the government's response to deteriorating macroeconomic indicators, including a government deficit that had surged to 9.3% of GDP in 2024 and rapid public debt accumulation reaching 54.8% of GDP.

The emergency package included three primary pillars:
1.  **Public Sector Wage and Pension Freezes**: A halt to wage increases and pension adjustments in the public sector to curb net expenditure growth, which had reached 19.9% in 2024, significantly exceeding recommended maxima under the EU economic governance framework.
2.  **Tax Reforms and Exemption Removal**: The removal of various tax exemptions and reforms to the microenterprise tax regime to broaden the tax base and increase recurrent revenues.
3.  **Fiscal Consolidation Commitment**: These immediate measures were framed within the broader [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)]] submitted by Romania to the European Commission in October 2024. The MTP, endorsed by the European Council in January 2025, outlines a seven-year adjustment period (2025–2031) aimed at reducing the budget deficit to 7% of GDP in 2025 and achieving long-term fiscal sustainability.

The entry into force of these measures was driven by urgent pressure from the [[European Commission]] and the need to comply with the [[Council Recommendation to correct the excessive deficit in Romania]], issued in January 2025. The measures were intended to address structural weaknesses, including low tax revenue as a percentage of GDP (26.2% compared to the EU average of 39%) and a high VAT gap of 33.7%. By implementing these austerity measures alongside the MTP, the Romanian government aimed to restore investor confidence, mitigate the risk of further fiscal slippage, and stabilize the current account deficit, which had widened to 8.4% of GDP in 2024.
