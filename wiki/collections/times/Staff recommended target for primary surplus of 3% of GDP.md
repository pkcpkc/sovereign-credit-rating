# Staff recommended target for primary surplus of 3% of GDP

## Event Details

In the context of the IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy, concluded by the Executive Board on July 18, 2025, staff issued a recommendation for Italy to achieve a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027. This target was set against the backdrop of Italy's high public debt level, which stood at 135.3% of GDP at the end of 2024, and concerns regarding medium-to-long-term fiscal sustainability driven by adverse demographic trends and aging-related costs.

While Italy demonstrated economic resilience in 2024 with GDP growth of 0.7% and a return to a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP, the IMF staff argued that a more front-loaded fiscal adjustment was necessary to firmly place public debt on a sustainable downward trajectory. The recommended 3% primary surplus target for 2027 contrasts with the Italian authorities' proposal for a more gradual seven-year adjustment under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), which aimed to bring the overall deficit below 3% by 2026. The authorities disputed the need for additional immediate measures beyond their committed path, emphasizing structural reforms to boost growth. The IMF assessed sovereign debt risk as moderate overall but high in the medium and long term, reinforcing the urgency of their fiscal recommendations.
