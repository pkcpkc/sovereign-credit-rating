# Fiscal outturn: Deficit 3.4% of GDP, Primary Surplus 0.4% of GDP

## Event Details

In 2024, Italy achieved a general government deficit of 3.4% of GDP, marking a significant improvement in fiscal stance compared to previous years. Crucially, this was accompanied by a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP, the first recorded since 2019. This return to primary balance was primarily driven by the phase-out of the costly "Superbonus" housing renovation tax credits and the reduction of energy support measures, rather than structural revenue increases.

Despite the improvement in the primary balance, the public debt-to-GDP ratio reversed its declining trend and is projected to rise to 136.7% in 2025. This increase is attributed to lagged borrowing needs arising from the cash-flow impact of past tax credits and high debt service costs, with interest expenditure weighing approximately 3.9% of GDP. The European Commission's 2025 Country Report highlights that while Italy has demonstrated policy credibility through adherence to the reformed Stability and Growth Pact and the implementation of its Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP), fiscal flexibility remains limited due to the high debt burden and rising pension costs associated with demographic aging.

This fiscal outturn serves as a foundational element for Italy's fiscal sustainability analysis under the [[European Semester]], where the country is balancing the need for debt reduction against the constraints of sluggish productivity growth and high public expenditure on pensions. The achievement of a primary surplus represents a critical milestone in Italy's post-pandemic fiscal consolidation path, endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]] which ended the Excessive Deficit Procedure against Italy in January 2025.
