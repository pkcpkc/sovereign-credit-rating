# Original publication and effective date of S&P Sovereign Rating Methodology

## Event Details

On December 18, 2017, **S&P Global Ratings** published and immediately implemented its updated **Sovereign Rating Methodology**. This document superseded the previous methodology from 2014 and integrated the 2013 Monetary Authorities Rating Methodology, establishing a standardized framework for assessing the creditworthiness of sovereign governments and monetary authorities regarding their ability and willingness to service financial obligations to nonofficial (commercial) creditors.

The 2017 methodology is built upon a five-pillar assessment framework, where each pillar is scored on a scale of 1 to 6 to determine an "indicative rating level," subject to supplemental adjustments. The key pillars include:
1.  **Institutional Assessment**: Evaluating policymaking effectiveness, transparency, debt payment culture, and external security risks.
2.  **Economic Assessment**: Analyzing GDP per capita, growth prospects, and economic diversity.
3.  **External Assessment**: Assessing currency status, external liquidity, and external position.
4.  **Fiscal Assessment**: Reviewing fiscal performance/flexibility, debt burden, and contingent liabilities.
5.  **Monetary Assessment**: Examining exchange rate regimes and monetary policy credibility.

Key changes introduced in this update include:
*   **Local vs. Foreign Currency Rating Cap**: The potential uplift for local-currency ratings was limited to one notch above foreign-currency ratings, down from the previous two notches, reflecting a more conservative view on the distinction between local and external debt servicing risks.
*   **Shift to Net Debt**: The initial fiscal assessment began focusing on the change in *net* general government debt (gross debt minus liquid financial assets) as a percentage of GDP, rather than gross debt, to better capture the true fiscal stance.
*   **External Metrics Adjustment**: Current Account Payments (CAP) replaced Current Account Receipts (CAR) in certain external ratios when external liquid assets exceed external debt, providing a more accurate measure of external indebtedness.
*   **Contingent Liabilities**: The fiscal assessment incorporated a detailed analysis of contingent liabilities, particularly from the banking sector, using the Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment (BICRA).

The methodology also defined specific criteria for monetary authorities of monetary unions (e.g., the ECB), where ratings are determined by a weighted average of member sovereign ratings adjusted by the authority's own monetary assessment. This framework replaced earlier publications, including the *Principles of Credit Ratings* (2011) and the *Criteria for Assigning 'CCC+', 'CCC', 'CCC-', And 'CC' Ratings* (2012).
