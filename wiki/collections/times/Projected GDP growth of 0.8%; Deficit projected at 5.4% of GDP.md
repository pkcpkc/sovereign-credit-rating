# Projected GDP growth of 0.8%; Deficit projected at 5.4% of GDP

## Event Details

In the **2025 Country Report for Belgium**, published by the **European Commission** on June 4, 2025, significant macroeconomic forecasts for 2025 were released, highlighting a deceleration in economic growth and a widening fiscal deficit.

*   **GDP Growth Deceleration**: Belgium's GDP growth is projected to slow to **0.8% in 2025**, down from the **1% growth recorded in 2024**. This slowdown is attributed to weakening exports, which are negatively impacted by external shocks such as US tariffs, as well as a cooling of domestic investment and consumption.
*   **Fiscal Deficit Expansion**: The general government deficit, which already expanded to **4.5% of GDP in 2024**, is forecast to rise further to **5.4% of GDP in 2025**. This deterioration is driven by rising net expenditure—projected at 5.0% for 2025—which exceeds the maximum rate defined in the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP).
*   **National Escape Clause Utilization**: The increase in expenditure is largely permitted under the **National Escape Clause** within the reformed EU Economic Governance Framework (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263). This mechanism allows Belgium to legally exceed net expenditure limits to accommodate increased **defence spending**, which is projected to reach 1.7% of GDP in 2025.
*   **Debt Trajectory Context**: The fiscal deterioration occurs against a backdrop of high public debt, which stood at 104.7% of GDP in 2024 and is projected to rise to 107.1% in 2025. Without policy intervention, debt is on an unsustainable trajectory to reach 126.4% of GDP by 2035.
*   **Structural Pressures**: The deficit growth is exacerbated by rigid expenditure structures, particularly ageing-related costs (pensions and long-term care) and the aforementioned defence commitments, set against a stable revenue base of roughly 50% of GDP.
