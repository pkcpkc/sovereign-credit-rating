# Hungary Submits 2026 Budget Law to Parliament

## Event Details

On May 6, 2025, the Hungarian government submitted its 2026 Budget Law to Parliament. This legislative action was a key component of the country's medium-term fiscal-structural plan, which had been previously endorsed by the [[European Union|EU]] Council on May 18, 2025. The submitted budget law revised the deficit target upwards from 2.9% to 3.7% of GDP, reflecting ongoing fiscal pressures and structural challenges within the Hungarian economy.

The submission occurred amidst a broader macroeconomic context characterized by subdued growth, with GDP projected to grow by 0.5% in 2024 and recover to 2.5% in 2026. Public debt was projected to rise to 74.5% of GDP, with debt-servicing costs remaining among the highest in the EU due to implicit interest rates near 6%. The budgetary framework has been criticized for lacking multiannual spending ceilings and relying on frequent revisions and emergency decrees, which undermine transparency and accountability.

This event is closely linked to the [[European Commission]]'s assessment of Hungary's fiscal sustainability, highlighting threats such as weak fiscal balance, poor revenue performance, and constrained ability to adjust taxes and spending. The government's approach has drawn scrutiny from the EU regarding compliance with stability and growth rules, particularly concerning the use of "state of danger" decrees that bypass standard parliamentary oversight.
