# IMF Staff Mission Concludes Discussions in Budapest

## Event Details

On 17 June 2025, the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) staff mission concluded its discussions with Hungarian authorities in Budapest, marking a critical juncture in the 2025 Article IV Consultation. The mission, led by [[Anke Weber]] (Mission Head), and supported by [[Kristina Kostial]] (Department Head, European Department) and [[Jacques Miniane]] (Department Head, Strategic Policy, Research and Statistics), engaged with key Hungarian officials including [[Márton Nagy]] (Minister, Ministry for National Economy), and Central Bank Deputies [[Barnabás Virág]], [[Csaba Kandrács]], and [[Zoltán Kurali]].

The discussions highlighted that Hungary’s economy was at a "challenging juncture," characterized by three years of stagnant output and inflation persistently above the [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]]’s 3 percent target. Core services inflation remained elevated at 7.6 percent year-on-year, driven by wage pressures and food prices. While GDP contracted in Q1 2025 following a modest 0.5 percent growth in 2024, the IMF emphasized the urgent need for high-quality fiscal adjustment to rebuild buffers against rising public debt, projected to reach 79 percent of GDP by 2030.

Key points of contention and alignment included:
*   **Fiscal Adjustment:** The IMF recommended additional fiscal measures of approximately 0.5 percent of GDP for 2025 to meet budget targets, with a cumulative adjustment of 1.3 percent of GDP in 2026-2027. The Hungarian authorities defended their [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan]] (MTFSP) and targeted support measures.
*   **Monetary Policy:** The [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]] was advised to maintain its base rate at 6.5 percent into 2026 and phase out distortive price and margin controls to avoid market inefficiencies.
*   **Structural Reforms:** Priority was placed on boosting productivity, enhancing energy security, and improving governance to unlock suspended [[European Union]] funds (€19 billion). Risks include high external debt, FX exposure, and contingent liabilities from State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs).

The authorities broadly agreed with the assessment but expressed greater confidence in their resilience, highlighting strong employment figures, pro-family measures, and recent investments in electric vehicle (EV) and battery production. This mission paved the way for the completion of the IMF Staff Report on 25 July 2025 and the Executive Board’s conclusion of the consultation on 29 August 2025.
