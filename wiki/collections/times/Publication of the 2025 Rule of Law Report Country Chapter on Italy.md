# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Italy

## Event Details

On **2025-07-08**, the European Commission published the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Italy. This publication evaluates the state of the rule of law in Italy across four key pillars: the justice system, the anti-corruption framework, media pluralism, and other institutional checks and balances.

### Key Developments and Findings

**Justice System Reform and Digitalisation**
*   **Digitalisation**: The mandatory use of the digital case management system (*APP*) in criminal courts and prosecutors' offices began on **2025-01-01**. While technical challenges led to derogations for some courts, the High Council for the Judiciary continued implementing tasks under the comprehensive justice reform legislation adopted in March 2024.
*   **Constitutional Reform**: The Chamber of Deputies approved a draft constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors on **2025-01-16**. This reform faces scrutiny in the Senate and strong opposition from the High Council for the Judiciary (which issued a critical opinion on **2025-01-08**) and magistrates’ associations, who argue it undermines judicial autonomy.
*   **Efficiency**: Despite recruitment efforts, persistent shortages of administrative staff and excessive length of proceedings remain critical issues. However, positive trends in reducing case backlogs were noted across all jurisdictions.

**Anti-Corruption Framework**
*   **ANAC Updates**: The National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) updated the National Anti-Corruption Plan in January 2025 and issued new guidelines on "revolving doors" and whistleblowing.
*   **Legislative Gaps**: No progress was made on establishing an operational lobbying register or addressing the practice of channeling donations through political foundations. Draft laws on lobbying and political party financing have not advanced.
*   **Judicial Decisions**: On **2025-05-13**, the Constitutional Court declared the law abrogating the offence of abuse of public office (which entered into force on **2024-08-01**) compliant with the Constitution.

**Media Freedom and Safety**
*   **Regulation**: The media regulator (AGCOM) functions independently and is financially equipped. RAI retained its license fee levels in 2025, providing financial stability, though governance reforms remain pending.
*   **Journalist Safety**: Concerns persist regarding restrictions on accessing judicial information and threats to journalists. The Council of Europe registered 11 alerts concerning Italy in 2025. Allegations of Paragon spyware targeting journalists prompted an investigation by the Parliamentary Committee for the Security of the Republic.

**Institutional Checks and Balances**
*   **Security Law**: On **2025-06-04**, Parliament adopted the new Security Law, which entered into force on **2025-04-01**. This law raises stakeholder concerns over its impact on civic space and fundamental freedoms, particularly regarding increased penalties for protest-related activities. The High Court of Cassation issued a report on the Security Law on **2025-06-23**.
*   **Governance Practices**: Stakeholders expressed concern over the government’s frequent use of urgency decrees (87 decrees in the current legislature) and confidence motions, which may affect the separation of powers.
*   **Human Rights**: No concrete measures have been undertaken to establish a National Human Rights Institution, with five draft laws remaining pending.
