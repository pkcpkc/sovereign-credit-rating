# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Italy

## Event Details

On **2025-07-08**, the European Commission published the 2025 [[Rule of Law Report]] focusing on Italy, highlighting significant progress in judicial and anti-corruption reforms alongside persistent challenges in political financing, media governance, and fundamental freedoms. The report covers developments from mid-2024 to mid-2025, citing the Italian government's comprehensive justice system reform as a key strength, including the implementation of directives by the [[High Council for the Judiciary]] on magistrate appointments and performance appraisals.

Key aspects of the report include:

*   **Judicial Reform:** The [[High Council for the Judiciary]] approved directives regarding out-of-tenure appointments, magistrates' performance appraisal, and a Code of Judicial Management. However, persistent staff shortages across court levels remain a threat. A controversial draft constitutional reform separating the careers of judges and prosecutors was approved by the [[Chamber of Deputies]] but faces strong opposition from the judiciary, who argue it undermines autonomy.
*   **Anti-Corruption:** The [[National Anti-Corruption Authority]] (ANAC), led by President [[Giuseppe Busia]], updated the National Anti-Corruption Plan and strengthened its resources. Despite this, comprehensive legislation on lobbying and conflicts of interest for political office holders remains stalled in Parliament. Public procurement continues to be exposed to organized crime infiltration, though digital safeguards and ANAC guidelines on revolving doors provide some mitigation.
*   **Media Governance:** The [[Regulator for Audiovisual Media and Electronic Communications]] (AGCOM) functions independently. A decision to reduce the [[RAI]] licence fee was reversed in the 2025 Budget Law, maintaining fees at EUR 90 and preserving financial resources. Structural governance reforms for the public broadcaster remain debated in the Senate, with a stalemate in appointing the [[RAI]] President.
*   **Civic Space and Security:** The adoption of a new [[Security Law]] via urgency decree raised concerns among UN Special Rapporteurs and the Council of Europe regarding its impact on freedom of assembly and expression. Civil society rates Italy's civic space as 'narrowed'. Journalists face threats from spyware targeting (e.g., Paragon case) and SLAPPs, with defamation law reforms stalled.
*   **Political Stability:** Concerns persist regarding frequent use of urgency decrees and political fragmentation linked to channeling donations through foundations. No progress was made on laws regarding party financing or establishing a National Human Rights Institution (NHRI).

The report identifies strengths such as high clearance rates in judicial backlogs, a well-capitalized media sector, and structural reforms providing long-term institutional stability, while noting threats from political fragmentation and incomplete implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments.
