# Venice Commission opinion on judicial and prosecutorial amendments

## Event Details

On **2025-06-16**, the [[Venice Commission]] (European Commission for Democracy through Law) issued its opinion on amendments to the Hungarian laws on judicial and prosecutorial organization, as well as the law on judges and the law on prosecutors. This event occurred shortly after the Hungarian National Assembly adopted **Act XLIX of 2025** on **2025-06-11**, which detailed significant reforms to the judicial system, including raising the minimum age for judges to 35 and allowing service until age 70.

The Venice Commission's opinion was part of a broader cycle of rule of law assessments, including the [[European Commission]]'s **2025 Rule of Law Report: Hungary**, published on **2025-07-08**. The Commission expressed concerns regarding the adequacy of the consultation process with the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] and the potential impact of the reforms on judicial independence. Specifically, the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] had withdrawn from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms on **2025-01-15**, following the resignation of its President in December 2024 amid protests against earlier reform proposals.

The opinion addressed issues highlighted in subsequent reports, such as the lack of transparency in case allocation at lower-instance courts and the perceived pressure on judges. The [[Kúria (Hungary)]] continued to enforce uniform case law, but concerns persisted regarding the impartiality of internal judicial debates and the influence of political factors on the appointment and dismissal of judges. The [[Venice Commission]]'s findings contributed to ongoing scrutiny by the [[Council of Europe]] and the [[European Union]] regarding the state of judicial independence and the rule of law in Hungary.
