# Publication of Draft Law Implementing Anti-SLAPP Directive

## Event Details

On 1 January 2025, the Government of Poland published a draft law aimed at implementing the Anti-SLAPP (Anti-Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation) Directive. This legislative initiative is part of a broader government Action Plan on the Rule of Law, designed to address long-standing concerns regarding judicial independence and media freedom.

The draft law seeks to protect individuals and entities from strategic lawsuits intended to silence public participation or media reporting. It aligns with European Union efforts to safeguard the right to participate in public debate and ensure that legal proceedings are not used to harass critics, journalists, or civil society actors. The publication of this draft law occurred alongside other significant judicial reforms, including measures to separate the functions of the [[Minister of Justice]] and the [[Prosecutor General]], and the establishment of the [[European Public Prosecutor Office]] in Poland.

Key aspects of the reform context include:
- **Judicial Independence:** The law complements other efforts to restore trust in the judiciary, such as drafting laws to address judges appointed by the post-2017 [[National Council for the Judiciary]] and to dismantle the [[Supreme Court]]’s Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs.
- **Media Freedom:** The initiative responds to ongoing challenges regarding media regulator independence and the safety of journalists, aiming to reduce politically motivated litigation against media outlets.
- **Institutional Framework:** The reform is part of Poland’s compliance with EU rule of law mechanisms, overseen by institutions such as the [[Venice Commission]] and monitored through the [[Rule of Law Mechanism]].

Despite these positive steps, perceived judicial independence remains very low (26% among the general public, 24% among companies), and significant risks to institutional checks and balances persist. The implementation of this law is being monitored by the [[European Court of Human Rights]] and other international bodies to ensure effective protection against strategic litigation.
