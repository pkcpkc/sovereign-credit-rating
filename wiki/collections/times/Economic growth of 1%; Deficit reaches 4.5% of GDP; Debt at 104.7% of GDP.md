# Economic growth of 1%; Deficit reaches 4.5% of GDP; Debt at 104.7% of GDP

## Event Details

In 2024, the Belgian economy recorded a growth rate of **1%**, driven primarily by resilient private consumption. Despite this modest expansion, the country faced significant macroeconomic headwinds, including weakening exports impacted by external shocks such as US tariffs, and a cooling of domestic investment.

Concurrently, public finances deteriorated markedly. The general government deficit widened to **4.5% of GDP** in 2024. This expansion was driven by rising net expenditure—particularly age-related costs and increased defence spending—against a stable revenue base of approximately 50% of GDP. The deficit was forecast to rise further to 5.4% of GDP in 2025.

The high deficit contributed to an increase in the public debt-to-GDP ratio, which reached **104.7%** in 2024. Without policy intervention, debt was projected to surge to 126.4% of GDP by 2035, driven by the high primary deficits associated with ageing costs and rising interest payments. These figures prompted the European Commission to highlight severe fiscal sustainability challenges in its 2025 Country Report, leading Belgium to submit a Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (2025–2029) to address the trajectory through structural reforms and fiscal consolidation.

The period also saw the activation of the **National Escape Clause** under the reformed EU Economic Governance Framework, allowing Belgium to legally exceed net expenditure limits to accommodate increased defence spending (projected to reach 1.7% of GDP in 2025) while maintaining compliance with the broader fiscal roadmap.
