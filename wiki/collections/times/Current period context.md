### 2025: IMF Article IV Consultation and Fiscal Framework Reforms

The central economic event of this period is the completion of the **[[International Monetary Fund]]** (IMF) 2025 Article IV Consultation with **[[Belgium]]**. The consultation, which concluded with Executive Board approval on **March 18, 2025**, highlights a resilient but structurally challenged economy. The primary focus is on urgent fiscal consolidation and labor market reforms to address high public debt and sluggish productivity.

*   **Fiscal Consolidation Strategy**: With public debt at **104.1% of GDP** in 2024 and projected to rise to 123% by 2030 under unchanged policies, the IMF supports the new Belgian government’s plan for a **7-year fiscal adjustment** under the new [[European Commission]] Economic Governance Framework (EGF). Staff recommends an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of approximately **0.6 percentage points of GDP** until 2031. The goal is to bring the deficit durably below 3% of GDP and reduce debt to ~78% of GDP.
*   **Labor Market and Wage Indexation**: A key structural recommendation is the reform of the **automatic wage indexation** system and the 1996 wage law, which are identified as drivers of rising unit labor costs and declining competitiveness. The IMF advises abolishing automatic indexation and reforming the wage corridor to improve international competitiveness.
*   **Government Formation**: Following the **2024-06 Belgian Federal Elections**, a new federal government was formed in **February 2025**. The IMF concluded virtual meetings with new Deputy Prime Minister **[[Jan Jambon]]** on February 21, 2025, signaling the start of the new administration's agenda for structural reforms.
*   **Financial Sector Stability**: Systemic risks remain moderate. The banking sector is well-capitalized with low non-performing loans (2.1%), though risks are shifting toward the **commercial real estate (CRE)** sector. Household indebtedness remains elevated at 58% of GDP.
*   **External Position**: Belgium’s external position was assessed as weaker than implied by fundamentals, with the real effective exchange rate (REER) overvalued by an estimated **4.9%–17.4%**. However, a large positive Net International Investment Position (57.3% of GDP) provides a buffer against external shocks.
