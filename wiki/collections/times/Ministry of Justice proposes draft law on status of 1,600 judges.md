# Ministry of Justice proposes draft law on status of 1,600 judges

## Event Details

In April 2025, the Polish Ministry of Justice proposed a draft law addressing the legal status of approximately 1,600 judges who were appointed at the request of the [[National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)]] (NCJ) during its post-2017 composition. This legislative proposal represents a significant component of the Polish Government's efforts to implement the Action Plan on the Rule of Law, aiming to resolve long-standing concerns regarding [[judicial independence]] and the legitimacy of these judicial appointments.

The draft law introduces a classification system to evaluate the status of these judges, which may result in their potential removal from office. This measure is part of broader reforms intended to enhance the independence of the judiciary and align Poland with EU rule of law standards. As of mid-2025, the proposal was pending consultation with the [[Venice Commission]], an advisory body of the Council of Europe.

This event occurred within a complex legal and political context characterized by:
*   An ongoing constitutional crisis regarding the legitimacy of the [[Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)]], with the Government refusing to publish its judgments.
*   Legislative amendments concerning the NCJ and the Constitutional Tribunal that had been adopted by Parliament but were pending review by the Constitutional Tribunal following a presidential referral.
*   Parallel reforms aimed at dismantling the Supreme Court's Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs to further strengthen judicial independence.
