# Adoption of Large Fiscal Reform Package

## Event Details

In July 2025, the Romanian government adopted a comprehensive fiscal consolidation package aimed at addressing widening twin deficits (fiscal and current account) and restoring macroeconomic stability. Following the formation of a new coalition government in June 2025, the administration implemented significant tax measures, including value-added tax (VAT) rate hikes and increased excise duties, to curb the fiscal deficit which had risen to 8.7 percent of GDP in 2024 due to costly pension reforms and public wage increases.

The primary objective of this reform package was to reduce the fiscal deficit to below 6 percent of GDP in 2026 and eventually bring it below 3 percent by 2031. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) welcomed these reforms as a necessary step toward restoring fiscal and [[Sovereign Credit Rating]] sustainability. However, the IMF noted persistent risks regarding the full execution of the consolidation plan, citing high fiscal deficit execution risks and potential for a rating downgrade if implementation delays occur.

Key components of the reform included:
*   Implementation of VAT rate hikes and excise increases.
*   Commitment to European Union fiscal rules.
*   Medium-term fiscal structural planning to manage pension spending pressures and high current fiscal deficits.

This event occurred in the context of broader structural reforms aimed at improving governance, accelerating the absorption of EU funds (specifically NextGenerationEU), and enhancing labor force participation. The adoption of this package was a critical factor in improving Romania's [[Policy Predictability and Reform Continuity]] score within the sovereign credit rating assessment, balancing against threats such as political uncertainty and administrative capacity constraints.
