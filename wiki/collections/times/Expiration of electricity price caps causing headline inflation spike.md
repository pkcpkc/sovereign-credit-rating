# Expiration of electricity price caps causing headline inflation spike

## Event Details

In January 2025, the expiration of government-imposed electricity price caps in Austria triggered a sharp spike in headline inflation. This one-off effect pushed headline inflation to 3.3%, as households and businesses faced higher network charges and market-based pricing following the end of temporary relief measures.

Despite the surge in headline figures, core inflation remained persistently elevated at 3.1%, driven by strong wage growth in the services sector (7-8%) and sticky service prices. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) noted that this inflation dynamic was distinct from the energy-driven shocks of previous years, reflecting underlying domestic demand and labor market tightness. The IMF projected that inflation would gradually converge toward the Euro Area average of 2% by the end of 2026, contingent on productivity-adjusted wage growth aligning with regional peers to maintain competitiveness.

This event occurred amidst a broader economic context characterized by two successive years of recession (2023 and 2024) and a fiscal deficit widening to 4.7% of GDP in 2024. The expiration of the caps highlighted the ongoing challenges in balancing price stability with fiscal consolidation and structural reforms, including pension adjustments and limits on public-sector wage increases.
