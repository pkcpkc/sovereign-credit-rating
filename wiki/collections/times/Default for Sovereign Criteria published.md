# Default for Sovereign Criteria published

## Event Details

The evolution of S&P Global Ratings' sovereign methodology reflects a transition toward more nuanced assessments of debt sustainability and systemic risk. Key milestones include:

*   **2011–2014:** The framework was established through the publication of the *Principles Of Credit Ratings* (2011-02-16), followed by specific methodologies for *Monetary Authorities* (2013-09-11) and a previous *Sovereign Rating Methodology* (2014-12-23).
*   **2016–2017:** The *Guarantee Criteria* were published in October 2016, leading into the significant update of the *Default for Sovereign Criteria* on April 13, 2017.
*   **2017 Methodology Overhaul:** On December 18, 2017, the *Current Sovereign Rating Methodology* became effective. This update introduced several critical shifts:
    *   **Net Debt & CAP:** The methodology moved toward evaluating **net general government debt** (subtracting liquid financial assets) and utilized **Current Account Payments (CAP)** in external assessment ratios.
    *   **Local-Currency Caps:** A new restriction was placed on local-currency ratings, limiting them to a maximum of one notch above the foreign-currency rating.
    *   **Contingent Liabilities:** The integration of the **Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment (BICRA)** allowed for a quantified assessment of banking system risks, with potential negative adjustments of up to three notches based on systemic risks from nonfinancial public-sector enterprises (NFPEs).
    *   **Pillar Framework:** The assessment was codified into five pillars: Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, and Monetary.
