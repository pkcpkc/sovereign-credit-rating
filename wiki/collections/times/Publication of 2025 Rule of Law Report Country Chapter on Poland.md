# Publication of 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Poland

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the European Commission published the Country Chapter on Poland for the 2025 Rule of Law Report (Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 921 final). The report covers developments up to mid-2025, highlighting significant government efforts to address long-standing rule of law concerns, particularly regarding judicial independence, as outlined in the Action Plan on the Rule of Law.

**Judicial Independence and Constitutional Crisis**
Key developments include the preparation of legislation to address the status of approximately 1,600 judges appointed by the post-2017 [[National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)]]. The government proposed a draft law involving a classification system and potential removal, pending consultation with the [[Venice Commission]]. Additionally, reforms to the [[Supreme Court (Poland)]] structure were proposed, including the dismantling of the Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs. Despite these efforts, a constitutional crisis persists regarding the [[Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)]]. Although Parliament adopted amendments to laws governing the NCJ and the Tribunal, they have not entered into force due to a presidential referral to the Tribunal itself. The Government continues to refuse to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, asserting the body lacks legitimacy, which has led to an ongoing infringement procedure by the European Commission. In March 2025, an Advocate General issued an opinion on the Tribunal infringement case.

**Prosecution Service and Anti-Corruption**
Significant progress was made in separating the office of the [[Minister of Justice]] from the [[Prosecutor General]], a move aimed at ensuring the functional independence of the prosecution service. A draft law incorporating [[Venice Commission]] recommendations was approved by the Cabinet Standing Committee in December 2024. The [[European Public Prosecutor's Office]] became fully operational in Poland on January 1, 2025. In the anti-corruption sphere, while the perception of corruption remains relatively high, large-scale cases involving public officials are being investigated. A new Central Office for Combating Corruption is to be established within the police, dissolving the [[Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (Poland)]]. However, challenges persist regarding the lack of a comprehensive anti-corruption strategy, the absence of lobbying rules, and broad immunities for top executives that hinder high-level prosecutions.

**Media Freedom and Civil Society**
Media pluralism showed mixed trends. Politically biased content in public service media decreased, and state advertising distribution became more even. However, concerns remain regarding the independence of the media regulator (KRRiT) and the governance of public broadcasters, which are currently in liquidation. The Government plans a media law reform to implement the [[European Media Freedom Act]]. The civic space in Poland was re-rated from "obstructed" to "narrowed" by CIVICUS in March 2025. While the Government established working groups with civil society organizations, negative rhetoric and operational challenges, such as border restrictions, persist.

**Checks and Balances and Economic Factors**
Poland strengthened rules for legislative consultations by introducing mandatory impact assessments for draft laws. The Ombudsperson continues to play a key role in checks and balances. However, the number of pending leading judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] increased to 54 by June 2025. Public procurement remains vulnerable to corruption risks, with significant single-bid rates, though investigations into EU fund misuse have led to arrests. The report notes that while Poland has a stable democratic framework and active engagement with EU institutions, frequent changes in legislation affect legal certainty and investor confidence.

**Timeline of Key Developments (2025)**
*   **January 1, 2025**: [[European Public Prosecutor's Office]] (EPPO) becomes fully operational in Poland.
*   **February 2025**: Codification Committee prepares proposals on post-2017 NCJ judges.
*   **March 2025**: Advocate General issues opinion on Constitutional Tribunal infringement case; Civic space rating updated from 'obstructed' to 'narrowed'.
*   **April 2025**: Ministry of Justice proposes draft law on the status of 1,600 judges; Draft law to dismantle Supreme Court Chamber of Extraordinary Control proposed.
*   **June 14, 2025**: Whistleblower protection law, enforced largely since September 2024, is noted as fully active in the 2025 context.
*   **June 16, 2025**: Report notes 54 pending leading judgments from the ECtHR.
*   **July 8, 2025**: Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Poland.
