# Publication of 2025 Country Report - Malta

## Event Details

On June 4, 2025, the [[European Commission]] published the **2025 Country Report for Malta** (SWD(2025) 218 final), marking a key milestone in the [[European Semester]] cycle. The report evaluates Malta’s economic performance for 2024 and provides forecasts for 2025–2026, highlighting a period of robust growth juxtaposed with significant structural and fiscal challenges.

### Economic Growth and Labor Market
Malta’s economy demonstrated strong resilience in 2024, with real GDP growing by **6.0%**, driven by robust domestic demand and exports in the service sector, particularly tourism and professional services. Projections indicate continued expansion at **4.1% in 2025** and **4.0% in 2026**. The labor market remains extremely tight, with the employment rate reaching **83.0%** in 2024, one of the highest in the EU. However, this growth is heavily dependent on immigration to address severe structural skills shortages and mismatches, particularly in ICT, construction, and healthcare. Despite high employment, the gender employment gap remains wide at 13.4 percentage points, and non-EU nationals face precarious working conditions and higher risks of poverty.

### Fiscal Consolidation and Long-Term Sustainability
The report underscores Malta's ongoing fiscal consolidation phase. The general government deficit is projected to decline from **3.7% of GDP in 2024** to **2.8% in 2026**, supported by a reduction in energy subsidies and improved tax revenues. The government debt-to-GDP ratio stands at a manageable **47.4%** (2024), well below the EU average and the 60% treaty reference value, with expectations to stabilize below 50% by 2026.

However, long-term fiscal sustainability faces critical threats from an aging population. Age-related expenditures (pensions, healthcare, and long-term care) are projected to rise from **17% of GDP in 2024 to 26% by 2070**. Specifically, pension spending is expected to double from 6% to 10.5% of GDP, and healthcare spending to surge from 5.1% to 7.2% of GDP. This represents the second-largest projected increase in pension spending among EU Member States, posing a significant pressure on future fiscal balances.

### Structural Challenges and Policy Priorities
Several structural weaknesses were identified as key risks to long-term competitiveness and sustainability:

*   **Innovation and R&D:** Malta has one of the lowest R&D intensities in the EU at **0.64% of GDP**, hindering innovation-led growth. The [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] (RRF) is expected to drive improvements, with over 50% of grants already disbursed for digitalization and energy efficiency.
*   **Business Environment and Justice:** The business environment is constrained by a high regulatory burden (OECD PMR indicator second weakest in the EU) and an inefficient judicial system. Civil and commercial cases take an average of **454 days** to resolve, while administrative cases take 1,350 days, hampering business confidence. Corruption perceptions remain a concern, with a CPI score of 46/100 compared to the EU average of 62.
*   **Taxation and Revenue:** Tax revenue is low at **26.6% of GDP** (vs EU 39%), with heavy reliance on corporate income tax. A significant VAT compliance gap of **25.9%** and risks of aggressive tax planning (particularly regarding outbound payments and non-domiciled companies) pose revenue leakage risks. The Commission notes that withholding taxes on outbound payments remain a key pending reform.
*   **Energy and Environment:** Malta’s renewable energy share in electricity generation was only **17%** in 2024. Fossil fuel subsidies account for 1.6% of GDP, distorting market signals. Environmental challenges also include high municipal waste generation (606 kg/capita) and severe water stress, with the Water Exploitation Index reaching 34% in 2022.

### Institutional Context and Outlook
The report coincides with the Council Recommendation of January 21, 2025, endorsing Malta’s Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan. The [[European Commission]] urges Malta to accelerate the implementation of its RRP and cohesion policy investments to address structural weaknesses, align with the [[Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform]] (STEP), and mitigate long-term demographic risks. The deadline for implementing key RRP measures is set for August 31, 2026.
