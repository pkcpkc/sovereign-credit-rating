# Publication of 2025 Country Report - Malta

## Event Details

On June 4, 2025, the European Commission published its 2025 Country Report for Malta. This event marks the formal assessment of Malta's economic and structural progress within the European Union framework, serving as a key benchmark for policy alignment and fiscal monitoring.

The report highlights an economy with strong growth momentum, driven by robust domestic demand and export performance in tourism, professional services, and IT. Real GDP growth reached 6.0% in 2024, with forecasts projecting continued expansion at approximately 4.0-4.1% through 2026. Despite exceptionally low unemployment rates (3.1%), the economy faces significant constraints due to severe labor shortages and skills mismatches in critical sectors such as ICT, construction, and healthcare.

Key fiscal developments noted in the report include a projected decrease in the general government deficit from 3.7% of GDP in 2024 to 2.8% in 2026, supported by decreasing energy subsidies and increased tax collection efforts. Government debt is projected to stabilize below 50% of GDP. However, long-term fiscal sustainability remains a concern due to rising age-related expenditures, particularly in pensions and healthcare.

Structural reforms are ongoing through the implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), with 39% of milestones and targets fulfilled as of the report's publication. The report emphasizes the need to address low investment in Research and Development (R&D), administrative burdens, and high dependence on fossil fuels. Institutional strengthening efforts include the establishment of Science Malta (Xjenza) in 2024 to streamline R&D governance.

This publication is part of a broader sequence of EU fiscal interventions and recommendations for Malta, including Council Recommendations from January 2025 regarding the medium-term fiscal-structural plan and the correction of excessive deficits.
