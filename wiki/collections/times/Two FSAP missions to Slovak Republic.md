# Two FSAP missions to Slovak Republic

## Event Details

In **2024**, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) conducted two Financial Stability Assessment Program (FSAP) missions to the Slovak Republic. These missions were part of the broader **2025 Article IV Consultation** framework, aimed at assessing the resilience of Slovakia’s financial system against severe shocks and identifying vulnerabilities.

The FSAP assessments highlighted that Slovakia's banking sector remains resilient, characterized by high capital adequacy ratios well above regulatory minima and ample liquidity buffers (Liquidity Coverage Ratio > 200%). Non-performing loans (NPLs) were low, and stress tests confirmed the sector's ability to withstand significant economic shocks. However, the missions identified key vulnerabilities, particularly in the **commercial real estate (CRE)** sector—specifically the office segment—and risks associated with the refixation of residential mortgages in the coming years. Household debt levels were also noted as a factor requiring monitoring.

Key recommendations emerging from the FSAP and related Article IV discussions included strengthening the macroprudential framework, such as adopting a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer, to mitigate these specific sectoral risks. The assessments were conducted alongside a Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA) and an External Sector Assessment (EBA-lite) to provide a comprehensive view of macroeconomic stability and financial health.

The findings from these missions informed the IMF Executive Board's conclusions in **March 2025**, which endorsed the Slovak government's fiscal consolidation plans while urging continued attention to structural reforms and financial sector oversight to maintain stability amidst geopolitical and economic headwinds.
