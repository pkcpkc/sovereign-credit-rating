# Final Report of the Investigative Commission on Political Influence in Judiciary

## Event Details

In July 2025, Austria published the Final Report of the Investigative Commission on Political Influence in Judiciary. This event occurred within a broader context of structural judicial reforms and political scrutiny regarding the independence of the judiciary.

The report addresses systemic vulnerabilities in judicial appointments, specifically highlighting concerns that the appointment mechanisms for administrative court (vice-)presidents lacked consistent judicial involvement. While public perception of judicial independence remained high (86% of the general public), the government committed to reforming these appointment processes to align with European standards.

Concurrently, Austria was undergoing significant legislative changes affecting the justice system's efficiency and transparency. Key developments included:
*   **Prosecutorial Reform:** Limited progress was made on establishing an independent Federal Prosecution Office, with draft legislation pending despite commitments in the New Government Programme presented in March 2025.
*   **Digitalization:** The "Justice 3.0" initiative aimed for 90% digital case management by year-end, and compulsory online publication of judgments was extended to Higher Regional Courts in January 2025.
*   **Access to Justice:** The Constitutional Court ruled in October 2024 that restrictions on legal aid in administrative proceedings were unconstitutional, with suspensions taking effect by March 2026, following a 23% average increase in court fees introduced in April 2025.

The publication of this final report underscores ongoing efforts to strengthen institutional governance, combat corruption, and ensure the fair distribution of state resources, including state advertising, while maintaining political stability within the EU framework.
