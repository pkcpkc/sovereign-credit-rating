# IMF Executive Board concludes 2025 Article IV consultation

## Event Details

On **March 18, 2025**, the IMF Executive Board concluded its 2025 Article IV consultation with **Belgium**, following discussions that ended on February 4, 2025. The consultation was led by Mission Chief **Jean-François Dauphin** and directed by **Mark Horton** of the IMF European Department. The final Staff Report was completed on March 3, 2025, with the Press Release and Staff Report subsequently published on March 20, 2025.

The consultation coincided with the formation of a new federal government in **February 2025** under Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister **Jan Jambon**. The IMF staff supported the new government's commitment to a seven-year fiscal adjustment plan under the new EU Economic Governance Framework (EGF), aimed at addressing Belgium's high public debt, which stood at 104.1% of GDP in 2024 and was projected to rise to 123% by 2030 under unchanged policies.

Key outcomes and recommendations from the consultation included:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: The IMF recommended an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of approximately 0.6 percentage points of GDP until 2031. This path aims to durably lower the deficit below 3% of GDP and reduce debt to roughly 78% of GDP. The strategy emphasizes rationalizing current spending (such as social benefits and public wages) while preserving or enhancing public investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education.
*   **Labor Market Reforms**: To boost potential growth, which had slowed to 1.0% in 2024, the IMF urged reforms to the wage-setting mechanism. Specifically, it recommended abolishing automatic wage indexation and reforming the 1996 wage law to improve competitiveness and address declining labor-cost competitiveness. Other measures included limiting unemployment benefit duration and reducing hiring and firing costs.
*   **Financial Stability**: The banking sector remained stable with low non-performing loans (2.1%) and robust liquidity buffers. However, risks were identified in the commercial real estate (CRE) sector due to price contractions and rising vacancies. Household indebtedness remained elevated at 58% of GDP. The IMF advised maintaining current capital buffers and mortgage lending limits.
*   **External Position**: Belgium’s external position was assessed as weaker than implied by fundamentals, with a real effective exchange rate (REER) overvaluation estimated between 4.9% and 17.4%. However, a strong Net International Investment Position (57.3% of GDP) provided a buffer. The current account deficit narrowed to 0.3% of GDP in 2024.
*   **Climate and Coordination**: The report highlighted a lack of coordination between federal and regional governments regarding climate targets and fiscal responsibilities. The IMF called for a coherent national strategy to meet 2030 climate goals, including carbon taxation and the phasing out of fossil fuel subsidies.

The overall sovereign credit risk for Belgium was assessed as **moderate**. While high debt levels and sluggish productivity growth pose threats, these are mitigated by strong institutional capacity, low sovereign spreads, a long average debt maturity (11.1 years), and Eurozone membership. The [[National Bank of Belgium]], under Governor **Philippe Wunsch**, continues to play a key role in macroprudential oversight alongside the [[European Central Bank]].
