# Publication of 2025 Country Report - Poland

## Event Details

The [[European Commission]] published the 2025 Country Report for [[Poland]] on June 4, 2025. The report highlights that Poland’s economy remains one of the fastest growing in the [[European Union]], with GDP growth reaching 2.9% in 2024 and projected to exceed this figure in 2025, driven by strong private consumption and investment.

Despite robust growth, the report identifies significant structural challenges, including competitiveness issues, an ageing population, and fiscal pressures. The general government deficit reached 5.3% of GDP in 2023, exceeding the 3% reference value. This led the [[Council of the European Union]] to initiate an [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]] for Poland in January 2025.

Poland responded by submitting a [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan]] aiming to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2028, which includes a binding expenditure path endorsed by the Council. The report notes that while macroeconomic stability is largely maintained, risks remain from delays in public investments, high energy prices, and exchange rate fluctuations.

Key findings from the report include:
*   **Fiscal Performance**: The [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]] was initiated in January 2025 following the 5.3% deficit. A [[Fiscal Council]] was established via legislation enacted in December 2024 and is set to become fully operational in January 2026.
*   **Banking Sector**: The sector remains resilient with well-capitalised banks, though legacy issues regarding [[Forex Mortgages]] pose legal and financial challenges.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Poland is addressing challenges in innovation, business environment, skills, and the clean energy transition. The country has emerged as a [[Clean Tech Hub]], particularly in battery manufacturing, but faces skill shortages and regulatory uncertainties.
*   **Demographics**: An ageing society is putting pressure on the pension system and health/long-term care, with low uptake of private pension schemes.
*   **Energy**: Poland remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels for electricity generation (70% in 2024) and records sizeable fossil fuel subsidies.
