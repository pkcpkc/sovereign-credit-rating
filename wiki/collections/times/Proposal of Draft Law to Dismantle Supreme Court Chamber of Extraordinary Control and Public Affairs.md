# Proposal of Draft Law to Dismantle Supreme Court Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs

## Event Details

On 21 April 2025, the Polish government proposed a draft law aimed at dismantling the Supreme Court's Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs. This legislative initiative is a key component of the government's Action Plan on the Rule of Law, designed to address long-standing criticisms regarding judicial independence and institutional balance.

The proposal seeks to eliminate a chamber widely criticized by the European Union, the Venice Commission, and civil society as a tool for political control over the judiciary. The Chamber had been accused of handling cases in a manner that undermined legal certainty and the independence of judges, particularly concerning decisions related to the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) and the Constitutional Tribunal.

The draft law was submitted alongside other judicial reforms, including legislation addressing judges appointed by the post-2017 NCJ. While the European Commission welcomed the initiative as a step toward resolving the judicial crisis, stakeholders emphasized the need for comprehensive implementation and consultation with relevant bodies, including the Venice Commission. The move is part of broader efforts to separate the functions of the Minister of Justice and the Prosecutor General, and to restore trust in Poland's judicial institutions amidst ongoing tensions with EU bodies.
