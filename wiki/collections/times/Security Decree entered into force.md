# Security Decree entered into force

## Event Details

On **April 1, 2025**, the controversial **Security Decree** (part of the broader legislative package known as the "Security Law" or "Legge Sicura") officially entered into force in Italy. This implementation followed the Parliament's adoption of the new Security Law on **June 4, 2025**, and preceded the issuance of a critical report by the High Court of Cassation regarding the law's provisions on **June 23, 2025**.

The enactment of the decree occurred within a complex legal and political context detailed in the **European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report on Italy**. Key contextual factors include:

- **Legislative History**: The decree's provisions were coordinated with **Decree-Law No. 25**, which was published on **May 13, 2025**, alongside the conversion law.
- **Constitutional and Judicial Review**: On **May 2025**, the **Constitutional Court** declared the law abrogating the offence of abuse of public office compliant with the Constitution, a move linked to the broader justice system reforms. However, the High Court of Cassation issued a report on **June 23, 2025**, raising concerns about the Security Law.
- **Rule of Law Concerns**: The European Commission noted that the frequent resort to urgency decrees by the Italian Government, including this Security Decree, raises significant concerns regarding the **separation of powers** and **policymaking stability**. Stakeholders expressed particular worry about the law's impact on **civic space** and **fundamental freedoms**, noting that it increases penalties for protest-related activities.
- **Related Institutional Debates**: This event coincided with other major institutional shifts, such as the **High Council for the Judiciary** issuing a critical opinion on the draft constitutional reform separating judges and prosecutors (**January 8, 2025**) and the mandatory implementation of the digital case management system (APP) in criminal courts (**January 1, 2025**).

The entry into force of the Security Decree is cited in the **2025 Rule of Law Report** as a key factor affecting **geopolitical and political stability**, with threats identified in the frequent use of decrees and stakeholder concerns over the restriction of civic space.
