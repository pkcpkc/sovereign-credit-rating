# Partial entry into force of federal law amending administrative transparency framework

## Event Details

In July 2024, a federal law amending the administrative transparency framework partially entered into force in Belgium. This legislative development occurred against a backdrop of ongoing efforts to improve access to official documents, a key component of the country's institutional checks and balances.

According to the European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report, this recent federal legislation contributed to an improved transparency framework. However, the report highlights that significant procedural shortcomings persist. Notably, the appeal bodies responsible for handling disputes over access to administrative documents remain non-binding, limiting the effectiveness of the new regulations. Furthermore, while the Council of Europe Convention on Access to Official Documents has been signed by Belgium, it has not yet been ratified.

This event is part of a broader trajectory of transparency and integrity reforms, occurring during a period of political transition that included a caretaker Government from June 2024 to February 2025. Despite the legal advancements, the non-binding nature of appeal mechanisms continues to be cited as a threat to accountability within the Belgian administrative system.
