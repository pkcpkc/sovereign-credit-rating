# Belgium requests activation of National Escape Clause for defence spending

## Event Details

On April 30, 2025, Belgium formally requested the activation of the **National Escape Clause** under the reformed EU Economic Governance Framework (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263) to accommodate increased defence expenditure. This request was part of Belgium's broader submission of its **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** for 2025–2029, which the European Commission subsequently endorsed with a positive recommendation on May 21, 2025.

The activation of the escape clause allows Belgium to legally exceed its net expenditure limits for the 2025 fiscal year. Specifically, defence spending is projected to rise to **1.7% of GDP** in 2025. This deviation is permitted within the new EU rules, provided that the member state delivers on agreed structural reforms and investment commitments. The mechanism enables Belgium to maintain compliance with its broader fiscal roadmap despite the short-term pressure on public finances caused by higher defence outlays.

This decision occurred against a backdrop of deteriorating public finances, with the general government deficit projected to widen from 4.5% of GDP in 2024 to **5.4% of GDP** in 2025. While the escape clause covers the defence-related expenditure increase, the Commission's 2025 Country Report highlighted that Belgium faces significant long-term fiscal sustainability challenges, including an ageing population and high debt levels (projected to reach 126.4% of GDP by 2035 without intervention). The endorsement of the MTP commits Belgium to a seven-year fiscal adjustment period, underpinned by structural reforms in pensions, taxation, and the labour market.
