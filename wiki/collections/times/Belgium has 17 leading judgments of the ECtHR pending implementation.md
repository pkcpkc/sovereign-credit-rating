# Belgium has 17 leading judgments of the ECtHR pending implementation

## Event Details

On January 1, 2025, it was reported that Belgium had 17 leading judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] (ECtHR) pending implementation. This statistic highlights limited progress by the Belgian Government in ensuring public authorities comply with final court rulings, a key indicator of the rule of law.

The pending judgments are part of broader structural issues identified by the Court, notably in the case of *Camara v. Belgium*. As a result, the Belgian Government remains under supervision by the [[Council of Europe]]'s Committee of Ministers regarding these structural deficiencies. The [[European Commission]]’s 2025 Rule of Law Report on Belgium cited this lack of compliance as a persistent challenge, noting that despite the high perceived independence of the judiciary, the executive branch has been insufficiently active in addressing the systemic failures flagged by the ECtHR.

This situation contrasts with efforts by the judiciary itself to enforce compliance; in July 2024, a joint memorandum by the three highest Belgian courts explicitly required the Government to respect court judgments. However, the delay in implementing the 17 leading ECtHR judgments underscores a gap between judicial directives and executive action, contributing to concerns about institutional accountability and the effective protection of human rights in Belgium.
