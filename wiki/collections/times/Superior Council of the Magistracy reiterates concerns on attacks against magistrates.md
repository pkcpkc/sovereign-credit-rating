# Superior Council of the Magistracy reiterates concerns on attacks against magistrates

## Event Details

In March 2025, the [[Superior Council of the Magistracy]] (CSM) of France reiterated its concerns regarding the increasing frequency and severity of personal attacks and threats directed against magistrates. This statement came in the context of heightened political polarization and significant public pressure on the judiciary, particularly concerning judicial decisions related to national elections.

According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report by the [[European Commission]], while France had implemented a reform of the status of magistrates in 2023 to enhance judicial independence and diversity, perceived judicial independence remained average. The CSM's intervention highlights a persistent tension between judicial autonomy and political forces. The report notes that judges face significant personal attacks and political pressure, prompting both the CSM and the President of the Republic to reaffirm the necessity of judicial independence.

This event is part of a broader landscape of challenges within the French justice system, where despite institutional strengths and a stable democratic framework, the practical independence of magistrates is tested by social unrest, political friction involving the use of Article 49-3 of the Constitution, and intimidation within a narrowing civic space.
