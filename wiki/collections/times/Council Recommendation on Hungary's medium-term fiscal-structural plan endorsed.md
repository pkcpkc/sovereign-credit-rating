# Council Recommendation on Hungary's medium-term fiscal-structural plan endorsed

## Event Details

The timeline of fiscal and economic oversight for Hungary leading into 2025 reflects a period of intense structural adjustment and budgetary scrutiny by the European Commission and the Council. 

Following a period of extreme economic pressure, where Hungary recorded a peak inflation rate of 17.0% in January 2023, the government began a series of legislative and fiscal maneuvers. In May 2024, the 2026 budget law was submitted to Parliament, followed by a significant amendment to legislation regarding private equity funds in November 2024.

A pivotal moment occurred in December 2024, when Hungary formally submitted its medium-term fiscal-structural plan. This submission was met with a two-stage Council response:
*   **February 18, 2025:** The Council adopted a Recommendation endorsing the plan while simultaneously addressing the need to correct excessive deficits.
*   **March 18, 2025:** The Council Recommendation on Hungary's medium-term fiscal-structural plan was officially endorsed, marking a key milestone in the country's fiscal alignment with EU standards.

The subsequent publication of the **2025 Country Report** on June 4, 2025, provided a comprehensive overview of these developments. The report highlighted that while the fiscal plan was endorsed, Hungary continues to grapple with high public debt (73.5% of GDP), a lack of transparent multiannual spending ceilings, and an expansionary budgetary bias. The report also underscored structural hurdles, including a severe skills shortage in STEM and green transition sectors, and the urgent need to decouple from Russian fossil fuels while improving water resilience and circular economy practices.
