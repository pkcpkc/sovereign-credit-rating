# GDP growth of 0.5% and inflation decline to 3.7%

## Event Details

In 2024, Hungary experienced a period of economic stabilization characterized by subdued growth and a significant reduction in inflationary pressures, following a contraction of 0.8% and a peak inflation rate of 17.0% in 2023. According to the **European Commission**'s 2025 Country Report on Hungary, GDP grew by only **0.5%** in 2024, reflecting weak external demand and domestic policy uncertainty. Despite the modest growth, the period marked a critical turning point in price stability, as inflation declined sharply to **3.7%**.

This economic performance occurred against a backdrop of persistent structural vulnerabilities, including low labor productivity which has stagnated for 15 years, and high public debt levels. While the decline in inflation provided some relief to households, underlying price pressures—particularly in food and construction—led to a pickup in inflation during the first quarter of 2025. The Commission notes that while the 2024 figures represent an improvement over the previous year, the growth prospects remain constrained by skills shortages, heavy reliance on the automotive industry, and high energy import dependence. The subsequent years saw forecasts for a slow rebound, with growth projected to reach 0.8% in 2025 and 2.5% in 2026, driven by new electric vehicle battery manufacturing capacities.
