# IMF Executive Board Consideration of Staff Report

## Event Details

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) Executive Board has conducted separate considerations of Staff Reports for **Poland** and **Slovakia**, addressing critical macroeconomic challenges, fiscal consolidation strategies, and financial stability risks in Central Europe.

### Poland: 2024 Article IV Consultation

The IMF Executive Board held its consideration of the Staff Report for the Republic of Poland's 2024 Article IV Consultation on **January 10, 2025**. This review followed staff discussions with Polish officials that concluded on October 17, 2024, and the completion of the final Staff Report on December 16, 2024. The official Press Release and Executive Board Assessment were subsequently published on **January 21, 2025**.

**Economic Context and Performance**
The consultation assessed Poland's economic performance through Q3/Q4 2024, projecting trends through 2029.
*   **GDP Growth**: The economy demonstrated a robust cyclical recovery, with GDP growth estimated at **2.8% in 2024**. Growth is projected to accelerate to **3.5% in 2025** and **3.3% in 2026**, driven by strong private consumption, real wage growth, and the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds. Medium-term growth is expected to moderate to slightly below 3% due to demographic headwinds.
*   **Inflation**: Headline inflation declined to approximately **4% in October 2024** from 2023 peaks but remained above the National Bank of Poland (NBP) target of 2.5% ±1%. Core inflation remained sticky due to tight labor markets and administered energy price increases.

**Key Policy Recommendations and Findings**
*   **Fiscal Policy**:
    *   **Deficit and Debt**: The general government deficit widened to **5.9% of GDP in 2024**, triggering the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP). Public debt is projected to rise from ~50% of GDP in 2023 to approximately **65% by 2029** under the baseline scenario.
    *   **Consolidation Strategy**: The IMF supported the Polish authorities' Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), which targets reducing the deficit to **2.9% of GDP by 2028**. However, the IMF recommended frontloading consolidation with a modest adjustment of **~0.5% of GDP in 2025** to support disinflation and create room for monetary easing. An additional structural adjustment of ~1.5% of GDP beyond identified measures was advised for the medium term to stabilize debt around 60% of GDP.
    *   **Structural Risks**: Long-term fiscal sustainability is challenged by population aging, which increases pension and healthcare costs, and the need for climate transition investments.
*   **Monetary Policy**:
    *   **Stance**: The IMF commended the NBP's prudent monetary policy for anchoring inflation expectations. It recommended maintaining a **tight monetary policy stance** until there is clear evidence of decelerating wage growth and a sustained decline in inflation toward the target.
    *   **Rate Cuts**: Potential for interest rate cuts was signaled around **mid-2025**, contingent on disinflation progress.
*   **Financial Sector**:
    *   **Resilience**: The banking sector remained well-capitalized and liquid with strong profitability.
    *   **Risks**: Significant litigation risks persisted from foreign-currency (CHF) mortgages and potential WIBOR disputes. The IMF recommended eliminating distortions such as the bank asset tax that favors public debt over private credit and allowing mortgage credit holidays to expire to support credit recovery.
*   **Structural and External Sector Issues**:
    *   **Competitiveness**: The real effective exchange rate had appreciated significantly since 2022, weighing on cost-competitiveness. The current account, which had a surplus of 0.3% of GDP in 2024, was projected to move into a **deficit of -1.5% of GDP** in the medium term.
    *   **Labor Market**: Approximately **976,000 Ukrainian refugees** were registered in Poland, providing a significant positive contribution to the labor market. The IMF recommended reforms to facilitate their long-term integration and increase labor force participation among women and older adults to counteract aging demographics.
    *   **Energy Transition**: The economy's high carbon intensity and reliance on coal were noted as long-term risks, urging accelerated permitting of green energy projects.

### Slovak Republic: 2025 Article IV Consultation

The IMF Executive Board concluded its consideration of the Staff Report for the Slovak Republic's 2025 Article IV Consultation on **March 24, 2025**. The Board consideration took place on **March 18, 2025**, following the completion of the Staff Report on March 3, 2025. Discussions with Slovak officials had ended on January 28, 2025, after virtual mission discussions began on December 4, 2024. The official Press Release and Executive Board Assessment were published on **March 24, 2025**.

**Economic Context and Performance**
The consultation assessed an economy recovering from recent headwinds, with growth accelerating to 2.0% in 2024, outpacing the Euro Area average.
*   **GDP Growth**: Real GDP growth is projected to moderate to **1.8% in 2025** due to fiscal consolidation and weak external demand, before recovering to **2.1% in 2026**.
*   **Inflation**: Inflation is expected to rise temporarily to **4.1% in 2025** driven by indirect tax increases, then decline to the **2% target by early 2027**. Core inflation remains higher than the Euro Area median due to wage growth.

**Key Policy Recommendations and Findings**
*   **Fiscal Policy**:
    *   **Deficit and Debt**: The fiscal deficit reached **5.8% of GDP in 2024**, triggering an Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) by the European Commission. Public debt is projected to rise, reaching **74.3% of GDP by 2030** under the baseline, with a moderate overall risk of sovereign stress.
    *   **Consolidation Strategy**: The IMF endorsed the Slovak government’s ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028), which aims to reduce the deficit to approximately **2.2% of GDP by 2028**. The staff noted that while 2025 budget measures are a step in the right direction, further deficit-reducing measures—particularly on the expenditure side (e.g., reducing subsidies, targeting social spending)—are required to meet medium-term targets.
    *   **Structural Risks**: Long-term fiscal sustainability faces high risk due to aging-related costs, which could increase fiscal burdens by **5.6% of GDP by 2050** without further reforms.
*   **Monetary Policy and External Sector**:
    *   **External Sector**: The current account deficit widened to **2.7% of GDP in 2024**, assessed as moderately weaker than fundamentals. The Net International Investment Position (NIIP) remains negative (**-53.5% of GDP**) but is improving, supported by strong FDI inflows.
    *   **Monetary Framework**: As a Eurozone member, Slovakia benefits from full integration into the Euro area, eliminating exchange rate risk and ensuring ample liquidity access. The National Bank of Slovakia operates within the ECB framework, with supervisory powers being strengthened.
*   **Financial Sector**:
    *   **Resilience**: The banking sector is deemed resilient, with capital adequacy ratios well above regulatory minima and ample liquidity buffers (LCR > 200%). Stress tests confirm resilience to severe shocks.
    *   **Risks**: Primary vulnerabilities lie in the **commercial real estate (CRE)** market, particularly the office segment, and the refixation of residential mortgages in the coming years. The IMF recommended strengthening the macroprudential framework, including adopting a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer.
*   **Structural Reforms and Governance**:
    *   **Economic Diversity**: Slovakia faces structural headwinds from heavy dependence on the **automotive sector** and geoeconomic fragmentation. The report calls for reforms to diversify the economy, boost innovation, and increase labor force participation.
    *   **Governance**: Strengthening governance, reducing corruption vulnerabilities, and enhancing judicial independence are highlighted as essential to maintaining investor confidence and ensuring the effective absorption of EU funds. Political polarization and recent government changes (October 2024) have complicated policymaking and budget preparation.
