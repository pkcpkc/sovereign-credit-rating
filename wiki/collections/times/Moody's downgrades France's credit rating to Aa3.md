# Moody's downgrades France's credit rating to Aa3

## Event Details

In December 2024, **Moody's Investors Service** downgraded France's sovereign credit rating to **Aa3**, citing concerns over rising public debt, fiscal slippages, and domestic political uncertainty. This downgrade occurred against a backdrop of high global economic uncertainty and trade tensions.

According to the **IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation**, France's general government gross debt reached **113.1% of GDP** in 2024, with persistent primary deficits and fiscal slippages in 2023 and 2024 weakening debt dynamics. The IMF highlighted that while France demonstrated economic resilience in 2024—supported by the Paris Olympics and a robust labor market—growth is projected to slow to **0.6% in 2025** due to weak external demand and geoeconomic fragmentation before recovering to 1.0% in 2026.

Key factors influencing the credit outlook include:
*   **Fiscal Consolidation Challenges**: The need for a credible, frontloaded fiscal adjustment under the **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)** to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. Without significant additional efforts, the deficit remains projected around 6% of GDP in the medium term.
*   **Political and Policy Uncertainty**: Domestic political instability and lack of consensus pose risks to the implementation of necessary structural reforms and fiscal adjustments.
*   **Debt Sustainability**: Rising debt service payments and high sensitivity of debt dynamics to interest rates and growth remain critical threats, despite a low short-term risk of sovereign debt distress and a strong record of honoring obligations.

Following the downgrade, French authorities, including Minister **François Lombard** and Minister **Guillaume de Montchalín**, engaged in political compromises on the 2025 budget and published an action plan in March 2025 to enhance public finance execution. The IMF Executive Board concluded its consultation in July 2025, emphasizing the need for decisive structural reforms to boost productivity and ensure long-term fiscal sustainability.
