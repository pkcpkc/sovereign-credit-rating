# Romania submits its Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)

## Event Details

In **October 2024**, the Romanian government submitted its **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** to the European Commission, outlining a strategic roadmap for fiscal consolidation and structural reforms extending through **2031**. This submission was a direct response to the country's deteriorating macroeconomic position, characterized by a surge in the government deficit to **9.3% of GDP** and public debt rising rapidly to **54.8% of GDP** in 2024.

### Context and Drivers
The plan was necessitated by severe fiscal imbalances driven by loose fiscal policy, generous public sector wage increases, and high public investment spending. Net expenditure growth reached **19.9%** in 2024, far exceeding the recommended maxima under the reformed EU economic governance framework. Concurrently, the current account deficit widened to **8.4% of GDP**, reflecting high import demand and weak external competitiveness.

### Key Components and Measures
The MTP commits to a seven-year adjustment period (2025–2031) with the following key elements:
*   **Immediate Fiscal Adjustment**: Emergency measures adopted in late 2024 aimed to save **2.0% of GDP** in 2025, including wage and pension freezes, removal of tax exemptions, and reforms to the microenterprise tax regime.
*   **Deficit Targets**: The government targets a reduction of the budget deficit to **7% of GDP** in 2025, with a gradual decline thereafter.
*   **Structural Reforms**: The plan emphasizes reforms to increase recurrent revenues by an estimated **1.7% of GDP**, address the high VAT gap (33.7%), and improve the business environment to combat regulatory uncertainty.

### European Union Response
*   **January 2025**: The **Council Recommendation** endorsed Romania's national medium-term fiscal-structural plan, acknowledging the commitment to the extended adjustment period.
*   **January 2025**: The **Council Recommendation** also called for the correction of the excessive deficit in Romania, highlighting the need for credible implementation of the MTP.

### Economic Outlook and Challenges
Despite the submission of the MTP, challenges remain significant. Real GDP growth decelerated to **0.8%** in 2024, with projections of **1.4%** for 2025 and **2.2%** for 2026. Structural barriers such as outdated infrastructure, low R&D intensity (0.52% of GDP), and severe skills shortages continue to hinder productivity and long-term growth. The success of the MTP hinges on overcoming administrative bottlenecks, improving public administration efficiency, and ensuring political stability to restore investor confidence.
