# Publication of 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Romania

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the European Commission published its 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter specifically addressing Romania. This publication serves as a critical assessment of the state of the rule of law, institutional integrity, and judicial independence within the country. The report highlights a dichotomy between legislative progress and persistent systemic challenges.

Key findings from the report indicate significant progress in judicial reforms, particularly in completing legislative processes initiated by the Venice Commission regarding Justice Laws. These reforms aim to strengthen the independence of high-ranking prosecutors and judicial police. However, perceived judicial independence remains average, and the length of civil and commercial proceedings has increased, creating barriers to justice.

In the realm of anti-corruption, while high-level prosecutions continue, legal uncertainty has arisen due to High Court of Cassation and Justice rulings on the statute of limitations. These rulings have led to the annulment of many corruption convictions and the discontinuation of cases, raising systemic risks. The National Integrity Agency continues to operate effectively but faces resource constraints and a Constitutional Court ruling that weakens asset declaration requirements.

Media freedom remains under threat, with no progress in strengthening public service media independence. Private media face opaque political financing, and journalists continue to encounter harassment, surveillance, and strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs). Legislative unpredictability, characterized by the frequent use of Government Emergency Ordinances and inadequate public consultations, remains a primary concern for businesses and civil society.

The publication also contextualizes recent political events, including the Constitutional Court's annulment of the 2024 presidential election first round and the subsequent repeat election in 2025. Accreditation processes for National Human Rights Institutions remain stalled, reflecting broader challenges in institutional checks and balances.

This report is part of the broader [[European Semester]] framework, influencing Romania's compliance with EU fiscal and structural recommendations. The findings directly impact perceptions of [[Sovereign Credit Rating]] factors, particularly regarding political and geopolitical risk, institutional governance strength, and policy predictability.
