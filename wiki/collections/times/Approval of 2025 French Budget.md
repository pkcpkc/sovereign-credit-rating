# Approval of 2025 French Budget

## Event Details

In **February 2025**, the French government finalized and approved the national budget for the fiscal year 2025. This pivotal fiscal decision occurred in the wake of significant political turbulence, specifically the fall of Prime Minister **Barnier**'s government in December 2024, which underscored the fragility of political consensus and multi-level government coordination.

The approval of the 2025 Budget was a critical component of the French authorities' **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)**. The plan aims to bring the budget deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029, addressing concerns raised by the **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** regarding high public debt, which stood at 113.1% of GDP in 2024, and a deficit of 5.8%. The IMF has recommended a credible package of structural fiscal measures, including frontloaded efforts to rationalize current spending across central, local, and social security levels, rather than increasing the already high tax burden.

Key figures involved in the economic governance and oversight of this period included **Lombard** (Minister for the Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty), **de Montchalín** (Minister for Public Accounts), and **Villeroy de Galhau** (Central Bank Governor). The approval process took place against a backdrop of **geoeconomic fragmentation**, trade tensions, and domestic policy uncertainty, with growth projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025. The event is closely linked to ongoing **Sovereign Risk and Debt Sustainability Analysis** conducted by the IMF, which highlights the risks of fiscal slippages and revenue shortfalls despite France's strong debt payment culture and deep sovereign debt market.
