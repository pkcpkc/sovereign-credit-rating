# End of IMF Staff Discussions with Polish Officials

## Event Details

On October 17, 2024, the [[International Monetary Fund (IMF)]] concluded its staff discussions with Polish officials as part of the [[Poland]] 2024 [[Article IV Consultation]]. The mission was led by [[Jan Kees Martijn]], with support from members including [[Kareem Ismail]], [[Alla Myrvoda]], and [[Yang Yang]]. The discussions formed the basis for the staff report completed on December 16, 2024, and subsequently considered by the IMF Executive Board in January 2025.

The consultations assessed Poland’s robust economic recovery, characterized by GDP growth accelerating to 2.8% in 2024 and projected to reach 3.5% in 2025. This growth is driven by strong domestic demand, real wage growth, and the absorption of [[Next Generation EU (NGEU)]] funds. However, the mission highlighted significant macroeconomic imbalances. The general government deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, triggering the [[European Union (EU)]] [[Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)]] due to increased public sector wages, social benefits, and defense spending. Public debt is projected to rise from approximately 50% of GDP in 2023 to 65% by 2029 under the baseline scenario.

Key policy discussions focused on the following areas:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: Polish authorities presented a [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)]] aiming to reduce the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028. The IMF staff recommended frontloading consolidation measures in 2025 to rebuild fiscal buffers, support disinflation, and create room for monetary easing. Long-term sustainability concerns remain driven by population aging and climate transition costs.
*   **Monetary Policy and Inflation**: Inflation remained elevated at approximately 5% (headline) in late 2024, with sticky core inflation due to tight labor markets and administered price increases. The [[National Bank of Poland (NBP)]] maintained a tight monetary policy stance, which the IMF commended. The staff advised that interest rate cuts should commence only when there is clear evidence of decelerating wage growth, potentially around mid-2025.
*   **Financial Sector Stability**: The Polish banking sector remains resilient, with capital buffers well above regulatory requirements. However, risks persist from litigation over Swiss Franc (CHF) mortgages and potential disputes over the [[WIBOR]] benchmark interest rate. The IMF recommended eliminating distortions, such as tax incentives favoring public debt over private credit, to support credit recovery.
*   **External Sector**: Poland’s current account surplus narrowed to 0.3% of GDP in 2024 and is projected to move into deficit over the medium term. The [[real effective exchange rate]] appreciated significantly, weighing on cost-competitiveness. However, the external position remains supported by ample foreign exchange reserves and strong access to international capital markets.
*   **Structural Reforms**: The mission emphasized the need to address demographic headwinds by increasing labor force participation, particularly among women and older adults, and facilitating the integration of Ukrainian refugees and foreign workers. Additionally, reforms to accelerate the [[climate transition]] and decarbonize the coal-dependent energy sector were highlighted as critical for long-term growth and fiscal sustainability.
*   **Institutional Framework**: Improvements were noted in fiscal governance, including amendments to the stabilizing expenditure rule and enhanced transparency of [[Polish Development Bank (BGK)]] funds. The planned establishment of an independent fiscal council in 2026 was welcomed as a step toward stronger accountability.

The conclusions of these discussions were formalized in the IMF Executive Board assessment released on January 21, 2025, urging continued prudent policy management to anchor inflation expectations, consolidate public finances, and implement structural reforms to sustain long-term potential growth.
