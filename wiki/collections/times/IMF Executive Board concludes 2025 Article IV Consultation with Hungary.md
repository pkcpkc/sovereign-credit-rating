# IMF Executive Board Concludes 2025 Article IV Consultation with Hungary

## Event Details

The **[[International Monetary Fund]]** (IMF) Executive Board concluded its 2025 **[[Article IV Consultation]]** with **[[Hungary]]**, characterizing the nation's economy as being at a "challenging juncture." The consultation, which spanned from the staff mission in June to the Executive Board conclusion in August 2025, highlighted significant macroeconomic headwinds including stagnant output and persistently high inflation.

### Chronological Timeline

*   **2025-06-17**: IMF Staff Mission Concludes Discussions in Budapest.
*   **2025-07-25**: IMF Staff Report Completion for 2025 **[[Article IV Consultation]]**.
*   **2025-08-29**: IMF Executive Board Concludes 2025 **[[Article IV Consultation]]** with **[[Hungary]]**.

### Key Economic Assessments

The IMF assessment focused on several critical areas of **[[Macroeconomic Stability]]** and **[[Fiscal Policy]]**:

*   **Growth and Inflation**: Output stagnated over the past three years, with GDP contracting in Q1 2025. **[[Inflation]]** remained well above the **[[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]]**'s 3 percent target, driven by core services inflation and external price pressures.
*   **Fiscal Adjustment**: The IMF recommended high-quality fiscal adjustment to rebuild buffers, citing **[[Public Debt]]** projected to reach 79 percent of GDP by 2030. Recommendations included broadening the tax base, rationalizing energy subsidies, and strengthening governance regarding **[[State-Owned Enterprises]]** (SOEs).
*   **Monetary Policy**: The central bank was advised to maintain a tight policy stance, keeping the base rate at 6.5 percent into 2026 to anchor **[[Inflation Expectations]]**. The Board urged the phasing out of distortive price and margin controls.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Key priorities included boosting productivity, enhancing **[[Energy Security]]**, and improving governance to unlock suspended **[[European Union]]** funds (€19 billion).

### Risk Assessment

Downside risks were identified as tilted downward, with significant threats including:
*   Deepening **[[Geoeconomic Fragmentation]]** and potential trade escalations.
*   Suspension of **[[European Union]]** funds due to rule of law concerns.
*   Delays in fiscal or **[[Governance Reforms]]** ahead of the 2026 national elections.
*   Refinancing risks from **[[Retail Bonds]]** and high interest expenses.

Despite these challenges, the Hungarian authorities expressed confidence in their resilience, citing strong employment, pro-family measures, and recent investments in **[[Electric Vehicle]]** and battery production. The authorities maintained their commitment to the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP).
