# IMF Executive Board concludes 2025 Article IV Consultation with Hungary

## Event Details

On **2025-08-29**, the [[IMF]] Executive Board concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with [[Hungary]]. The consultation was based on a staff report completed on **2025-07-25**, following discussions with Hungarian officials that ended on **2025-06-17**. The mission was led by [[Anke Weber]], Head of Mission, in collaboration with [[Kristina Kostial]] and [[Jacques Miniane]], Department Heads at the IMF, and engaged with [[Márton Nagy]], Minister of the [[Ministry for National Economy]].

### Macroeconomic Outlook
The IMF assessed the Hungarian economy as being at a challenging juncture, characterized by stagnant output over the past three years and persistently high inflation.
*   **Growth**: Real GDP stagnated, with Q1 2025 showing a contraction of 0.4% y/y. Growth is projected to be modest at 0.7% in 2025, driven by private consumption and wage dynamics, with a recovery to 2.0% expected in 2026 contingent on an investment revival.
*   **Inflation**: Inflation remained well above the [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB)]]'s 3% target, standing at 4.4-4.6% in mid-2025, driven by core services and wage pressures. The IMF projects inflation to decelerate to 3% by 2027, conditioned on a tight monetary stance.

### Fiscal Policy and Debt Sustainability
The consultation highlighted significant concerns regarding fiscal sustainability and the need for additional adjustment.
*   **Debt Levels**: Public debt reached 73.5% of GDP in 2024 and is projected to rise to approximately 79% by 2030 under current policies.
*   **Deficit Targets**: Current announced policies fall short of authorities' budget targets. The IMF recommends an additional cumulative fiscal adjustment of ~2% of GDP over 2025-2028 to bring the deficit below 3% by 2027 and achieve a structural primary surplus of around 1.75% of GDP.
*   **Risks**: High gross financing needs (averaging 17% of GDP), rising foreign exchange (FX) debt share (over 30%), and significant contingent liabilities from [[State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs)]] pose risks. The IMF assesses the risk of sovereign stress as "moderate."

### Monetary Policy and Financial Sector
*   **Monetary Stance**: The [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB)]] maintained its base rate at 6.5% since September 2024. The IMF supported maintaining a tight stance into 2026 to durably return inflation to target.
*   **Regulatory Distortions**: The IMF criticized regulatory interventions such as price, interest, and margin caps, arguing they distort market signals, defer price pressures, and weaken monetary policy transmission. These measures are advised to be phased out.
*   **Financial Stability**: The banking sector is assessed as well-capitalized (CAR 20.5%) and liquid. However, vulnerabilities are increasing, particularly regarding the sovereign-bank nexus, corporate sector risks amid high interest rates, and buoyant housing prices fueled by subsidized lending.

### External Sector and Geopolitical Risks
*   **EU Funds**: A critical downside risk is the suspension of approximately €19 billion in [[European Union]] funds (including [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] and [[Cohesion funds]]) due to rule of law and governance concerns. The completion of necessary governance reforms is essential to unlock these funds.
*   **Current Account**: Hungary recorded a current account surplus of 2.2% of GDP in 2024, which is assessed as substantially stronger than fundamentals imply.
*   **Geoeconomic Fragmentation**: Risks include deepening geoeconomic fragmentation, potential escalation in trade measures (particularly US tariffs), and intensification of regional conflicts affecting energy security.

### Structural Reforms and Governance
The IMF emphasized the need for structural reforms to boost productivity, enhance energy security, and improve governance. Key recommendations included:
*   Reducing barriers to factor mobility and limiting state aid to foster firm dynamism.
*   Strengthening access to risk capital for young firms.
*   Phasing out energy subsidies and administrative overhead.
*   Improving transparency in off-budget financing vehicles and SOE performance.
