# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report (Italy Chapter)

## Event Details

The publication of the **2025 Rule of Law Report (Italy Chapter)** on July 8, 2025, serves as a comprehensive evaluation of Italy's legal landscape, highlighting a period of intense legislative activity and structural transformation.

### Justice System and Constitutional Reform
The report documents a significant push toward judicial reform, notably the **Chamber of Deputies' approval on January 16, 2025**, of a draft constitutional reform aimed at separating the careers of judges and prosecutors. While this move was met with reservations from the High Council for the Judiciary, it reflects a broader trend of structural change. Complementing these reforms, new rules on mediation and assisted negotiation came into force on **January 25, 2025**, designed to decrease the volume of legal proceedings.

Technologically, Italy has seen successful digitalization in tax, civil, and administrative justice. However, the criminal justice sector remains a bottleneck due to technical hurdles with the *APP* platform. Despite these issues, the report notes a positive trend in backlog management, with clearance rates exceeding 100% in many jurisdictions throughout 2023.

### Anti-Corruption and Governance
A milestone in anti-corruption was reached in **May 2025**, when the Constitutional Court declared the law abrogating "abuse of public office" compliant with the Constitution and international standards. While corruption perception remains stable, the report identifies ongoing needs for a comprehensive national lobbying register and more robust public procurement integrity.

### Media Freedom and Civil Space
The report highlights a complex environment for media freedom. While the regulator AGCOM remains functionally independent, journalists face increasing challenges, including a "chilling effect" from the *Costa amendment* and rising intimidation. The report also references the **June 6, 2025**, COPASIR report regarding "Graphite" spyware. Furthermore, the adoption of the new **Security Law on June 4, 2025**, has sparked international concern regarding the potential narrowing of civic space and restrictions on the freedom of assembly.

### Institutional Checks
The report concludes by noting persistent challenges in human rights and international obligations, specifically the slow implementation of European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) judgments, with an average delay of over six years.
