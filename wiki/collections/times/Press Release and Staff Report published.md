# Press Release and Staff Report published

## Event Details

On **July 22, 2025**, the [[International Monetary Fund]] published the Press Release and Staff Report concluding the 2025 Article IV Consultation with [[Italy]]. This publication followed the conclusion of discussions with Italian officials in May 2025 and the IMF Executive Board's conclusion of the consultation on July 18, 2025.

The report, authored by a staff team led by **Lone Christiansen**, with Department Heads **Helge Berger** and **Rishi Goyal**, and supported by Executive Director **Riccardo Ercoli**, assesses Italy's economic performance and outlook. Key findings from the published report include:

*   **Economic Performance**: Italy demonstrated resilience in 2024 with real GDP growth of 0.7% and record-high employment levels. The outlook for 2025 remains moderate (0.5% growth) with an expected pick-up to 0.8% in 2026, supported by [[National Recovery and Resilience Plan]] (NRRP) investments.
*   **Fiscal Policy**: The 2024 fiscal deficit improved to 3.4% of GDP, allowing Italy to return to a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP. However, public debt remains elevated at 135.3% of GDP. The IMF staff recommended a more front-loaded fiscal adjustment to reach a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027. The Italian authorities, represented by Minister **Giancarlo Giorgetti**, committed to a gradual seven-year adjustment under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), aiming for a deficit below 3% by 2026.
*   **Financial Stability**: The banking sector showed strong profitability and low Non-Performing Loans (2.7%). The [[Systemic Risk Buffer]] (SyRB) was increased to 1%. Concerns were raised regarding vulnerabilities in Less Significant Institutions (LSIs) and the continued significance of the sovereign-bank nexus.
*   **Structural Challenges**: The report highlights persistent issues including weak productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-average female labor force participation. While NRRP implementation accelerated (54% of milestones achieved), spending execution remains sluggish. The IMF emphasized the need for post-NRRP reforms to boost human capital and innovation.
*   **External Sector**: Italy posted a current account surplus of 1.1% of GDP in 2024, with the Net International Investment Position improving to 15.3% of GDP. The IMF assessed the external position as weaker than warranted by fundamentals, advocating for further fiscal consolidation and productivity reforms.

The publication underscores the need for continued structural reforms to enhance growth potential and fiscal sustainability amidst global uncertainties, including trade tensions and geopolitical risks.
