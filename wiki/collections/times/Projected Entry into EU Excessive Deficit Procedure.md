# Projected Entry into EU Excessive Deficit Procedure

## Event Details

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) projects that Malta will enter the European Union’s Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) in **2025**, despite the country’s strong economic performance and sovereign debt levels remaining well below the EU’s 60% of GDP ceiling. This projection stems from the IMF’s 2024 Article IV Consultation, which highlighted persistent fiscal challenges driven largely by energy subsidies.

### Key Drivers and Context

*   **Fiscal Consolidation Targets**: The Maltese government, led by Minister for Finance and Employment **Clyde Caruana**, has committed to reducing the structural deficit to **2.6% of GDP by 2027**. However, the current fiscal stance relies heavily on broad spending restraint and subsidies.
*   **Energy Subsidies**: A primary factor in the projected EDP entry is the cost of fixed-energy price policies, which account for approximately **20% of the fiscal deficit**. The IMF recommends phasing out these broad subsidies in favor of targeted support to free up fiscal space for investment, innovation, and green transition initiatives.
*   **Sovereign Debt Profile**: While the deficit trajectory raises EDP concerns, Malta’s debt sustainability remains strong. Sovereign debt is projected to rise marginally to ~50% of GDP but stays safely below the EU limit. The country benefits from low interest rates relative to growth, a large positive Net International Investment Position (~95% of GDP), and the elimination of external financing constraints via Euro adoption.
*   **Policy Recommendations**: To mitigate risks and ensure long-term stability, the IMF Executive Board endorsed recommendations to:
    *   Develop a long-term fiscal framework addressing aging-related spending and climate transition costs.
    *   Reform Corporate Income Tax (CIT) aligned with the EU’s Pillar II directive (implementation deadline set for 2029).
    *   Shift growth strategy from labor-intensive services (tourism, gaming) toward productivity-driven innovation.

### Timeline of Related Events

*   **November 15, 2024**: Conclusion of IMF Staff Discussions with Malta officials.
*   **December 17, 2024**: Completion of the IMF Staff Report for the 2024 Article IV Consultation.
*   **January 15, 2025**: IMF Executive Board concludes the Article IV Consultation.
*   **2025**: Projected entry of Malta into the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure.
*   **2027**: Target year for achieving the structural deficit reduction goal of 2.6% of GDP.
*   **2029**: Deadline for full implementation of the EU Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive.
*   **2030**: Target year for R&D spending to reach 2% of GDP and phase-out of the tax refund system.
