# Enactment of Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation Order

## Event Details

On **July 1, 2024**, Malta enacted a specific legal order concerning Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs). This legislative action was undertaken as part of the government's efforts to align national frameworks with European standards and protect journalists and civil society actors from abusive litigation.

According to the **European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report**, this enactment was identified as a positive step within the **Media Pluralism and Freedom** pillar. The report notes that while the new anti-SLAPP law was enacted, it faced criticism for its scope. Specifically, the legislation was criticized for not covering domestic lawsuits, potentially limiting its effectiveness in protecting local journalists and activists from strategic litigation originating within the country.

This event occurred during a period of broader scrutiny regarding media freedom in Malta, where the government was also preparing additional legislation to align with the **European Media Freedom Act**. Despite the enactment of the anti-SLAPP order, the report highlights that no progress was made in safeguarding the independent governance of public service media, which continues to face high risks of political bias. Furthermore, additional pending legislation aimed at protecting journalists and improving access to official documents remained unbundled in Parliament as of mid-2025.

### Key Context & Implications

- **Legislative Scope**: The order addressed cross-border strategic lawsuits, aligning with EU directives, but was noted for its limitation regarding domestic cases.
- **Media Freedom Landscape**: The enactment occurred amidst broader challenges, including pending legislation to improve the working environment for journalists and the lack of independent governance for public service media.
- **Related Developments**:
  - **April 29, 2025**: The Court of Justice of the European Union ruled that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme was contrary to EU Law.
  - **May 13, 2025**: The government tabled comprehensive constitutional reforms concerning the justice sector.
  - **Mid-2025**: Draft legislation for a National Human Rights Institution and a civil society regulatory framework remained under preparation, with civil society space assessed as "narrowed."
