# Chamber of Deputies approved the draft constitutional reform separating careers of judges and prosecutors

## Event Details

On **January 16, 2025**, the **Chamber of Deputies** of Italy approved the draft constitutional reform aimed at separating the careers of judges and prosecutors. This legislative move was part of a broader effort to enhance governmental stability and address institutional checks and balances, as noted in the **European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report**.

The reform had been a stated priority of the Italian Government, with the **Minister for Institutional Reforms** reiterating its importance as early as December 2024. However, the proposal faced significant resistance from the judicial community. Prior to the Chamber's approval, the **High Council for the Judiciary** issued a critical opinion on the draft in January 2025, expressing concerns that the separation of careers could undermine judicial autonomy and representation. These reservations were echoed by various magistrates’ associations, who argued against the potential erosion of the unified judicial career structure.

Following the Chamber's approval, the reform entered the scrutiny phase in the **Senate**. The European Commission highlighted this development as a key factor in Italy's institutional stability, noting both the government's push for reform and the resulting tensions within the justice system. The ongoing debate reflects broader challenges in balancing political reform with the principles of judicial independence and separation of powers.
