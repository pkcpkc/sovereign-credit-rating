# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Hungary

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the European Commission published its assessment of the rule of law situation in Hungary. The report provides a comprehensive overview of the country's legal framework, highlighting a dichotomy between modernization in judicial administration and systemic erosion of democratic checks and balances.

**Key Developments leading to and within the report:**
- **Judicial Reform and Remuneration:** The assessment notes significant progress in increasing salaries for judges and prosecutors, with a goal of a 48% increase by 2027. However, it highlights friction with the National Judicial Council (NJC), which withdrew from a four-party agreement in early 2025 due to a lack of consultation on reforms increasing the minimum judge age to 35 and extending retirement to 70.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework:** While low-level codes of conduct have been established, the report identifies a persistent failure to address high-level corruption. The Integrity Authority faces significant data access hurdles, and transparency remains low regarding asset declarations for senior political functions.
- **Media and Civil Society:** The report paints a restrictive picture of media pluralism, noting that the National Media and Infocommunications Authority (NMHH) remains high-risk. It specifically mentions the increasing pressure on journalists from the Sovereignty Protection Office and the "obstructed" environment for civil society organizations (CSOs) due to restrictions on foreign funding.
- **Institutional Governance:** A major concern cited is the continued use of emergency powers ("state of danger"), which allows the government to override parliamentary acts, creating legal uncertainty for the single market and private investments.

The report concludes that while digitalization and remuneration improvements are present, the lack of public consultation on legislation and the "chilling effect" on the judiciary remain primary obstacles to the rule of law.
