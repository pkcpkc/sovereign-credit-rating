# Council Recommendation endorsing Italy's MTFSP and ending Excessive Deficit Procedure

## Event Details

On **January 21, 2025**, the Council of the European Union adopted a recommendation endorsing Italy’s **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP)**. This pivotal decision officially ended the **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)** against Italy, marking a significant shift in the EU-Italy fiscal relationship under the reformed Stability and Growth Pact framework.

The endorsement followed the submission of Italy's MTFSP on **October 15, 2024**, which outlined a seven-year fiscal adjustment path aimed at ensuring debt sustainability. The Council's decision was based on an assessment that Italy had demonstrated compliance with EU fiscal rules and a credible commitment to structural reforms.

### Context and Key Drivers

The termination of the EDP and the approval of the MTFSP were driven by several key factors documented in the **2025 Country Report on Italy**:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation:** Italy achieved a **primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP in 2024**, the first since 2019. This improvement was primarily driven by the phase-out of costly tax credits, such as the "Superbonus" housing renovation scheme, and energy support measures.
*   **Debt Dynamics:** Despite the primary surplus, Italy's public debt-to-GDP ratio was projected to rise to **136.7% in 2025**. This increase was attributed to lagged borrowing needs from past tax credits and high interest expenditure (approx. 3.9% of GDP). The MTFSP aims to address these medium-term sustainability risks, particularly those stemming from rising pension costs due to demographic aging.
*   **Structural Reforms:** The plan aligns with broader structural reform agendas supported by the **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRP)**. Key areas include improving judicial efficiency, enhancing labor market participation (particularly for women and youth), and addressing regional disparities between the North and the South (Mezzogiorno).
*   **Institutional Strength:** The endorsement highlighted the strength of Italy's institutional framework for fiscal surveillance, particularly the role of the independent **Parliamentary Budget Office (PBO)**, and the government's commitment to the new EU fiscal rules.

### Implications

The ending of the EDP allows Italy greater flexibility in fiscal policy while maintaining strict oversight through the MTFSP mechanism. It signals confidence in Italy's ability to manage its high debt levels through structural reforms and gradual fiscal adjustment. However, challenges remain, including sluggish productivity growth, low R&D investment, and persistent regional inequalities. The success of the MTFSP will depend on Italy's ability to implement these reforms effectively and maintain fiscal discipline in the medium term.
