# Discussions with Italian officials end

## Event Details

The discussions between the **International Monetary Fund** (IMF) staff and Italian authorities regarding the 2025 Article IV Consultation concluded on **May 28, 2025**. These talks were part of a broader engagement period that began on May 14, 2025, aimed at assessing Italy's economic resilience, fiscal sustainability, and structural reform progress.

The consultations focused on several key areas:

*   **Economic Performance**: Recognition of Italy's resilient economic activity in early 2025, with real GDP growing 0.7% in 2024 and employment reaching record highs.
*   **Fiscal Policy**: Debates over the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). While the IMF staff recommended a front-loaded adjustment to achieve a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027 to ensure debt sustainability, Italian authorities advocated for a more gradual seven-year adjustment path targeting a deficit below 3% by 2026.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Assessment of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) implementation, which had reached 54% of milestones but faced challenges in spending execution. Discussions highlighted the need for post-NRRP reforms to address weak productivity growth, population aging, and low female labor force participation.
*   **Financial Stability**: Review of the banking sector's health, noting strong capital buffers and low Non-Performing Loans (NPLs), while identifying vulnerabilities among Less Significant Institutions (LSIs) and the persistent sovereign-bank nexus.

Following the conclusion of discussions, the IMF Staff Report was completed on July 1, 2025. The IMF Executive Board subsequently concluded the 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy on July 18, 2025, and the Press Release and Staff Report were published on July 22, 2025.

Key participants in the discussions included **Lone Christiansen** (IMF Staff Team Head), **Helge Berger** (Department Head), **Rishi Goyal** (Department Head), and **Riccardo Ercoli** (Executive Director for Italy) from the IMF, alongside Italian officials including **Giancarlo Giorgetti** (Minister of Economy and Finance) and **Fabio Signorini** (Senior Deputy Governor, Bank of Italy).
