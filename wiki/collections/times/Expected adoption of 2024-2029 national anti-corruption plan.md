# Expected adoption of 2024-2029 national anti-corruption plan

## Event Details

In July 2025, France was expected to adopt its new **2024-2029 National Anti-Corruption Plan**, a strategic framework aimed at reinforcing the country's anti-corruption architecture and addressing persistent gaps in transparency and prosecution consistency. This adoption occurs within a broader context of institutional strengthening, following the 2023 reform of the status of magistrates and ongoing reorganizations at key bodies such as the [[Anti-Corruption Agency]] (AFA) and the [[High Authority for Transparency in Public Life]] (HATVP).

While France maintains a relatively low corruption perception score, the European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report highlighted that challenges remain regarding high-level transparency and the practical implementation of anti-corruption measures. Specifically, lobbying disclosure requirements continue to exclude officials at the top-executive level, representing a significant gap in the regulatory framework. The new plan is anticipated to address these structural weaknesses alongside other initiatives, such as the expected launch of an online register for foreign lobbyists by the HATVP. The plan's adoption is part of France's response to EU monitoring mechanisms, aiming to improve the efficiency of corruption prosecution—where case resolution times have been notably high—and enhance the protection of whistleblowers, which currently faces resource constraints.
