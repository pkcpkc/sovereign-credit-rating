# Hungary Submits Addendum to Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan

## Event Details

In December 2024, the [[Hungary]] government submitted an addendum to its Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSF) to the [[European Commission]]. This submission occurred in the context of ongoing macroeconomic challenges, including subdued growth, persistent inflationary pressures, and significant structural vulnerabilities. The MTFSF process is a key component of the EU's Economic Governance framework, requiring member states to outline their fiscal and structural reform paths over a multi-year horizon.

The 2025 Country Report for Hungary, issued by the European Commission in June 2025, highlights several critical factors influencing this fiscal planning:

*   **Fiscal Sustainability Concerns:** Public debt was projected to rise to 74.5% of GDP by 2025, with debt-servicing costs remaining among the highest in the EU. The report criticized the budgetary framework for lacking multiannual spending ceilings, frequent revisions, and the use of emergency decrees that limit transparency and accountability.
*   **Budgetary Framework Weaknesses:** The 2026 budget law revised the deficit target from 2.9% to 3.7% of GDP. The medium-term budgetary framework was criticized for being unstable and lacking clear policy direction, with frequent revisions undermining predictability.
*   **Structural Challenges:** Hungary faces severe challenges in its business environment, marked by regulatory volatility, sector-specific taxes, and state intervention that crowds out private actors. Innovation is hampered by low R&D intensity and skills shortages.
*   **Social and Regional Disparities:** Poverty and inequality are rising, with the at-risk-of-poverty-or-social-exclusion (AROPE) rate increasing to 20.2% in 2024. Significant economic and social disparities exist between Budapest/Pest and other regions, with less developed regions facing higher unemployment and poverty rates.
*   **EU Fund Implementation:** As of mid-2025, Hungary had fulfilled 0% of the milestones and targets in its Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP), delaying disbursements. Implementation is hindered by a lack of effective mechanisms to protect EU financial interests, anti-corruption weaknesses, and control procedure failures.

The submission of the addendum was part of a broader series of fiscal and structural adjustments, including the submission of the initial MTFSF in November 2024 and the activation of the National Escape Clause for Defence Spending in April 2025. These actions reflect the government's response to external shocks and internal structural constraints, while facing scrutiny from EU institutions regarding rule of law compliance and fiscal discipline.
