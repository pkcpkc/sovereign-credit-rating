# Publication year of S&P sovereign credit rating methodology document

## Event Details

In **2017**, [[S&P Global Ratings]] published the comprehensive framework for its sovereign credit rating methodology, which serves as the foundational document for assessing sovereign creditworthiness. This methodology was subsequently highlighted and dissected in a 2024 presentation titled "Financial Economics for Citizens: The S&P Rating Methodology - Overview" by [[Alex Stomper]], a lecturer at [[HU Berlin]].

The 2017 methodology document establishes a structured, five-pillar assessment framework used to determine sovereign credit ratings:

1.  **Institutional Assessment**: Evaluates government effectiveness, policymaking stability, transparency, accountability, "debt payment culture," and security risks.
2.  **Economic Assessment**: Analyzes GDP per capita (indicating the tax/funding base), growth prospects, and economic diversity/volatility.
3.  **External Assessment**: Examines currency status, external liquidity (ability to meet foreign obligations), and the external asset/liability position.
4.  **Fiscal Assessment**: Described as the "heart" of the rating process, this pillar is divided into fiscal performance/flexibility (flows) and debt burden/structure (stocks). It also includes an analysis of contingent liabilities, such as banking industry risks (assessed via [[Banking Industry Country Risk Analysis|BICRA]]), public enterprises, and off-budget guarantees.
5.  **Monetary Assessment**: Considers the exchange rate regime and the credibility of monetary policy, including central bank independence and inflation control.

The publication of this methodology is significant not only for its structured approach but also for the explicit allowance of "analytical discretion." The framework permits "supplemental adjustment factors" (e.g., event risk, extreme liquidity weakness) and allows analysts to override indicative ratings. This discretion has led to critiques regarding the "replicability" of ratings, prompting academic initiatives like the "Citizen Science" project proposed by Stomper to test the consistency of the methodology's application across European countries.
