# European Court of Justice rules Malta’s investor citizenship scheme contrary to EU Law

## Event Details

On **April 29, 2025**, the **Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU)** issued a landmark ruling declaring that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme is contrary to **EU Law**. The Court characterized the scheme as the "commercialisation" of Union citizenship, a decision that followed a referral by the **European Commission** in 2023.

### Context and Implications

This ruling was a significant development highlighted in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta**, published on July 8, 2025, by the European Commission. The report places the decision within the broader **Anti-Corruption Framework** assessment for Malta, noting it as evidence of past regulatory weaknesses in the financial sector.

The judgment underscores ongoing challenges regarding institutional accountability and the rule of law in Malta. While the report notes strengths such as high perceived judicial independence and the completion of the transfer of prosecutorial powers to the **Office of the Attorney General**, it also highlights critical threats including:
*   **Regulatory Weaknesses:** The illegal nature of the citizenship scheme pointed to insufficient oversight mechanisms.
*   **Corruption Perceptions:** Malta maintained a low **Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI)** score of 46/100, with weak capacity observed in the **Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC)**.
*   **Judicial Efficiency:** Persistent delays in judicial proceedings and case backlogs continue to undermine legal certainty and economic activities.

The CJEU's decision serves as a pivotal moment in the EU's enforcement of rule of law standards, reinforcing the principle that citizenship cannot be treated as a commodity. It aligns with broader concerns raised by the **Venice Commission** and the **European Court of Human Rights** regarding checks and balances and institutional integrity in Malta.
