# Real GDP Growth 0.7%

## Event Details

In 2024, Italy’s real GDP growth stabilized at **0.7%**, according to the European Commission's **2025 Country Report**. This moderate expansion was primarily driven by investment financed through the **[[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] (RRP)** and supported by net external demand. However, domestic private consumption remained subdued as households prioritized rebuilding savings.

The growth figure of 0.7% reflects a broader economic picture characterized by mixed indicators: while the labor market showed resilience with record employment rates, the economy continued to face structural vulnerabilities, including sluggish productivity growth, low R&D investment, and significant regional disparities between the North and the South (**[[Mezzogiorno]]**).

This performance occurred against a backdrop of fiscal consolidation, where Italy achieved a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP in 2024, partly due to the phase-out of the "Superbonus" housing tax credits. Despite the positive growth and primary balance, the public debt-to-GDP ratio was projected to rise to 136.7% in 2025, driven by lagged borrowing needs and high interest expenditure, underscoring the limited fiscal flexibility available to the government.
