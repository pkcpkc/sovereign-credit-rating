# Publication of 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Slovakia

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the [[European Commission]] published the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter for [[Slovakia]]. This report, issued by the Directorate-General for Justice and Consumers (DG JUST), provides a comprehensive assessment of the rule of law situation in Slovakia, highlighting significant concerns regarding judicial independence, anti-corruption frameworks, media pluralism, and institutional checks and balances.

The report indicates a stagnation or deterioration in key indicators, driven by systemic risks rather than isolated incidents. Key findings include the erosion of judicial independence, where perceived independence remains low (32% among the general public and 34% among companies). The independence of the [[Judicial Council]] is described as compromised due to vague dismissal procedures and political appointments. Furthermore, reforms to the [[Criminal Code]] introduced in 2024, which lowered sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption, have negatively impacted high-level prosecutions, leading to the closure or reclassification of several cases.

In the anti-corruption domain, the dissolution of specialized bodies such as the [[National Crime Agency (NAKA)]] and the [[Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO)]] has resulted in operational delays, loss of specialized expertise, and a significant drop in registered corruption cases. The [[Prosecutor General]] retains broad powers to annul decisions and reassign cases, which undermines investigative independence. Additionally, public procurement rules have been weakened, increasing corruption risks in strategic investments.

Regarding media and civil society, public service media ([[Slovak Television and Radio]]) has become more politicized following restructuring that increased political control over governance. Journalist safety has deteriorated, with no progress on legal safeguards against Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs). Civil society faces increased administrative burdens due to new NGO reporting laws adopted in April 2025, which are viewed by stakeholders as tools for political control.

The report also highlights the frequent use of fast-track legislative procedures, with 58 out of 131 laws in 2024 adopted with limited public participation. Proposed constitutional amendments affecting the primacy of [[European Union]] law were discussed but postponed, remaining a point of intense dialogue between the Commission and Slovak authorities.

Key figures involved in these developments include [[Robert Fico]] (Prime Minister), [[Robert Kaliňák]] (Minister of Interior), [[Elena Podkonická]] (Minister of Culture), [[Marian Jančík]] (Prosecutor General), [[Matej Števčík]] (President of the Judicial Council), and [[Peter Kresák]] (President of the Supreme Court).
