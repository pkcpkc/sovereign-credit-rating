# High Court of Cassation and Justice decision 37/2024 on lex mitior

## Event Details

On **2024-06-17**, the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]] (HCCJ) of Romania issued **Decision 37/2024**, a landmark ruling concerning the application of *lex mitior* (the principle that a more lenient criminal law applies retroactively) to statute of limitations issues. This decision is part of a series of rulings, including **Decision 16/2024** issued on 2024-09-16, which have significantly impacted the Romanian anti-corruption framework.

### Context and Rationale
The decision emerged from the complex legal landscape following the **2018-06-25** [[Constitutional Court of Romania]] ruling on the statute of limitations (Article 155(1)), which created a legal void between 2018 and 2022. This void was partially addressed by the **2022-05-30** [[Government Emergency Ordinance]] that filled the gap, followed by the entry into force of the [[Justice Laws]] in **2022-10**. However, subsequent interpretations by the HCCJ have applied *lex mitior* to cases extending beyond this specific period, arguing that the uncertainty in the law during the transition periods should benefit the accused.

### Impact and Consequences
The application of Decision 37/2024 has led to systemic repercussions for the [[National Anti-Corruption Directorate]] (DNA) and the broader [[Rule of Law]] in Romania:
*   **Discontinuation of Cases:** Numerous high-profile corruption cases have been discontinued, and existing convictions annulled, due to the expiration of limitation periods as interpreted by the Court.
*   **Impunity Risks:** The [[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Romania]] (published on 2025-07-08 by the [[European Commission]]) highlights these rulings as raising serious concerns regarding impunity and compatibility with [[European Union]] law. The report notes that the case-law extends beyond the 2018-2022 legal void, potentially weakening the effectiveness of anti-corruption efforts.
*   **Perception of Independence:** The decisions have contributed to an average perception of judicial independence and scrutiny over the transparency of judicial promotions and disciplinary sanctions.

### Subsequent Developments
Due to the systemic implications and questions regarding EU law compatibility, the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]] sought clarification from the [[Court of Justice of the European Union]] (CJEU). On **2025-04**, the HCCJ referred a question to the CJEU in **Case C-280/25 (Lin II)**, marking a critical juncture in determining the final interpretation of these statute of limitations issues within the EU legal framework.
