# Publication of 2025 Rule of Law Report Poland Chapter

## Event Details

On **8 July 2025**, the European Commission published the 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Poland. This publication serves as a critical assessment of the rule of law situation in [[Poland]], highlighting significant but uneven progress under the government led by [[Donald Tusk]]. The report emphasizes the implementation of a comprehensive Action Plan, which aims to address long-standing systemic issues regarding judicial independence, anti-corruption frameworks, and media pluralism.

Key developments documented in the report include:

*   **Judicial Reform and Independence**: The government introduced draft laws to separate the functions of the [[Minister of Justice]] and the [[Prosecutor General]], a move designed to ensure prosecutorial independence. Additionally, legislation was proposed to address judges appointed by the controversial post-2017 [[National Council for the Judiciary]] (NCJ) and to dismantle the [[Supreme Court]]'s Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs. Despite these efforts, perceived judicial independence remains very low (26% among the general public).
*   **Constitutional Tribunal Crisis**: A major institutional conflict persists with the [[Constitutional Tribunal]]. Since March 2024, the government has refused to publish the Tribunal's judgments, arguing its illegitimacy. The Tribunal has ruled on the constitutionality of certain EU treaty provisions, leading to an ongoing infringement procedure before the [[Court of Justice of the European Union]].
*   **Anti-Corruption Measures**: Positive steps include the full operationalization of the [[European Public Prosecutor Office]] (EPPO) in Poland as of January 2025 and the establishment of a new Central Office for Combating Corruption. However, systemic risks in public procurement and lobbying regulations remain significant concerns.
*   **Media Pluralism**: The report notes a reduction in politically biased content in public service media and more even distribution of state advertising. However, challenges regarding the independence of the [[National Broadcasting Council]] (KRRiT), media ownership transparency, and journalist safety persist.
*   **Checks and Balances**: The [[Ombudsperson]], held by [[Waldemar Kowalewski]], continues to hold UN 'A' status. Civil society space has improved from 'Obstructed' to 'Narrowed'. Implementation of judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] remains slow, with 54 leading judgments pending as of June 2025.

The publication underscores that while Poland has made strides in legislative reforms, perceived judicial independence remains critically low, and significant risks to institutional checks and balances remain.
