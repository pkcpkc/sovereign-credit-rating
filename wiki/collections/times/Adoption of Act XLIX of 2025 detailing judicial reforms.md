# Adoption of Act XLIX of 2025 detailing judicial reforms

## Event Details

On June 11, 2025, the Hungarian National Assembly adopted **Act XLIX of 2025**, a legislative package detailing significant judicial reforms. This adoption occurred against a backdrop of escalating tensions between the government and the judiciary, marked by the December 2024 resignation of the President of the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] following protests over previous reform measures.

The Act followed a breakdown in negotiations, specifically the withdrawal of the National Judicial Council from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms in January 2025, due to concerns over the consultation process and the substance of the proposed changes. Key elements associated with the reform cycle leading up to this Act included raising the minimum age for judges to 35 and allowing service until age 70.

The adoption triggered immediate scrutiny from international bodies. On June 16, 2025, the [[Venice Commission]] issued an opinion on the judicial and prosecutorial amendments, expressing concerns regarding their compatibility with rule of law standards. The [[European Commission]] highlighted these developments in its 2025 Rule of Law Report, noting that while judicial salaries had increased, the reforms lacked adequate consultation with the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] and raised concerns about judicial independence and the transparency of case allocation in lower courts. The report also noted persistent issues with the "chilling effect" within the judiciary, as previously identified by the Council of Europe Committee of Ministers in March 2025.
