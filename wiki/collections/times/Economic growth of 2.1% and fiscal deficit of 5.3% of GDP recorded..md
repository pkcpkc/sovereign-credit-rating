# Economic growth of 2.1% and fiscal deficit of 5.3% of GDP recorded.

## Event Details

In 2024, Slovakia recorded an economic growth rate of **2.1%**, driven primarily by domestic consumption. Despite this positive growth trajectory, the country continued to face significant fiscal challenges, with the general government deficit remaining high at **5.3% of GDP**. This persistent excessive deficit was largely attributed to permanent spending increases in structural areas such as pensions, healthcare, and military salaries, which outpaced revenue collection efforts.

The fiscal imbalance occurred against a backdrop of deteriorating competitiveness, where sluggish labour productivity growth failed to keep pace with rising unit labour costs. Additionally, the economy remained heavily reliant on foreign-owned manufacturing, particularly the automotive sector, making it vulnerable to external shocks and trade tensions. While the growth figure of 2.1% provided a short-term stabilizing effect, the underlying structural rigidities and high structural deficit highlighted the urgent need for the comprehensive fiscal consolidation package (including VAT and corporate tax hikes) that was subsequently approved in October 2024.
