# IMF Executive Board Concludes 2025 Article IV Consultation with France

## Event Details

The **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with France on July 11, 2025, following a mission that began in May 2025. The consultation process involved multiple phases, including staff discussions starting May 12, report completion on June 25, and final executive board discussion and conclusion on July 11 and 14, 2025, respectively.

The IMF Executive Board welcomed France's economic resilience, robust labor market conditions, and progress in disinflation despite high global uncertainty. However, directors emphasized urgent concerns regarding **Sovereign Debt** levels, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, and significant fiscal slippages. The IMF recommended a credible package of structural fiscal measures to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029, advocating for rationalization of current spending rather than increasing the already high tax burden.

Key outcomes and assessments from the consultation include:

*   **Economic Outlook**: Growth is projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025 due to trade tensions, weak external demand, and policy uncertainty, with a recovery to 1% expected in 2026. Risks are skewed to the downside due to geoeconomic fragmentation and domestic political instability, including the fall of **Prime Minister Barnier**'s government in late 2024.
*   **Fiscal Policy**: The IMF highlighted the need for a frontloaded structural fiscal effort of 1.1% of GDP in 2026. The French authorities' **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)** aims to address these challenges, though implementation risks remain high due to unidentified measures and potential revenue shortfalls.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Emphasis was placed on boosting productivity through the green and digital transitions, particularly **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** adoption. Recommendations included easing regulatory burdens and enhancing access to finance for high-growth firms.
*   **Financial Sector Health**: The 2025 **Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP)** confirmed the resilience of the French banking sector, with healthy capital and liquidity buffers. Risks from corporate indebtedness and commercial real estate require continued monitoring.
*   **Key Personnel**: The consultation involved key French officials including **Minister Lombard**, **Minister de Montchalín**, and **Central Bank Governor Villeroy de Galhau**, alongside IMF staff led by Mission Head **Manuela Goretti** and Managing Director **Christine Lagarde**. Executive Director **Arnaud Buisse** represented France on the IMF Executive Board.

This consultation marks a critical juncture for French fiscal sustainability, occurring in the wake of a **Moody's** downgrade of France's credit rating to Aa3 in December 2024 and amidst the aftermath of the Paris Olympics' economic impact.
