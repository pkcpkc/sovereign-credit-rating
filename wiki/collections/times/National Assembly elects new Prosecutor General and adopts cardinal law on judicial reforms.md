# National Assembly elects new Prosecutor General and adopts cardinal law on judicial reforms

## Event Details

On June 11, 2025, the Hungarian National Assembly reached a significant milestone in its judicial restructuring by electing a new Prosecutor General and adopting a cardinal law regarding judicial reforms. 

This legislative action occurred within a broader context of significant changes to the Hungarian justice system, including:
- **Judicial Remuneration:** A push toward a 48% increase in salaries for judges and prosecutors by 2027.
- **Appointment Rules:** The implementation of new rules that raised the minimum age for judges to 35 and extended the retirement age to 70.
- **Judicial Oversight:** The introduction of a mechanism allowing judges to review prosecutorial decisions regarding non-investigation of corruption, which saw a doubling in usage since 2024.

The reforms were part of a complex period where the National Judicial Council (NJC) expressed concerns over the lack of consultation on legislative amendments, while the government continued to navigate the "state of danger" emergency powers and efforts to balance judicial efficiency with institutional independence.
