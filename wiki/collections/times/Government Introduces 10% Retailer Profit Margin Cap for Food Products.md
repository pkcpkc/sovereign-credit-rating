# Government Introduces 10% Retailer Profit Margin Cap for Food Products

## Event Details

In March 2025, the Hungarian Government implemented a regulatory measure capping retailer profit margins on food products at 10%. This decision was part of a broader strategy to address persistent inflationary pressures and ensure the affordability of essential goods for citizens. The policy was introduced alongside a similar 15% margin cap on personal hygiene and cleaning products introduced in May 2025.

The move drew scrutiny from European Union institutions, as it was viewed by some as a distortion of market functioning and potentially non-compliant with Single Market rules. This regulatory approach followed earlier actions by the Hungarian government regarding agricultural price fixing, which had been ruled against by the European Court of Justice in September 2024. The margin caps were cited in the [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Country Report for Hungary as examples of sector-specific interventions that distort market mechanisms and create regulatory volatility within the business environment.
