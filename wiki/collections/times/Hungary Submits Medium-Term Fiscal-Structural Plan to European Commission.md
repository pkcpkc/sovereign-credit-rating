# Hungary Submits Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan to European Commission

## Event Details

In late 2024, the [[Hungary]] government submitted its Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP) to the [[European Commission]], marking a significant step in its fiscal governance framework under EU oversight. This submission was followed by an addendum in December 2024, aiming to address specific structural and fiscal targets.

The plan was formally endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]] in May 2025, signaling political acceptance of Hungary's reform trajectory. This endorsement came after the Hungarian government submitted its 2026 Budget Law to Parliament in May 2025, which revised the deficit target from 2.9% to 3.7% of GDP.

The context for this submission is defined by significant fiscal and structural vulnerabilities identified in the 2025 Country Report for Hungary. Key concerns include:

*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Public debt was projected to rise to 74.5% of GDP by 2025, with debt-servicing costs remaining among the highest in the EU. The budgetary framework is criticized for frequent revisions, lack of multiannual spending ceilings, and the use of emergency decrees that bypass parliamentary oversight.
*   **Structural Weaknesses:** Hungary faces severe challenges in its business environment, including regulatory volatility, sector-specific taxes, and state intervention. Innovation is hampered by low R&D intensity (1.4%) and skills shortages, while the energy sector relies heavily on Russian fossil fuels, posing security risks.
*   **Institutional Quality:** The report highlights widespread corruption, weak rule of law, and limited transparency as key institutional threats. The Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) implementation was at 0% of milestones as of mid-2025, delaying disbursements and undermining investor confidence.
*   **Social and Economic Dynamics:** Despite projected GDP growth rebounding to 2.5% in 2026, driven partly by electric vehicle manufacturing investments, underlying inflationary pressures and high poverty rates (AROPE at 20.2%) persist. Regional disparities between Budapest/Pest and less developed regions remain stark.

The submission of the MTFSP and its subsequent endorsement represents a critical juncture in Hungary's economic convergence efforts, balancing the need for fiscal adjustment with structural reforms to improve competitiveness and social fairness.
