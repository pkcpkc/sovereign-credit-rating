# National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) approves update to the National Anti-Corruption Plan

## Event Details

In January 2025, the [[National Anti-Corruption Authority]] (ANAC), under the leadership of its President [[Giuseppe Busia]], approved an update to the [[National Anti-Corruption Plan]]. This development was highlighted in the 2025 Rule of Law Report issued by the [[European Commission]] as a key strength in Italy's institutional and governance framework.

The updated plan and ANAC's reinforced human resources were recognized as part of a comprehensive effort to combat corruption, particularly within the high-risk sector of public procurement. ANAC issued specific guidelines addressing the risk of corruption in public contracts and the operationalization of "revolving doors" between public administration and private interests.

Despite these advancements, the report noted persistent challenges, including a lack of comprehensive rules on conflicts of interest for political office holders and the absence of concrete measures to establish a [[National Human Rights Institution]]. While digitalized public procurement platforms help counter infiltration by organized crime, the sector remains exposed to such risks. The update to the plan stands in contrast to legislative stagnation in other anti-correlated areas, such as lobbying and political party financing, which had not advanced or been discussed in Parliament by mid-2025.
