# European Council initiates Excessive Deficit Procedure against Slovakia

## Event Details

In July 2024, the [[European Council]] formally initiated an [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]] (EDP) against the [[Slovak Republic]]. This procedural step was triggered by Slovakia's significant breach of EU fiscal rules, specifically characterized by a large fiscal deficit that reached 5.8% of GDP in 2024.

The initiation of the EDP coincided with a period of economic transition in Slovakia, following a change of government in October 2024. The procedure underscores the European Commission's concerns regarding the country's fiscal sustainability, particularly given the rising public debt trajectory and the heavy interest burden relative to the primary balance.

According to the [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV Consultation, the EDP serves as a critical backdrop for Slovakia's subsequent fiscal policy decisions. The Slovak government responded by proposing an ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028) aimed at reducing the deficit to approximately 2.2% of GDP by 2028. While the IMF endorsed this plan as a step in the right direction, it emphasized that additional expenditure-based measures would be required beyond the initial 2025 budget to fully align with medium-term targets and address the risks highlighted by the EDP.

The procedure highlights broader structural challenges facing Slovakia, including governance concerns that could affect the disbursement of EU funds and the need for structural reforms to diversify the economy away from its heavy dependence on the automotive sector. The EDP remains a key factor in assessing Slovakia's policy credibility and institutional strength within the [[Euro Area]].
