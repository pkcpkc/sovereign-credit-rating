# European Council Initiates Excessive Deficit Procedure Against Slovakia

## Event Details

On July 23, 2023, the [[European Council]] initiated an Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) against Slovakia. This action was taken due to Slovakia's persistent fiscal deficits and public debt levels exceeding the thresholds established by the European Union's Stability and Growth Pact.

The initiation of the EDP highlighted significant concerns regarding Slovakia's fiscal sustainability, particularly its high deficit and rising public debt. As noted in subsequent assessments, such as the IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation, these fiscal challenges were exacerbated by structural issues including high spending on aging-related costs and a wide current account deficit. The procedure underscored the need for ambitious fiscal consolidation measures, which later influenced Slovakia's 2025 budget targets aiming to reduce the headline deficit from 5.8 percent of GDP in 2024 to 4.7 percent, with a medium-term goal of reaching close to 2 percent by 2028.

Key figures involved in the political landscape during this period included [[Kažimír]], Governor of the National Bank of Slovakia, and [[Kamenický]], Minister of Finance, who were tasked with navigating the fiscal adjustments required to comply with EU fiscal rules. The event marked a critical juncture in Slovakia's economic governance, prompting discussions on structural reforms, debt sustainability, and the effective absorption of EU funds to mitigate governance risks.
