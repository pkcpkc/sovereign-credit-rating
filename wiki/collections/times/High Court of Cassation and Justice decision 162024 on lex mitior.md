# High Court of Cassation and Justice decision 16/2024 on lex mitior

## Event Details

On **16 September 2024**, the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]] of Romania issued **Decision 16/2024** regarding the application of *lex mitior* (the more lenient law) in cases involving the statute of limitations. This ruling is part of a broader judicial interpretation that has significantly impacted the Romanian anti-corruption framework.

### Key Implications

*   **Discontinuation of Cases and Annulment of Convictions**: The decision, along with [[High Court of Cassation and Justice decision 37/2024 on lex mitior]] (issued earlier in June 2024), led to the discontinuation of numerous corruption investigations and the annulment of existing convictions. This occurred because the courts applied the principle that the more favorable statute of limitations period (calculated during a "legal void" between 2018 and 2022) should apply retrospectively.
*   **Systemic Risks and Impunity**: The [[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Romania]] identifies this case-law as a major threat to the [[anti-corruption framework]]. It raises systemic risks regarding impunity and potential incompatibility with [[European Union]] law, as it undermines the effectiveness of prosecuting high-level corruption.
*   **Judicial Independence Concerns**: While the [[European Commission]] noted progress in implementing [[Venice Commission]] recommendations on the [[Justice Laws]], the perception of [[judicial independence]] remains challenged by such rulings, which critics argue may be influenced by political pressures or inconsistent legal interpretations.
*   **Referral to the Court of Justice of the European Union**: In response to the uncertainty and potential EU law conflicts arising from these decisions, the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]] sought clarification from the [[Court of Justice of the European Union]] in **April 2025** (Case C-280/25 *Lin II*).

### Contextual Timeline

*   **18 June 2018**: [[Constitutional Court of Romania]] rules on the statute of limitations (Article 155(1)), creating a period of legal uncertainty.
*   **16 September 2024**: **Decision 16/2024** is issued, reinforcing the application of *lex mitior* to limit the prosecution of corruption cases.
*   **April 2025**: The HCCJ refers questions related to this interpretation to the CJEU, highlighting the tension between national judicial interpretations and EU legal standards.
