# Publication of the 2025 Country Report - Austria

## Event Details

The chronological development of Austria's economic and social landscape, as detailed in the 2025 Country Report, highlights a period of significant structural transition and fiscal pressure.

**Historical Context and Economic Shifts**
Since 2019, Austria has faced a notable loss in cost competitiveness, with nominal unit labor costs rising 10% more than the Euro area average. Despite these pressures, a milestone was reached in 2024 when real disposable household incomes surpassed their 2008 peak. However, the economy entered a three-year recessionary trend, with 2024 seeing a 1.2% contraction and 2023 a 1% contraction.

**Current Economic Outlook (2025)**
The 2025 Country Report, published on June 4, 2025, projects a continued GDP contraction of 0.3% for the year. The economy is currently grappling with high inflation (projected at 2.9%), a shortage of skilled labor (specifically in ICT and care), and high administrative "red tape" that delays industrial projects by up to 23 months. In response, the government is pursuing fiscal consolidation, aiming for savings of EUR 6.4 billion in 2025 and EUR 8.4 billion in 2026 to manage high deficits.

**Energy Transition and Future Targets**
Austria has made significant strides in its green transition, achieving a record 87% renewable electricity generation in 2024. Following the termination of Russian gas imports in early 2025, the country successfully diversified its supply from Norway, Germany, and Italy. Looking forward, the nation has set ambitious targets:
- **2030:** Achieving 100% renewable electricity and VHCN coverage.
- **2040:** Reaching the target for climate neutrality, which will require an estimated EUR 53 billion in grid investments.

**Long-term Demographic and Fiscal Risks**
The report identifies significant long-term challenges related to an ageing population. While public spending on age-related items is currently high, it is projected to reach 30% of GDP by 2070, with public healthcare expenditure specifically expected to rise to 8.9% of GDP by that same year.
