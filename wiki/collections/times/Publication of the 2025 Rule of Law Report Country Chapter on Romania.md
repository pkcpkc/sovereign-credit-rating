# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Romania

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the [[European Commission]] published the Country Chapter on Romania within the [[2025 Rule of Law Report]] (SWD(2025) 923 final). This document assesses the rule of law situation in Romania up to mid-2025, highlighting significant progress alongside persistent systemic challenges.

### Judicial Independence and Justice Laws
The report notes significant progress in completing the process to implement [[Venice Commission]] recommendations on the [[Justice Laws in Romania]], which entered into force in October 2022. Legislative steps are being explored to extend the mandates of high-ranking prosecutors from three to five years. However, perceived judicial independence has decreased among the general public and companies. A critical development involves the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]]’s interpretation of *lex mitior* (referencing decisions 37/2024 and 16/2024), which has led to the discontinuation of numerous corruption cases and the annulment of convictions due to expired limitation periods. This case-law raises concerns about impunity and compliance with EU law, prompting the HCCJ to refer a question to the [[Court of Justice of the European Union]] (Case C-280/25 Lin II) in April 2025. The Ministry of Justice has shown openness to ensuring judicial independence in the reform of the IT system for courts.

### Anti-Corruption and Institutional Accountability
The implementation of the [[2021-2025 National Anti-Corruption Strategy]] is on track, with a new strategy under preparation. The [[National Anti-Corruption Directorate]] (DNA) maintains a stable occupancy rate and continues to secure convictions in high-level cases, supported by the strengthened criminalisation of foreign bribery that entered into force on December 30, 2024. However, the [[National Integrity Agency]]’s asset declaration system faces challenges following a [[Constitutional Court of Romania]] ruling on May 29, 2025, which deemed the requirement to declare spouses’ and children’s assets unconstitutional. This ruling, combined with the lack of lobbying rules for Members of Parliament and pending legislation on political party financing, weakens transparency and accountability mechanisms.

### Media Freedom and Pluralism
Media freedom faces ongoing threats, including untransparent political advertising and harassment of journalists. No progress was made in enhancing the independent governance and editorial independence of public service media. The [[National Audiovisual Council]] lacks sufficient human resources and an updated IT system, impacting its effectiveness. Issues regarding media ownership transparency extend beyond the audiovisual sector, and political pressure on editorial independence remains a concern for journalists.

### Legislative Quality and Civil Society
Legislative unpredictability, driven by the frequent use of [[Government Emergency Ordinances]] (199 adopted in 2024), remains a primary concern for businesses and investors. Public consultations are often criticized as ineffective "box-ticking" exercises. While the [[Strategy for Open Government in Romania 2025-2030]] was adopted on April 9, 2025, to address challenges for civil society, the space for [[Civil Society Organizations]] is considered narrowed due to administrative barriers and attacks. Additionally, the accreditation process for [[National Human Rights Institutions]] (the [[Romanian Institute for Human Rights]] and [[People's Advocate]]) remains stalled due to questions from the [[Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions]] regarding independence and resources. The [[Romanian Institute for Human Rights]] faces severe budget cuts and staffing shortages.

### Political Context
The report context is influenced by recent political events, including the annulment of the first round of the [[2024 Presidential elections in Romania]] by the Constitutional Court on December 6, 2024, due to irregularities, leading to repeated elections in May 2025. The [[2025 Rule of Law Report]] underscores the need for continued efforts to stabilize the political landscape and strengthen institutional checks and balances.
