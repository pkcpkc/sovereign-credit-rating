# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report (Belgium Chapter)

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the European Commission released the **2025 Rule of Law Report (Belgium Chapter)**, providing a comprehensive assessment of the country's judicial independence, anti-corruption measures, media freedom, and civic space.

The report highlights several key developments and ongoing challenges:

- **Judicial Independence and Resources:** While 64% of the public perceives judicial independence as "fairly or very good," the report notes structural resource deficiencies. The government plans to transfer budgetary management powers to the judiciary, though this remains tied to pending reforms of evaluation and disciplinary systems.
- **Digitalization and Efficiency:** In response to a 2024 Court of Audit report regarding the lack of an overarching digitalization strategy, the government is working to improve judicial efficiency and address significant backlogs, particularly in the Brussels Court of Appeal.
- **Anti-Corruption Progress:** Belgium's ranking on the Corruption Perceptions Index improved to 9th in the EU (69/100). Key initiatives include the planned creation of a new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) and specialized prosecution for financial crimes. However, gaps remain in the verification of asset declarations and the lack of formal "revolving door" legislation.
- **Media Freedom:** The report notes progress in journalist protections through the decriminalization of defamation and the transposition of the EU anti-SLAPP Directive. It also notes budgetary pressures on public service media, specifically a projected shortfall for RTBF.
- **Human Rights and Civic Space:** Concerns were raised regarding a 25% budget cut to the interfederal equality body (Unia) and the non-implementation of 18 leading European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) judgments as of June 2025. Additionally, stakeholders warned of a "narrowed" civic space due to proposed sanctions for individuals involved in previous demonstrations.
