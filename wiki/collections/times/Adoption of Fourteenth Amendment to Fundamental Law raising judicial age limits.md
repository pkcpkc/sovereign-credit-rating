# Adoption of Fourteenth Amendment to Fundamental Law raising judicial age limits

## Event Details

In December 2024, the Hungarian National Assembly adopted the Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law of Hungary. This constitutional amendment significantly altered the age regulations for the judiciary, raising the minimum age requirement for appointment as a judge to 35 years and establishing a maximum service age of 70 years.

The adoption of this amendment was part of a broader series of judicial reforms that faced substantial criticism regarding the adequacy of the consultation process with the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]]. The [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Rule of Law Report highlighted that while judicial salaries had increased, the effectiveness of the consultation with the Judicial Council on these new rules was deemed ineffective. The tension surrounding these reforms contributed to a significant institutional crisis, including the resignation of the President of the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] in December 2024 following judicial protests. Subsequently, in January 2025, the National Judicial Council withdrew from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms, citing the lack of genuine dialogue and the imposition of changes such as those in the Fourteenth Amendment.
