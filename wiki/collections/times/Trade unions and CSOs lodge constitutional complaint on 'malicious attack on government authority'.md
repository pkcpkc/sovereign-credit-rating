# Trade unions and CSOs lodge constitutional complaint on 'malicious attack on government authority'

## Event Details

On **2024-10-07**, trade unions and Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) in Belgium lodged a constitutional complaint characterizing certain government measures as a "malicious attack on government authority." This legal challenge occurred amidst a broader climate of tension between the judiciary and the executive branch, which had been exacerbated by public criticism of the judiciary by politicians and ongoing concerns regarding compliance with final court rulings.

The complaint is situated within the context of the "narrowed" civic space identified in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Belgium** (published on 2025-07-08). The European Commission highlighted that stakeholders raised concerns over new obstacles to the right to demonstrate, including proposed judicial bans for individuals convicted of acts during protests. The Constitutional Court had previously issued relevant judgments in late 2024, including an annulment of a provision in a Flemish Community decree on subsidies on **2024-09-19** and a ruling on urgent court orders and strikes on **2024-11-14**.

This event reflects wider institutional challenges in Belgium, including the joint memorandum issued by the three highest courts on **2024-07-19** requiring the Government to respect court judgments. The complaint underscores the friction between civil society actors, who rely on the right to protest and unionize, and government authorities implementing measures perceived by some as restricting these fundamental rights. The period also followed a caretaker Government phase (June 2024 – February 2025) during which political fragmentation may have influenced the timing and nature of such constitutional interventions.
