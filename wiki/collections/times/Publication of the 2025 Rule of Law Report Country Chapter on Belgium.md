# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Belgium

## Event Details

On 2025-07-08, the European Commission published the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Belgium. The report provides a comprehensive assessment of the rule of law situation in Belgium, covering the period from late 2024 to mid-2025. It evaluates the country's performance across four key pillars: the justice system, the anti-corruption framework, media pluralism and freedom, and institutional checks and balances.

### Justice System
The report highlights that perceived judicial independence remains high, with 64% of the general public and 72% of companies expressing confidence in the judiciary's independence. The Government has signaled a commitment to institutional stability by exempting the judiciary from budget cuts and intending to transfer budgetary management powers for justice administration from the executive to the judiciary. However, structural challenges persist, including severe resource deficiencies, unfilled vacancies due to unattractive working conditions, and long-standing efficiency issues such as excessive length of proceedings. Specific attention is drawn to backlogs in the Court of Appeal of Brussels. The Government is addressing criticisms from the Belgian Court of Audit regarding shortcomings in the digitalisation strategy of the justice department, including fraud risks and lack of an overarching approach.

### Anti-Corruption Framework
Belgium faces persistent gaps in its anti-corruption framework despite relatively low scores on corruption perceptions. While new structures are planned, such as the new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) and a specialised prosecution service for financial crime, concerns remain regarding the handling of high-level corruption cases. Allegations of information leaks and undue political influence in investigations handled by the Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC) continue to raise questions about integrity. The integrity framework for politicians is criticized for lacking adequate verification and transparency in asset and interest declarations, which remain sealed and inaccessible except during criminal investigations. Progress on lobbying transparency and "revolving door" rules is limited, with the new Government yet to decide on draft lobbying rules. Police integrity remains weak due to disparate policies across 181 local police zones.

### Media Pluralism and Freedom
Media freedom in Belgium benefits from strong legal foundations and independent regulators, including the Flemish Regulator for the Media (VRM), Conseil Supérieur de l’Audiovisuel (CSA), and Medienrat. These bodies have strengthened their operational independence, particularly under the Digital Services Act. Public service media (VRT, RTBF, BRF) face significant governance and budgetary pressures, with the RTBF facing a projected EUR 78 million shortfall. Legislative steps have improved protections for journalists, notably the decriminalisation of defamation and the introduction of harsher penalties for crimes against journalists. However, journalists report increasing harassment and legal intimidation.

### Institutional Checks and Civic Space
Compliance with final court rulings remains an area of limited progress. The Belgian Government is under supervision by the Council of Europe for structural issues identified by the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) in the *Camara v. Belgium* case. As of January 1, 2025, 17 leading ECtHR judgments were pending implementation. Civic space is rated as "narrowed," with stakeholders raising concerns over new obstacles to the right to demonstrate, including proposed judicial bans for individuals convicted of acts during protests. Access to public documents has seen some legislative improvement, but procedural shortcomings remain, such as the non-binding nature of appeal bodies.

### Key Chronological Context
- **2024-06 to 2025-02**: Belgium experienced a caretaker Government period, contributing to political fragmentation and delays in governance.
- **2024-07-19**: A joint memorandum by the three highest courts required the Government to respect court judgments.
- **2024-12-19**: An Act was adopted on amicable settlements of cases to help reduce judicial backlogs.
- **2025-03-27**: The King sanctioned the law on whistleblower protection for the House of Representatives and Senate.
