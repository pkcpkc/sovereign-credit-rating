# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Belgium

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the European Commission published the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Belgium. This report, issued as Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 901 final, provides a comprehensive assessment of the rule of law situation in Belgium, covering developments up to mid-2025.

The report highlights a mixed but generally positive trajectory, noting strengths in **Judicial Independence** and perceptions of integrity, alongside persistent challenges regarding justice system efficiency, integrity framework enforcement, and the protection of **Civic Space**.

Key findings from the report include:

*   **Judicial System**: Belgium maintains high perceived judicial independence among both the public and businesses. The government has announced plans to transfer budgetary management powers for the justice administration from the executive branch to the judiciary, aiming to enhance managerial autonomy. However, significant backlogs remain, particularly at the **Court of Appeal of Brussels**, and the digitalization strategy has faced scrutiny from the Court of Audit regarding governance and fraud controls.
*   **Anti-Corruption**: Belgium scores 69/100 on the Corruption Perceptions Index, indicating relatively low perceived corruption. The new government plans to establish specialized investigative and prosecutorial structures targeting financial crime and corruption, specifically strengthening the role of the **Central Office for the Repression of Corruption** (CDBC-OCRC). Concerns persist regarding political influence in high-profile cases and low prosecution rates for foreign bribery.
*   **Integrity and Transparency**: While asset declarations are submitted by nearly all required officials, verification remains limited to criminal investigations. Integrity policies within the police and for public officials show gaps in monitoring, particularly concerning "revolving doors" and lobbying regulations. No progress was reported on comprehensive lobbying reform.
*   **Media and Civic Space**: Media regulators across all communities are considered independent, and recent laws have decriminalized defamation and increased penalties for attacks on journalists. However, **Civic Space** is described as "narrowed," with concerns over disproportionate measures against protesters and legislative obstacles to the right to demonstrate. The Constitutional Court has annulled some restrictive provisions, but obstacles remain.
*   **Checks and Balances**: Compliance with final rulings of national courts and judgments from the **European Court of Human Rights** remains a weak spot. The government is under ongoing supervision by the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe for structural issues, including the implementation of judgments such as *Camara v. Belgium*.

This publication serves as a critical benchmark for ongoing judicial, administrative, and political reforms in Belgium, influencing international perceptions of the country's institutional stability and governance quality.
