# Formation of new Belgian Federal Government

## Event Details

On **February 2025**, Belgium successfully formed a new federal government, ending a period of protracted coalition negotiations that had previously been cited by the **International Monetary Fund** (IMF) as a source of political fragmentation and policymaking uncertainty. The formation of the government was a critical milestone in restoring policymaking stability and addressing the country's significant structural challenges, particularly regarding fiscal sustainability and labor market efficiency.

Following the election results in June 2024, the new government's agenda focused on structural reforms and fiscal consolidation. By **February 21, 2025**, the IMF held a concluding virtual meeting with **Jan Jambon**, the newly appointed Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Finance, to discuss the government's plans.

### Key Context and Outcomes
*   **Fiscal Consolidation Commitment**: The new government committed to a **7-year fiscal adjustment plan** under the new **EU Economic Governance Framework** (EGF). This plan aims to reduce the structural primary balance by approximately 0.6 percentage points of GDP annually until 2031, bringing the general government deficit durably below 3% of GDP and reducing public debt from 104.1% (in 2024) to approximately 78% of GDP.
*   **Structural Reform Agenda**: The government prioritized reforms to the wage-setting mechanism, specifically targeting **automatic wage indexation** and the 1996 wage law, which were identified as drivers of declining competitiveness and sticky inflation. Other key areas included enhancing labor force participation, rationalizing current spending (social benefits and public wages), and improving the efficiency of public investment in healthcare, education, and infrastructure.
*   **IMF Assessment**: In its 2025 Article IV Consultation (concluded in March 2025), the IMF welcomed the government's clear agenda. The Fund assessed the overall risk of sovereign stress as "moderate," noting that while debt levels remain high, they are mitigated by long debt maturity (11.1 years) and a strong domestic investor base. The IMF emphasized the need for coordinated efforts between federal and regional entities to meet climate targets and strengthen the fiscal framework through a revitalized fiscal council.
