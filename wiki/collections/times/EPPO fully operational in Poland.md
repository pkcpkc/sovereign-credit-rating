# EPPO fully operational in Poland

## Event Details

On January 1, 2025, the [[European Public Prosecutor's Office]] (EPPO) became fully operational in Poland, marking a significant milestone in the country's rule of law reforms and anti-corruption framework.

According to the [[European Commission]]'s [[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Poland]], this operational status represents progress in addressing long-standing concerns regarding judicial independence and accountability. The EPPO's full functionality is noted alongside other key developments in the Polish anti-corruption sphere, such as the planned establishment of a new [[Central Office for Combating Corruption]] within the police, which involves dissolving the existing [[Central Anti-Corruption Bureau]].

While the EPPO's operation is a strength, the report highlights that challenges persist in the broader anti-corruption framework, including the lack of a comprehensive anti-corruption strategy, the absence of lobbying rules, and continued broad immunities for top executives. Despite these systemic issues, the EPPO's operational capacity enhances the EU's ability to investigate and prosecute crimes affecting the Union's financial interests within Polish jurisdiction.
