# Borrower-based measures (KIM-V) for residential real estate lapse; CRE buffer (SSyRB) becomes effective

## Event Details

On July 1, 2025, two significant macroprudential adjustments took effect in Austria as highlighted in the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation**.

1.  **Lapse of Borrower-Based Measures (KIM-V):** The borrower-based measures (Kreditnehmerbezogene Instrumente im Vermögen, KIM-V) for residential real estate lending expired. These measures had previously imposed prudent lending standards on mortgages. The **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** staff recommended that Austrian authorities enact legislation to make these lending standards permanent. The IMF warned that allowing the measures to lapse without replacement could lead to a resurgence of macro-financial risks in the housing sector, despite the banking system's overall health.

2.  **Effective Commercial Real Estate (CRE) Buffer (SSyRB):** Concurrently, the sectoral systemic risk buffer (SSyRB) for Commercial Real Estate became effective. This measure addresses rising Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) in the CRE sector, a key vulnerability identified by the IMF. While Austrian banks remain well-capitalized with a CET1 ratio of 17.7%, the CRE sector poses specific risks due to data gaps and potential credit quality deterioration. The SSyRB is designed to enhance bank resilience against these sector-specific shocks.

The IMF emphasized the importance of maintaining vigilance on CRE data gaps and ensuring that the expiration of residential mortgage safeguards does not undermine the long-term stability of the financial sector. These changes occurred against a backdrop of weak economic growth (-0.1% projected for 2025) and high inflation (3.2% in 2025), underscoring the need for robust financial safety nets.
