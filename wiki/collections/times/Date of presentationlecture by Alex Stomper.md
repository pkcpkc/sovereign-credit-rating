# Date of presentation/lecture by Alex Stomper

## Event Details

On **2024**, **[[Alex Stomper]]**, a Lecturer and Author at [[HU Berlin]], delivered a presentation titled **"Financial Economics for Citizens: The S&P Rating Methodology - Overview."** This lecture provided a comprehensive dissection of the **[[S&P Global Ratings]]** sovereign credit rating methodology, specifically referencing the 2017 S&P methodology framework.

### Core Content and Methodology Analysis
The presentation outlined the five-pillar assessment structure used by S&P to determine sovereign credit ratings:

1.  **Institutional Assessment**: Evaluates government effectiveness, policymaking stability, transparency, accountability, and security risks. A key metric discussed was "debt payment culture," where a weak culture can cap the institutional assessment score.
2.  **Economic Assessment**: Analyzes GDP per capita as an indicator of the tax and funding base, alongside growth prospects and economic diversity.
3.  **External Assessment**: Examines currency status, external liquidity (the ability to meet foreign obligations), and the net external asset/liability position.
4.  **Fiscal Assessment**: Described as the "heart" of the rating process, this pillar assesses fiscal performance/flexibility (flows) and debt burden/structure (stocks). It also includes an analysis of **contingent liabilities**, such as banking industry risks (via [[Banking Industry Country Risk Analysis|BICRA]]), non-financial public enterprises, and off-budget guarantees.
5.  **Monetary Assessment**: Considers the exchange rate regime and the credibility of monetary policy, including central bank independence, inflation control, and financial market depth.

### Critical Themes: Discretion and Replicability
A central theme of Stomper’s lecture was the significant role of **analyst discretion** within the S&P framework. The presentation highlighted:
*   **Supplemental Adjustment Factors**: Analysts can apply discretionary adjustments for factors like event risk or extreme liquidity weakness.
*   **Non-Replicability**: Due to subjective elements (e.g., assessing "cohesive civil society" or "effectiveness of policymaking") and the ability to override indicative ratings, the rating process is partially non-replicable.

### Citizen Science Initiative
The lecture concluded with a proposal for a **"Citizen Science"** project. The goal is to apply the S&P methodology to European countries to document discrepancies and test the replicability of sovereign credit ratings, thereby exposing the subjective nature of these financial determinations.
