# Publication of 2025 Country Report - France

## Event Details

On June 4, 2025, the [[European Commission]] published the "2025 Country Report - France," providing a comprehensive assessment of France's economic trajectory amid heightened political uncertainty and structural challenges. The report highlights a significant economic slowdown, with real GDP growth forecasted to decelerate from 1.2% in 2024 to just 0.6% in 2025, driven by subdued investment and high corporate bankruptcies.

Key findings include a deteriorating fiscal position, marked by a general government deficit of 5.8% of GDP in 2024 and public debt rising to 113% of GDP. The Commission emphasizes the necessity of adhering to the endorsed medium-term fiscal-structural plan, which was previously submitted in October 2024 and endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]] in January 2025, alongside the recommendation to end the excessive deficit situation.

Structural obstacles remain prominent, including a complex business environment with high administrative burdens, stagnant business R&D intensity, and delays in renewable energy deployment due to permitting issues. While France has achieved 95% low-carbon electricity production, the green transition faces hurdles in building decarbonization and renewable energy targets. Socially, the report notes worsening poverty risks and persistent skills mismatches. The fragmented political landscape following the 2024 general elections has contributed to unprecedented budgetary delays, complicating the implementation of necessary reforms. France has advanced significantly in implementing its [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] (RRF) measures, fulfilling 82% of milestones, but must accelerate cohesion policy implementation to ensure sustainable growth.
