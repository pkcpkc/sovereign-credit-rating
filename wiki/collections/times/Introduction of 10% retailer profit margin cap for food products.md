# Introduction of 10% retailer profit margin cap for food products

## Event Details

In **March 2025**, the Hungarian government introduced a cap on retailer profit margins for food products, limiting them to **10%**. This regulatory measure was implemented as part of broader state intervention strategies to combat inflationary pressures and stabilize domestic prices.

The policy is situated within a context of significant economic challenges identified by the [[European Commission]] in its **2025 Country Report on Hungary**. The report highlights that while inflation declined significantly from a peak of 17.0% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2024, it picked up again in early 2025 due to underlying price pressures and rising food costs. The margin cap is one of several administrative controls utilized by the Hungarian government, alongside a **15% margin cap for personal hygiene products** introduced in **May 2025** and previously imposed agricultural price caps (subject to a **September 2024** [[European Court of Justice]] ruling).

The European Commission criticized such interventions, noting that heavy state involvement—including subsidized lending, sector-specific taxes, and administrative price controls—distorts markets, crowds out private actors, and limits competition. These measures contribute to the broader concerns regarding Hungary's business environment, characterized by regulatory volatility and weak competition enforcement.
