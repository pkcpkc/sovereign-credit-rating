# Action plan to enhance public finance execution published

## Event Details

In **March 2025**, the French government published an **action plan to enhance public finance execution** as a critical component of its broader Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). This initiative was launched in response to persistent fiscal slippages observed in 2023 and 2024, which had contributed to a rise in general government gross debt to 113.1% of GDP by the end of 2024. The plan aimed to address implementation risks associated with the MTFSP, which targets reducing the budget deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029 and placing public debt on a sustainable downward path.

The context for this action plan was shaped by significant domestic and external challenges. Following a political compromise on the 2025 budget in February 2025, authorities committed to a frontloaded structural fiscal effort. The IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation, concluded in July 2025, highlighted that while the financial sector remained resilient with strong capital buffers, fiscal performance was weakened by revenue shortfalls and spending overruns. The action plan specifically targeted the complex, multi-layered structure of local government spending, aiming to strengthen transparency, clarify competencies, and adopt multi-year budgetary frameworks to improve efficiency and oversight.

Key objectives of the enhanced execution framework included:
*   **Rationalizing Current Spending**: Prioritizing spending reviews across all government levels to identify savings opportunities, given France's high tax-to-GDP ratio which limits revenue-based consolidation options.
*   **Strengthening Local Government Oversight**: Addressing fiscal slippages contributed by local authorities by improving monitoring mechanisms and aligning local budgets with national fiscal targets.
*   **Improving Implementation Credibility**: Ensuring that the MTFSP's ambitious targets were met through decisive administrative actions to reduce policy uncertainty and boost private investment confidence.

The publication of this plan was assessed positively by the European Commission in June 2025, which recognized France's efforts in adhering to EU fiscal rules. However, the IMF staff report emphasized that the success of these measures depended on sustained political consensus and the ability to implement difficult decisions regarding equity in fiscal consolidation. The action plan served as a foundational step in the broader agenda of structural reforms aimed at boosting productivity growth through innovation, digital adoption, and labor market improvements, which were deemed essential for long-term economic resilience amidst geoeconomic fragmentation and trade tensions.
