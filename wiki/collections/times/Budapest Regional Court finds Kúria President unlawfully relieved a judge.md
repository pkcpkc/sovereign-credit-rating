# Budapest Regional Court finds Kúria President unlawfully relieved a judge

## Event Details

On March 17, 2025, the [[Budapest Regional Court]] ruled that the President of the [[Kúria]] (the Hungarian Supreme Court and Court of Cassation) had unlawfully relieved a judge from their duties. This judicial decision emerged during a period of heightened tension within Hungary's justice system, characterized by concerns over judicial independence and internal pressures.

The ruling is part of a broader context of deterioration in rule of law indicators for Hungary, as highlighted in the **2025 Rule of Law Report** published by the [[European Commission]] on July 8, 2025. The report notes that despite the *Kúria* generally enforcing uniform case law, there are persistent concerns regarding "undue pressure on judges regarding internal debates."

This event followed a series of significant developments in the Hungarian judiciary:
- **December 2024**: The President of the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] resigned following protests against proposed judicial reforms.
- **January 15, 2025**: The National Judicial Council withdrew from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms and salary increases, citing ineffective consultation processes regarding new rules that raised the minimum age for judges to 35 and allowed service until age 70.
- **March 6, 2025**: The [[Council of Europe]] Committee of Ministers addressed the "chilling effect" on the Hungarian judiciary.

The Budapest Regional Court's decision underscores ongoing conflicts between judicial leadership and institutional safeguards, contributing to the European Commission's assessment that Hungary has stagnated or deteriorated in key rule of law areas, including the independence of the judiciary.
