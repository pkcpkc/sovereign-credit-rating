# Submission of Austria's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan

## Event Details

On **2025-05-13**, the Austrian government submitted its **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP)** to the European Commission. This submission was a key component of the European Semester process, designed to outline Austria’s fiscal consolidation and structural reform strategy for the 2025–2029 period.

The plan was developed against the backdrop of a deteriorating economic situation, with Austria entering its third consecutive year of recession in 2025. The submission coincided with broader fiscal pressures, including a projected government deficit of approximately 4.4% of GDP for 2025—well above the EU treaty threshold of 3%—and a rising gross debt ratio projected to reach 84.0% of GDP.

Key elements and context surrounding the submission include:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation Commitments:** The new government committed to EUR 6.4 billion in savings for 2025 as part of the consolidation measures outlined in the plan. These measures included the abolition of paid educational leave (*Bildungskarenz*) in January 2025 and further parliamentary approval of budget consolidation measures in March 2025.
*   **European Commission Assessment:** The plan was evaluated in the **2025 Country Report on Austria**, published by the European Commission on 2025-06-04. The Commission acknowledged the submission but highlighted significant challenges, including high public spending driven by inflation indexation and aging-related costs, particularly in pensions and healthcare.
*   **Structural Reform Focus:** Beyond fiscal adjustment, the plan addressed structural issues such as labor productivity growth, skills shortages, and regulatory burdens. The European Commission emphasized the need for further reforms to boost competitiveness, simplify regulations, and improve access to risk capital.
*   **Long-Term Sustainability:** The MTFSP also addressed long-term fiscal sustainability concerns, particularly the projected rise in age-related public spending to 30% of GDP by 2070. The plan incorporated green budgeting principles to align fiscal policy with Austria’s 2040 climate neutrality target.

The submission represents a critical step in Austria’s compliance with the Stability and Growth Pact and its efforts to restore fiscal stability amid economic headwinds.
