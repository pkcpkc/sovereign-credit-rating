# IMF Press Release issued

## Event Details

On July 14, 2025, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) issued the press release marking the conclusion of the 2025 Article IV consultation with France. This event followed the Executive Board's conclusion of the consultation on July 11, 2025, and the completion of the IMF Staff Report on June 25, 2025.

The consultation, led by Head of Mission Manuela Goretti, assessed France's economic performance and policy framework. The report highlighted that while the French economy demonstrated resilience in 2024—supported by the Paris Olympics and a robust labor market—it faces significant challenges regarding high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024.

Key outcomes emphasized in the press release include:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: The IMF Executive Board and staff stressed the urgent need for a credible, frontloaded fiscal consolidation under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The goal is to bring the general government deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029 to place public debt on a downward path. The Board recommended prioritizing spending rationalization over tax increases, given France's high tax-to-GDP ratio.
*   **Economic Outlook**: Real GDP growth is projected to slow to 0.6% in 2025, weighed down by deepening geoeconomic fragmentation, trade tensions, and weak private investment. A recovery to 1.0% in 2026 and 1.2% in the medium term is expected, contingent on easing trade tensions and structural reforms. Inflation has disinflated significantly, falling to 0.6% year-on-year in May 2025, with expectations well-anchored at 2%.
*   **Structural Reforms**: To address sluggish productivity growth, the IMF recommended advancing reforms to foster entrepreneurship, enhance access to finance for high-growth firms, and reduce regulatory burdens. Specific focus areas include leveraging AI and green technologies, improving education, and addressing labor market fragmentation.
*   **Financial Sector Resilience**: The accompanying Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP), led by Charles Cohen, confirmed the resilience of the French banking sector, citing healthy capital buffers (19.8% regulatory capital ratio) and strong liquidity. However, the report noted risks from weak bank profitability, high corporate indebtedness, and the need to monitor non-bank financial intermediaries.
*   **Long-term Pressures**: The consultation acknowledged long-term fiscal pressures from an aging population and pension costs, noting that the 2023 pension reform (raising the retirement age to 64) helps mitigate these risks. Issues regarding local government fiscal oversight and transparency were also highlighted as areas for improvement.

This assessment follows earlier developments including the political compromise on the 2025 budget in February 2025, the publication of an action plan for public finance execution in March 2025, and a positive assessment of French fiscal efforts by the European Commission in June 2025. The report comes amidst heightened global uncertainty, including Moody's downgrade of France's credit rating to Aa3 in December 2024.
