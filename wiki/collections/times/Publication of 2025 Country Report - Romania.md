# Publication of 2025 Country Report - Romania

## Event Details

On **June 4, 2025**, the [[European Commission]] published the **2025 Country Report for Romania** (SWD(2025) 223 final), marking a critical juncture for the Romanian economy. The report highlights a divergence between rising living standards and growing macroeconomic vulnerabilities, driven by loose fiscal policy, strong domestic demand, and deteriorating external balances.

### Macroeconomic and Fiscal Context
The report analyzes data from 2024, where Romania’s real GDP growth decelerated to **0.8%**, supported by resilient private consumption but hindered by weak net exports and a slowdown in private investment. Key macroeconomic indicators revealed significant pressures:
*   **Fiscal Deficit:** The government deficit surged to **9.3% of GDP** in 2024, primarily due to generous public sector wage increases and higher public investment spending. Net expenditure growth reached 19.9%, far exceeding recommended maximums.
*   **Public Debt:** Public debt rose rapidly to **54.8% of GDP** in 2024, though it remains below the EU average of 81.6%. Projections indicate a further increase to 59.4% in 2025 and 63.3% in 2026.
*   **External Position:** The current account deficit widened to **8.4% of GDP**, reflecting high import demand and weak external competitiveness. The negative net international investment position stands at 41.5% of GDP.
*   **Inflation:** Inflation fell to 5.8% in 2024 from 10% in 2023, with projections hovering around 5% for 2025.

### Fiscal Consolidation and Policy Response
Central to the government's strategy is the **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)**, submitted in October 2024 and endorsed by the [[European Council]] in January 2025. This plan commits Romania to a seven-year adjustment period (2025-2031). Immediate measures adopted in late 2024 include:
*   Freezing public wages and pensions.
*   Removing tax exemptions and reforming the microenterprise tax regime.
*   Targeting a budget deficit of 7% of GDP in 2025.

Despite these measures, the report warns that fiscal consolidation faces risks from political instability, weak administrative capacity, and the implementation challenges of structural reforms.

### Structural Challenges and Reform Delays
The report identifies persistent structural weaknesses as barriers to sustainable growth:
*   **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP):** Implementation is significantly delayed, with only 14% of milestones fulfilled. Bottlenecks include procurement delays, political instability, and weak administrative oversight.
*   **Business Environment:** Regulatory uncertainty and legislative instability are cited by 90% of firms as the top barrier to long-term investment. The fiscal code has been amended hundreds of times since 2015, undermining legal certainty.
*   **Labor Market:** While the employment rate improved to 69.5%, there are severe skills mismatches, particularly in STEM and digital sectors. Labor force participation remains low among women and youth.
*   **Innovation and Infrastructure:** Romania has the lowest R&D intensity in the EU (0.52% of GDP). Transport infrastructure, particularly motorways and railways, remains underdeveloped compared to EU peers.

### Social and Environmental Dimensions
*   **Social Inequality:** The at-risk-of-poverty-or-exclusion (AROPE) rate remained high at 27.9% in 2024, with significant disparities between urban and rural areas.
*   **Energy Transition:** Renewables accounted for 48% of electricity generation in 2024. However, energy affordability and grid limitations remain challenges, alongside the need to phase out fossil fuel subsidies.
*   **Climate Risks:** Romania is highly exposed to climate-related risks, including floods and droughts, with ongoing issues in wastewater treatment and water management.

### Institutional and Governance Factors
The report underscores governance issues affecting creditworthiness and stability:
*   **Political Stability:** Frequent changes in top decision-makers and populist measures (e.g., price controls) affect investor confidence.
*   **Corruption and Rule of Law:** Public procurement and tax administration remain high-risk sectors for corruption. While the justice system shows improvements in case length, enforcement of existing rules (e.g., SME tests) is weak.
*   **Schengen Accession:** Romania’s accession to the Schengen Area on January 1, 2025, is expected to facilitate trade and foreign investment, providing a potential strength amid other vulnerabilities.

### Outlook
Growth is projected to pick up to 1.4% in 2025 and 2.2% in 2026, supported by substantial EU funding (over EUR 90 billion). However, the report emphasizes that without resolute fiscal consolidation and deep structural reforms, Romania remains vulnerable to external shocks and shifts in investor sentiment. The [[National Bank of Romania]] maintains a credible inflation-targeting framework, and the banking sector remains well-capitalized (CET1 ratio of 20%), providing some resilience against fiscal pressures.
