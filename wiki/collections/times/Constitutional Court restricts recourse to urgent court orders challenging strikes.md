# Constitutional Court restricts recourse to urgent court orders challenging strikes

## Event Details

On 14 November 2024, the [[Constitutional Court of Belgium]] issued a ruling that significantly restricted the legal recourse available to challenge strikes through urgent court orders. This decision was highlighted in the 2025 [[Rule of Law Report]] for [[Belgium]], published by the European Commission on 8 July 2025, as a key development affecting the [[Right to Demonstrate]] and civic space.

The ruling addressed legislative obstacles and judicial mechanisms surrounding the right to strike and demonstrate. While the Constitutional Court had previously annulled certain restrictive provisions regarding subsidies for organizations (in September 2024), this November 2024 decision focused specifically on the procedural ability to halt or challenge strikes via urgent judicial applications. The report notes that despite such judicial interventions, broader concerns persist regarding "narrowed" civic space and disproportionate measures against protesters, including potential judicial bans on demonstrations.

This event occurs within a broader context of [[Judicial Independence]] in Belgium, which is perceived as high by both the public and businesses. However, compliance with final rulings of national courts remains a weak spot, with ongoing supervision by the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe regarding structural issues in justice administration.
