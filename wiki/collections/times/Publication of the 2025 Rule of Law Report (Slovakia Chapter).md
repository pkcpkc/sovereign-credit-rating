# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report (Slovakia Chapter)

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the European Commission released the **2025 Rule of Law Report for Slovakia**, detailing a period marked by legislative progress in financial transparency and criminal code alignment, contrasted by significant regressions in judicial independence and media freedom.

**Key Chronological Milestones and Developments:**

*   **June 2023:** The implementation of the reformed judicial map was initiated, though subsequent reporting indicates a sharp deterioration in the efficiency of administrative courts, with resolution times reaching 1,040 days.
*   **December 2024:** Slovakia adopted additional amendments to its Criminal Codes to align with the **PIF Directive**. While intended for compliance, these changes have sparked concerns regarding a "chilling effect" on the judiciary due to potential criminal liability for judicial decisions.
*   **Early 2025:** The report highlights the ongoing status of **ECtHR leading judgments**, with 33 judgments remaining pending for implementation as of June 2025.
*   **March 12, 2025:** The Constitutional Court intervened to suspend amendments to the **Freedom of Information Act**, which had been criticized for introducing "paywalls" on critical information.
*   **April 16, 2025:** A new legal framework was adopted regarding the financial transparency of NGOs, imposing heavy reporting and disclosure obligations that stakeholders characterize as an increased administrative burden.
*   **July 8, 2025:** Publication of the full report, which identifies the dissolution of the **National Crime Agency (NAKA)** and the **Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO)** as factors contributing to a drop in corruption cases and a regression in the 2024 Corruption Perceptions Index (49/100).

The report further notes the establishment of the new **Slovak Television and Radio (STVR)**, which is viewed as a risk to editorial independence, and the continued use of fast-track procedures—notably 28 out of 58 laws in 2024—to bypass meaningful stakeholder consultation.
