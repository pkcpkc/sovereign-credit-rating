# Commission adopts Recommendation to endorse Belgium's medium-term fiscal-structural plan

## Event Details

On **May 21, 2025**, the European Commission adopted a Recommendation endorsing Belgium's **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** for the period 2025–2029. This decision followed Belgium's submission of the plan on March 18, 2025, which outlined a seven-year fiscal adjustment period under the reformed **EU Economic Governance Framework** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263).

### Key Aspects of the Endorsement

*   **National Escape Clause Activation**: The Commission acknowledged Belgium's use of the **National Escape Clause** to accommodate increased defence spending. This mechanism allowed Belgium to legally exceed standard net expenditure limits, with defence spending projected to rise to **1.7% of GDP** in 2025. Consequently, net expenditure growth was permitted to reach 5.0% in 2025, above the MTP's maximum rate, while maintaining compliance with the broader fiscal roadmap.
*   **Fiscal Sustainability Measures**: The endorsed plan commits to structural reforms aimed at curbing Belgium's deteriorating public finances. With the general government deficit widening to **4.5% of GDP** in 2024 and projected to rise to **5.4% in 2025**, the MTP aims to address an unsustainable debt trajectory currently projected to reach **126.4% of GDP by 2035**.
*   **Structural Reforms**: The plan includes significant structural adjustments, notably:
    *   **Pension Reform**: Implementation of a bonus-malus system and reduced assimilated periods to address ageing-related costs, which are projected to increase by 5.1 percentage points of GDP by 2070.
    *   **Labour Market Activation**: Measures to improve participation rates, including limiting unemployment benefits to two years for those under 55 and linking benefits to income.
    *   **Tax Reform**: Labour tax cuts financed by adjustments to capital gains and property taxes.

### Context and Assessment

The endorsement is part of the **2025 Country Report on Belgium**, published by the Commission on June 4, 2025. The report highlights that while the MTP represents a positive step, Belgium's national fiscal framework remains weak due to:
*   Lack of effective budgetary coordination between federal and regional entities.
*   Insufficient autonomy and resources for the **High Council of Finance**.
*   Persistent challenges in labour productivity growth and competitiveness, exacerbated by high labour costs and index-linked wages.

The Commission's recommendation supports Belgium's path toward fiscal consolidation while allowing necessary flexibility for defence investments, contingent on the delivery of the outlined structural reforms.
