# IMF Staff Report completed

## Event Details

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has completed significant Staff Reports and Article IV consultations for both Austria and Romania, providing critical assessments of their respective economic trajectories and structural reforms.

### Austria
On June 9, 2025, the IMF completed its Staff Report regarding the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation - Austria**. This report followed a mission conducted between March 26 and April 8, 2025, involving key stakeholders such as the Ministry of Finance (represented by **Marterbauer**) and the Central Bank (led by **Holzmann**). 

Key findings for Austria included:
*   **Economic Outlook:** Analysis of the 1.3% GDP contraction in 2024 and projected near-zero growth for 2025, with recovery expected in 2026.
*   **Fiscal Policy:** Evaluation of the government's consolidation plan to reduce the deficit below 2% of GDP.
*   **Labor and Demographics:** Identification of structural reforms needed to increase labor supply, specifically targeting female and elderly participation.
*   **Financial Stability:** Assessment of the banking sector's capital ratios (CET1 at 17.7%) against rising Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) in the commercial real estate sector.
*   **Governance:** Monitoring of Austria's AML/CFT framework and efforts to reduce corporate "red tape."
The consultation concluded with the IMF Executive Board on July 3, 2025.

### Romania
The IMF also concluded its 2025 Article IV consultation with Romania, following mission discussions that took place between September 3 and September 12, 2025. The Staff Report was completed on October 15, 2025, with a formal press release issued on November 14, 2025.

Key highlights for Romania included:
*   **Fiscal Consolidation & Debt Risk:** Romania faces a high risk of sovereign stress due to persistent high fiscal deficits. The IMF welcomes the 2025–26 reform package (VAT hikes, excise duties) but stresses that additional adjustments are required from 2027 to stabilize public debt at approximately 60% of GDP by 2030.
*   **Inflationary Pressures:** Headline inflation remains elevated until mid-2026 due to supply-side shocks, the end of electricity price caps, and increased VAT.
*   **External Imbalances:** The current account deficit reached 8.2% of GDP in 2024. The IMF noted a significant real effective exchange rate (REER) overvaluation, suggesting a need for gradual increases in two-way exchange rate flexibility.
*   **Financial Sector Resilience:** While the banking system is well-capitalized, concerns remain regarding the "sovereign-bank nexus" and sizable unhedged FX loans to non-financial corporations.
*   **Structural Reform & EU Funding:** Romania has struggled with low absorption of EU funds (38% for NGEU) due to administrative bottlenecks. The IMF highlighted the need for an independent agency to oversee State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs).
*   **Green Transition:** Romania is moving toward a low-carbon economy via the ETS 2 and updated National Energy Strategies, with recommendations to strengthen carbon pricing.
