# IMF Staff Report completed

## Event Details

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has completed Article IV Consultation staff reports for multiple member countries in mid-2025, highlighting divergent economic challenges and policy recommendations.

On **2025-06-25**, the IMF staff completed its Article IV Consultation report with **France**, titled "IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation with France: Staff Report and Executive Board Assessment." The mission was led by [[Manuela Goretti]] (Head of Mission) and [[Charles Cohen]] (Head of Financial Sector Assessment Program), in consultation with French authorities including Minister for the Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty [[François Lombard]] and Banque de France Governor [[François Villeroy de Galhau]]. The report assessed France's economic performance in 2024, which demonstrated resilience driven by the Paris Olympics and a robust labor market, resulting in 1.1% real GDP growth. However, the staff highlighted significant fiscal challenges, noting that general government gross debt reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024 due to revenue shortfalls and spending overruns. The report emphasized the urgent need for a credible, frontloaded fiscal consolidation under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. Key recommendations included a frontloaded structural fiscal effort of 1.1% of GDP in 2026, prioritizing spending rationalization over tax increases, alongside structural reforms in education, digital adoption, and labor market flexibility. The completion of this staff report preceded the conclusion of the Article IV consultation by the IMF Executive Board on **2025-07-11** and the issuance of the corresponding press release on **2025-07-14**.

On **2025-07-25**, the IMF staff completed its 2025 Article IV Consultation report with **Hungary**, titled "IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation: Hungary Staff Report and Executive Board Assessment." The mission was led by [[Anke Weber]] (Head of Mission), with input from Department Heads [[Kristina Kostial]] (EUR) and [[Jacques Miniane]] (SPR), in consultation with Hungarian authorities including Minister for National Economy [[Márton Nagy]] and [[Gábor Meizer]] and [[Dániel Palotai]] (OED). The report characterized the Hungarian economy as being at a challenging juncture, marked by stagnant output over the past three years, persistently high inflation above the central bank’s 3% target, and elevated public debt. While the economy showed resilience in 2024 with a current account surplus, growth is projected to be modest (0.7% in 2025) before recovering to 2.0% in 2026. The IMF highlighted significant fiscal sustainability concerns, with public debt reaching 73.5% of GDP in 2024 and projected to rise to ~79% by 2030 under current policies. The staff recommended an additional cumulative fiscal adjustment of ~2% of GDP over 2025-2028 to bring the deficit below 3% by 2027, emphasizing revenue enhancements and the phasing out of energy subsidies. Key risks identified included deepening geoeconomic fragmentation, potential escalation in trade measures, and the suspension of €19 billion in EU funds due to rule of law concerns. The financial sector was assessed as broadly sound but vulnerable to sovereign-bank linkages and corporate sector risks. The completion of this staff report preceded the conclusion of the Article IV consultation by the IMF Executive Board on **2025-08-29**.
