# S&P Sovereign Credit Rating Methodology Publication

## Event Details

**2017: S&P Sovereign Credit Rating Methodology Publication**
S&P released its formal framework for determining sovereign creditworthiness, establishing a multi-dimensional assessment divided into five core pillars:

*   **Institutional Assessment:** Focuses on government policymaking, transparency, accountability, and external security risks.
*   **Economic Assessment:** Measures capacity for tax revenue generation, GDP per capita, growth prospects, and economic diversity.
*   **External Assessment:** Evaluates global positioning, including currency status in international transactions and external liquidity.
*   **Fiscal Assessment:** The "heart" of the process, analyzing both "Flows" (fiscal performance/flexibility) and "Stocks" (debt burden), including contingent liabilities from financial institutions (via [[BICRA]]) and nonfinancial public-sector enterprises (NFPEs).
*   **Monetary Assessment:** Evaluates the central bank's ability to manage shocks, inflation trends, and exchange rate regimes.

The methodology provides a structured "indicative rating" but allows for significant analyst discretion. Analysts can override indicative ratings by more than one notch based on "unspecified factors," particularly regarding subjective criteria like "cohesive civil society" and "policy predictability," as well as supplemental adjustments for "event risk."

**2024: Presentation by Alex Stomper at HU Berlin**
[[Alex Stomper]] presented an analysis of the 2017 S&P methodology at HU Berlin. The presentation highlighted the tension between the structured framework and the subjective "discretionary judgment" used by analysts, posing challenges for the replicability of these ratings. A planned "Citizen Science Project" was mentioned to apply this methodology to European countries to document specific instances where replicability fails.
