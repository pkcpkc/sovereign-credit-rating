# EU Commission 2025 Country Report Austria Publication

## Event Details

On 4 June 2025, the European Commission published its 2025 Country Report for Austria (SWD(2025) 220 final). The report identifies Austria as being in a state of persistent economic recession, marking three consecutive years of GDP contraction (1% in 2023, 1.2% in 2024, and projected -0.3% in 2025). The economic downturn is attributed to industrial weakness, declining exports (particularly to the [[United States]]), and broad-based cost pressures.

Key economic indicators highlighted in the report include:
*   **Fiscal Deficit:** The government deficit is projected to reach 4.4% of GDP in 2025, exceeding EU treaty thresholds. The government has committed to achieving EUR 6.4 billion in savings for 2025 and EUR 8.4 billion for 2026 to address these deficits.
*   **Inflation and Competitiveness:** Inflation, which peaked at 8.6% in 2022, had declined to 2.9% by 2024 but is expected to remain above the 2% target in 2025. Austria has lost cost competitiveness since 2019, with nominal unit labor costs rising 10% more than the [[Euro Area]] average.
*   **Labor Market:** While the unemployment rate remains contained at 5.3%, the working-age population is declining, and total weekly hours worked are decreasing due to a rise in part-time work.
*   **Productivity:** Labor productivity growth has slowed significantly since 2008, averaging 0.6% annually, posing long-term risks to growth prospects.

Structural challenges identified include a narrow tax base, skills shortages in ICT and construction, and high public spending driven by aging costs. Pension expenditure is projected to rise from 14.2% of GDP in 2024 to 15% in 2030, and healthcare spending is also expected to increase. The report calls for comprehensive structural reforms, including pension and healthcare adjustments, tax mix optimization, and regulatory simplification to restore competitiveness and ensure long-term fiscal sustainability.
