# Release of IMF Press Release and Executive Board Assessment

## Event Details

On January 21, 2025, the [[International Monetary Fund (IMF)]] Executive Board concluded its discussions on the [[Republic of Poland]]'s 2024 [[Article IV Consultation]]. This assessment followed the completion of the [[IMF Staff Report]] on December 16, 2024, which analyzed Poland's economic performance through Q3/Q4 2024 and provided projections through 2029. The mission was led by [[Jan Kees Martijn]], with oversight from [[Uma Ramakrishnan]] and [[Fabian Bornhorst]] of the [[European Department]], and [[Patryk Łoszewski]] serving as the Executive Director for Poland.

The Board commended the Polish authorities for their prudent monetary policy and the implementation of a [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)]] aimed at fiscal consolidation. Key findings and recommendations from the assessment included:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation and Debt Sustainability:** The general government deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, triggering the EU's [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]]. Public debt is projected to rise from approximately 50% to 65% of GDP by 2029. The Board endorsed the MTFSP target of reducing the deficit to 2.9% of GDP by 2028 but recommended frontloading consolidation with an adjustment of ~0.5% of GDP in 2025 to support disinflation and allow for earlier monetary easing.
*   **Monetary Policy and Inflation:** While headline inflation declined to ~4-5%, core inflation remained sticky due to wage growth in a tight labor market. The Board agreed that the [[National Bank of Poland (NBP)]] should maintain a tight monetary stance until there is clear evidence of decelerating wage growth, potentially allowing rate cuts to begin in mid-2025.
*   **Growth and External Sector:** GDP growth accelerated to 2.8% in 2024 and is projected to reach 3.5% in 2025, supported by [[Next Generation EU (NGEU)]] fund absorption. However, the [[Real Effective Exchange Rate]] appreciation has weighed on cost-competitiveness, and the current account is projected to move into deficit (-1.5% of GDP) over the medium term.
*   **Structural Reforms:** The Board highlighted the need for reforms to address [[Population Aging]] and the [[Climate Transition]], including pension adjustments to link retirement ages to life expectancy and accelerating green energy permitting. The integration of approximately 976,000 [[Ukrainian refugees]] into the labor market was noted as a positive factor mitigating demographic headwinds.
*   **Financial Sector Stability:** The [[Banking Sector]] remained resilient with strong capital buffers, though risks from [[CHF Mortgages]] litigation and distortions in private credit due to tax incentives for public debt were identified. The Board recommended eliminating these distortions to support credit recovery.

The assessment also noted improvements in institutional frameworks, including amendments to the stabilizing expenditure rule to align with new EU fiscal rules and plans to establish an [[Independent Fiscal Council]] in 2026 to enhance transparency and accountability, particularly regarding [[Polish Development Bank (BGK)]] activities.
