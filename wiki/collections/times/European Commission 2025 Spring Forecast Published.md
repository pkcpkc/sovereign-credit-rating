# European Commission 2025 Spring Forecast Published

## Event Details

On April 2025, the European Commission published its 2025 Spring Forecast, providing a comprehensive assessment of Hungary's macroeconomic outlook. The report projected GDP growth to rebound from 0.5% in 2024 to 0.8% in 2025, with a stronger acceleration to 2.5% anticipated in 2026, driven largely by manufacturing investments in the electric vehicle sector. However, the outlook remains subject to significant uncertainty regarding global value chain integration and potential tariff impacts.

Inflation was noted to have declined to 3.7% in 2024 from a peak of 17.0% in 2023, yet underlying pressures remained elevated at 5.9%, complicating monetary policy effectiveness. A primary concern highlighted in the forecast was fiscal sustainability, with public debt projected to rise to 73.5% of GDP in 2024 and further to 74.5% by 2025. Debt-servicing costs were identified as among the highest in the European Union, with implicit interest rates near 6%, posing medium-to-long-term sustainability risks.

The report also scrutinized Hungary's budgetary framework, criticizing the 2026 budget law for revising the deficit target upward from 2.9% to 3.7% of GDP. Structural weaknesses, including the absence of multiannual spending ceilings and the frequent use of "state of danger" decrees that bypass parliamentary oversight, were cited as factors undermining policy predictability and accountability.

Subsequent events related to this forecast included the submission of Hungary's 2026 Budget Law to Parliament on May 6, 2025, and the European Council's endorsement of Hungary's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan on May 18, 2025. The government had previously introduced retail profit margin caps on food products in March 2025 and personal hygiene/cleaning products in May 2025, measures that were later challenged by the European Court of Justice in September 2024 regarding agricultural price fixing. Additionally, Hungary submitted its Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan and an addendum to the European Commission in November and December 2024, respectively, before requesting the activation of a national escape clause for defence spending on April 30, 2025.
