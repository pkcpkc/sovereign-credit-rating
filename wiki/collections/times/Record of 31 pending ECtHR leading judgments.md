# Record of 31 pending ECtHR leading judgments

## Event Details

As of January 1, 2025, Slovakia reached a record high of 31 leading judgments from the **European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR)** that remained pending implementation. This figure serves as a key indicator of the weak enforcement of international judicial decisions within the country's legal framework. The accumulation of these unimplemented judgments highlights systemic challenges in aligning domestic practices with ECtHR case law.

By June 2025, the number of pending leading judgments had risen further to 33, suggesting a continued lack of progress in addressing these outstanding human rights violations. This trend is identified in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Slovakia** as a significant threat to institutional strength and policy credibility. The failure to promptly implement ECtHR judgments contributes to the broader concerns regarding the effectiveness of the justice system and the protection of fundamental rights in Slovakia.

The persistence of these pending cases occurs amidst a broader context of rule of law deterioration, including low perceived judicial independence, interference by the Prosecutor General, and the dissolution of specialized anti-corruption entities such as the **National Crime Agency (NAKA)**. The **European Commission** has noted that while an intensive dialogue is ongoing, the practical application of international obligations remains insufficient, with the rising count of pending judgments reflecting a lack of effective mechanisms to ensure compliance with final international court decisions.
