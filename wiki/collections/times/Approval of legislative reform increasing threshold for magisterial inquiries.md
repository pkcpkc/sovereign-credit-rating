# Approval of legislative reform increasing threshold for magisterial inquiries

## Event Details

On April 1, 2025, the Maltese government approved a controversial legislative reform (Bill 125) that increases the legal threshold required for citizens to initiate magisterial inquiries. Magisterial inquiries serve as a critical mechanism for citizen-led investigations into public interest matters, particularly high-level offenses and potential corruption.

The reform has drawn significant criticism from civil society stakeholders and international observers, including the European Commission, who argue that raising the barriers for public petitions undermines accountability mechanisms. Concerns were raised that the reform could hinder the ability of citizens to trigger investigations into high-level corruption, effectively narrowing the space for civic engagement and reducing checks on executive power.

This event is cited in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta** as a key example of limited progress in the "Checks and Balances" pillar. The report notes that while the government plans to introduce a Public Consultation Department, the approval of this reform reflects a broader trend of constraining civil society space and reducing transparency in legislative processes regarding high-level accountability.
