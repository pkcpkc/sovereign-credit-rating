# Constitutional Court ruling on differentiated autonomy reform

## Event Details

In December 2024, the Italian **Constitutional Court** issued a ruling partially declaring the **differentiated autonomy reform** unconstitutional. This judicial decision resolved significant political uncertainty surrounding the legislative framework for regional autonomy, which had fragmented legislative frameworks at regional levels and posed a threat to policymaking stability.

The European Commission's 2025 Country Report on Italy identifies this ruling as a key factor in the assessment of Italy's **geopolitical and political stability**. While the reform aimed to address regional disparities, the Constitutional Court's intervention highlighted the constitutional limits of such differentiated autonomy proposals. The report notes that political uncertainty regarding the implementation of these reforms had been a threat to stability, contributing to fears of regional instability and socio-economic divergence between the North and South (Mezzogiorno).

The ruling is cited in the context of **policymaking stability**, where the fragmentation of legislative frameworks due to differentiated autonomy proposals was listed as a specific threat. By partially striking down the reform, the Court reinforced the unity of the legislative framework, although the report continues to flag the heavy reliance on Decree-Laws for legislative output as a separate risk to long-term policy predictability. This event underscores the tension between regional decentralization efforts and constitutional constraints, a factor monitored closely by credit rating agencies and EU institutions as part of Italy's institutional strength and governance risk profile.
