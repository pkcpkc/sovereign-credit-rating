# Implementation of new pension reforms to tighten early retirement access

## Event Details

**Date**: 2025–2026  
**Context**: Implementation of new pension reforms to tighten early retirement access in Austria

The implementation of new pension reforms in Austria between 2025 and 2026 represents a critical structural measure aimed at addressing long-term fiscal sustainability challenges driven by an aging population. This reform is part of a broader set of consolidation measures adopted by the newly formed government to achieve EUR 6.4 billion in savings for 2025, as outlined in the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan submitted to the [[European Commission]].

According to the [[2025 Country Report - Austria (European Commission)]], age-related public spending in Austria is projected to rise from 28% of GDP in 2024 to 30% by 2070. A primary driver of this increase is the low effective retirement age compared to the EU average, which has led to rising pension expenditures. The reforms specifically target the tightening of access to early retirement schemes to mitigate this trend and ensure the long-term viability of the pension system amidst a shrinking working-age population, which began declining in 2024.

These measures are closely linked to the government's broader fiscal consolidation efforts, which include the abolition of paid educational leave (*Bildungskarenz*) effective January 6, 2025, and parliamentary approval of budget consolidation measures on March 6, 2025. While the labor market has remained resilient with an unemployment rate of 5.3% in 2025, the reforms address structural issues such as declining labor productivity growth and the need to extend working lives to support economic stability during a period of recession.

The European Commission highlights that while Austria has strong institutional trust and a comprehensive national fiscal framework, the complexity of regulations and the need for clear reference points in institutional tasks (shared between [[WIFO]] and [[FISK]]) pose challenges. The pension reforms are thus a key component in aligning fiscal policy with long-term sustainability goals, reducing the pressure on the budget caused by demographic shifts and ensuring that the state can manage its gross debt ratio, projected to reach 84.0% of GDP in 2025.
