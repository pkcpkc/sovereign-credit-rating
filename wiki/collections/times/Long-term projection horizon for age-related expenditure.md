# Long-term projection horizon for age-related expenditure

## Event Details

The **2025 Country Report - Malta** published by the [[European Commission]] establishes **2070** as the critical long-term projection horizon for assessing fiscal sustainability regarding age-related expenditures in Malta. This horizon highlights significant structural pressures arising from an aging population, identifying Malta as facing the second-largest projected increase in pension spending among EU Member States.

### Projected Expenditure Trajectories (Baseline to 2070)

According to the Commission's analysis, total age-related expenditure (encompassing pensions, healthcare, and long-term care) is projected to rise substantially:

*   **Total Age-Related Expenditure**: Increase from **17% of GDP** in the base year to **26% of GDP** by 2070.
*   **Pension Spending**: Projected to rise from **6% of GDP** to **10.5% of GDP**, effectively doubling its share of the economy.
*   **Healthcare Spending**: Expected to surge from **5.1% of GDP** to **7.2% of GDP**.

### Fiscal Sustainability Implications

While Malta currently benefits from a low government debt ratio (47.4% of GDP in 2024), the report warns that these long-term demographic trends pose a "significant long-term pressure" on fiscal sustainability. The widening gap between projected age-related costs and current revenue streams necessitates proactive policy adjustments. Key factors influencing this trajectory include:

*   **Demographic Aging**: The primary driver of increased demand for pensions and healthcare services.
*   **Healthcare Financing**: Current out-of-pocket costs and potential unmet needs in long-term care contribute to the rising expenditure baseline.
*   **Fiscal Space**: Current low debt levels provide immediate fiscal space for adjustment, but the 2070 horizon underscores the urgency of implementing structural reforms under the [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan]].

The report emphasizes that without timely intervention, the cumulative growth in net expenditure related to these demographics could threaten the long-term stability of Malta's public finances, despite its strong short-to-medium term growth forecasts and robust external position.
