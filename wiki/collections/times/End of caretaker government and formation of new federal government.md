# End of caretaker government and formation of new federal government

## Event Details

In February 2025, the period of the **Caretaker Government** in Belgium concluded with the formation of a new federal government. This political transition marked a significant shift in the country's institutional landscape, following a prolonged phase of administrative stability under caretaker arrangements.

The formation of the new government was characterized by specific policy intentions and structural challenges identified in subsequent assessments:

*   **Government Agreement:** A formal government agreement was signed for the 2025-2029 term in March 2025, outlining the new administration's priorities.
*   **Judicial Reform Intentions:** The new government expressed a clear intention to transfer budgetary management powers for the justice administration from the executive branch to the judiciary. This move was intended to enhance managerial autonomy and address concerns regarding **Judicial Independence**, which was perceived as high by both the general public (64%) and businesses (72%).
*   **Integrity Framework:** The administration planned to establish specialized investigative and prosecutorial structures targeting financial crime and corruption, aiming to improve the effectiveness of the **Central Office for the Repression of Corruption** (CDBC-OCRC).
*   **Continuity Concerns:** Despite the formation of a new government, uncertainty persisted regarding the continuation of lobbying reforms and the consistency of integrity policies across local police zones. The new government remained undecided on draft lobbying regulations, and progress on "revolving doors" rules was limited to non-binding circulars.

This event is contextualized within the broader 2025 Rule of Law Report for Belgium, which highlighted that while political stability was a strength, the fragmentation of legal frameworks between federal and regional levels remained a threat to institutional governance.
