# Draft law to dismantle Supreme Court Chamber of Extraordinary Control proposed

## Event Details

In April 2025, the [[Ministry of Justice (Poland)]] proposed a draft law aimed at dismantling the [[Supreme Court (Poland)]] Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs. This legislative initiative was part of significant efforts by the Polish Government to address long-standing [[Rule of Law]] concerns and implement the Action Plan on the Rule of Law, specifically targeting the structure of the judiciary to enhance its independence.

The proposal to dismantle the Chamber of Extraordinary Control was closely linked to other judicial reforms, including a separate draft law addressing the status of approximately 1,600 judges appointed at the request of the [[National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)]] (NCJ) in its post-2017 composition. These measures sought to resolve structural issues within the Polish judiciary that had been flagged by the [[European Commission]] and the [[Venice Commission]].

According to the [[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Poland]] published in July 2025, these proposals represented a key milestone in the government's engagement with EU institutions to restore trust in judicial institutions. The dismantling of the Chamber was cited as a step towards strengthening the separation of powers and aligning Poland's judicial framework with [[European Union]] standards, although the broader implementation of rule of law reforms continued to face challenges, including the ongoing constitutional crisis regarding the [[Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)]].
