# Council endorses Poland's medium-term fiscal-structural plan and initiates excessive deficit procedure

## Event Details

On January 21, 2025, the Council of the European Union formally endorsed Poland's medium-term fiscal-structural plan and simultaneously initiated the excessive deficit procedure (EDP) against the Member State. This decision followed the publication of the European Commission's 2025 Country Report on Poland, which highlighted a general government deficit of 6.6% of GDP in 2024, significantly exceeding the Treaty reference value of 3%.

The EDP initiation reflects concerns over Poland's rising public debt, projected to reach 58.0% of GDP in 2025, and the need for credible fiscal consolidation. The endorsed plan outlines a trajectory to reduce the deficit below the 3% threshold by 2028. The fiscal adjustment path is complex, constrained by high and rising defense spending (expected to reach 2.8% of GDP in 2025) and the necessity to maintain public investment levels. The plan leverages the "national escape clause" flexibility allowed under the new EU fiscal framework to balance consolidation with investment needs.

This event marks a critical juncture in Poland's economic governance, occurring alongside the establishment of an independent Fiscal Council (operational from 2026) aimed at strengthening policy credibility and institutional strength. The Council's endorsement acknowledges the government's commitment to fiscal discipline while monitoring the implementation of structural reforms aimed at improving competitiveness, addressing skills shortages, and ensuring the long-term sustainability of the pension and healthcare systems. Poland submitted its Annual Progress Report on the fiscal plan's implementation on April 30, 2025, demonstrating ongoing compliance efforts under the EDP.
