# Previous Sovereign Rating Methodology published

## Event Details

On **2014-12-23**, S&P Global Ratings published the previous version of its Sovereign Rating Methodology. This framework served as the precursor to the current 2017 methodology, which introduced significant shifts in how sovereign creditworthiness is evaluated.

Key differences between the previous methodology and the 2017 update include:
*   **Local-Currency Caps:** The previous methodology allowed for a local-currency rating up to two notches above the foreign-currency rating, whereas the 2017 update restricted this to a maximum of one notch.
*   **Debt Metrics:** The earlier framework did not prioritize the shift toward **net general government debt** (deducting liquid financial assets) as the primary metric for fiscal performance.
*   **External Ratios:** The calculation of external assessment ratios relied on different denominators before the integration of **Current Account Payments (CAP)**.

The publication of this methodology provided the foundational framework for assessing a sovereign's ability and willingness to service financial obligations to nonofficial creditors, eventually evolving into the five-pillar assessment (Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, and Monetary) used today.
