# Belgian Court of Audit reports shortcomings in justice digitalisation strategy

## Event Details

In December 2024, the [[Belgian Court of Audit]] issued a report identifying serious shortcomings in the digitalisation strategy of the Belgian Justice Department. The audit highlighted a lack of an overarching approach to digital transformation and pointed to significant fraud risks associated with the current management practices. This finding was cited in the [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Rule of Law Report as a key threat to policy credibility and institutional strength.

Prior to the final report, the Government had sent an initial action plan to the Court of Audit in September 2024 regarding these digitalisation concerns. The 2025 Rule of Law Report noted that the Government is actively addressing these criticisms to mitigate the identified risks. These digitalisation challenges exist within a broader context of structural resource deficiencies and efficiency issues in the Belgian justice system, where backlogs and length of proceedings remain critical concerns, particularly in the Court of Appeal of Brussels.

The shortcomings in digital strategy are part of wider integrity and management issues identified by international bodies, including the [[Council of Europe]] and [[GRECO]], which have monitored Belgium's compliance with rule of law standards. The Government's response to these audit findings is being viewed as a test of its commitment to judicial reform and institutional stability, especially given its stated intention to transfer budgetary management powers for justice administration from the executive to the judiciary.
