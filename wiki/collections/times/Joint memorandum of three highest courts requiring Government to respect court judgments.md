# Joint memorandum of three highest courts requiring Government to respect court judgments

## Event Details

On **July 19, 2024**, the three highest courts of Belgium issued a joint memorandum explicitly requiring the Government to respect and implement court judgments. This unprecedented intervention occurred against a backdrop of limited progress in ensuring public authorities comply with final rulings, a key concern highlighted in the European Commission's **2025 Rule of Law Report**.

The memorandum served as a critical accountability mechanism to address structural deficiencies in executive compliance. At the time, the Belgian Government was already under supervision by the **Council of Europe** (Committee of Ministers) regarding structural issues identified by the **European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR)** in the landmark case *Camara v. Belgium*. By **January 1, 2025**, it was reported that 17 leading judgments of the ECtHR remained pending implementation in Belgium.

The judicial intervention underscored the tension between the judiciary and the executive branch, particularly following a period of political fragmentation during the caretaker Government phase between June 2024 and February 2025. The memorandum reinforced the principle of judicial independence and the binding nature of court decisions, countering threats to institutional credibility such as alleged information leaks and undue political influence in sensitive cases. It stands as a significant strength in the accountability pillar of Belgium's rule of law framework, emphasizing the judiciary's role in enforcing constitutional and international legal obligations on the executive.
