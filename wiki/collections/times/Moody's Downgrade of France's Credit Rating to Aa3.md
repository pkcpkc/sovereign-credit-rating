# Moody's Downgrade of France's Credit Rating to Aa3

## Event Details

On December 2024, credit rating agency Moody's downgraded France's sovereign credit rating to Aa3. This downgrade occurred in the context of significant fiscal deterioration and political instability, specifically following the fall of Prime Minister Barnier's Government in December 2024. The downgrade reflects concerns over France's high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, alongside fiscal slippages and revenue shortfalls that have persisted in recent years.

The rating action was influenced by several key factors identified in subsequent assessments, including the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation. These factors include:

- **Fiscal Performance and Flexibility:** Threats from high and rising public debt, fiscal slippages, and rising debt service costs.
- **Debt Burden:** High general government gross debt (113.1% of GDP) with an upward trajectory under baseline scenarios, despite predominantly local currency debt and long average maturity (8 years).
- **Policy Predictability:** Risks associated with abrupt policy reversals, such as the government fall, and unclear medium-term fiscal measures.
- **External Position:** High sovereign refinancing needs and a negative net international investment position (NIIP).

Despite these challenges, France retains strengths such as a robust financial oversight system, an established rule of law, a strong record of timely debt repayment, and the Euro's status as a global reserve currency. The downgrade highlights the urgent need for credible structural fiscal measures, as recommended by the IMF, to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029 and place debt on a sustainable path.
