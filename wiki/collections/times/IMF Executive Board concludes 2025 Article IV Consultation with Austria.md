# IMF Executive Board concludes 2025 Article IV Consultation with Austria

## Event Details

On **July 3, 2025**, the [[International Monetary Fund]] Executive Board concluded the 2025 Article IV Consultation with Austria, following a staff report completed on June 9, 2025. The mission, led by Mr. Fletcher, began in Vienna on March 26, 2025, and involved discussions with key officials including Finance Minister [[Christian Karner Marterbauer]] and Oesterreichische Nationalbank Governor [[Robert Holzmann]].

The consultation highlighted a challenging economic environment for Austria, characterized by two successive years of recession in 2023 and 2024, driven by energy price shocks and Euro Area monetary tightening. GDP contracted by 1.3% in 2024 and is projected to shrink further by 0.1% in 2025 before a modest recovery of 0.8% in 2026. Inflation remained above the Euro Area average at 3.2% in 2025 due to sticky services inflation and the expiration of energy support measures, though it is expected to converge toward 2% by 2026.

Fiscal concerns are paramount, with the deficit widening to 4.7% of GDP in 2024 and public debt rising to 81.2%. While the new ÖVP-SPÖ-NEOS coalition government, formed in March 2025, has announced near-term consolidation measures, the IMF emphasized the need for substantial medium-term adjustment to stabilize the debt ratio against rising pressures from demographic aging, defense spending, and the green transition. The Fund recommended reducing the deficit to below 2% of GDP through pension reforms, healthcare efficiency, and tax mix improvements.

Structurally, the IMF urged reforms to boost labor supply—particularly among women and the elderly—to counteract demographic decline and sluggish productivity. It also advised reducing regulatory barriers in services and housing. In the financial sector, while Austrian banks remain well-capitalized with a CET1 ratio of 17.7%, risks persist from Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposures. The IMF recommended making borrower-based mortgage lending standards, which lapse in July 2025, permanent to prevent macro-financial risks. Austria’s external position remains robust with a current account surplus of 2.4% of GDP, though competitiveness risks exist if inflation differentials persist.
