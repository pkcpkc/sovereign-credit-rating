# Change of government in Slovakia

## Event Details

In October 2024, Slovakia underwent a change of government, a political transition that significantly impacted the country's budget preparation timeline and policymaking stability. This shift in leadership occurred against a backdrop of intense political polarization and preceded key international assessments of the nation's economic health.

The new administration faced immediate challenges regarding fiscal consolidation and governance. By July 2024 (prior to the finalization of the new government's full term, reflecting the transitional period), the European Council initiated an Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) against Slovakia due to a widening fiscal deficit, which reached 5.8% of GDP in 2024. The change in government necessitated a rapid adoption of an ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028) to address the deficit and put public debt on a sustainable downward path.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) accounted for this political transition in its 2025 Article IV Consultation, noting that the "recent government change impacting budget preparation time" was a threat to policymaking stability. However, the IMF also highlighted strengths in the new administration's approach, specifically the adoption of the consolidation plan and the strong institutional support from the Council for Budgetary Responsibility. The consultation, concluded in March 2025, endorsed the government's fiscal targets while emphasizing the need for further expenditure-based savings and structural reforms to address long-term risks such as demographic aging and economic dependence on the automotive sector.
