# End of caretaker Government period; formation of new Government

## Event Details

In **February 2025**, Belgium marked the end of a prolonged period of political uncertainty following the **start of the caretaker Government period in June 2024**. This eight-month interval was characterized by political fragmentation and delays in forming a stable administration, which the **European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report** identified as a significant threat to **geopolitical and political stability**.

The formation of the **new Government** in February 2025 brought both immediate commitments and lingering institutional challenges:

*   **Judicial Stability and Budgeting**: The new Government demonstrated a commitment to institutional stability by exempting the judiciary from proposed budget cuts. It also outlined intentions to transfer budgetary management powers for justice administration from the executive to the judiciary, although this remains subject to ongoing debate and criticism from judicial bodies like the High Council for Justice regarding the linkage to disciplinary measures.
*   **Anti-Corruption and Integrity**: While the new Government planned the creation of a new financial investigation service (**FIOD-SRFF**) and a specialised prosecution service for financial crime, significant gaps in the anti-corruption framework persisted. Concerns remained regarding the handling of high-level corruption cases by the **Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC)**, including allegations of information leaks and potential political interference. Furthermore, the new Government had not yet taken a decision on draft lobbying rules, and progress on "revolving door" regulations remained limited to non-binding circulars.
*   **Legislative Actions**: The period surrounding the new Government's formation saw several key legislative milestones, including the King sanctioning the law on **whistleblower protection** for the House of Representatives and Senate on **27 March 2025**.
*   **Ongoing Institutional Challenges**: Despite the formation of the new Government, Belgium continued to face criticism for shortcomings in the digitalisation of the justice department, lack of transparency in political party financing and asset declarations, and insufficient action on structural issues identified by the **European Court of Human Rights** (specifically *Camara v. Belgium*). The new administration also faced scrutiny over proposed measures that could restrict civic space, including judicial demonstration bans.

The transition from the caretaker period to the new Government was thus marked by a mix of stabilizing actions, such as protecting judicial budgets, and persistent structural deficits in accountability, lobbying transparency, and compliance with international court rulings.
