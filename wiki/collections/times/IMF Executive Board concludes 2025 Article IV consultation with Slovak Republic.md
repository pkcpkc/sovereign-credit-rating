# IMF Executive Board concludes 2025 Article IV consultation with Slovak Republic

## Event Details

The IMF Executive Board concluded its 2025 Article IV consultation with the Slovak Republic on **March 24, 2025**, following staff report consideration on March 18, 2025. The consultation, led by IMF mission head Saxegaard in engagement with Slovak officials including National Bank of Slovakia Governor [[Kažimír]] and Finance Minister [[Kamenický]], assessed an economy that recovered from recent headwinds with growth accelerating to 2.0% in 2024, outpacing the Euro Area average.

### Key Findings and Assessment

*   **Fiscal Consolidation Plan**: The Board endorsed the Slovak government’s ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028), which aims to reduce the fiscal deficit from 5.8% of GDP in 2024 to approximately 2.2% by 2028. This initiative is a response to the Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) initiated by the European Commission in July 2024. While the 2025 budget measures were viewed as a step in the right direction, the IMF staff emphasized the need for further deficit-reducing measures, particularly on the expenditure side, to meet medium-term targets and ensure public debt sustainability.
*   **Growth and Inflation Outlook**: Real GDP growth is projected to moderate to 1.8% in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation and weak external demand, before recovering to 2.1% in 2026. Inflation is expected to rise temporarily to 4.1% in 2025, driven by indirect tax increases, before returning to the 2% target by early 2027.
*   **Debt Sustainability**: Public debt is projected to rise in the medium term, reaching 74.3% of GDP by 2030 under the baseline scenario. The long-term risk of sovereign stress is assessed as high due to significant aging-related costs, which could increase fiscal burdens by 5.6% of GDP by 2050 without further structural reforms.
*   **Financial Sector Resilience**: The banking sector remains resilient, with capital adequacy ratios well above regulatory minima and ample liquidity buffers. However, vulnerabilities persist in the commercial real estate (CRE) sector, specifically the office segment, and from the upcoming refixation of residential mortgages. The IMF recommended strengthening the macroprudential framework.
*   **Structural Reforms and Governance**: The report highlighted the need for reforms to diversify the economy away from its heavy dependence on the automotive sector, boost innovation, and increase labor force participation. Strengthening governance, reducing corruption vulnerabilities, and enhancing judicial independence were identified as essential for maintaining investor confidence and ensuring the effective absorption of EU funds.
*   **External Sector**: The current account deficit widened to 2.7% of GDP in 2024, assessed as moderately weaker than fundamentals. The Net International Investment Position (NIIP) remains negative (-53.5% of GDP) but is improving, supported by strong Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) inflows.
