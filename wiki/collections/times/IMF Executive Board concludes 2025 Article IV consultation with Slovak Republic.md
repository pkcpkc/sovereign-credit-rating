# IMF Executive Board Concludes 2025 Article IV Consultation with Slovak Republic

## Event Details

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) Executive Board concluded the 2025 Article IV consultation with the Slovak Republic on March 24, 2025. The conclusion was reached on a lapse-of-time basis, endorsing the staff appraisal without a formal meeting, following discussions with Slovak officials that concluded on January 28, 2025. This event marks the culmination of a review process that began with staff discussions in late 2024 and the completion of the IMF Staff Report on March 3, 2025.

The consultation followed a change of government in Slovakia in October 2024 and occurred against the backdrop of the European Council initiating an Excessive Deficit Procedure against Slovakia in July 2023. The IMF endorsed the Slovak authorities' ambitious fiscal consolidation plan, which targets reducing the headline deficit to 4.7 percent of GDP in 2025, down from 5.8 percent in 2024. The goal is to bring the deficit close to 2 percent of GDP by 2028 to ensure long-term debt sustainability, particularly given rising aging-related costs.

Economically, Slovakia demonstrated resilience in 2024, with growth accelerating to 2.0 percent, outpacing the euro area average, driven by strong private consumption and robust public investments funded by EU resources. However, growth is projected to moderate to 1.8 percent in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation measures before recovering to 2.1 percent in 2026. Inflation dynamics remain a key focus, with headline inflation rising temporarily to 4.1 percent in 2025 due to higher indirect taxes and global food price pressures, before moderating to the 2 percent target by early 2027.

The IMF highlighted persistent vulnerabilities in the financial sector, particularly regarding residential and commercial real estate markets, although the banking sector remains resilient with strong capital buffers. Structural reforms were emphasized as critical, including diversifying the economy away from heavy dependence on the automotive sector, addressing demographic headwinds, and strengthening governance and judicial independence to mitigate corruption risks and ensure effective absorption of EU funds. Key officials involved in the dialogue included Kažimír, Governor of the National Bank of Slovakia; Kamenický, Minister of Finance; and Taraba, Minister of the Environment, alongside IMF representatives Oya Celasun and Stefania Fabrizio.
