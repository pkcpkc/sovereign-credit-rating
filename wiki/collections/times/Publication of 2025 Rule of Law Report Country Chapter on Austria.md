# Publication of 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Austria

## Event Details

On **2025-07-08**, the European Commission published the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Austria, assessing the state of the rule of law across four key pillars: the justice system, anti-corruption framework, media pluralism and freedom, and other institutional checks and balances. The report, based on the Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 920 final, highlights that Austria maintains a high level of perceived judicial independence among the general public (86% in 2025, up from 82% in 2024) and a highly efficient justice system, with civil and commercial cases resolved in an average of 141 days.

However, the Commission identified significant structural challenges and limited progress in several areas. Key concerns include the lack of progress in introducing judicial involvement in the appointment of administrative court presidents, which raises concerns regarding European standards. Additionally, the reform to establish an independent Federal Prosecution Office has seen limited advancement; the Minister of Justice retains the power to issue instructions in individual cases, a practice that continues despite an independent investigative commission's confirmation of past political influence attempts on prosecutorial actions.

In the anti-corruption sphere, while Austria scores 67/100 in the Corruption Perceptions Index and the Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK) detected 83 potential cases in 2024, there is limited progress on asset declaration rules for Members of Parliament and no progress on strengthening the lobbying framework, which remains weak and dates back to 2013. The media landscape shows strong regulatory independence via KommAustria but faces economic pressures and concerns regarding the fair distribution of state advertising, where spending more than doubled to EUR 417.9 million in 2024. Institutional checks and balances are supported by effective oversight bodies like the Ombudsman Board and the Court of Audit, though appointment procedures for independent authorities remain susceptible to politicization.

The report contextualizes these findings within recent legislative and administrative developments, including the entry into force of criminal procedure law amendments and the expansion of compulsory online publication of judgments to Higher Regional Courts on **2025-01-01**. Other relevant events during the reporting period include the presentation of the New Government Programme in **2025-03**, the reformation of ORF management bodies in **2025-06**, the implementation of significant court fee increases (approx. 23%) on **2025-04-01**, and the start of audits of party statements by the Court of Audit under new rules on **2025-01-01**. The Freedom of Information Act is expected to enter into force in **2025-09**. The Commission reiterates recommendations for Austria to address judicial appointments, finalize prosecutorial independence, enhance parliamentary transparency, strengthen lobbying rules, and ensure fair state advertising distribution.
