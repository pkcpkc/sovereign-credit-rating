# Adoption of Law on Conflicts of Interest and Gifts

## Event Details

On 6 March 2025, Romania adopted the **Law on Conflicts of Interest and Gifts**, a significant legislative reform aimed at strengthening institutional integrity and preventing corruption. This law was part of a broader set of Justice Laws completed in response to recommendations from the **Venice Commission**.

The adoption of this law addresses key concerns regarding legislative quality and transparency in public office. It complements other reforms, such as measures to extend the mandate of high-ranking prosecutors and ensure the independence of judicial police. The legislative process was noted for its alignment with **Rule of Law** standards, contributing to Romania's institutional and governance strengths in the eyes of international observers.

Despite these improvements, the law operates within a context where legislative unpredictability remains a challenge, often characterized by the frequent use of **Government Emergency Ordinances** and inadequate public consultation. The law is also contextualized by concurrent events, including the annulment of the first round of **Presidential Elections** by the **Constitutional Court** in December 2024 and the subsequent repeat election in May 2025.

The implementation of this law is part of Romania's ongoing efforts to combat high perceived corruption in the public sector and to improve the investment climate. It works in tandem with the **National Integrity Agency**, which, despite facing resource constraints, continues to play a critical role in maintaining transparency and accountability. The law also intersects with broader **Anti-Corruption Strategy** initiatives, including the 2021-2025 strategy and the adoption of the **Strategy for Open Government** in January 2025.
