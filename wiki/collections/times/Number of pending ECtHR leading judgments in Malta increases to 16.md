# Number of pending ECtHR leading judgments in Malta increases to 16

## Event Details

**Date:** 2025-06-16  
**Context:** 2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta

As of June 16, 2025, the number of pending leading judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] (ECtHR) regarding Malta increased to 16. This statistic is highlighted in the European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report as a significant threat to Malta's geopolitical and political stability, indicating a persistent challenge in the timely implementation of international human rights obligations.

The accumulation of these pending judgments is linked to broader systemic issues within the Maltese justice system, particularly concerning the efficiency of judicial proceedings and the enforcement of court decisions. While perceived judicial independence remains high among the general public and companies, the report notes that excessive length of proceedings and backlogs negatively impact legal certainty. The failure to promptly execute ECtHR judgments undermines institutional accountability and raises concerns about the effectiveness of checks and balances in the country.

This development occurs amidst a backdrop of mixed progress in Malta's rule of law framework:
- **Justice Sector:** While the transfer of prosecutorial powers to the [[Office of the Attorney General]] was completed in September 2024, enhancing prosecutorial independence, resource constraints and delays in proceedings continue to hamper the system.
- **Anti-Corruption:** The report cites weak capacity of the [[Permanent Commission Against Corruption]] and high perceived levels of corruption (CPI score 46/100) as ongoing threats.
- **Checks and Balances:** Concerns were raised regarding legislative reforms that increased the threshold for citizen-led magisterial inquiries, potentially limiting accountability mechanisms.

The increase in pending judgments serves as a key indicator for sovereign credit rating assessments, specifically under the "Geopolitical and Political Stability" factor, where the "Pending implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments" is listed as a distinct threat alongside criticisms of legislative reforms limiting citizen-led inquiries.
