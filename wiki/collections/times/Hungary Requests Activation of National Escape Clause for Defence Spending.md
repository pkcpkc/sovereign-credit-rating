# Hungary Requests Activation of National Escape Clause for Defence Spending

## Event Details

On 30 April 2025, the Government of Hungary formally requested the activation of the National Escape Clause within the framework of the EU's fiscal rules, specifically citing increased defence spending as the justification. This move was made in the context of broader macroeconomic pressures, including high debt-servicing costs and persistent inflationary trends.

The request occurred shortly after the European Commission published its 2025 Spring Forecast, which highlighted Hungary's subdued growth prospects and fiscal vulnerabilities. The government's decision to seek this exemption reflects a strategic prioritization of defence expenditures, likely influenced by regional geopolitical tensions and the country's role in EU defence initiatives.

This action is part of a wider series of fiscal adjustments by Hungary throughout 2025, which included the submission of the 2026 Budget Law to Parliament in May and the subsequent endorsement of its Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan by the Council in mid-May. The invocation of the National Escape Clause underscores the tension between Hungary's domestic fiscal autonomy and its obligations under EU economic governance frameworks, particularly regarding budget credibility and expenditure control.
