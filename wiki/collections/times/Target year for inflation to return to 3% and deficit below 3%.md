# Target year for inflation to return to 3% and deficit below 3%

## Event Details

**2027** is projected by the [[IMF]] as the target year for Hungary to achieve two critical macroeconomic milestones: returning the inflation rate to the [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]] (MNB) target of 3% and reducing the fiscal deficit to below 3% of GDP.

According to the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation Staff Report, this convergence relies on sustained policy measures over the preceding years (2025–2026). The baseline projections indicate that under current policies, the fiscal deficit would remain elevated at approximately 4.5% of GDP through the medium term, and public debt would rise to ~79% by 2030. To achieve the <3% deficit target by 2027, the IMF recommends an additional cumulative fiscal adjustment of ~2% of GDP between 2025 and 2028. This adjustment involves implementing a structural primary surplus of around 1.75% of GDP, high-quality revenue enhancements (reducing tax exemptions), and spending rationalization, including the phase-out of energy subsidies and administrative overhead.

Simultaneously, the return of inflation to 3% is conditioned on the MNB maintaining a tight monetary stance into 2026 to durably anchor expectations and the phasing out of distortive regulatory measures such as price, interest, and margin caps. These caps are currently cited as deferring rather than eliminating inflationary pressures and weakening monetary policy transmission. The IMF projects that with these conditions met, inflation will gradually decelerate from mid-2025 levels of 4.4–4.6% to the 3% target by 2027.

Key risks to achieving these 2027 targets include:
*   **Geoeconomic fragmentation:** Deepening trade tensions, particularly exposure to US tariffs and EU supply chain disruptions.
*   **Fiscal slippage:** Risk of expansionary fiscal measures triggered by national elections expected in early 2026.
*   **EU Funding Uncertainty:** Continued suspension or potential cancellation of €19 billion in EU funds due to rule of law concerns, which poses a significant downside risk to fiscal and external balances.
*   **External Shocks:** Intensification of regional conflicts increasing energy security risks and inflationary pressure.
