# High Council for Justice publishes report on Court of Appeal of Brussels backlogs

## Event Details

On November 28, 2024, the **High Council for Justice** published a specific report addressing significant backlogs and efficiency issues within the **Court of Appeal of Brussels**. This publication was highlighted in the European Commission’s **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Belgium** (published July 8, 2025) as a key instance of judicial self-regulation and monitoring efforts.

The report coincided with broader concerns regarding judicial efficiency in Belgium, where excessive length of proceedings remains a critical structural challenge. Specifically:

*   **Focus on Brussels:** The Court of Appeal of Brussels was identified as having particularly problematic case backlogs. The High Council for Justice released data on criminal case durations to shed light on these delays.
*   **Context of Reforms:** This action occurred amidst a government initiative to transfer budgetary management powers for the justice administration from the executive to the judiciary. However, the High Council for Justice and the **Court of Cassation** criticized the government's approach, particularly the linkage of efficiency reforms to disciplinary measures.
*   **Complementary Measures:** Around the same period, the Belgian government sought to address structural resource deficiencies and digitalisation shortcomings (criticized by the **Belgian Court of Audit** in December 2024). In December 2024, an act was adopted on amicable settlements of cases to further reduce backlogs.
*   **Systemic Issues:** Despite these efforts, structural resource shortages, unattractive working conditions, and a lack of overarching digitalisation strategies continued to hinder efficiency improvements across the justice system.

The publication of this report exemplifies the High Council for Justice's role in monitoring judicial performance and identifying specific institutional bottlenecks, such as those in the Brussels Court of Appeal, while operating within a framework of high perceived judicial independence but persistent efficiency gaps.
