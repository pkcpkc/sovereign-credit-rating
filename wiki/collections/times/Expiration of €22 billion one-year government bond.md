# Expiration of €22 billion one-year government bond

## Event Details

In September 2024, Belgium faced the expiration of a €22 billion one-year government bond. This event occurred during a period of heightened fiscal scrutiny and market sensitivity, following the Belgian Federal Elections in June 2024 and preceding the formation of a new federal government in February 2025.

At the time of the bond's maturity, Belgium's public debt stood at 104.1% of GDP, with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) projecting a rise to 123% by 2030 under unchanged policies. The expiration highlighted the challenges associated with managing high public debt burdens as low-interest debt matured and interest costs began to rise. Despite these pressures, the Belgian sovereign credit rating remained supported by strong institutional capacity, high-quality debt management practices, and a long average residual debt maturity of 11.1 years.

The IMF’s 2025 Article IV Consultation noted that while the banking sector remained well-capitalized with low non-performing loans (2.1%), the broader fiscal landscape required urgent consolidation. The new government, formed in February 2025, committed to a seven-year fiscal adjustment plan under the new EU Economic Governance Framework to reduce the deficit durably below 3% of GDP and place debt on a downward path. The successful rollover or repayment of the €22 billion bond was a key test of market confidence in Belgium's fiscal sustainability during this transition period.
