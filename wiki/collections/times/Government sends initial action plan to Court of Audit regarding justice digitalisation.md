# Government sends initial action plan to Court of Audit regarding justice digitalisation

## Event Details

On September 2, 2024, the Belgian Government submitted an initial action plan to the [[Belgian Court of Audit]] in response to serious shortcomings identified in the digitalisation strategy of the justice department. The submission was a direct reaction to criticisms regarding the lack of an overarching approach and significant fraud risks associated with the existing digitalisation efforts.

The [[Belgian Court of Audit]] had previously reported these deficiencies, highlighting that the justice sector's digitalisation strategy suffered from poor management and structural weaknesses. The Government's action plan was intended to address these structural issues and mitigate the identified risks. This event is part of broader concerns regarding judicial efficiency in Belgium, where the European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report noted that while the Government is taking steps to address Court of Audit criticisms, challenges such as backlogs and excessive length of proceedings persist. The digitalisation issues are linked to broader structural resource deficiencies within the justice system.

This initial response preceded a subsequent report by the Court of Audit in December 2024, which further detailed the shortcomings in the justice digitalisation strategy. The Government's commitment to reforming this area is also reflected in its intention to transfer budgetary management powers for the justice administration from the executive to the judiciary, although this has faced some criticism from judicial bodies regarding the linkage to disciplinary reforms.
