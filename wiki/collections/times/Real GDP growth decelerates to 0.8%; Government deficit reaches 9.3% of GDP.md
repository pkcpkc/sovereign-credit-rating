# Real GDP growth decelerates to 0.8%; Government deficit reaches 9.3% of GDP

## Event Details

In 2024, the Romanian economy experienced a significant deceleration in real GDP growth, slowing to **0.8%**. This contraction was driven by a slowdown in private investment and weak net exports, despite continued support from resilient private consumption. Concurrently, the government's fiscal stance deteriorated sharply, resulting in a government deficit that reached **9.3% of GDP**. This surge in the deficit was primarily attributed to generous public sector wage increases, higher public investment spending, and other income policies, causing net expenditure growth to hit 19.9% in 2024—far exceeding recommended maximums under the reformed EU economic governance framework.

These macroeconomic imbalances were reflected in a widening current account deficit of 8.4% of GDP and a rapid accumulation of public debt, which rose to 54.8% of GDP. The poor fiscal performance and growth deceleration prompted the European Commission to highlight critical vulnerabilities in its 2025 Country Report, citing regulatory uncertainty, legislative instability, and weak administrative capacity as key barriers to long-term investment and sustainable growth. In response, the Romanian government adopted emergency measures in late 2024, including wage and pension freezes, to initiate a consolidation path outlined in the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP).
