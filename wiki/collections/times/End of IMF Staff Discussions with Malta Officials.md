# End of IMF Staff Discussions with Malta Officials

## Event Details

On November 15, 2024, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) concluded its staff-level discussions with Maltese officials regarding the 2024 Article IV Consultation. The mission, led by Mission Chief [[Kotaro Ishi]], engaged with key government figures including Minister for Finance and Employment [[Clyde Caruana]], Minister for the Economy [[Silvio Schembri]], and Acting Governor of the Central Bank of Malta [[Alexander Demarco]]. The discussions were subsequently reported to the IMF Executive Board, with the staff report completed on December 17, 2024, and the Executive Board concluding the consultation on January 15, 2025.

The consultation highlighted Malta’s strong economic performance over the past decade, driven by export-oriented services such as tourism and online gaming. However, the IMF emphasized the need to shift toward a productivity-driven growth model to address capacity constraints, including tight labor markets and strained infrastructure. Key recommendations endorsed by the Executive Board included:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: Phasing out broad fixed-energy price policies, which account for 20% of the fiscal deficit, and reallocating resources to investment, innovation, and green transition. The authorities committed to a deficit target of 2.6% of GDP by 2027.
*   **Financial Stability**: Addressing substantial real estate exposure (70% of bank loans) by tightening macroprudential policies, specifically raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer and broadening its scope.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Developing a roadmap for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reform aligned with the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive (implementation deadline 2029) and enhancing educational outcomes to support long-term productivity.
*   **Governance**: Strengthening the Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) framework and continuing judicial reforms.

Malta’s external position remains robust, with a large net international investment position (~95% of GDP) and a current account surplus. Sovereign debt is well below the EU’s 60% ceiling, with low risk of sovereign stress. The IMF noted that while growth is expected to remain high relative to other European countries, risks from geopolitical spillovers and domestic capacity constraints persist. The findings inform Malta’s trajectory under "Malta Vision 2050," a long-term strategic plan aimed at sustainable development.
