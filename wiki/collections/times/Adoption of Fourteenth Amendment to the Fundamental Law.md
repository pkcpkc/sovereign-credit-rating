# Adoption of Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law

## Event Details

The adoption of the Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law of Hungary, occurring in December 2024, introduced significant structural changes to the nation's judicial framework. According to the [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Rule of Law Report, this amendment raised the minimum age for judges to 35 and extended the mandatory retirement age to 70. These changes were implemented under the extended "state of danger" legal regime, which allows the government to override parliamentary acts and has been criticized for undermining legal certainty.

Judicial associations argue that these amendments compromise judicial independence by allowing political influence over court composition and tenure. The report notes that the National Judicial Council continues to exercise powers acquired through prior reforms, but consultation processes are deemed ineffective. Furthermore, the amendment occurred amidst ongoing concerns regarding undue pressure on judges, including public criticism from the [[Kúria]] President and smear campaigns against service courts. The adoption of this amendment is cited as a key factor in the systemic deterioration of the rule of law framework, contributing to legal uncertainty for both domestic and foreign investors.
