# Proposal of Draft Law on Judges Appointed by Post-2017 National Council for the Judiciary

## Event Details

On 24 April 2025, the Polish government proposed a draft law aimed at addressing the status of judges appointed by the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) constituted after 2017. This legislative initiative is a central component of the government's Action Plan on Rule of Law implementation, designed to resolve long-standing tensions between Polish judicial institutions and the European Union.

The proposal seeks to rectify the legitimacy crisis surrounding the post-2017 NCJ, whose composition has been deemed non-compliant with EU standards by the Court of Justice of the European Union. The draft law aims to provide a legal pathway for reviewing the appointments of judges selected by this controversial body, thereby addressing concerns regarding judicial independence and the rule of law.

This event follows key developments in Poland's judicial reform agenda, including the adoption on 6 December 2024 of a draft law separating the functions of the Minister of Justice and the Prosecutor General. It also coincides with other judicial restructuring efforts, such as the proposal on 21 April 2025 to dismantle the Supreme Court Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs. These measures are part of broader efforts to restore institutional checks and balances, improve anti-corruption frameworks through the European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO), and comply with EU rule of law mechanisms. Despite these progress, perceived judicial independence remains very low, with only 26% of the general public and 24% of companies expressing trust in the judiciary.
