# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Austria

## Event Details

The publication of the 2025 [[Rule of Law]] Report Country Chapter on [[Austria]] by the European Commission highlights a jurisdiction with strong overall institutional foundations but identifies critical areas requiring legislative and structural reform. Austria maintains very high public perception of [[Judicial Independence]] and efficiency, alongside low perceived corruption levels. However, systemic vulnerabilities persist in appointment mechanisms for administrative court presidents and the structure of the prosecution service.

Key developments surrounding this report include:

- **Judicial Reform & Appointments**: While perceived judicial independence remains very high (86% of the general public), no progress was made on reforming the appointment of administrative court (vice-)presidents, which lacks consistent judicial involvement. Draft legislation for an independent Federal Prosecution Office is still pending, despite commitments in the New Government Programme.
- **Access to Justice**: Significant challenges remain regarding high court fees, with a 23% average increase effective April 1, 2025. The [[Constitutional Court]] ruled in October 2024 that restrictions on legal aid in administrative proceedings were unconstitutional, with suspensions taking effect by March 2026.
- **Digitalization**: The "Justice 3.0" initiative continues to advance, aiming for 90% digital case management by year-end. Compulsory online publication of judgments was extended to Higher Regional Courts on January 1, 2025.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework**: Austria ranks 10th in the EU on corruption perception. Limited progress was made on introducing effective asset and interest declaration rules for Members of Parliament, and the lobbying framework remains limited in scope. The Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption received 147 reports by end-2024.
- **Media Freedom & Pluralism**: Reforms to [[ORF]] management bodies were implemented in March 2025 to increase independence following a Constitutional Court ruling. The economic situation for private media providers is deteriorating due to advertising revenue shifts to online platforms.
- **State Advertising**: Limited progress was made on ensuring fair distribution of state advertising; while transparency rules have improved, stakeholders criticize the lack of effective sanction mechanisms for violations.

The report recommends targeted actions to strengthen judicial appointments, prosecute corruption independently, and ensure fair distribution of state advertising.
