# Signing of four-party agreement on judicial reforms and salary increases

## Event Details

On November 22, 2024, a four-party agreement regarding judicial reforms and salary increases was signed in Hungary. This agreement was a central component of the government's efforts to address long-standing concerns within the justice system, specifically aiming to increase judicial salaries while implementing structural reforms.

The agreement involved key stakeholders, including the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] and the [[Kúria (Hungary)]], alongside government representatives. While the agreement initially represented a consensus on financial improvements for judges, it became a focal point of tension regarding the broader judicial reform package. The reforms included controversial measures such as raising the minimum age for judges to 35 and extending the mandatory retirement age to 70.

The collaborative framework proved fragile. Following significant protests within the judiciary and the subsequent resignation of the President of the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] in December 2024, the National Judicial Council withdrew from the four-party agreement on January 15, 2025. Despite the withdrawal, the government proceeded with legislative changes, culminating in the adoption of Act XLIX of 2025 in June 2025, which detailed the judicial reforms. The European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report noted that while progress was made on salary increases, the consultation process with the Judicial Council was deemed ineffective, and concerns regarding judicial independence persisted.
