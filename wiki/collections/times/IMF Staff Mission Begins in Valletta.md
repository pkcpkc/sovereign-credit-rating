# IMF Staff Mission Begins in Valletta

## Event Details

On November 4, 2024, an [[International Monetary Fund]] staff mission commenced in Valletta, Malta, to conduct the 2024 [[Article IV Consultation]]. This mission aimed to assess Malta’s macroeconomic stability, fiscal performance, and structural reforms, engaging with key government officials including Minister for Finance and Employment [[Clyde Caruana]] and acting Central Bank Governor [[Alexander Demarco]].

The discussions focused on several critical areas:
*   **Fiscal Policy**: The mission reviewed Malta’s commitment to fiscal consolidation, targeting a reduction of the fiscal deficit to approximately 2¼% of GDP by 2029. A key recommendation involved the gradual exit from broad energy subsidies to create fiscal space for investment in green infrastructure and innovation.
*   **Financial Sector Health**: Discussions highlighted the soundness of Malta’s financial system, characterized by strong capital buffers, but noted significant risks arising from substantial bank exposure to real estate sectors (residential, commercial, and construction). The IMF advised tightening macroprudential policies to mitigate housing market shocks.
*   **Structural Reforms**: The staff emphasized the need for continued productivity growth through digitalization, STEM education, and support for start-ups via the Venture Capital Fund, aligned with the long-term strategic direction of "Malta Vision 2050".
*   **External Position**: Malta’s external position was assessed as substantially stronger than fundamentals imply, driven by robust service exports in tourism and online gaming, despite downside risks from geopolitical conflicts such as the wars in Ukraine and Israel-Gaza.

The policy discussions concluded on November 15, 2024. Following the mission, an [[IMF]] staff report was completed on December 17, 2024, and the [[IMF]] Executive Board officially concluded the Article IV Consultation on January 15, 2025. The mission endorsed staff appraisals that highlighted Malta’s economic strengths while urging vigilance against emerging risks such as trade-based money laundering and infrastructure strains from population density.
