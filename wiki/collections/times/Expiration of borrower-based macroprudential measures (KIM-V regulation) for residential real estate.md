# Expiration of borrower-based macroprudential measures (KIM-V regulation) for residential real estate

## Event Details

On **2025-07**, Austria implemented the expiration of borrower-based macroprudential measures, specifically the KIM-V regulation, which had previously constrained lending for residential real estate. This policy shift occurred concurrently with the introduction of a 1% Sectoral Systemic Risk Buffer (SSyRB) targeting Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposures, reflecting a strategic rebalancing of financial stability tools by the Austrian authorities and the Oesterreichische Nationalbank.

The expiration of the KIM-V regulation took place during a period of significant economic recalibration, including the formation of a new coalition government (ÖVP, SPÖ, NEOS) in March 2025 and the conclusion of the IMF Article IV Consultation on July 3, 2025. While the removal of borrower-based constraints for residential housing aimed to support market normalization following successive years of recession, it was counterbalanced by enhanced scrutiny on the commercial property sector. The banking sector, maintaining a high CET1 capital ratio of 17.7%, continued to face risks from CRE exposures, with non-performing loan (NPL) ratios on commercial property reaching approximately 5% by mid-2024. The IMF welcomed the targeted approach, noting that while residential lending constraints were lifted, vigilance remained critical regarding credit risks from the commercial sector and regional spillovers from Central, Eastern, and Southeastern Europe (CESEE).
