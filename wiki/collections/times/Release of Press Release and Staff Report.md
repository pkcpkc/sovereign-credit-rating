# Release of Press Release and Staff Report

## Event Details

On March 20, 2025, the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) released the Press Release and Staff Report for Belgium's 2025 Article IV Consultation. The report followed the conclusion of the Executive Board's discussions on March 18, 2025, and the completion of the staff report on March 3, 2025. The consultations were led by [[Jean-François Dauphin]], Mission Chief, with oversight from [[Mark Horton]], Director of the IMF European Department, and input from [[Jan Jambon]], Deputy Prime Minister of Belgium.

The released documents assess Belgium's economic resilience amidst significant structural challenges, particularly concerning fiscal sustainability and labor market efficiency. Key findings and recommendations from the report include:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: With public debt at 104.1% of GDP in 2024 and projected to rise to 123% by 2030 under unchanged policies, the IMF supports the Belgian government's seven-year fiscal adjustment plan under the new [[European Commission]] Economic Governance Framework (EGF). The staff recommends an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of approximately 0.6 percentage points of GDP until 2031 to durably reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP.
*   **Labor Market Reform**: The report highlights the need to reform the wage-setting mechanism, specifically abolishing automatic wage indexation and reforming the 1996 wage law, to improve competitiveness and address sluggish labor productivity. Additional measures include adjusting social benefits to incentivize labor force participation.
*   **Financial Stability**: Systemic risks in the [[National Bank of Belgium]]-supervised financial sector remain moderate. The banking sector is well-capitalized with low non-performing loans (2.1%), though risks are increasing in the commercial real estate sector. Household indebtedness remains elevated at 58% of GDP.
*   **External Position**: Belgium's external position was assessed as weaker than implied by fundamentals in 2024, with a real effective exchange rate overvaluation estimated between 4.9% and 17.4%. However, a strong net international investment position (57.3% of GDP) provides a buffer against external shocks.
*   **Climate and Coordination**: The report calls for a coherent national strategy to meet 2030 climate targets, emphasizing better coordination between federal and regional entities, which has been historically weak. It also advocates for revitalizing the fiscal council to enforce binding burden-sharing rules.

The report notes that while growth slowed to 1.0% in 2024, it is projected to return to potential growth of 1.3% by 2027, contingent on the successful implementation of structural reforms in labor and product markets.
