# Staff report completed

## Event Details

### Italy: 2025 Article IV Consultation

On **July 1, 2025**, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) completed the Staff Report for the **2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy**. This report concluded the mission that began in Rome on May 14, 2025, with discussions ending on May 28, 2025. The report was finalized ahead of the IMF Executive Board's conclusion of the consultation on July 18, 2025, with the Press Release and Staff Report published on July 22, 2025.

The completed Staff Report highlights an economy demonstrating notable resilience, with GDP growing **0.7% in 2024** and reaching record-high employment levels. The near-term outlook remains moderately positive but is clouded by significant global uncertainty, including trade tensions and geopolitical risks. Structural challenges, particularly weak productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-average female labor force participation, continue to weigh on Italy's medium-term growth prospects.

Key findings from the report include:

*   **Economic Performance**: Real GDP grew 0.7% in 2024, supported by National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) investments and net exports. Growth is projected to moderate to 0.5% in 2025 before picking up to 0.8% in 2026. Headline inflation is expected to average 1.7% in 2025, converging to the ECB’s 2% target in 2026.
*   **Fiscal Situation**: The fiscal deficit shrank to 3.4% of GDP in 2024, enabling a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP. However, public debt remains high at **135.3% of GDP**. The IMF staff recommends a more front-loaded fiscal adjustment to reach a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027 to firmly place debt on a downward trajectory. The authorities committed to a gradual seven-year fiscal adjustment under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), aiming to bring the deficit below 3% by 2026, and dispute the need for additional immediate measures.
*   **Structural Reforms**: The NRRP implementation has accelerated, with 54% of milestones achieved, though spending execution remains sluggish. The IMF emphasizes the need for continued structural reforms to boost productivity, enhance labor supply (particularly among women), and transition to renewable energy to offset demographic headwinds. Italy’s R&D investment remains low at 1.3% of GDP, well below the EU average.
*   **Financial Sector**: The banking sector’s soundness has improved, with high profitability, sound asset quality (NPLs at 2.7%), and adequate capital buffers. The Systemic Risk Buffer (SyRB) was increased to 1%. However, vulnerabilities persist among some Less Significant Institutions (LSIs), and the sovereign-bank nexus remains a concern. The IMF calls for continued vigilance on loan quality, especially regarding firms exposed to trade shocks.
*   **External Sector**: Italy posted a current account surplus of 1.1% of GDP in 2024, driven by lower energy imports and strong exports. The Net International Investment Position (NIIP) improved to 15.3% of GDP. The IMF assesses the external position as weaker than warranted by fundamentals, recommending further fiscal consolidation and productivity-boosting reforms.

The report was prepared by an IMF mission led by **Ms. Lone Christiansen** (Staff Team Head), including staff members **Mr. Thomas Elkjaer**, **Mr. Gee Hee Hong**, **Ms. Yueling Huang**, **Ms. Sylwia Nowak**, **Mr. Alain Kabundi**, **Ms. Carolina Claver**, **Ms. Yao Deng**, **Ms. Emily Fisher**, and **Ms. Jenny Lee**, under the oversight of **Mr. Helge Berger** (Department Head) and **Mr. Rishi Goyal** (Department Head). Italian officials engaged included **Mr. Giancarlo Giorgetti** (Minister of Economy and Finance) and **Mr. Fabio Signorini** (Senior Deputy Governor, Bank of Italy). The Executive Director for Italy was **Mr. Riccardo Ercoli**, supported by Senior Advisor **Ms. Annalisa Korinthios**.

### Austria: 2025 Article IV Consultation

On **June 9, 2025**, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) completed the Staff Report for the **2025 Article IV Consultation with Austria**. This report concluded the mission that began in Vienna on March 26, 2025, and discussions ending on April 8, 2025. The report was finalized ahead of the IMF Executive Board's conclusion of the consultation on July 3, 2025.

The completed Staff Report highlights a challenging economic environment for Austria, characterized by two successive years of recession (2023 and 2024) driven by energy price shocks and Euro Area monetary tightening. Key findings from the report include:

*   **Economic Performance**: GDP contracted by 0.9% in 2023 and 1.3% in 2024. Growth is projected to remain weak at -0.1% in 2025, with a modest recovery expected in 2026.
*   **Fiscal Situation**: The fiscal deficit widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, pushing public debt to 81.2%. While the new coalition government (formed in March 2025) has announced near-term consolidation measures, the IMF emphasizes the need for substantial medium-term adjustment to stabilize the debt ratio against aging and defense spending pressures.
*   **Inflation**: Headline inflation averaged 3.2% in 2025, exceeding the Euro Area average, but is projected to converge toward 2% by 2026.
*   **Structural Reforms**: The report urges reforms to boost labor supply (particularly among women and the elderly) and reduce regulatory barriers in services and housing to counteract demographic drag and sluggish productivity.
*   **Financial Sector**: While the banking sector remains well-capitalized (CET1 ratio of 17.7%), risks persist from Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposures. The report recommends making borrower-based mortgage lending standards permanent as they expire in July 2025.

The report was prepared by an IMF mission led by **Mr. Fletcher** (Mission Head), including staff members **Mr. Mohommad**, **Ms. Pinat**, and **Mr. Saiyid**, under the oversight of **Oya Celasun** (Deputy Director, European Department) and **Bergljot Barkbu** (Director, Strategy, Policy, and Review Department). Austrian officials engaged included **Christian Karner Marterbauer** (Minister of Finance) and **Robert Holzmann** (Governor, Oesterreichische Nationalbank).

### Slovak Republic: 2025 Article IV Consultation

On **March 3, 2025**, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) completed the Staff Report for the **2025 Article IV Consultation with the Slovak Republic**. This report concluded the mission that began with virtual discussions on December 4, 2024, with discussions ending on January 28, 2025. The report was finalized ahead of the IMF Executive Board's consideration on March 18, 2025, and conclusion of the consultation on March 24, 2025.

The completed Staff Report highlights an economy recovering from recent headwinds, with growth accelerating to **2.0% in 2024**, outpacing the Euro Area average. However, this recovery has been accompanied by a significant widening of the fiscal deficit, which reached **5.8% of GDP** in 2024, triggering an Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) by the European Commission. The IMF staff and Executive Board endorse the Slovak government’s ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028), which aims to reduce the deficit to approximately **2.2% of GDP by 2028**.

Key findings from the report include:

*   **Economic Performance**: Real GDP growth is projected to moderate to 1.8% in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation and weak external demand, before recovering to 2.1% in 2026. Key risks are tilted to the downside, driven by trade policy uncertainty, potential slowdowns in EU fund disbursements due to governance concerns, and structural challenges including an aging population and dependence on the automotive sector.
*   **Fiscal Situation**: The large fiscal deficit (5.8% of GDP in 2024) and rising public debt (58% of GDP in 2024, projected to rise to 74.3% by 2030) pose significant challenges. Long-term risk of sovereign stress is assessed as high due to aging-related costs, which could increase fiscal burdens by 5.6% of GDP by 2050 without further reforms. The IMF notes that additional measures beyond the 2025 budget, prioritizing expenditure-based savings (e.g., reducing subsidies) over revenue hikes, are required to meet medium-term targets.
*   **Inflation**: Headline inflation is expected to rise temporarily to 4.1% in 2025 driven by indirect tax increases, then decline to the 2% target by early 2027.
*   **Financial Sector**: The banking sector is deemed resilient, with capital adequacy ratios well above regulatory minima and ample liquidity. Stress tests confirm resilience to severe shocks. Primary vulnerabilities lie in the commercial real estate (CRE) market, particularly the office segment, and the refixation of residential mortgages in the coming years. The IMF recommends strengthening the macroprudential framework.
*   **Structural Reforms and Governance**: Slovakia faces structural headwinds from geoeconomic fragmentation and demographics. The report calls for reforms to diversify the economy away from automotive dependence, boost innovation, and increase labor force participation. Crucially, strengthening governance, reducing corruption vulnerabilities, and enhancing judicial independence are highlighted as essential to maintaining investor confidence and ensuring the effective absorption of EU funds.
*   **External Sector**: The current account deficit widened to 2.7% of GDP in 2024, assessed as moderately weaker than fundamentals. The Net International Investment Position (NIIP) remains negative (-53.5% of GDP) but is improving, with high FDI inflows mitigating risks of sudden capital outflows.

The report was prepared by an IMF mission led by **Mr. Saxegaard** (Mission Head), under the oversight of **Oya Celasun** (Department Head, IMF EUR) and **Stefania Fabrizio** (Department Head, IMF SPR). Slovak officials engaged included **Mr. Kamenický** (Minister of Finance), **Mr. Taraba** (Minister of the Environment), and **Mr. Kažimír** (Governor, National Bank of Slovakia).
