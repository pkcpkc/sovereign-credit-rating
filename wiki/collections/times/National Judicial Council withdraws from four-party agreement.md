# National Judicial Council withdraws from four-party agreement

## Event Details

On **2025-01-15**, the [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] officially withdrew from a four-party agreement signed on **2024-11-22**. This agreement had originally aimed to address judicial reforms and implement salary increases for judges. The withdrawal occurred amidst a period of significant institutional tension and deterioration in judicial independence, as highlighted in the [[European Commission]]'s [[2025 Rule of Law Report: Hungary]].

The decision followed a series of escalating conflicts between the judiciary and the government. Key preceding events included:

*   **Resignation of Leadership:** On **2024-12-03**, the President of the National Judicial Council resigned following intense judicial protests against proposed reforms.
*   **Legislative Changes:** In **December 2024**, the government adopted the Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law, which raised judicial age limits (minimum age to 35, service allowed until 70). The European Commission deemed the consultation process with the Judicial Council regarding these changes ineffective.
*   **Breakdown of Consultation:** The consultation process surrounding these reforms was described as lacking adequate engagement, leading to the Council's decision to exit the agreement.

The withdrawal signaled a failure in the collaborative framework intended to manage judicial reforms and compensation. It underscored the broader concerns raised by the [[European Commission]] and the [[Venice Commission]] regarding the lack of genuine consultation, the imposition of legislative changes without procedural safeguards, and the erosion of the National Judicial Council's role as a self-governing body. The event is cited as a key indicator of the strained relationship between the executive branch and the judiciary in Hungary during this period.
