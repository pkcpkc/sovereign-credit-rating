# High Council for the Judiciary approves Code of Judicial Management

## Event Details

On 3 December 2024, the [[High Council for the Judiciary]] of Italy approved the [[Code of Judicial Management]]. This approval is a key component of the comprehensive justice system reform adopted by the Italian Government in March 2024, aiming to enhance transparency and efficiency within the judicial administration.

The approval of the Code follows other significant directives issued by the High Council for the Judiciary in 2024, including rules on out-of-tenure appointments for magistrates (approved 25 September 2024) and directives regarding the appraisal of magistrates’ performance (approved 13 November 2024). These measures are part of ongoing efforts to implement structural reforms aimed at reducing case backlogs and improving judicial independence.

While the reform process has seen progress, it faces challenges including persistent staff shortages across court levels and concerns from judicial bodies regarding subsequent constitutional reforms. For instance, in January 2025, the High Council for the Judiciary issued a critical opinion on a draft constitutional reform separating the careers of judges and prosecutors, which was approved by the Chamber of Deputies on 16 January 2025. The High Council argued that such separation could undermine judicial autonomy, a stance supported by the National Association of Magistrates who presented eight proposals on justice administration in March 2025.

The implementation of these judicial reforms is monitored under the European Union’s Rule of Law framework, with the 2025 Rule of Law Report highlighting Italy's progress in justice system reform as a strength, despite remaining threats such as the lack of comprehensive rules on conflicts of interest for political office holders and slow implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments.
