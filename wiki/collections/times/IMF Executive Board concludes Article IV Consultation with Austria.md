# IMF Executive Board concludes Article IV Consultation with Austria

## Event Details

On 3 July 2025, the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) Executive Board concluded its Article IV Consultation with Austria, marking the final stage of a mission that ran from late March to early April 2025. The consultation focused on addressing Austria’s challenging economic environment, characterized by two successive years of recession in 2023 and 2024, driven by energy price shocks, monetary tightening, and global trade uncertainty.

Key outcomes and discussions included:

*   **Economic Assessment**: The Board acknowledged a projected near-zero growth for 2025, with a recovery expected from 2026 onwards, supported by unwinding household savings and monetary easing. However, medium-term prospects remain constrained by demographic aging, sluggish productivity growth, and rising spending pressures from defense commitments, the green transition, and an aging population.
*   **Fiscal Policy Recommendations**: With the fiscal deficit widening to 4.7% of GDP and public debt rising to 81% of GDP in 2024, the IMF recommended a bold fiscal consolidation package. This includes pension reforms, limits on public-sector wage increases, and increased reliance on property, inheritance, and excise taxes to put the debt ratio on a declining path.
*   **Financial Sector Stability**: The banking sector was assessed as healthy, with a high CET1 capital ratio of 17.7%. However, the Board noted risks related to Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposures and regional spillovers from Central, Eastern, and Southeastern Europe (CESEE). The introduction of a Sectoral Systemic Risk Buffer (SSyRB) for CRE assets, effective July 2025, was welcomed.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Critical recommendations included boosting labor supply by narrowing gaps in female and elderly labor force participation, expanding childcare and eldercare facilities, reforming pension systems, and easing regulatory barriers to deepen the EU Single Market.

The consultation involved high-level engagements with Austrian officials, including [[Marterbauer]], Minister of Finance, and [[Holzmann]], Central Bank Governor, alongside IMF staff members [[Oya Celasun]], [[Bergljot Barkbu]], and [[Fletcher]], who served as the Mission Head. The report highlighted the importance of maintaining fiscal sustainability amidst low sovereign stress risks but rising interest payment pressures due to a debt-to-GDP ratio of 81%.
