# Publication of Criteria for Assigning 'CCC+', 'CCC', 'CCC-', And 'CC' Ratings

## Event Details

On February 16, 2011, **S&P Global Ratings** published the criteria for assigning default and near-default credit ratings, specifically 'CCC+', 'CCC', 'CCC-', and 'CC'. This publication established the specific thresholds and qualitative assessments used to distinguish between issuers with a very high risk of default and those in actual default or imminent default.

This methodological update was part of a broader series of revisions to S&P's sovereign and credit rating frameworks. Notable related publications in S&P's methodological history include:
- **2012-10-01**: Publication of the specific criteria for these low-rung ratings, refining the initial 2011 principles.
- **2013-09-11**: Publication of the *Monetary Authorities Rating Methodology*.
- **2014-12-23**: Publication of the previous *Sovereign Rating Methodology*.
- **2017-12-18**: Publication of the updated *Sovereign Rating Methodology*, which integrated monetary authority assessments and refined fiscal/external metrics.

The 'CCC' range ratings indicate that default is currently expected, while 'CC' indicates imminent default. The publication of these criteria provided transparency regarding how S&P assesses the willingness and ability of sovereigns and other issuers to meet their financial obligations when under severe financial distress. Key factors in these assessments include institutional stability, fiscal flexibility, external liquidity, and the culture of debt payment.
