# Publication of 2025 Country Report - Austria

## Event Details

On June 4, 2025, the [[European Commission]] published the [[2025 Country Report - Austria]] as part of the European Semester framework. The report, titled "Commission Staff Working Document SWD(2025) 220 final," outlines Austria's economic, social, and fiscal landscape for 2025, highlighting a continued economic downturn and significant structural challenges.

**Economic Performance**
Austria entered its third consecutive year of recession in 2025, with GDP projected to contract by 0.3%. This follows declines of 1% in 2023 and 1.2% in 2024. Key drivers of this contraction include weak industrial production (down 5% in 2024), declining exports (down 5.9%), and broad-based cost pressures. Despite the recession, the labor market remains relatively resilient, with an unemployment rate of 5.3% and an employment rate of 77.2%. However, structural issues persist, including a shrinking working-age population, declining labor productivity growth, and significant skills shortages, particularly in the ICT and care sectors.

**Fiscal Outlook and Consolidation**
The report highlights substantial fiscal pressure, with the government deficit projected to reach 4.4% of GDP in 2025, well above the EU's 3% treaty threshold. Gross government debt is expected to rise to 84.0% of GDP. In response, the new government has committed to fiscal consolidation measures aiming for €6.4 billion in savings in 2025. A Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan for 2025-2029 was submitted to the Commission in May 2025. Long-term fiscal sustainability is threatened by aging-related spending, which is projected to rise from 28% of GDP in 2024 to 30% by 2070, driven by healthcare and pension costs.

**Structural Reforms and Challenges**
The Commission emphasizes the need for structural reforms to boost competitiveness and sustainability. Key areas include:
*   **Regulatory Environment:** Simplifying regulations and reducing administrative burdens, which currently hinder investment and business dynamism.
*   **Energy Transition:** While 87% of electricity was generated from renewables in 2024, Austria faces challenges with high energy costs, grid expansion bottlenecks, and the cessation of Russian gas imports in early 2025.
*   **Skills and Education:** Addressing worsening basic skills among students (as indicated by PISA assessments) and stagnant adult training participation. There is a critical shortage of teachers and a gap in digital and technical skills.
*   **Social Protection:** Mitigating rising poverty risks, particularly among children, single parents, and people with migrant backgrounds, as the at-risk-of-poverty or social exclusion (AROPE) rate increased to 16.9% in 2024.

**Institutional and Political Context**
The report notes Austria's strong institutional trust and stable government, though it flags risks related to corruption in public procurement and high-level political cases. The banking sector remains well-capitalized (CET1 ratio of 17.5%) but faces exposure to Russian banking subsidiaries and rising non-performing loans in corporate real estate. Climate adaptation risks are also highlighted, with economic losses from extreme weather events reaching €14.7 billion between 1980 and 2023, necessitating increased investment in resilient infrastructure.
