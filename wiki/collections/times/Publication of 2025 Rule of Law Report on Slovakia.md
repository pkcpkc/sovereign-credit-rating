# Publication of 2025 Rule of Law Report on Slovakia

## Event Details

On **2025-07-08**, the **European Commission** published the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Slovakia** (SWD(2025) 925 final). The report assesses developments up to early 2025 and highlights that Slovakia has made limited to no progress in addressing key rule of law concerns, despite an intensive dialogue with the Commission.

### Key Findings

**Justice System and Judicial Independence**
*   **Perception:** Judicial independence perception remains low, with only **32%** of the public and **34%** of companies perceiving the judiciary as independent.
*   **Governance:** No progress was made in establishing sufficient safeguards for the dismissal of members of the **Judicial Council** (specifically those not elected by judges). Amendments to judges' laws introduced bonuses and evaluation changes, raising concerns about potential political influence, though some efficiency measures were noted.
*   **Prosecutorial Independence:** The **Prosecutor General** retains broad powers to annul final decisions and interfere in high-profile corruption cases, undermining accountability.
*   **Efficiency:** The justice system faces significant efficiency challenges, particularly in administrative cases, where resolution time rose sharply to **1,040 days** in 2023.

**Anti-Corruption Framework**
*   **Institutional Weakening:** The dissolution of specialized anti-corruption entities, including the **National Crime Agency (NAKA)** and the **Specialized Prosecutor's Office (SPO)**, has led to operational inefficiencies, delays, and a drop in investigated corruption cases.
*   **Investigation Capacity:** There is no progress in ensuring effective, independent investigation of high-level corruption. A lack of specialized investigators persists following the closure of NAKA.
*   **Legislative Alignment:** While criminal codes were amended in **2024** and **2024-12** to align with the EU **Protection of the European Union’s Financial Interests (PIF) Directive**, risks remain regarding the "abuse of law" crime used against judges, lacking sufficient safeguards against misuse.

**Media Freedom and Pluralism**
*   **Public Service Media:** Concerns persist regarding the politicization of **Slovak Television and Radio (STVR)** under its new governance structure (Act entered into force in **2024-07**).
*   **Access to Information:** Amendments to the **Freedom of Information Act** (entered into force in **2025-03**) introduced fees and longer response times, facing constitutional challenges.
*   **Journalist Safety:** The environment for journalists remains hostile, with no legislative progress on defamation reform.

**Checks and Balances and Civil Society**
*   **Legislative Process:** Stakeholder involvement remains limited, with **28%** of laws in 2024 adopted via fast-track procedures bypassing consultation.
*   **Civil Society Pressure:** New reporting obligations for NGOs and reduced funding for human rights advocates increase pressure on civil society. A law amending NGO functioning acts was adopted in **2024-04**.
*   **Constitutional Risks:** Proposed constitutional amendments raising concerns about the primacy of EU law were postponed after their first reading in **2025-04**.

**International Obligations**
*   **ECtHR Compliance:** Slovakia failed to implement leading judgments from the **European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR)**. The number of pending leading judgments rose from **31** (as of **2025-01-01**) to **33** (as of **2025-06-16**).

### Relevant Institutions and Figures

*   **[[Robert Fico]]**: Prime Minister of Slovakia.
*   **European Commission**: Issuer of the report.
*   **Judicial Council (Slovakia)**: Key body for judicial governance, facing criticism over member dismissal safeguards.
*   **Prosecutor General’s Office (Slovakia)**: Criticized for broad interventionist powers.
*   **National Crime Agency (NAKA)**: Dissolved specialized anti-corruption entity.
*   **Office for the Protection of Whistleblowers**: Fined the Ministry of Interior for procedural violations in **2024-10**.
*   **Transparency International**: Monitoring corruption levels.
*   **Venice Commission** & **Council of Europe**: International bodies observing institutional checks.
