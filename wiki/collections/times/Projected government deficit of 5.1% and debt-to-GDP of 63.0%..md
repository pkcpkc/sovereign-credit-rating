# Projected government deficit of 5.1% and debt-to-GDP of 63.0%.

## Event Details

According to the [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Country Report for Slovakia, the general government deficit is projected to rise slightly to 5.1% of GDP in 2026, following a forecasted level of 4.9% in 2025. This deterioration in the fiscal balance occurs despite the implementation of a comprehensive consolidation package approved in October 2024, which included increases in VAT, corporate income tax (CIT), and financial transaction taxes. The deficit trajectory is driven by persistent structural spending pressures, particularly permanent increases in pension, healthcare, and military salary expenditures.

Concurrently, general government debt is projected to continue its upward trend, reaching 63.0% of GDP by 2026, up from 59.3% recorded in 2024. While these levels remain below the EU average, the trajectory indicates a deteriorating debt sustainability position. The rising debt burden is exacerbated by a high interest-to-GDP ratio relative to nominal GDP growth, which slows the stabilization of public debt. Long-term fiscal sustainability remains threatened by demographic aging, with age-related expenditure projected to increase from 20% to 25% of GDP by 2070, challenging medium-term solvency despite recent pension reforms linking the retirement age to life expectancy.
