# Council initiates excessive deficit procedure for Poland

## Event Details

In January 2025, the [[Council of the European Union]] officially initiated an excessive deficit procedure (EDP) against Poland. This action was triggered by the general government deficit reaching 5.3% of GDP in 2023, which significantly exceeded the EU Treaty reference value of 3%. The initiation of the EDP marked a critical moment in Poland's fiscal relationship with the European Union, highlighting concerns over fiscal sustainability despite the country's status as one of the fastest-growing economies in the EU.

As part of the procedure, Poland submitted a medium-term fiscal-structural plan aimed at reducing the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2028. A key component of this plan was a binding expenditure path, which was subsequently endorsed by the Council. This endorsement signified EU acceptance of Poland's corrective measures, aiming to restore fiscal compliance while managing structural challenges such as high public debt and rising defence spending.

The event occurred against a backdrop of robust economic growth, with Poland's GDP growing by 2.9% in 2024 and projected to continue expanding in 2025, driven by strong private consumption and EU-funded investments. However, the fiscal deficit was exacerbated by increased net expenditure, which grew by 12.7% in 2024, largely due to higher public consumption and defence investments. Poland had also requested the activation of a national escape clause under the Stability and Growth Pact to accommodate this higher defence spending within the EU framework.

The excessive deficit procedure highlighted ongoing tensions between Poland's fiscal autonomy, particularly regarding defence and social spending, and its obligations under EU fiscal surveillance rules. The procedure was further contextualized by the enactment of legislation establishing a Fiscal Council in December 2024, designed to monitor fiscal compliance and provide advisory oversight, although it was not fully operational until January 2026.
