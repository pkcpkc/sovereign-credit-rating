# Government tables comprehensive constitutional reform concerning the justice sector

## Event Details

On **May 13, 2025**, the Maltese government tabled a comprehensive constitutional reform package concerning the justice sector. This legislative initiative was highlighted in the **European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report** as a significant step toward enhancing the independence of the judiciary.

### Key Reform Components and Context

*   **Judicial Appointment Independence**: A central pillar of the reform is the involvement of the judiciary in the appointment process of the **Chief Justice**. This measure aims to reduce executive influence over the highest judicial office, aligning with recommendations from the **Venice Commission** and broader EU rule of law standards.
*   **Perceived Judicial Independence**: The reform occurs against a backdrop of relatively high public confidence in the justice system. Surveys cited in the Rule of Law Report indicate that **69% of the general public** and **63% of companies** perceive the judiciary as independent. However, the reforms are intended to solidify these perceptions institutionally.
*   **Integration with Prosecutorial Reform**: The constitutional changes follow the completion of the transfer of prosecutorial powers for non-summary cases to the **Office of the Attorney General**, which was finalized in **September 2024**. This transfer centralized prosecutorial authority for serious offenses, further separating prosecution from executive control.

### Associated Challenges and Timeline

While the constitutional reform addresses structural independence, the **2025 Rule of Law Report** notes that the justice sector continues to face significant operational challenges:

*   **Efficiency Issues**: Despite the reform, the system struggles with excessive length of proceedings. As of 2023, the disposition time for first-instance civil/commercial cases was **454 days**, and criminal cases took **713 days**. Administrative justice saw a deterioration to **1,350 days**.
*   **Resource Constraints**: Shortages of judges and court personnel persist, impacting the ability to clear backlogs.
*   **Related Legislative Controversies**: The constitutional reform stands in contrast to other legislative changes perceived negatively by stakeholders. Notably, in **April 2025**, a reform (Bill 125) increased the threshold for citizens to petition **magisterial inquiries**, raising concerns about reduced accountability for high-level offenses.

This event is part of a broader sequence of justice sector developments in Malta, including the **CJEU ruling on the investor citizenship scheme** in April 2025 and ongoing efforts to align with the **European Media Freedom Act**.
