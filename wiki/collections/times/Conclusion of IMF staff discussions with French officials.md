# Conclusion of IMF staff discussions with French officials

## Event Details

On May 22, 2025, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) concluded its staff-level discussions with French officials as part of the 2025 Article IV Consultation. The mission, led by IMF Head of Mission **Manuela Goretti** and **Charles Cohen** (Head of the Financial Sector Assessment Program), engaged with key French authorities, including **François Villeroy de Galhau** (Governor of Banque de France), **François Lombard** (Minister for the Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty), and **Guillaume de Montchalín** (Minister for Public Accounts).

These discussions formed the basis for the subsequent Staff Report and Executive Board Assessment, which highlighted several critical economic and fiscal dynamics:

*   **Economic Resilience and Outlook**: The IMF noted that the French economy demonstrated resilience in 2024, supported by the Paris Olympics and a robust labor market, despite high domestic and external uncertainty. Real GDP growth slowed to 1.1% in 2024 and is projected to further decelerate to 0.6% in 2025, weighed down by trade tensions, geoeconomic fragmentation, and weak private investment. A recovery to 1.0% growth is expected in 2026, contingent on structural reforms and easing external pressures.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation Imperative**: A central theme of the discussions was the urgent need for credible fiscal consolidation. With general government gross debt reaching 113.1% of GDP in 2024 and persistent fiscal slippages, the IMF emphasized the importance of the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The goal is to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029 and place debt on a sustainable downward trajectory. Staff recommended a frontloaded structural fiscal effort, prioritizing spending rationalization over tax increases given France’s already high tax-to-GDP ratio.
*   **Structural Reforms and Productivity**: To address sluggish productivity growth, which remains significantly below pre-pandemic levels, the IMF stressed the necessity of structural reforms. Key recommendations include leveraging AI and green technologies, enhancing access to finance for high-growth firms, reducing regulatory burdens, and improving education and skills to foster entrepreneurship.
*   **Financial Sector Stability**: The concurrent 2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP) confirmed the resilience of France’s banking sector, citing healthy capital buffers (19.8% regulatory capital ratio) and strong liquidity coverage. However, the discussions also highlighted risks such as weak bank profitability due to net interest margin compression, high corporate indebtedness, and rising bankruptcies among SMEs. The IMF recommended continued monitoring of non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs) and cyber/climate risks.
*   **Pension and Local Government Challenges**: Long-term fiscal sustainability pressures from an aging population were discussed, with the 2023 pension reform (raising the retirement age to 64) noted as a positive step. Additionally, the IMF pointed to local government spending as a contributor to recent fiscal slippages, recommending enhanced fiscal oversight and multi-year budgetary frameworks to improve efficiency at the local level.

Following these discussions, the IMF Staff Report was completed on June 25, 2025, leading to the Executive Board’s conclusion of the Article IV consultation on July 11, 2025, and the subsequent issuance of a press release on July 14, 2025.
