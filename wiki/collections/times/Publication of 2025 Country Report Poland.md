# Publication of 2025 Country Report: Poland

## Event Details

On June 4, 2025, the [[European Commission]] published its 2025 Country Report for [[Poland]], assessing economic developments in 2024 and providing an outlook for 2025–2028. The report highlights Poland as one of the fastest-growing economies in the EU, with real GDP growth of 2.9% in 2024, driven by strong private consumption and significant EU-funded investments.

**Fiscal Situation and Excessive Deficit Procedure**
A central focus of the report is Poland's deteriorating fiscal position, with the general government deficit reaching 6.6% of GDP in 2024 and public debt projected to rise to 58.0% of GDP in 2025. In response to these figures, which exceed the Treaty reference values, the [[Council of the European Union]] initiated the **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)** in January 2025. Simultaneously, the Council endorsed Poland's medium-term fiscal-structural plan, which aims to reduce the deficit below 3% by 2028. This consolidation effort is complicated by high and rising defense spending (expected to reach 2.8% of GDP in 2025) and the need for substantial public investment. Poland is utilizing the "national escape clause" flexibility within the new EU fiscal framework to manage this adjustment.

**Structural Challenges and Competitiveness**
Despite strong growth, Poland faces significant structural hurdles:
*   **Productivity and Competitiveness:** Labor productivity remains low at 67% of the EU average. Competitiveness is further hampered by high unit labor costs, a complex tax framework (ranking last in the EU for complexity), and a volatile regulatory environment characterized by frequent legislative changes with short preparation times.
*   **Labor Market and Demographics:** Unemployment hit a historic low of 2.9% in 2024, yet the labor market suffers from severe skills shortages, particularly in STEM and vocational fields. These shortages are exacerbated by demographic decline, an aging population, and low fertility rates. The disability employment gap widened to 35.6%, one of the largest in the EU.
*   **Education:** Basic skills among 15-year-olds have declined significantly since 2018, and adult learning participation remains low (20.3%), hindering the green and digital transitions.

**Energy Transition and Industrial Policy**
Poland is emerging as a clean tech hub, producing 60% of Europe’s lithium batteries. However, the energy mix remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels, which accounted for 70% of electricity generation in 2024. High electricity prices, driven by taxation and generation costs, pose a threat to industrial competitiveness. The Commission urges accelerated deployment of renewables, grid modernization, and the phase-out of fossil fuel subsidies to support decarbonization.

**Social Sustainability and Regional Disparities**
*   **Pensions and Healthcare:** The pension system faces long-term sustainability risks due to demographic pressures and a low statutory retirement age for women (60 years). The healthcare system remains hospital-centric with insufficient preventive care and high treatable mortality rates.
*   **Regional Inequalities:** Significant disparities persist between the capital region (Warsaw) and western areas, which attract the majority of foreign direct investment and exhibit higher productivity, compared to eastern regions that struggle with infrastructure deficits and demographic decline.

**Institutional Developments**
The report notes the establishment of an independent **Fiscal Council**, with legislation enacted in late 2024 and operations projected to start in June 2025. This institution aims to strengthen policy credibility and institutional strength. Additionally, Poland has made progress in implementing its **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF)** plan, fulfilling 25% of milestones as of mid-2025, though acceleration is needed to fully absorb funds.
