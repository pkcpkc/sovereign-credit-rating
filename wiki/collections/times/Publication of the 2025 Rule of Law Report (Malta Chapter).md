# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report (Malta Chapter)

## Event Details

The timeline of legal and institutional developments in Malta leading up to and including the 2025 Rule of Law Report is characterized by significant structural reforms and ongoing challenges in judicial efficiency.

**2024: Structural Foundations**
*   **September 2024:** A major milestone in prosecutorial reform was achieved with the completion of the transfer of non-summary case prosecutions from the Police to the Office of the Attorney General.
*   **October 22, 2024:** The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) issued its judgment in *J.B. and Others v. Malta*, impacting the country's human rights landscape.

**2025: Legislative Reform and Judicial Rulings**
*   **April 29, 2025:** The European Court of Justice (ECJ) ruled that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme is illegal under EU law, marking a significant regulatory setback.
*   **May 13, 2025:** The Government tabled a comprehensive constitutional reform aimed at the justice sector, specifically seeking to involve the judiciary in the appointment of the Chief Justice and reducing executive involvement in specialized tribunals.
*   **June 5, 2025:** A jury reached a finding in the high-profile Daphne Caruana Galizia murder case.
*   **July 8, 2025:** The European Commission officially published the **2025 Rule of Law Report (Malta Chapter)**. The report highlighted a landscape of mixed progress:
    *   **Judicial Independence:** Public perception remains high, though the report notes significant backlogs and a low judge-to-inhabitant ratio.
    *   **Anti-Corruption:** Malta scored 46/100 on the Corruption Perceptions Index, with the report calling for a more robust track record of final judgments and better resources for the Permanent Commission Against Corruption (PCAC).
    *   **Media and Civil Society:** The report expressed concern over the "very high risk" score for public service media independence and the lack of progress on a National Human Rights Institution.
