# Economic activity resilient in early 2025

## Event Details

In the first quarter of 2025, Italy’s economic activity demonstrated notable resilience, consistent with the broader positive performance observed in 2024, which saw real GDP growth of 0.7%. This resilience was supported by robust net exports and continued investments related to the [[National Recovery and Resilience Plan]] (NRRP).

According to the [[International Monetary Fund]]'s 2025 Article IV Consultation, the economy maintained strength despite significant global headwinds, including elevated trade policy uncertainty, geopolitical fragmentation, and potential supply chain disruptions. Key indicators supporting this resilience included:

*   **Labor Market Strength**: Record-high employment levels persisted, reflecting a robust labor market even as structural challenges like rapid population aging and below-EU average female labor force participation continued to constrain long-term potential growth.
*   **External Position**: Italy posted a current account surplus of 1.1% of GDP in 2024, driven by lower energy import costs and a diversified export basket. The Net International Investment Position (NIIP) improved to 15.3% of GDP, indicating a strengthened external balance.
*   **Fiscal Improvement**: The fiscal stance supported economic stability with the deficit shrinking to 3.4% of GDP in 2024, allowing the country to return to a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP. Public debt stood at 135.3% of GDP at the end of 2024.

While early 2025 activity was resilient, the IMF staff projected that growth would moderate to 0.5% in 2025 before picking up to 0.8% in 2026, contingent on the effective implementation of structural reforms and NRRP milestones. The resilience during this period underscored the importance of Italy’s diversified export destinations and the gradual recovery of domestic demand, although risks remained tilted to the downside due to global trade tensions and delayed NRRP spending execution.
