# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Malta

## Event Details

On **July 8, 2025**, the [[European Commission]] published the Country Chapter on Malta as part of the **2025 Rule of Law Report**. The report, based on the EU Rule of Law Mechanism, assessed Malta's performance across four key pillars: justice, anti-corruption, media pluralism, and checks and balances. The assessment highlighted a mixed landscape, noting high perceived judicial independence alongside significant challenges in system efficiency, corruption enforcement, and institutional transparency.

### Justice Sector
The report confirmed that perceived judicial independence remains high, with 69% of the public and 63% of companies expressing confidence. Key institutional reforms included the completion of the transfer of prosecutorial powers for serious offenses to the [[Office of the Attorney General]] in September 2024. In May 2025, the government tabled comprehensive constitutional reforms to enhance the independence of the [[Chief Justice]] appointment process by involving the judiciary. However, systemic efficiency issues persist; backlogs are increasing, and disposition times remain critical (e.g., 713 days for criminal cases and 1,350 days for administrative justice in 2023). Resource constraints, including shortages of judges and court personnel, continue to hamper legal certainty and business confidence.

### Anti-Corruption Framework
Despite the implementation of the National Anti-Fraud and Corruption Strategy, the perceived level of corruption remains high, reflected in a [[Corruption Perceptions Index]] score of 46/100. The [[Permanent Commission Against Corruption]] (PCAC) was cited as having weak capacity and having never referred a case for prosecution due to resource limitations. While investigative authorities like the Financial Crime Investigation Department and the Attorney General’s office have increased resources, there is a lack of robust track records for final judgments in high-level corruption cases. Integrity measures for high-level politicians remain inadequate, with [[Ministers']] asset declarations filed in March 2025 remaining unpublished. A major legal setback occurred on **April 29, 2025**, when the [[Court of Justice of the European Union]] ruled that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme was contrary to EU Law, characterizing it as the commercialization of Union citizenship.

### Media Pluralism and Freedom
Progress in this sector was limited. While Malta enacted a law on cross-border [[Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation]] (SLAPPs) in July 2024, it faced criticism for excluding domestic lawsuits. No progress was made in safeguarding the independent governance of [[public service media]], which the [[Media Pluralism Monitor]] rated at "very high risk" for political bias. The government is preparing legislation to align with the [[European Media Freedom Act]], but other measures to protect journalists and improve access to official documents remain pending.

### Checks and Balances and Civic Space
The report identified a "narrowed" civil society space. No formal framework for public participation in the legislative process was established, though a new Public Consultation Department is planned. Draft legislation for a [[National Human Rights Institution]] remained unbundled in Parliament. Controversially, legislative reforms approved on **April 1, 2025** (Bill 125) increased the threshold for citizens to petition magisterial inquiries, drawing criticism from stakeholders and the [[Venice Commission]] for potentially reducing accountability in high-level offenses. Additionally, the number of pending leading judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] increased to 16 by June 2025, highlighting ongoing compliance challenges.
