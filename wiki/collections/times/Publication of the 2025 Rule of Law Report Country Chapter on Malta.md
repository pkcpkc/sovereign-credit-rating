# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report Country Chapter on Malta

## Event Details

The publication of the 2025 [[Rule of Law Report]] country chapter on [[Malta]] by the [[European Commission]] in July 2025 highlighted mixed progress in the country's adherence to EU legal standards. While [[Judicial Independence]] is perceived as high by the public and businesses, the report identified critical weaknesses in [[Anti-Corruption Framework]] implementation, specifically regarding the capacity of the Permanent Commission Against Corruption and the lack of final judgments in high-level corruption cases.

Key developments covered by the report include the completion of transferring non-summary prosecution powers to the [[Attorney General]] in September 2024, which has increased prosecutorial authority. However, concerns persist regarding the independence of specialised tribunals, such as the [[Immigration Appeals Board]], following a 2024 [[European Court of Human Rights]] judgment. The justice system continues to face significant delays, adversely business confidence and [[Economic Strength]].

Significant geopolitical and legal events noted in the report include the [[European Court of Justice]] ruling on 29 April 2025 that [[Malta]]'s [[Investor Citizenship Scheme]] is contrary to EU law, citing the commercialisation of Union citizenship. Additionally, parliamentary approval in April 2025 of Bill No. 125 reforming [[Magisterial Inquiry]] procedures raised concerns about limiting accountability for high-level offences. The report also flagged high risks to [[Media Freedom]] and pluralism, particularly concerning the independence of media regulators and the protection of journalists, including those in the wake of the Daphne Caruana Galizia case. Recommendations focus on strengthening judicial independence, improving corruption investigation outcomes, and establishing a [[National Human Rights Institution]].
