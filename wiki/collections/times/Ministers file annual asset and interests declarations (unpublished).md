# Ministers file annual asset and interests declarations (unpublished)

## Event Details

On **March 1, 2025**, Maltese Ministers filed their annual asset and interests declarations. However, these declarations remain unpublished, representing a significant transparency gap identified in the European Commission’s **[[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta]]**.

The failure to publish these declarations is cited as a key weakness in the country's **accountability** framework. Specifically:

*   **Inadequate Integrity Measures:** The report highlights that integrity measures for high-level politicians and officials remain inadequate. The unpublished status of ministerial asset declarations undermines public trust and oversight mechanisms.
*   **Regulatory Gaps:** Members of Parliament are not currently covered by public administration conflict of interest rules. The **OECD** has recommended reforms to the asset declaration system, which the Maltese government is considering, but implementation has not yet resulted in public disclosure.
*   **Context of Anti-Corruption Challenges:** This lack of transparency exists alongside a high perceived level of corruption in the public sector, reflected in a Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) score of 46/100. While prosecutorial powers were transferred to the [[Office of the Attorney General]] in 2024, and the [[Permanent Commission Against Corruption]] exists, the lack of visible integrity reporting from the executive branch remains a critical concern for institutional accountability.

This event underscores the ongoing tension between formal procedural compliance (filing declarations) and substantive transparency (public access), a theme recurrent in the EU's assessment of Malta's rule of law adherence.
