# Submission of the 2026 budget law to Parliament

## Event Details

On **May 6, 2025**, the Hungarian government submitted the 2026 budget law to the Parliament. This submission occurs within a challenging macroeconomic context characterized by subdued growth, persistent inflationary pressures, and significant fiscal vulnerabilities, as highlighted in the [[EU Commission 2025 Country Report: Hungary]] published on June 4, 2025.

### Fiscal Context and Projections
The 2026 budget was prepared against a backdrop of deteriorating fiscal sustainability metrics:
*   **Deficit Projections**: While the government deficit decreased to **4.9% of GDP** in 2024, it is projected to remain elevated at **4.7% of GDP** in 2026, complicating compliance with EU fiscal rules.
*   **Debt Burden**: Public debt rose to **73.5% of GDP** in 2024. The Commission notes high medium-term sustainability risks, exacerbated by aging demographics and rigid spending commitments.
*   **Net Expenditure**: Net expenditure growth in 2025 is projected at **6.1%**, exceeding EU recommendations, though partially justified by defense spending under the national escape clause.

### Strategic and Structural Considerations
The budget framework faces criticism regarding policy credibility and predictability:
*   **Growth Drivers**: The budget anticipates a GDP growth rebound to **2.5% in 2026**, primarily driven by new manufacturing capacities in the electric vehicle (EV) battery sector.
*   **Policy Distortions**: The report identifies widespread state interventions distorting markets, including subsidized lending schemes, administratively controlled mortgage rates, and sector-specific taxes (banking, retail, energy).
*   **Regulatory Volatility**: Frequent use of emergency decrees and regulatory changes have historically hindered long-term business planning and reduced policy reliability.
