# Introduction of lenient sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption

## Event Details

In 2024, the Slovak government, led by Prime Minister [[Robert Fico]], enacted significant amendments to the Criminal Code that introduced lenient sanctions and shortened statutes of limitations for corruption offenses. These reforms were part of a broader package of judicial changes and had immediate, tangible impacts on the country's anti-corruption framework.

The implementation of these measures led to the closure or reclassification of several high-level corruption cases, resulting in a measurable drop in prosecution rates. The European Commission noted that while subsequent amendments adopted in late 2024 (specifically in December 2024) were designed to align Slovak law with the EU's PIF Directive, the initial impact of the 2024 reforms had already materialized in reduced judicial effectiveness.

This legislative shift occurred within a context of weakened institutional checks, including the dissolution of specialized anti-corruption bodies such as the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Special Prosecutor's Office (SPO). The combination of shorter statutes of limitations, reduced sanctions, and fragmented investigative competence contributed to a stagnation or deterioration in the rule of law indicators, particularly regarding the perceived independence of justice and the effectiveness of anti-corruption efforts.

Key figures involved in this policy direction included Prime Minister [[Robert Fico]], Minister of Interior [[Robert Kaliňák]], and Prosecutor General [[Marian Jančík]], whose office holds significant power to annul decisions and redirect cases, further complicating the investigative landscape.
