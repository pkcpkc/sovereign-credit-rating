# Poland enters EU Excessive Deficit Procedure

## Event Details

In 2024, Poland entered the European Union's Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP), a formal corrective mechanism triggered by the country's deteriorating fiscal position. According to the IMF's 2024 Article IV Consultation Staff Report (published in January 2025), Poland's general government deficit widened significantly to 5.9% of GDP in 2024. This deterioration was driven by permanent increases in public sector wages, expanded social benefits, and high defense spending, which outweighed savings from reduced energy support measures.

The entry into the EDP coincided with a rise in public debt, which was projected to increase from approximately 50% of GDP in 2023 to around 65% of GDP by 2029 under baseline scenarios. In response to these fiscal pressures and the EDP designation, Polish authorities adopted a Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) with the objective of reducing the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028. The IMF recommended frontloading some consolidation measures in 2025 to help rebuild fiscal buffers, support disinflation, and create space for monetary easing, while emphasizing the need for long-term structural reforms to address demographic aging and climate transition costs.
