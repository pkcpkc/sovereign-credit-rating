# Expected third consecutive year of recession

## Event Details

Austria is experiencing its third consecutive year of economic recession, a trend highlighted in the 2025 Country Report published by the European Commission on June 4, 2025. After declines of 1% in 2023 and 1.2% in 2024, GDP is projected to contract further by 0.3% in 2025. This downturn is driven by weak industrial production, which dropped by 5% in 2024, and declining goods exports, which fell by 5.9%, exacerbated by lost cost competitiveness and potential trade tariffs.

Despite the broader economic contraction, the labor market has remained resilient. The unemployment rate is contained at 5.3% in 2025, and the employment rate remains stable at 77.2%. However, the recession occurs against a backdrop of structural challenges, including declining labor productivity growth since 2008, significant skills shortages (particularly in ICT and care sectors), and a shrinking working-age population that began declining in 2024.

The economic weakness is accompanied by substantial fiscal pressure. The government deficit is projected to exceed the EU treaty threshold, reaching 4.4% of GDP in 2025, with the gross debt ratio rising to 84.0% of GDP. In response to these economic and fiscal headwinds, the new government has committed to EUR 6.4 billion in savings for 2025 and submitted a Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan to address sustainability concerns amidst high public spending driven by inflation and aging costs.
