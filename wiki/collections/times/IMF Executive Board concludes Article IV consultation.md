# IMF Executive Board Concludes Article IV Consultation

## Event Details

The [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) Executive Board has concluded Article IV Consultations for multiple member states, including [[Malta]], [[Poland]], and [[Romania]]. These consultations involve comprehensive assessments of each country's economic performance, structural challenges, and policy frameworks, resulting in specific staff reports and executive board conclusions.

### Malta: 2024 Article IV Consultation

The IMF Executive Board concluded the 2024 Article IV Consultation with [[Malta]] on January 15, 2025. The consultation process began with a staff mission in Valletta on November 4, 2024, followed by policy discussions that concluded on November 15, 2024. The staff report was completed on December 17, 2024.

The Board endorsed staff appraisals highlighting a substantial improvement in [[Malta]]'s external position and a sound financial system. Key findings included:
- **Economic Growth:** [[Malta]] experienced remarkable economic growth driven by export-oriented service industries, particularly tourism and online gaming. Growth is projected to remain among the highest in Europe, supported by tight labor markets and foreign worker inflows.
- **Fiscal Consolidation:** The authorities are committed to reducing the fiscal deficit to around 2¼ percent of GDP by 2029, with public debt remaining around 50 percent of GDP. The IMF recommended a gradual but decisive exit from fixed energy price policies, shifting resources toward green infrastructure and innovation.
- **Financial Sector Risks:** While the financial system is sound with strong capital buffers, significant risks stem from banks' substantial exposure to real estate (residential, commercial, and construction). The IMF advised raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer rate.
- **Geopolitical Risks:** Downside risks include spillovers from regional conflicts (Ukraine, Israel-Gaza) and geoeconomic fragmentation.

Key figures involved in the consultation included [[Clyde Caruana]] (Minister for Finance and Employment), [[Riccardo Ercoli]] (Executive Director, IMF), and the staff team led by [[Kotaro Ishi]].

### Poland: 2024 Article IV Consultation

The IMF Executive Board concluded the 2024 Article IV Consultation with [[Poland]] on January 21, 2025. The process included staff mission discussions in Warsaw concluding on October 17, 2024, a staff report completion date of December 16, 2024, and an Executive Board discussion on January 10, 2025.

The assessment highlighted a robust economic recovery driven by domestic demand and the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds. Key findings included:
- **Economic Recovery:** Poland's economy accelerated to 2.8% growth in 2024, with projections of 3.5% for 2025-2026, supported by private consumption and public investment.
- **Fiscal Challenges:** The fiscal deficit widened to an estimated 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and high defense spending. The IMF recommended a medium-term plan to reduce the deficit to 2.9% of GDP by 2028.
- **Monetary Policy:** The National Bank of Poland (NBP) maintained a tight monetary stance with a 5.75% policy rate. The IMF advised that interest rate cuts should commence only when wage growth decelerates and inflation is firmly declining towards the 2.5% target.
- **Structural Reforms:** Critical reforms include boosting productivity, facilitating resource reallocation, and ensuring an adequate labor supply by integrating women, older adults, and refugees into the labor market.

Key figures involved included [[Jan Kees Martijn]] (Head of IMF Mission), [[Patryk Loszewski]] (Executive Director, Poland), and [[Kareem Ismail]] (IMF Staff).

### Romania: 2025 Article IV Consultation

The IMF Executive Board concluded the 2025 Article IV Consultation with [[Romania]] in November 2025. The consultation process included an IMF staff mission discussion held from September 3–12, 2025.

The assessment focused on a critical juncture for Romania's macroeconomic stability, marked by slowed growth and widening twin deficits. Key findings included:
- **Fiscal Consolidation:** The Romanian government adopted a large fiscal reform package in 2025, including VAT rate hikes and other tax measures. The goal is to reduce the fiscal deficit to below 6 percent of GDP in 2026 and below 3 percent by 2031.
- **Inflation Dynamics:** Headline inflation rose to 9.9 percent in September 2025 due to the removal of energy price caps and VAT increases, but is expected to fall within the National Bank of Romania’s (NBR) tolerance band by end-2026.
- **Monetary Policy:** The NBR paused policy rate cuts, maintaining the rate at 6.5 percent, with cuts to resume only after wage growth and inflation moderate sustainably.
- **Sovereign Credit Risk:** A sovereign credit rating downgrade remains a risk due to doubts about the full execution of the consolidation plan and the sustainability of public finances. Romania maintains an investment-grade rating, but concerns persist regarding high current fiscal deficits and rising public debt.
- **Banking Sector:** The banking system remains well-capitalized, but vulnerabilities exist regarding the growing sovereign-bank nexus, buoyant consumer credit growth, and sizable unhedged foreign exchange (FX) loans.

Key figures involved included [[Ilie Bolojan]] (Prime Minister), [[Alexandru Nazare]] (Finance Minister), [[Mugur Isarescu]] (Central Bank Governor), and IMF Executive Directors [[Helge Berger]] and [[Bergljot Barkbu]].
