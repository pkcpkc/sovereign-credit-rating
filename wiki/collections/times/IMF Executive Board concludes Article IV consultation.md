# IMF Executive Board Concludes Article IV Consultation

## Event Details

The [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) Executive Board has concluded recent Article IV consultations with key Eurozone members, highlighting divergent economic challenges and policy priorities between [[France]] and [[Malta]].

### France Consultation (2025)

On July 11, 2025, the IMF Executive Board concluded its 2025 Article IV consultation with [[France]]. The consultation, led by mission head [[Manuela Goretti]], involved detailed discussions with French officials, including [[François Lombard]] (Minister for the Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty) and [[Guillaume de Montchalín]] (Minister for Public Accounts), as well as [[François Villeroy de Galhau]] of the [[Banque de France]]. A parallel [[Financial Sector Assessment Program]] (FSAP) was conducted, with [[Charles Cohen]] serving as the Head of FSAP.

**Key Findings and Assessment:**
The Executive Board and staff assessed the French economy as demonstrating resilience in 2024, supported by the Paris Olympics and a robust labor market, despite significant domestic and external uncertainties. However, the report highlighted urgent challenges regarding high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, and persistent fiscal slippages.

**Economic Outlook:**
*   Growth slowed to 1.1% in 2024 and is projected to slow further to 0.6% in 2025 due to trade tensions, geoeconomic fragmentation, and weak private investment.
*   A recovery to 1.0% growth in 2026 and 1.2% in the medium term is expected, contingent on structural reforms and easing external pressures.
*   Inflation disinfled significantly, reaching 0.6% year-over-year in May 2025, with expectations well-anchored at 2%.

**Fiscal Policy and Debt Sustainability:**
*   The IMF emphasized the need for a credible, frontloaded fiscal consolidation under the [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan]] (MTFSP) to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029.
*   Staff recommended a structural fiscal effort of 1.1% of GDP in 2026, followed by 0.9% annually, prioritizing spending rationalization over tax increases given France's high tax-to-GDP ratio.
*   Without these measures, the deficit is projected to remain around 6% of GDP, keeping debt on an upward trajectory.
*   The [[High Council for Public Finances]] continues to reinforce monitoring, though transparency in local government spending remains an area for improvement.

**Structural Reforms:**
*   Raising productivity is critical to long-term growth. The IMF recommended advancing reforms to foster entrepreneurship, enhance access to finance, and reduce regulatory burdens.
*   Key focus areas include leveraging AI and green technologies, improving education, and promoting less fragmented careers to address demographic aging.
*   The 2023 pension reform, which raised the retirement age to 64, was noted as a positive step toward long-term sustainability.

**Financial Sector:**
*   The 2025 FSAP confirmed the resilience of the banking sector, with healthy capital buffers (19.8% regulatory capital ratio) and strong liquidity coverage.
*   Risks include weak bank profitability due to net interest margin compression, high corporate indebtedness, and rising SME bankruptcies.
*   Monitoring of non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs) and cyber/climate risks remains essential.

### Malta Consultation (2024)

On January 15, 2025, the IMF Executive Board concluded its 2024 Article IV consultation with [[Malta]]. The mission was led by Chief [[Kotaro Ishi]], with input from Directors [[Mark Horton]] and [[Geremia Palomba]], and [[Riccardo Ercoli]] serving as Executive Director. Discussions involved Maltese officials including [[Clyde Caruana]] (Minister for Finance and Employment), [[Miriam Dalli]] (Minister for the Environment, Energy, and Enterprise), and [[Alexander Demarco]] (Acting Governor, [[Central Bank of Malta]]).

**Key Findings and Assessment:**
The consultation highlighted Malta’s remarkable economic performance over the past decade, driven by export-oriented service industries such as tourism and online gaming. While growth is expected to moderate, it remains among the highest in Europe (projected 4% medium-term growth). However, this labor-intensive growth model has led to significant capacity constraints, including strained infrastructure, public services, and tight labor markets.

**Fiscal Policy and Energy Subsidies:**
*   The IMF recommended gradually phasing out the fixed-energy price policy, which accounts for 20% of the fiscal deficit. Resources saved should be reallocated to investment, innovation, and public services.
*   Authorities committed to deficit reduction, targeting 2.6% of GDP by 2027, while developing a long-term fiscal framework to address aging and climate transition pressures.
*   Debt remains manageable at ~50% of GDP, well below the EU 60% ceiling, with low risk of sovereign stress.

**Financial Stability and Real Estate Risk:**
*   The financial system is robust, with well-capitalized banks (Tier 1 ratio ~21%) and strong liquidity buffers (LCR >350%).
*   Risks are concentrated in the real estate sector, which accounts for 70% of bank loans. The IMF advised tightening macroprudential policies by raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB) and expanding its scope to include construction and commercial real estate.
*   Vigilant monitoring of cyber risks and commercial real estate data gaps is warranted.

**Structural Reforms and Taxation:**
*   **Growth Model:** The IMF urged a shift toward productivity-driven growth, emphasizing improved educational outcomes, STEM enrollment, and support for innovation via venture capital.
*   **Corporate Income Tax (CIT):** Malta has deferred implementation of the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive until end-2029. The IMF urged a clear CIT reform roadmap to prevent revenue loss and reduce distortions between domestic and multinational firms.
*   **External Sector:** Malta maintains a large positive net international investment position (~95% of GDP) and a current account surplus, providing a strong buffer against external shocks.

**Governance and Climate:**
*   Progress has been made in strengthening the [[Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism]] (AML/CFT) framework. The IMF recommended continuing to monitor emerging threats like trade-based money laundering and advancing judicial reforms.
*   Current policies are insufficient to meet the 19% GHG emissions reduction target by 2030. The IMF called for robust implementation of the Low Carbon Development Strategy and phasing out energy subsidies to incentivize conservation.
