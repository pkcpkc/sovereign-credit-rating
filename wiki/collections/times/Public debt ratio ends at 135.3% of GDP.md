# Public debt ratio ends at 135.3% of GDP

## Event Details

In 2024, Italy's public debt-to-GDP ratio concluded at **135.3%**, reflecting a high but stable sovereign debt burden amidst a broader economic recovery. This figure was reported during the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF)’s 2025 Article IV Consultation, which assessed the country's fiscal sustainability and macroeconomic stability.

### Fiscal Context and Performance
*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: The high debt stock coexisted with improved fiscal dynamics in 2024. The fiscal deficit shrank to **3.4% of GDP**, better than initially projected, allowing Italy to return to a **primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP**.
*   **Debt Trajectory**: Despite the high stock, the debt trajectory improved relative to previous projections due to better-than-expected fiscal outturns and strong retail appetite for domestic government bonds. However, the IMF highlighted that the interest-growth differential remains positive, posing risks to long-term sustainability without further adjustment.

### Policy Responses and Outlook
*   **IMF Recommendations**: The IMF staff recommended a more front-loaded fiscal adjustment, specifically targeting a **primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027** to firmly place debt on a downward trajectory. They assessed sovereign debt risk as moderate overall but high in the medium and long term, citing adverse demographic trends (aging population) and mechanical signals of fiscal strain.
*   **Government Position**: Italian authorities, led by Minister of Economy and Finance [[Giancarlo Giorgetti]], committed to a gradual seven-year fiscal adjustment under the **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)**. They disputed the need for immediate additional measures, aiming instead for a deficit below 3% by 2026 and emphasizing structural reforms to boost potential growth.
*   **Structural Challenges**: Key factors influencing the debt outlook include weak productivity growth, below-EU average female labor force participation, and significant regional disparities. The implementation of the **National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP)** was noted as accelerating (54% of milestones achieved), though spending execution remains sluggish.

### Financial Stability Implications
*   **Sovereign-Bank Nexus**: The high debt level contributes to the sovereign-bank nexus, which remains sizable despite reduced linkages compared to pre-crisis levels. The banking sector showed resilience with high profitability and low Non-Performing Loans (2.7%), but vulnerabilities persist in some Less Significant Institutions (LSIs).
*   **External Position**: Italy maintained a current account surplus of 1.1% of GDP in 2024, driven by lower energy imports and strong exports, with the Net International Investment Position improving to 15.3% of GDP.
