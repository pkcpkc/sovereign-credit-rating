# French economic resilience and fiscal slippages

## Event Details

The French economy demonstrated notable resilience in 2024, supported by the Paris Olympics and a robust labor market, despite a backdrop of high domestic political uncertainty and external pressures. However, this period was characterized by significant fiscal slippages, with the general government gross debt reaching **113.1% of GDP** in 2024 and the fiscal stance remaining expansionary due to revenue shortfalls and spending overruns.

### Key Developments and IMF Assessment (2025)

The **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** conducted its 2025 Article IV Consultation, culminating in an Executive Board assessment in July 2025. The consultation highlighted urgent challenges regarding public debt sustainability and the need for credible fiscal consolidation.

*   **Economic Outlook:** Real GDP growth slowed to **1.1% in 2024** and is projected to decelerate further to **0.6% in 2025**, weighed down by geoeconomic fragmentation, trade tensions, and weak private investment. A recovery to **1.0% in 2026** is expected, contingent on easing trade tensions and structural reform momentum. Inflation has disinfled significantly, falling to **0.6% YoY** in May 2025, with expectations well-anchored at the ECB's 2% target.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation Imperative:** The IMF emphasized the critical need to implement the **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)** to bring the deficit below **3% of GDP by 2029**. Without a frontloaded structural fiscal effort (recommended at 1.1% of GDP in 2026), the deficit is projected to remain around 6% of GDP, keeping debt on an upward trajectory. The recommendation prioritizes rationalizing current spending over tax increases, given France's already high tax-to-GDP ratio.
*   **Political and Policy Context:** A political compromise on the **2025 budget** reached in February 2025 marked a positive step, demonstrating capacity for fiscal adjustment. An action plan to enhance public finance execution was published in March 2025. The **High Council for Public Finances** continues to reinforce monitoring and forecasting, while the **European Commission** positively assessed French fiscal efforts in June 2025.

### Structural Reforms and Productivity

To address sluggish credit growth and weak business investment, the IMF stressed the importance of structural reforms to boost productivity growth, which remains significantly below pre-pandemic levels. Key recommendations include:
*   Leveraging **AI and green technologies** to boost Total Factor Productivity (TFP).
*   Enhancing access to finance for high-growth firms.
*   Reducing regulatory burdens and fostering entrepreneurship.
*   Improving education and skills to address demographic aging and labor market fragmentation.

### Financial Sector and External Position

The **2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP)** confirmed the resilience of the French banking sector, citing healthy capital buffers (**19.8% regulatory capital ratio**) and strong liquidity coverage ratios. However, weak bank profitability due to net interest margin compression and high corporate indebtedness among SMEs remain areas of concern. The **Non-Bank Financial Intermediaries (NBFIs)** sector and cyber/climate risks require continued monitoring.

Externally, France faces a negative **Net International Investment Position (NIIP)** of -20.3% of GDP, but benefits from the stability of the **Euro** as a global reserve currency and a deep, liquid domestic debt market.
