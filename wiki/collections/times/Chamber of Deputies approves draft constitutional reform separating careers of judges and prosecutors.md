# Chamber of Deputies approves draft constitutional reform separating careers of judges and prosecutors

## Event Details

On **2025-01-16**, the Italian [[Chamber of Deputies]] approved a significant draft constitutional reform aimed at separating the careers of judges and prosecutors. This legislative move is part of a broader comprehensive justice system reform adopted by the Government in March 2024, which has seen substantial implementation through various directives from the [[High Council for the Judiciary]].

The reform seeks to address long-standing structural issues within the Italian judicial system by decoupling the career paths of magistrates, a measure intended to enhance judicial independence and impartiality. However, the proposal has faced notable resistance from key judicial stakeholders. On **2025-01-08**, the [[High Council for the Judiciary]] issued a critical opinion regarding the separation of judges and prosecutors, arguing that the measure could undermine judicial autonomy.

The approval by the Chamber represents a major step in enhancing governmental stability, a goal also pursued through other constitutional measures such as the potential direct election of the Prime Minister. Despite the Chamber's approval, the reform continues to face scrutiny and opposition, with concerns raised about its impact on the balance of power within the judiciary. The [[National Association of Magistrates]] has also presented documents with proposals on the administration of justice, reflecting ongoing debates within the sector.

Key figures involved in or affected by this legislative process include:
- [[Cassellati]], President of the Chamber of Deputies, who oversaw the parliamentary proceedings.
- The [[First President of the High Court of Cassation]], representing judicial leadership concerns.
- The [[Minister of Justice]] and the [[Minister for Institutional Reforms]], who are central to the implementation of these structural changes.
