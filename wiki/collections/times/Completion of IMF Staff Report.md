# Completion of IMF Staff Report

## Event Details

The "Completion of IMF Staff Report" event tracks the conclusion of key Article IV consultation staff reports for various member states, highlighting critical economic assessments and policy recommendations.

**Poland: 2024 Article IV Consultation**

On **2024-12-16**, the International Monetary Fund completed its staff report for Poland’s 2024 Article IV Consultation, following the end of discussions with Polish officials on **2024-10-17**. This assessment occurred as Poland entered the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) in **2024** due to a widening fiscal deficit. The report was subsequently considered by the IMF Executive Board on **2025-01-10**, with the Press Release and Staff Report released on **2025-01-21**.

The completed report highlighted several critical economic assessments:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation & EDP**: The IMF noted the general government deficit had widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, driven by wage increases, social benefits, and defense spending. With public debt projected to rise from ~50% of GDP in 2023 to approximately 65% by 2029, the IMF supported the authorities' Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) aimed at reducing the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028. The staff recommended frontloading fiscal consolidation with an adjustment of ~0.5% of GDP in 2025 to support disinflation and allow for earlier monetary easing.
*   **Monetary Policy & Inflation**: While headline inflation declined to 4% in October 2024, it remained above the National Bank of Poland’s (NBP) target due to sticky core inflation and tight labor markets. The IMF commended the NBP’s independent monetary stance, urging that policy remain tight until wage growth decelerates, potentially allowing for rate cuts starting mid-2025.
*   **Financial Stability**: The banking sector was assessed as resilient with strong capital buffers and low non-performing loans. However, risks persisted from litigation over Swiss Franc (CHF) mortgages and WIBOR benchmark disputes. The IMF recommended eliminating distortions such as the bank asset tax that favors public debt and allowing mortgage credit holidays to expire to support credit recovery.
*   **Structural Challenges & Growth**: Long-term growth is constrained by population aging and a coal-dependent energy sector. The IMF urged reforms to boost productivity, including pension reforms (equalizing retirement ages, linking to life expectancy), increasing labor force participation, and accelerating green energy permitting. Approximately 976,000 Ukrainian refugees have been integrated into the labor market, offsetting some demographic headwinds.
*   **External Position**: Poland’s external position was deemed stronger than implied by fundamentals, with ample foreign exchange reserves (approx. 160% of IMF adequacy metric) and declining gross external debt (~45% of GDP). However, the current account was projected to move into deficit (-1.5% of GDP) over the medium term as imports rise. The real effective exchange rate appreciation since 2022 was noted as weighing on cost-competitiveness.
*   **Institutional Framework**: The report welcomed amendments to the stabilizing expenditure rule to align with the new EU fiscal framework and improved transparency of the Polish Development Bank (BGK). The planned establishment of an independent fiscal council in **2026** was highlighted as a step toward enhanced accountability.

**Belgium: 2025 Article IV Consultation**

On **2025-03-03**, the International Monetary Fund completed its staff report for Belgium’s 2025 Article IV Consultation. This milestone followed the formation of a new Belgian Federal Government in **February 2025** and concluding virtual meetings with Deputy Prime Minister [[Jan Jambon]] on **2025-02-21**.

The completed report highlighted several critical economic assessments:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: The IMF supported the new government’s plan for a seven-year fiscal adjustment under the EU Economic Governance Framework. The staff recommended an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of approximately 0.6% of GDP until 2031 to durably lower the deficit below 3% of GDP and reduce public debt (which stood at 104.1% of GDP in 2024) to approximately 78% by 2031. Without policy changes, debt was projected to rise to 123% by 2030.
*   **Labor Market Reforms**: The report identified automatic wage indexation and the 1996 wage law as key drivers of rising unit labor costs and declining competitiveness. Recommendations included abolishing automatic indexation, reforming the wage corridor, and adjusting tax and social benefits to enhance labor force participation.
*   **Financial Stability**: Systemic risks were assessed as moderate, with a well-capitalized banking sector and low non-performing loans (2.1%). However, risks in the commercial real estate sector and elevated household indebtedness (58% of GDP) were noted.
*   **External Position**: Belgium’s external position was deemed weaker than implied by fundamentals, with a real effective exchange rate overvaluation estimated between 4.9% and 17.4%. Despite a current account deficit, the large positive Net International Investment Position (57.3% of GDP) provided a buffer.

The completion of this report preceded the IMF Executive Board’s conclusion of the consultation on **2025-03-18** and the subsequent release of the Press Release and Staff Report on **2025-03-20**.

**Malta: 2024 Article IV Consultation**

On **2024-12-17**, the IMF completed its staff report for Malta’s 2024 Article IV Consultation, following the end of discussions with Maltese officials on **2024-11-15**. This assessment occurred amid Malta’s strong economic performance, driven by export-oriented services like tourism and online gaming, but marked by significant capacity constraints.

Key findings and recommendations from the Malta report included:

*   **Fiscal Policy & Energy Subsidies**: The IMF welcomed the authorities' commitment to fiscal consolidation under the new EU framework, targeting a structural deficit of 2.6% of GDP by 2027. A critical recommendation was the gradual phase-out of broad fixed-energy price policies, which accounted for 20% of the fiscal deficit. Resources saved should be reallocated to investment, innovation, and public services. The IMF urged the development of a long-term fiscal framework to address spending pressures from aging and climate transition.
*   **Financial Stability & Real Estate Risk**: While banks were well-capitalized (Tier 1 ratio ~21%) with low NPLs (~2%), exposure to real estate reached 70% of the loan portfolio. The IMF advised tightening macroprudential policies by raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB) rate and expanding its scope to include construction and commercial real estate. Vigilant monitoring of cyber risks and commercial real estate data gaps was also warranted.
*   **Growth Model & Productivity**: The report emphasized the need to shift from a labor-intensive growth model to a productivity-driven strategy to address infrastructure bottlenecks and tight labor markets. Recommendations included improving educational outcomes, increasing STEM enrollment, and supporting innovation through effective tax incentives and venture capital.
*   **Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Reform**: With the implementation of the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive deferred until end-2029, the IMF urged the development of a clear CIT reform roadmap to guide taxpayers and investors, preventing revenue loss to other jurisdictions and reducing distortions.
*   **External Sector Strength**: Malta’s external position was substantially stronger than fundamentals imply, with a current account surplus projected at 6.3% of GDP in 2024 and a large positive net international investment position (~95% of GDP), providing a strong buffer against external shocks.

The IMF Executive Board concluded the Article IV consultation for Malta on **2025-01-15**. The report also noted geopolitical risks from regional conflicts and domestic risks including higher-than-expected wage growth fueling inflation and pressure on public infrastructure from tourism and immigration.
