# Completion of IMF Staff Report

## Event Details

The IMF has completed Staff Reports for several nations during the 2024–2025 reporting periods, establishing the technical foundations for subsequent Executive Board conclusions and public releases.

### Malta
On December 17, 2024, the IMF completed the Staff Report for Malta following a series of economic policy discussions with Maltese officials held in Valletta from November 4–15, 2024. The report highlighted Malta's transition toward a productivity-driven growth model to address capacity constraints and labor shortages. Key findings included:
- **Fiscal Policy:** Recommendations to phase out fixed energy price subsidies to redirect fiscal space toward public services and innovation.
- **Financial Stability:** Identification of high real estate exposure in the banking sector (70% of loan portfolios) and suggestions to raise the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB).
- **Structural Reform:** A call for a Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reform roadmap to align with the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive.
- **Governance:** Emphasis on judicial reform to improve efficiency and bolster investor confidence.

This report preceded the IMF Executive Board's conclusion on the 2024 Article IV consultation with Malta on January 15, 2025.

### Poland
On December 16, 2024, the IMF completed the Staff Report for Poland following staff discussions held in Warsaw from October 8–17, 2024. The report evaluated Poland's economic recovery, noting a projected growth acceleration to 3.5% in 2025–2026 driven by domestic demand and the absorption of Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds. Key findings included:
- **Fiscal Position:** Identification of a widening fiscal deficit (estimated at 5.9% of GDP in 2024) due to public sector wage increases and defense spending, placing the country under the Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP).
- **Policy Recommendations:** Support for the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) to reduce the deficit to 2.9% by 2028 and stabilize debt at 60% of GDP.
- **Monetary Policy:** Recognition of the National Bank of Poland's (NBP) tight stance to combat inflation, with a shift toward fiscal consolidation as the primary buffer-rebuilding tool.
- **Structural Reform:** Emphasis on increasing labor supply (including refugee integration), fostering innovation through private equity, and decarbonizing the economy to halve coal-fueled power by 2030.
- **Financial Stability:** Assessment of a resilient banking system with high capital buffers, alongside recommendations to eliminate the mortgage credit holiday and redesign the bank asset tax.

The completion of the Polish Staff Report preceded the IMF Executive Board consideration on January 10, 2025, and the public press release of the Article IV consultation conclusion on January 21, 2025.

### Belgium
On March 3, 2025, the IMF completed the Staff Report following a consultation period (January 22 – February 4, 2025) with Belgian authorities. The report evaluated Belgium's economic resilience against structural headwinds, specifically focusing on the need for sustained fiscal consolidation to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP and managing public debt, which reached 104.1% of GDP in 2024.

The report highlighted several key areas for reform:
- **Fiscal Sustainability:** Support for a seven-year adjustment period under the new EU economic governance framework (EGF), requiring an annual reduction in the structural primary balance of approximately 0.6 percentage points of GDP until 2031.
- **Labor and Productivity:** Recommendations to review the 1996 wage law and automatic indexation to improve labor-market efficiency and competitiveness.
- **Spending Rationalization:** A focus on targeting the public wage bill and social benefits while protecting investments in healthcare, education, and the green transition.
- **Financial Stability:** An assessment of systemic risks as "Moderate," with advice to maintain current capital buffer requirements and prudential limits on mortgage loans.

This report served as the primary technical foundation for the IMF Executive Board conclusion on March 18, 2025, and the public release of the Article IV consultation results on March 20, 2025.

### Italy
On July 1, 2025, the IMF completed the Staff Report for Italy following a series of discussions in Rome that began on May 14 and concluded on May 28, 2025. The report noted Italy's resilience in 2024 and early 2025, highlighted by a return to a primary surplus of 0.4% and a record-high employment rate of 62.7% in April 2025.

Key findings and recommendations for Italy included:
- **Fiscal Adjustment:** A recommendation to reach a primary surplus of 3% by 2027 to ensure the debt-to-GDP ratio declines.
- **Structural Challenges:** Addressing weak productivity growth, a shortage of high-skilled workers, and rapid population aging.
- **NRRP Progress:** Recognition of Italy's progress on the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP), having achieved 54% of milestones by end-2024.
- **Financial Stability:** An assessment of "Moderate" sovereign risk, supported by high banking profitability and the activation of a 1% Systemic Risk Buffer (SyRB).

The completion of the Italian Staff Report preceded the Executive Board consideration on July 18, 2025, and the subsequent press release of the Executive Board conclusion on July 22, 2025.

### Slovak Republic
On March 3, 2025, the IMF completed the Staff Report for the Slovak Republic following a consultation period that concluded on January 28, 2025. The report noted an accelerating recovery with growth reaching 2.0% in 2024, while highlighting significant structural headwinds such as an aging population and low productivity.

Key findings and recommendations for Slovakia included:
- **Fiscal Policy:** Support for an ambitious 4-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028) to reduce the headline deficit (targeted at 4.7% in 2025) and stabilize public debt. The IMF recommended a shift toward expenditure-based rationalization, such as reducing subsidies and targeting social spending.
- **Financial Stability:** An assessment of a resilient banking system with healthy capital buffers, while advising monitoring of the new Financial Transaction Tax (FTT) and refined macroprudential tools for real estate markets.
- **Structural Reforms:** A call to intensify Active Labor Market Policies (ALMPs), increase R&D spending, and accelerate reforms to diversify the economy and improve governance.
- **Demographics & Governance:** Warnings regarding the long-term fiscal sustainability of the pension system due to aging and a call to strengthen judicial independence and anti-corruption frameworks.

This report served as the technical basis for the Executive Board consideration on March 18, 2025, and the final conclusion of the Article IV consultation on March 24, 2025.
