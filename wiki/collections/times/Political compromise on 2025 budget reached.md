# Political compromise on 2025 budget reached

## Event Details

In February 2025, French political authorities reached a significant compromise on the 2025 budget, marking a pivotal moment in the country's recent fiscal history. This agreement was characterized by the IMF as a "positive step forward" amid a period of high domestic policy uncertainty and political instability.

The compromise was instrumental in demonstrating the government's capacity for fiscal adjustment, a key factor assessed during the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation with France. By securing this budgetary agreement, the French government aimed to mitigate social discontent and provide a foundation for the broader Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). The MTFSP aims to bring the public deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029, addressing the urgent challenge of high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024.

While the 2025 budget compromise was a necessary precursor to stabilization, the IMF Executive Board and staff emphasized that further decisive actions were required. The authorities committed to a frontloaded structural fiscal effort, particularly targeting a 1.1% of GDP reduction in 2026, to ensure debt sustainability and restore market confidence. This event followed Moody's downgrade of France's credit rating to Aa3 in December 2024 and preceded the publication of an action plan to enhance public finance execution in March 2025. The compromise helped build broad political support for fiscal plans under EU rules, although risks regarding implementation and local government spending oversight remained.
