# Conclusion of Policy Discussions with Italian Authorities

## Event Details

On May 28, 2025, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) concluded its policy discussions with Italian authorities, marking a critical juncture in the 2025 Article IV Consultation. The discussions, involving key figures such as [[Giorgetti]], Minister of Economy and Finance, and [[Signorini]], Senior Deputy Governor of the Bank of Italy, focused on assessing Italy’s macroeconomic stability and fiscal trajectory.

The IMF staff highlighted Italy’s notable economic resilience despite heightened global uncertainty, noting real GDP growth of 0.7 percent in 2024. A significant achievement discussed was the return to a primary surplus in 2024, driven by improved tax compliance and robust labor market performance. However, the discussions underscored persistent structural challenges, including sluggish productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-EU average female labor force participation.

Key areas of policy discussion included the implementation of the [[National Recovery and Resilience Plan]] (NRRP), which had achieved 54 percent of its milestones by this stage. The IMF emphasized that full and timely implementation of the NRRP, along with comprehensive structural reforms focusing on productivity, labor supply, and the green transition, was essential to durably lift growth and offset demographic headwinds.

The staff recommended reaching a primary surplus of 3 percent of GDP by 2027 through measures such as improving tax compliance, rationalizing tax expenditures, and streamlining pension-related spending. This was deemed necessary to place Italy’s persistently high public debt, which stood at 135.3 percent of GDP, on a clear downward trajectory. The discussions also touched upon the health of the banking sector, noting its overall soundness with high profitability and adequate capital buffers, though vulnerabilities among less significant institutions remained a concern.

This conclusion of policy discussions preceded the completion of the IMF staff report on July 1, 2025, and the final approval by the IMF Executive Board on July 18, 2025.
