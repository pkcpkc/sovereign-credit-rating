# Introduction of new pension system reforms (index funds for Pillar II).

## Event Details

On January 1, 2025, Slovakia introduced significant reforms to its pension system, specifically targeting the second pillar (Pillar II) by integrating index funds. This legislative change was implemented against a backdrop of severe long-term fiscal sustainability risks highlighted in the [[2025 Country Report - Slovakia]] published by the [[European Commission]]. The reforms are part of a broader strategy to address demographic ageing, which poses a critical threat to the country's fiscal health, with age-related expenditure projected to rise from 20% to 25% of GDP by 2070.

The introduction of index funds for Pillar II aligns with previous structural measures, such as linking the retirement age to life expectancy, aimed at improving long-term solvency. These reforms are particularly urgent given the persistent fiscal imbalances in Slovakia, which recorded a general government deficit of 5.3% of GDP in 2024. Despite a fiscal consolidation package approved in October 2024 involving VAT and corporate tax hikes, the deficit is projected to remain elevated at 4.9% in 2025 and rise to 5.1% in 2026. The high structural deficit is driven largely by permanent spending increases in pensions and healthcare, underscoring the necessity of the Pillar II adjustments to alleviate pressure on the state budget.

The reform occurs within a complex economic and institutional environment. While the [[Council for Budget Responsibility]] remains a strong fiscal anchor, the country faces challenges related to rule of law backsliding, including the dismantling of anti-corruption agencies and reduced policy transparency. Furthermore, the low private R&D intensity (0.58% of GDP) and an underdeveloped capital market present hurdles for the effective operation and growth of new pension investment vehicles. The success of these pension reforms is viewed as a critical component of Slovakia's [[Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)]] and its Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan, which was endorsed by the Council in January 2025.
