# Council of Europe Committee of Ministers addresses 'chilling effect' in Hungarian judiciary

## Event Details

On **2025-03-06**, the **Council of Europe Committee of Ministers** issued a statement addressing the "chilling effect" on the Hungarian judiciary. This intervention occurred against a backdrop of escalating tensions within Hungary's legal framework, specifically following the resignation of the **National Judicial Council (Hungary)** President in December 2024 after judicial protests against new reforms. The Committee expressed deep concern over measures perceived to undermine judicial independence, including pressure on judges and inadequate consultation processes regarding the raising of judicial age limits and service terms.

The Committee's address highlighted that these developments contributed to an environment where judges might feel compelled to align with government interests rather than independent legal judgment. This event was part of a broader deterioration in rule of law indicators cited in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Hungary** by the **European Commission**, which noted stagnation or decline in judicial independence, anti-corruption frameworks, and media pluralism. The "chilling effect" specifically referenced the impact of political pressure on the judiciary, exacerbated by the withdrawal of the National Judicial Council from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms in January 2025 and subsequent legislative changes adopted in June 2025. The Committee's stance aligned with criticisms from the **Venice Commission** and the **European Court of Human Rights**, emphasizing the need for procedural safeguards and genuine independence of oversight bodies.
