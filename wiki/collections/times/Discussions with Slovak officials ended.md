# Discussions with Slovak officials ended

## Event Details

The IMF staff's discussions with Slovak officials concluded on January 28, 2025. These virtual discussions, which began on December 4, 2024, formed the basis for the **2025 Article IV Consultation** with the Slovak Republic.

### Context and Participants
The discussions took place against the backdrop of significant political changes in Slovakia, following the change of government in October 2024. Key participants included:
*   **IMF Mission Head**: Saxegaard
*   **Slovak Officials**: 
    *   [[Kamenický]] (Minister of Finance)
    *   [[Kažimír]] (Governor, National Bank of Slovakia)
    *   [[Taraba]] (Minister of the Environment)

### Key Outcomes and Assessments
The consultations assessed an economy recovering from recent headwinds, with GDP growth accelerating to 2.0% in 2024. However, the period was marked by a widening fiscal deficit, which reached 5.8% of GDP in 2024, triggering an **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)** by the European Commission in July 2024.

The IMF staff and subsequent Executive Board consideration (March 2025) highlighted several critical points derived from these discussions:

1.  **Fiscal Consolidation Plan**: The Slovak government presented an ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028) aimed at reducing the deficit to approximately 2.2% of GDP by 2028. While the IMF endorsed the direction, staff noted that additional measures, particularly on the expenditure side, would be required beyond the 2025 budget to meet medium-term targets and ensure debt sustainability.
2.  **Macroeconomic Outlook**: Growth was projected to moderate to 1.8% in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation and weak external demand, before recovering to 2.1% in 2026. Inflation was expected to rise temporarily to 4.1% in 2025 due to tax increases, returning to the 2% target by early 2027.
3.  **Debt Sustainability**: Public debt was projected to rise in the medium term (to 74.3% of GDP by 2030), with long-term risks exacerbated by demographic aging. The IMF emphasized the need for structural reforms to address aging-related costs and diversify the economy away from its heavy dependence on the automotive sector.
4.  **Financial Sector Health**: The banking sector was deemed resilient with strong capital buffers, though vulnerabilities in commercial real estate and upcoming mortgage refixations were noted as areas requiring enhanced macroprudential frameworks.
5.  **Governance and Structural Reforms**: The discussions underscored the importance of strengthening governance, reducing corruption vulnerabilities, and enhancing judicial independence to maintain investor confidence and ensure the effective absorption of EU funds.

Following the conclusion of discussions, the IMF Staff Report was completed on March 3, 2025, and considered by the Executive Board on March 18, 2025. The consultation was formally concluded by the IMF Executive Board on March 24, 2025.
