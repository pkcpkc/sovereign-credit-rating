# Publication of the 2025 Country Report by the European Commission.

## Event Details

The [[European Commission]] published the 2025 Country Report for Slovakia, highlighting a critical divergence between short-term economic stability and deepening structural, fiscal, and institutional vulnerabilities. The report covers the forecasting horizon of 2024–2026 and identifies severe risks to debt sustainability and competitiveness, exacerbated by rule of law backsliding and sluggish structural reforms.

### Economic Outlook and Competitiveness
Slovakia’s economy grew by 2.1% in 2024, driven by consumption. However, the growth outlook has deteriorated significantly, with GDP growth forecast at only 1.5% in 2025 and 1.4% in 2026. This slowdown is attributed to:
*   Waning foreign direct investment (FDI), with FDI stock falling to 46% of GDP in 2023.
*   Trade tensions, particularly potential US tariffs impacting the export-oriented automotive sector.
*   Deteriorating competitiveness caused by sluggish labour productivity growth failing to keep pace with rising unit labour costs.
*   Persistent current account deficits exposing the economy to external financing needs.

### Fiscal Sustainability and Deficit Risks
Fiscal sustainability is identified as the most significant risk area. Slovakia continues to face an excessive deficit:
*   **Deficit Trajectory**: The general government deficit stood at 5.3% of GDP in 2024. Despite a consolidation package implemented in late 2024 (including VAT, corporate tax, and financial transaction tax increases), the deficit is projected to remain elevated at 4.9% in 2025 and rise to 5.1% in 2026 due to permanent spending increases in pensions, healthcare, and military salaries.
*   **Debt Dynamics**: General government debt is projected to rise from 59.3% in 2024 to 63.0% by 2026. The high interest-to-GDP ratio relative to nominal GDP growth slows debt stabilization.
*   **Long-term Risks**: Demographic ageing poses a severe long-term threat, with age-related expenditure projected to increase from 20% to 25% of GDP by 2070. While recent pension reforms (linking retirement age to life expectancy) improve sustainability relative to previous projections, the pressure on public finances remains high.

### Rule of Law and Institutional Strength
The report highlights severe governance deficits that undermine the business environment and investor confidence:
*   **Anti-Corruption Backsliding**: The dismantling of specialized anti-corruption law enforcement agencies, including the [[National Crime Agency]] and Special Prosecution Office, has reduced the capacity to investigate and prosecute high-level corruption.
*   **Legislative Instability**: Frequent changes to legislation and the use of fast-track procedures bypassing impact assessments have created an unpredictable regulatory environment. The dissolution of the Civil Service Council further weakens institutional checks on the executive.
*   **Perception**: Slovakia ranked last in the EU for doing business conditions, with 85% of companies perceiving a high level of corruption.

### Structural Weaknesses and Energy Transition
*   **Industrial Concentration**: The economy remains overly reliant on the automotive manufacturing sector, which limits value-added retention and creates sector-specific risks. Low private R&D intensity (0.58% of GDP) and an underdeveloped capital market (low venture capital activity) hinder diversification and innovation.
*   **Energy Security**: Slovakia faces a significant energy security dilemma, maintaining high dependence on Russian crude oil (over 80%) and natural gas (~70%). While the nuclear fleet is expanding, the transition to renewables is slow, with only 24% of electricity from renewable sources in 2024. High energy prices for non-household consumers further hinder industrial competitiveness.
*   **Labor Market**: While unemployment is at historic lows (5.1%), there are severe shortages of skilled workers in healthcare and education. Socio-economic disparities remain pronounced, with the Roma community experiencing an unemployment rate exceeding 53%.

### Policy Responses and Outlook
*   **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)**: Slovakia has fulfilled 33% of RRP milestones, but implementation is slowed by inefficient public procurement and administrative fragmentation. Accelerating absorption is critical for the green and digital transitions.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: The government approved a comprehensive fiscal consolidation package in October 2024, aiming to address the excessive deficit. However, rigid budget composition limits the ability to swiftly adjust discretionary spending.
*   **External Support**: Access to EU funding (Cohesion and RRF) and ECB liquidity backstops supports external balances and mitigates short-term rollover risks, given Slovakia’s status as a Eurozone member.

### Key Timeline
*   **2024**: Economic growth of 2.1% and fiscal deficit of 5.3% of GDP recorded.
*   **2024-06**: Publication of the 2025 Country Report by the [[European Commission]].
*   **2024-10**: Approval of comprehensive fiscal consolidation package (VAT, CIT, and financial transaction tax).
*   **2025-01**: Council endorsement of Slovakia's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan; Introduction of new pension system reforms (index funds for Pillar II).
*   **2025-06-30**: Deadline for submission of Slovakia's National Social Climate Plan.
*   **2026-08-31**: Deadline for full implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP).
