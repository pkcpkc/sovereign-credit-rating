# Authorities' target for deficit below 2% of GDP

## Event Details

In the context of the **IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation** with Italy, the Italian authorities outlined their medium-term fiscal strategy under the **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)**. While the immediate goal for 2026 is to reduce the general government deficit to below 3% of GDP, the broader strategic objective targets bringing the deficit **below 2% of GDP by 2029**.

This fiscal trajectory contrasts with the IMF staff's recommendation, which advocates for a more front-loaded adjustment to achieve a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027 to firmly place Italy's high public debt (135.3% of GDP in 2024) on a downward trajectory. The authorities, led by **Minister of Economy and Finance Giancarlo Giorgetti**, argue for a gradual seven-year adjustment path, emphasizing that structural reforms—particularly those related to productivity growth, labor market participation, and the implementation of the **National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP)**—will support a sustainable debt path without requiring immediate, sharp fiscal tightening.

Key milestones and related fiscal data include:

*   **2024**: Public debt ended at 135.3% of GDP; headline inflation averaged 1.1%; primary surplus reached 0.4% of GDP; fiscal deficit shrunk to 3.4% of GDP.
*   **2026-06-30**: Deadline for meeting NRRP milestones and targets.
*   **2029**: Target year for the authorities' goal of maintaining a deficit below 2% of GDP.
*   **2027**: IMF staff recommended target for a primary surplus of 3% of GDP (disputed by authorities in favor of gradual adjustment).

The successful execution of this plan relies on continued improvement in tax compliance, broadening of the tax base, and the efficient implementation of structural reforms to boost potential growth amid challenges such as rapid population aging and weak productivity growth.
