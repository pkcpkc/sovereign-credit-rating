# Target Year for Structural Deficit Reduction (2.6% of GDP)

## Event Details

The year **2027** is identified as the target deadline for Malta to achieve a structural deficit of **2.6% of GDP**. This objective is central to the country's fiscal consolidation strategy under the new EU fiscal framework, as highlighted in the **[[International Monetary Fund (IMF)]]** 2024 Article IV Consultation.

### Context and Rationale
*   **Fiscal Consolidation Commitment**: The Maltese authorities have committed to fiscal consolidation to address long-term spending pressures arising from an aging population and climate transition costs. The target is part of a broader effort to stabilize public debt, which is projected to rise marginally to approximately 50% of GDP but remains well below the EU's 60% ceiling.
*   **Reform of Energy Subsidies**: A key component of meeting this target involves phasing out broad fixed-energy price policies. These subsidies currently account for **20% of the fiscal deficit**. The IMF recommends transitioning these resources toward targeted subsidies and reallocation toward investment, innovation, and green transition initiatives to boost productivity.
*   **EU Framework Alignment**: The target aligns with the European Union's fiscal rules. The IMF noted that Malta is projected to enter the **[[Excessive Deficit Procedure]]** in 2025, necessitating strict adherence to consolidation paths to ensure sustainability.

### Strategic Implications
*   **Revenue Administration**: To support the deficit reduction target, the IMF encourages continued improvements in revenue administration efficiency through reforms by the **[[Malta Tax and Customs Administration (MTCA)]]**.
*   **Long-Term Framework**: The achievement of the 2027 target is viewed as a stepping stone toward establishing a more robust long-term fiscal framework that can accommodate future structural spending needs without compromising debt sustainability.
