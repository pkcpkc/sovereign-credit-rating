# Projected GDP growth of 1.4%; Target budget deficit of 7% of GDP

## Event Details

In 2025, Romania’s economic trajectory is defined by a projected real GDP growth of 1.4% and a target budget deficit of 7% of GDP. These figures represent a significant shift from the preceding year, where real GDP growth decelerated to 0.8% and the government deficit surged to 9.3% of GDP.

The projection of 1.4% growth reflects an expected pickup in economic activity, supported by substantial EU funding (over EUR 90 billion) available for infrastructure and reforms, as well as the country's crucial automotive sector. However, growth remains constrained by structural weaknesses, including outdated infrastructure, low R&D capacity, skills shortages, and a challenging business environment marked by regulatory uncertainty.

The target deficit of 7% of GDP is a central component of Romania's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP), which was submitted in October 2024 and endorsed by the European Council in January 2025. This plan commits to a seven-year adjustment period (2025-2031) to restore fiscal sustainability. To achieve the 7% deficit target for 2025, the government implemented emergency measures in late 2024, including wage and pension freezes and the removal of tax exemptions, aimed at saving 2.0% of GDP.

Despite these consolidation efforts, macroeconomic vulnerabilities persist. The current account deficit widened to 8.4% of GDP in 2024, and public debt is projected to rise from 54.8% of GDP in 2024 to 59.4% in 2025. The European Commission highlights that without resolute fiscal consolidation and structural reforms, Romania remains vulnerable to external shocks and shifts in investor sentiment. The accession to the Schengen Area on January 1, 2025, is expected to facilitate trade and foreign investment, providing a potential strength amidst these fiscal challenges.
