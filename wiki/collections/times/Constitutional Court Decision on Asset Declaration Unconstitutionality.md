# Constitutional Court Decision on Asset Declaration Unconstitutionality

## Event Details

On **2025-06-29**, the [[Constitutional Court of Romania]] issued a landmark decision ruling that the existing legal framework for asset declarations was unconstitutional. This ruling is a critical component of Romania's anti-corruption and judicial reform landscape, as assessed in the [[2025 Rule of Law Report Romania]].

The decision significantly impacts the operational capacity of the [[National Integrity Agency]], which is responsible for verifying the wealth and interests of public officials. The Constitutional Court's annulment of the asset declaration requirements creates a legal vacuum that weakens transparency and accountability mechanisms within the Romanian government.

### Key Impacts and Context

*   **Weakening of Transparency Mechanisms**: The ruling limits the ability to enforce asset declaration standards, potentially reducing the effectiveness of checks against illicit enrichment among high-ranking officials.
*   **Resource Constraints for Oversight Bodies**: The [[National Integrity Agency]] already faces resource shortages; this decision further complicates its mandate to ensure integrity in public office.
*   **Judicial Perception**: The decision contributes to the average perception of judicial independence in Romania, as identified by the European Commission. It highlights ongoing tensions between legislative frameworks and constitutional oversight.
*   **Broader Anti-Corruption Strategy**: This event occurs against the backdrop of Romania's 2021-2025 Anti-Corruption Strategy. While high-level prosecutions continue, systemic legal uncertainties, such as statute of limitations rulings by the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]], have led to the annulment of many corruption convictions. This specific Constitutional Court decision exacerbates these systemic risks by removing a key preventive tool for corruption.
