# Increase in pending ECHR leading judgments to 26

## Event Details

On January 1, 2025, the number of pending leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) against France increased to 26. This rise in pending cases highlighted ongoing challenges in the implementation of ECHR rulings within the French judicial system. While the French government maintained that efforts were being made to resolve historical backlogs, the increase signaled persistent delays in addressing specific human rights violations identified by the Court.

This surge occurred amidst a broader period of scrutiny regarding France's rule of law record, as detailed in the 2025 Rule of Law Report by the [[European Commission]]. The report noted that while significant progress would be made later in the year—with pending cases decreasing to 22 by June 2025—the initial increase to 26 underscored the complexity of resolving long-standing issues, such as those related to land expulsion measures which had remained unresolved for over a decade. The fluctuation in pending judgments served as a key metric for evaluating the effectiveness of France's mechanisms for complying with international human rights obligations.
