# Peak of headline inflation at 13.1% y/y (historical reference)

## Event Details

In October 2024, Belgium experienced the historical peak of headline inflation at 13.1% year-over-year. This extreme price pressure occurred amidst a broader period of economic disruption, following the expiration of a €22 billion one-year government bond in September 2024 and occurring shortly after the June 2024 Federal Elections.

According to the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation: Belgium Staff Report**, this inflationary peak was part of a broader trajectory where headline inflation had begun to decline by early 2025 (reaching 4.4% in January 2025). However, the IMF noted that core inflation remained persistent and sticky, largely driven by Belgium's unique institutional framework of **automatic wage indexation** and indexed rents. This mechanism, particularly the 1996 wage law, contributed to rising unit labor costs and declining competitiveness, sustaining inflationary pressures even as the initial shock subsided.

The IMF report identifies this period as a critical reference point for understanding the challenges facing Belgium's fiscal sustainability. The high inflation environment exacerbated structural deficits and contributed to the widening of the general government deficit to 4.4% of GDP in 2024. The persistence of core inflation, rooted in the indexation system, complicated the return to price stability and underscored the IMF's recommendation for structural reforms to the wage-setting mechanism to improve long-term competitiveness and fiscal health.
