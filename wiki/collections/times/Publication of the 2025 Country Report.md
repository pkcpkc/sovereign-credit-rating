# Publication of the 2025 Country Report

## Event Details

On **2025-06-04**, the European Commission released the **2025 Country Reports** (including SWD(2025) 201 final for Belgium, SWD(2025) 210 final for France, and SWD(2025) 217 final for Hungary). These comprehensive assessments evaluated member states' economic, social, and budgetary policies, highlighting progress toward EU targets and the transition to a green and digital economy.

### Regional Highlights:

**Belgium**
*   **Economic Outlook:** A projected GDP growth of 0.8% and a headline deficit of 5.4% for 2025, with public debt reaching 107.1%.
*   **Labor Market:** Despite a record employment rate of 72.3% in 2024, the country faces significant skills mismatches in STEM and healthcare.
*   **Fiscal Pressures:** Concerns were raised regarding the rising costs of an aging population, with pension spending projected to increase by 3.5% of GDP between 2022 and 2070.
*   **Sustainability:** Belgium is currently projected to fall short of its 2030 climate effort-sharing targets, requiring accelerated investment in the energy transition.

**France**
*   **Economic Slowdown:** Real GDP growth is expected to decelerate to 0.6% in 2025, driven by high political uncertainty and restrictive monetary policy.
*   **Fiscal Sustainability:** Public debt is projected to reach 116% of GDP by the end of 2025, with the general government deficit rising to 5.8% in 2024.
*   **Labor Market & Productivity:** France maintains a high employment rate (75.1% in 2024) but faces high unemployment (7.4%) and a shortage of skilled staff.
*   **Energy & Innovation:** While achieving 95% low-carbon electricity, France faces hurdles in decarbonizing transport and buildings. Business R&D intensity (1.44%) currently lags behind the 3% target.
*   **Social & Regional Issues:** Territorial gaps are widening, with five regions falling below 75% of the EU GDP per head. The poverty rate is increasing, with 20.5% of the population at risk of social exclusion in 2024.

**Hungary**
*   **Economic & Fiscal Outlook:** Hungary saw GDP growth of 0.5% in 2024, with forecasts reaching 0.8% in 2025 and 2.5% in 2026. Public debt reached 73.5% of GDP in 2024, with high debt-servicing costs and an implicit interest rate near 6% in 2025.
*   **Business & Innovation:** The report highlights regulatory volatility and market distortions due to state intervention. R&D intensity remains low (1.4% vs 2.2% EU average), and venture capital investment has dropped significantly.
*   **Energy & Decarbonisation:** Hungary remains heavily dependent on Russian fossil fuels (over 70% of natural gas). While it leads in solar production (25% of EU share), it maintains high fossil fuel subsidies (1.01% of GDP) not planned for phase-out before 2030.
*   **Labour & Social Fairness:** A significant shortage of skilled workers persists, and tertiary education attainment is low (32.3%). The country faces a severe housing crisis, with prices rising 230% between 2010 and 2024, and rising poverty risks among vulnerable groups.
