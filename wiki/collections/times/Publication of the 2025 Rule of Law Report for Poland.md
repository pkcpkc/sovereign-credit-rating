# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report for Poland

## Event Details

The chronological progression of rule of law reforms and assessments in Poland is marked by significant legislative shifts and institutional restructuring:

*   **July 14, 2024:** The process of structural separation began with the tabling of a draft law aimed at separating the office of the Prosecutor General from the Minister of Justice.
*   **December 6, 2024:** The Standing Committee of the Council of Ministers formally adopted the draft law on prosecution reform, a key step in addressing judicial independence.
*   **January 1, 2025:** The status of pending European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) leading judgments remained a critical point of observation for judicial oversight.
*   **March 11, 2025:** The Advocate General issued an opinion in case C-448/23 regarding the Constitutional Tribunal, highlighting ongoing concerns over the tribunal's constitutional function.
*   **March 2025:** The assessment of civic space in Poland was updated from "obstructed" to "narrowed," reflecting a shift in the legal and social environment for civil society.
*   **July 8, 2025:** The European Commission officially published the **2025 Rule of Law Report for Poland**. The report detailed mixed progress: while significant strides were made in separating the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General and auditing irregularities in the National Prosecution Office, profound concerns persisted regarding the independence of the Constitutional Tribunal and the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ). The report also noted the liquidation of the Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (CBA) in favor of police-led coordination and the full operationalization of the European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO).
