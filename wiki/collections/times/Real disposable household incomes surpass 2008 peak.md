# Real disposable household incomes surpass 2008 peak

## Event Details

In a significant economic milestone, real disposable household incomes in Austria have officially surpassed the peaks observed in 2008. This development occurs against a backdrop of complex economic shifts:

*   **Economic Trajectory:** While household incomes reached this historical high in 2024, the broader economy is navigating a three-year recessionary trend. Following contractions of 1% in 2023 and 1.2% in 2024, the GDP is projected to fall by another 0.3% in 2025.
*   **Cost and Inflation Dynamics:** The rise in disposable income follows a period of intense inflation, which peaked at 8.6% in 2022. Since 2019, nominal unit labor costs have risen 10% more than the Euro area average, impacting industrial competitiveness.
*   **Fiscal and Structural Context:** Despite the income milestone, Austria faces significant fiscal challenges, including a high deficit and rising age-related expenditures. The government is currently pursuing fiscal consolidation to manage these costs while attempting to transition away from heavy labor taxation toward growth-friendly bases.
*   **Energy Transition:** Parallel to these economic shifts, Austria has made strides in sustainability, achieving a record 87% renewable electricity generation in 2024, with long-term targets set for 100% renewable electricity by 2030 and climate neutrality by 2040.
