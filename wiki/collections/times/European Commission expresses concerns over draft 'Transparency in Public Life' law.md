# European Commission expresses concerns over draft 'Transparency in Public Life' law

## Event Details

On **2025-05-25**, the **European Commission** expressed significant concerns regarding the Hungarian government's draft law on "Transparency in Public Life." This legislative proposal, introduced in May 2025, aims to restrict foreign funding for entities deemed to endanger national sovereignty. The draft law has sparked widespread protest from **Civil Society Organizations (CSOs)** and media outlets, who view it as a direct threat to critical voices and independent journalism.

The Commission's concern is situated within the broader context of a deteriorating environment for civil society in Hungary. The report highlights that the **Sovereignty Protection Office** has increasingly launched investigations into anti-corruption CSOs and those receiving EU funds, often citing alleged "foreign influence." The proposed transparency law is seen as part of a wider pattern of regulatory pressure intended to limit civic space and restrict organizations that challenge the government.

Key aspects of the concern include:
- **Restriction of Foreign Funding**: The draft targets organizations receiving foreign support, effectively chilling independent civic activity under the guise of national security.
- **Threat to Civil Society Space**: The environment for CSOs has notably deteriorated, with the law adding legal uncertainty and potential sanctions for critical entities.
- **Broader Rule of Law Erosion**: This event is linked to other systemic issues identified in the **2025 Rule of Law Report**, including the extensive use of emergency powers, weakened institutional checks and balances, and pressure on **Media Pluralism**.

This development underscores the ongoing tension between the Hungarian government's policies and EU standards on the rule of law, particularly regarding the independence of civil society and the protection of fundamental rights.
