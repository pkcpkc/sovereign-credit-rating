# Adoption of Draft Law Separating Minister of Justice and Prosecutor General Functions

## Event Details

On 6 December 2024, the Polish Cabinet adopted a significant draft law aimed at separating the functions of the Minister of Justice from those of the Prosecutor General. This legislative initiative was identified in the 2025 [[Rule of Law Report]] on Poland as a primary mechanism to ensure the functional independence of the prosecution service, addressing long-standing concerns regarding political influence over criminal investigations and prosecutorial decisions.

Key provisions of the adopted draft law include:
- The formal disentanglement of political and prosecutorial leadership roles.
- The discontinuation of criminal investigations against Supreme Court judges based on their judicial decisions, a practice previously used to exert pressure on the judiciary.
- The cessation of using prosecutor secondments as disciplinary sanctions, aiming to protect prosecutorial autonomy.

This action forms part of the broader government Action Plan on Rule of Law implementation. While the adoption marks notable progress in anti-corruption frameworks and judicial independence, the European Commission notes that perceived judicial independence remains very low (26% among the general public and 24% among companies). The reform is intended to complement other ongoing efforts, such as the full operationalization of the [[European Public Prosecutor Office]] in Poland and the establishment of a new Central Office for Combating Corruption. Despite these advancements, risks regarding systemic corruption in public procurement and the legitimacy crisis of the [[Constitutional Tribunal]] persist.
