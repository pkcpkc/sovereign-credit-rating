# Target for climate neutrality

## Event Details

The transition toward climate neutrality is being pursued through distinct national strategies and regional fiscal adjustments, primarily highlighted in the policies of Austria and Poland.

### Austria's Strategic Roadmap
Austria has established a rigorous timeline for its environmental and energy transition, with a primary milestone of achieving **climate neutrality by 2040**. Key components of this strategy include:
- **Renewable Energy:** A goal to reach 100% renewable electricity and VHCN coverage by 2030. In 2024, the country reached a record 87% renewable electricity generation.
- **Infrastructure & Investment:** To meet the 2040 target, an estimated EUR 53 billion is required for grid expansion.
- **Industrial Decarbonisation:** Efforts are concentrated on energy-intensive manufacturing, which currently accounts for 35% of emissions.
- **Energy Security:** Following the termination of Russian imports in early 2025, Austria is diversifying gas supplies from [[Norway]], [[Germany]], and [[Italy]].

### Poland's Economic and Energy Transition
While targeting climate neutrality by 2050, Poland faces specific structural hurdles in its decarbonization journey:
- **Energy Mix:** Fossil fuels currently account for 70% of electricity generation, and the district heating sector remains 69% coal-dependent.
- **Fiscal Constraints:** Poland is under an excessive deficit procedure (initiated January 2023). A binding expenditure path aims to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2028 to fund structural transitions.
- **Industrial Leadership:** Despite decarbonization challenges, Poland has emerged as a leader in clean technology, producing 60% of all European-made lithium batteries.
- **Economic Growth:** The country maintains a fast-growing economy, with 2024 GDP growth at 2.9% and projections for higher growth in 2025, providing the fiscal base for future green investments.
