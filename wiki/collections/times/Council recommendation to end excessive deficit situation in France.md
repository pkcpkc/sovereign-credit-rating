# Council recommendation to end excessive deficit situation in France

## Event Details

On **21 January 2025**, the Council of the European Union issued a recommendation to end the excessive deficit situation in France. This decision was part of the broader endorsement of France's medium-term fiscal-structural plan, marking a significant step in the country's compliance with the reformed EU economic governance framework.

The recommendation followed France's submission of its medium-term fiscal-structural plan on **31 October 2024**. The Council's move signifies the transition from the corrective phase of the Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) to a more preventive monitoring approach, contingent upon France's adherence to its seven-year fiscal adjustment path.

Key factors influencing this decision and the subsequent recommendations included:

*   **Fiscal Context**: At the time of the recommendation, France faced persistently high general government deficits (recorded at 5.8% of GDP in 2024) and a rising public debt burden (113% of GDP). The Council acknowledged the commitment to a medium-term fiscal-structural plan aimed at achieving debt sustainability.
*   **Political Climate**: The recommendation was issued against a backdrop of a fragmented political landscape following the 2024 general elections, which had led to unprecedented delays in budget adoption and political instability affecting the timely implementation of fiscal decisions.
*   **Implementation Timeline**: The Council set a deadline of **30 April 2025** for the implementation of reforms underpinning the fiscal adjustment, extending previous timelines to account for political complexities.

This event is closely linked to the publication of the **2025 Country Report - France** on **4 June 2025**, which further analyzed the economic developments and key policy challenges, reinforcing the need for robust spending reviews and targeted structural reforms to ensure fiscal sustainability and boost potential growth amidst a slowing economic trajectory.
