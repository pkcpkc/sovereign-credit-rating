# Publication of the 2025 Country Report Belgium by the European Commission

## Event Details

The publication of the 2025 Country Report for [[Belgium]] by the [[European Commission]] served as a critical assessment of the nation's economic and structural trajectory. Issued in 2025, the report highlighted significant headwinds facing the Belgian economy, including high public debt, deteriorating fiscal balances, and structural rigidities within the labor market and business environment.

Key findings from the report indicated that economic growth was projected to decelerate to 0.8% in 2025, driven by robust private consumption but hindered by declining exports and external shocks. Public finances were identified as under pressure, with the headline deficit projected to increase to 5.4% of GDP. Without policy action, public debt was expected to rise to 126.4% by 2035, largely due to aging-related costs and increased defense spending.

The report emphasized urgent needs for structural reforms, particularly regarding pension sustainability, long-term care efficiency, and labor market activation. A major point of concern was the complexity of Belgium's tax system, which created disincentives for work and investment. The Commission called for a shift in the tax burden away from labor, simplification of regulations, and improved coordination between federal and regional government levels. Additionally, the transition to a green economy was flagged as requiring accelerated deployment of renewable energy and reduced fossil fuel subsidies.
