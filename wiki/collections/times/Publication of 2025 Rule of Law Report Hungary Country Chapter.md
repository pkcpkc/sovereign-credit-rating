# Publication of 2025 Rule of Law Report Hungary Country Chapter

## Event Details

On **2025-07-08**, the **European Commission** published the *2025 Rule of Law Report: Hungary Country Chapter* (SWD(2025) 917 final), assessing the rule of law situation in Hungary up to mid-2025. The report highlights a stagnation or deterioration in key areas, including the judiciary, anti-corruption framework, media pluralism, and institutional checks and balances.

### Key Findings

**Judicial Independence and Reforms**
While judicial salaries increased, concerns persist regarding independence due to pressure on judges and ineffective consultation processes. The **National Judicial Council (Hungary)** faced significant challenges, including the resignation of its President in December 2024 following protests against reforms that raised the minimum age for judges to 35. The Council subsequently withdrew from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms. Case allocation transparency in lower-instance courts remains a critical deficiency, although the **Kúria (Hungary)** maintains high transparency.

**Anti-Corruption Framework**
Hungary's corruption perception remains high (CPI score 41/100). There is no robust track record of prosecuting high-level corruption. The **Integrity Authority (Hungary)** continues to face obstacles in oversight tasks, and the asset declaration system lacks effective verification. Lobbying and revolving door regulations remain pending despite scheduled legislative plans.

**Media Pluralism and Freedom**
Threats to media pluralism are unaddressed. The **National Media and Infocommunications Authority (NMHH)** lacks functional independence, and state advertising disproportionately benefits pro-government outlets (e.g., KESMA). Public service media remains subject to government influence. Journalists face increased pressure, including investigations by the **Sovereignty Protection Office (Hungary)** for alleged "foreign interests."

**Checks and Balances and Civic Space**
Institutional checks are weakened by the extensive use of emergency powers to override parliamentary legislation and frequent late-stage legislative amendments bypassing public consultation. The environment for civil society organizations (CSOs) has deteriorated, with the Sovereignty Protection Office launching investigations into anti-corruption CSOs. A draft law on "transparency in public life" proposed in May 2025 aims to restrict foreign funding for entities deemed to endanger sovereignty, sparking widespread protest.

**Foreign Investment and Regulatory Pressure**
Foreign companies, particularly from other EU Member States, face intensified regulatory pressure in strategic sectors through special taxes, export restrictions, and discriminatory practices. Legal uncertainty is exacerbated by the government's ability to interfere with merger control rules via decrees of "strategic importance," which are not subject to judicial review.
