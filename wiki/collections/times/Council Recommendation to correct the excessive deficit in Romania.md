# Council Recommendation to correct the excessive deficit in Romania

## Event Details

On January 21, 2025, the [[Council of the European Union]] adopted a [[Council Recommendation to correct the excessive deficit in Romania]]. This recommendation formally instructed Romania to take necessary measures to correct its excessive deficit position as determined by the Council's previous decision under the Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP).

The recommendation followed the endorsement of Romania's [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)]] on January 10, 2025, which committed the country to a seven-year fiscal adjustment period extending to 2031. The EDP correction was necessitated by a severe deterioration in Romania's fiscal position in 2024, where the general government deficit reached 9.3% of GDP and net expenditure growth surged to 19.9%, significantly exceeding EU recommended maxima. This fiscal slippage was driven by generous public sector wage increases, pension hikes, and elevated public investment spending, contributing to a rapid rise in public debt to 54.8% of GDP.

In response to these pressures and in alignment with the Council's recommendation, the Romanian government implemented emergency consolidation measures starting December 1, 2024. These measures included freezing public sector wages and pensions, removing tax exemptions, and reforming the microenterprise tax regime, aimed at saving 2.0% of GDP in 2025. The government targeted a reduction of the budget deficit to 7.0% of GDP for 2025, with a gradual path toward sustainability through 2031.

The recommendation highlighted the need for Romania to address underlying structural weaknesses, including a widened current account deficit of 8.4% of GDP in 2024, regulatory uncertainty hindering investment, and delayed implementation of [[Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)]] reforms. The Council urged Romania to ensure the full implementation of the MTP, enhance fiscal credibility, and pursue structural reforms to support growth and competitiveness, particularly in the context of Romania's recent accession to the [[Schengen Area]] on January 1, 2025.
