# Belgium submits Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (2025-2029)

## Event Details

On **March 18, 2025**, Belgium submitted its **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** covering the period 2025–2029 to the European Commission. This submission was part of the country's compliance with the reformed EU Economic Governance Framework (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263). The plan addresses Belgium's deteriorating public finances, characterized by a general government deficit of 4.5% of GDP in 2024 and a projected rise to 5.4% in 2025, alongside a public debt ratio of 104.7% of GDP (2024).

### Key Plan Components & Commission Assessment

The MTP utilizes the **National Escape Clause** to accommodate a temporary deviation from net expenditure limits, specifically to finance increased **defence spending** (projected to rise to 1.7% of GDP in 2025) and investment needs. This request was formally made on April 30, 2025. On **May 21, 2025**, the European Commission adopted a recommendation endorsing the plan, positively assessing its alignment with new governance rules despite the deviation. The endorsement was contingent on Belgium committing to a seven-year fiscal adjustment period supported by structural reforms.

### Structural Reforms Included in the Plan

The endorsed plan outlines several critical structural reforms aimed at improving long-term fiscal sustainability and competitiveness:

*   **Pension Reform**: Implementation of a bonus-malus system and reduction of assimilated periods to mitigate the impact of demographic aging. Age-related expenditure is projected to rise by 5.1 percentage points of GDP by 2070 without intervention.
*   **Labour Market Activation**: Measures to increase participation rates, including limiting unemployment benefits to two years for workers under 55 and linking benefits to income, addressing the low activity rate (77.2% vs EU average 83.3%).
*   **Tax Reform**: Plans to reduce the high tax wedge on labour by cutting labour taxes, financed through increases in capital gains and property taxes.
*   **Spending Reviews**: Commitments to review and control expenditure growth, particularly in rigid areas such as pensions and long-term care.

### Contextual Challenges

The submission occurred against a backdrop of slowing economic growth (1% in 2024, projected 0.8% in 2025) and significant fiscal pressures. The Belgian fiscal framework faces institutional weaknesses, including poor coordination between federal and regional entities and an under-resourced High Council of Finance. Despite these challenges, the MTP represents a pivotal step in Belgium's effort to stabilize its debt trajectory, which is projected to reach 126.4% of GDP by 2035 without policy action. The European Commission published its detailed assessment in the **2025 Country Report - Belgium** on June 4, 2025, highlighting the critical nature of these reforms for maintaining fiscal sustainability and addressing competitiveness losses driven by high labour costs and weak productivity growth.
