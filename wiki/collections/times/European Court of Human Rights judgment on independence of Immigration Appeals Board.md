# European Court of Human Rights judgment on independence of Immigration Appeals Board

## Event Details

On **October 22, 2024**, the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) issued a significant judgment regarding the independence of Malta's Immigration Appeals Board. This decision is highlighted in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta**, published by the European Commission on July 8, 2025.

The judgment is cited as a key indicator of ongoing challenges in Malta's justice sector and adherence to international human rights standards. According to the report, by mid-2025, the number of pending leading ECtHR judgments in Malta had increased to 16, with the Immigration Appeals Board case remaining among the critical instances requiring implementation and legislative alignment.

This event underscores broader concerns regarding:
*   **Judicial Independence**: The need to ensure that administrative bodies, such as the Immigration Appeals Board, operate with sufficient independence from the executive branch, consistent with ECtHR standards.
*   **Rule of Law Compliance**: The pending implementation of such judgments raises questions about institutional accountability and the effectiveness of checks and balances in Malta.
*   **Administrative Justice Efficiency**: The case contributes to the wider context of administrative justice reforms, where delays in proceedings and backlog of cases have been identified as critical issues affecting legal certainty.

The judgment remains a focal point for civil society organizations and international bodies monitoring Malta's progress in strengthening its rule of law framework, particularly in areas concerning immigration, asylum, and the independence of adjudicatory bodies.
