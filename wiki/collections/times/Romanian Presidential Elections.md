# Romanian Presidential Elections

## Event Details

The **2025 Romanian Presidential Elections** occurred in May 2025, marking a pivotal moment for the country's political and economic trajectory. The elections led to the formation of a **New Coalition Government** in June 2025, which immediately prioritized addressing significant macroeconomic imbalances.

Key outcomes and subsequent developments linked to this political transition include:

*   **Fiscal Consolidation Package (July 2025):** To counteract a fiscal deficit that rose to 8.7 percent of GDP in 2024, largely due to costly pension reforms and public wage increases, the new government adopted a comprehensive fiscal consolidation package. This included **VAT Rate Hike** and **Excise Increases**, implemented in August 2025. The goal was to reduce the fiscal deficit to below 6 percent of GDP in 2026 and eventually below 3 percent by 2031.
*   **Sovereign Credit Risks:** The IMF highlighted that political uncertainty ahead of and following the elections, along with implementation delays in fiscal consolidation, posed threats to **Debt Sustainability**. Despite these risks, Romania maintained an **Investment-grade sovereign credit rating**, though a downgrade remained a concern due to high current fiscal deficits and slow absorption of **EU Funds**.
*   **Macroeconomic Stability:** The transition period saw continued focus on **Monetary Policy** by the National Bank of Romania, which maintained a cautious stance with policy rates at 6.5 percent. Inflation spiked to 9.9 percent in September 2025 due to the removal of energy price caps and VAT increases, expected to normalize by mid-2026.
*   **Institutional Strength:** The new administration committed to **Structural Reforms** to improve administrative capacity, strengthen **Sovereign Credit Rating** prospects, and unlock **NGEU-funded investment**. Key figures involved in this period included Prime Minister **Ilie Bolojan**, Finance Minister **Alexandru Nazare**, and Central Bank Governor **Mugur Isarescu**.

The elections effectively triggered a series of policy shifts aimed at restoring **Macroeconomic Stability** and ensuring long-term fiscal sustainability amidst regional geopolitical uncertainties.
