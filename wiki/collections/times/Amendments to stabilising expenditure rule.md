# Amendments to stabilising expenditure rule

## Event Details

In July 2024, Poland enacted legislative amendments to its stabilising expenditure rule, a key component of its fiscal framework aimed at ensuring long-term budgetary sustainability. This reform was part of a broader strategy to address fiscal pressures, including a general government deficit that reached 5.3% of GDP in 2023, which exceeded the European Union's 3% reference value.

The amendments were implemented following the enactment of legislation establishing a [[Fiscal Council]] in December 2024, which was set to become fully operational in January 2026. The stabilising expenditure rule serves as a binding constraint on government spending, designed to support Poland's medium-term fiscal-structural plan. This plan aims to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2028 and includes a binding expenditure path endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]].

By amending the stabilising expenditure rule, Poland sought to enhance fiscal discipline and predictability while managing competing priorities such as high defence spending and investment needs. The reform contributes to the country's compliance with [[EU fiscal rules]] and supports its efforts to exit the excessive deficit procedure initiated by the Council in January 2025.
