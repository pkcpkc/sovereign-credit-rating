# Conclusion of 2025 Article IV Consultation

## Event Details

On 18 March 2025, the [[International Monetary Fund]] concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with Belgium. This event marked the formal end of the staff mission and the release of the staff report and press release, focusing on the country's economic resilience and structural challenges.

The consultation highlighted that while Belgium’s economy remains resilient to recent shocks, it faces significant fiscal pressures, with public debt projected to reach 123 percent of GDP by 2030 under unchanged policies. Key drivers include aging-related spending and elevated interest costs. The general government deficit had widened to 4.4 percent of GDP in 2024, but the IMF endorsed the new government’s commitment to a seven-year fiscal adjustment under the [[European Union]] Economic Governance Framework. This plan aims to durably reduce the deficit below 3 percent of GDP and place debt on a solidly downward path.

Key recommendations from the conclusion include rationalizing current spending, particularly social benefits and the public wage bill, while preserving public investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education. The report emphasized the need for structural reforms to boost productivity and labor force participation, including changes to the automatic wage indexation mechanism. Financial sector risks were assessed as moderate, with robust bank capitalization, though exposure to commercial real estate requires monitoring. The successful formation of a new federal government in February 2025 was noted as a critical step in overcoming previous political fragmentation and stalled policymaking.
