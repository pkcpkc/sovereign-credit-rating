# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report (Austria Chapter)

## Event Details

On July 8, 2025, the European Commission published the 2025 Rule of Law Report focusing on Austria. The report provides a comprehensive analysis of the country's judicial independence, anti-corruption measures, and media freedom.

**Key Developments Leading to and Following the Report:**
*   **July 2024:** An investigative commission released a final report regarding prosecutorial influence, highlighting systemic issues in the legal framework.
*   **March 2025:** The Austrian Government officially committed to a new programme to establish an independent Federal Prosecution Office as a collegiate body. This remains a primary focus for legislative drafting to reduce the Minister of Justice's power to instruct individual cases.
*   **June 2025:** Following a Constitutional Court ruling, the ORF Act was reformed to increase the independence of public service media management bodies. Additionally, by this date, the number of pending leading judgments from the European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) had decreased to four.
*   **July 8, 2025 (Report Publication):** The report highlights that while 86% of the public perceives judicial independence as "fairly or very good," significant progress is needed regarding the appointment of administrative court presidents and the disclosure of assets for Members of Parliament. It also notes the successful operation of web-based whistleblower reporting channels established in 2023.
*   **September 2025 (Projected):** The Freedom of Information Act is expected to enter into force, granting citizens a subjective right to information from public authorities.

The report underscores a transition period for Austria, balancing high public confidence in the judiciary with necessary structural reforms in lobbying transparency and media economic stability.
