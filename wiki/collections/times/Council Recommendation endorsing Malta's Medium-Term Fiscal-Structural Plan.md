# Council Recommendation endorsing Malta's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan

## Event Details

On **January 21, 2025**, the Council adopted a Recommendation endorsing Malta's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan. This endorsement forms a critical component of the [[European Semester]] process, specifically addressing the requirements of the revised [[Stability and Growth Pact]].

### Context and Rationale
The Council's endorsement came amidst a period of strong but complex economic performance for Malta. Following a real GDP growth of 6.0% in 2024, the economy was projected to maintain robust expansion at 4.1% in 2025 and 4.0% in 2026. The fiscal framework was necessary to address two primary divergences:

1.  **Net Expenditure Deviation**: Malta’s cumulative net expenditure growth exceeded the Council's reference value. This deviation was primarily driven by significant capital expenditures (including those related to the national airline) and substantial energy subsidies implemented during the period.
2.  **Structural Reform Needs**: While short-term growth was strong, long-term sustainability was threatened by low investment in [[Research and Innovation]] (0.64% of GDP) and high regulatory burdens.

### Key Fiscal Parameters Endorsed
The endorsed plan outlines a trajectory for fiscal consolidation:
*   **Deficit Reduction**: The general government deficit is projected to decline from 3.7% of GDP in 2024 to 2.8% in 2026.
*   **Debt Stability**: With a government debt-to-GDP ratio of 47.4% in 2024 (well below the 60% treaty reference), the plan aims to stabilize debt levels below 50% by 2026.
*   **Revenue Enhancement**: The strategy relies on improved tax collection efforts to address a significant VAT compliance gap (25.9% vs. EU average of 7%) and to mitigate reliance on corporate income tax, which constitutes a disproportionately high share of total tax revenue.

### Strategic Priorities and Challenges
The Council Recommendation highlights several structural priorities that the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan must address to ensure long-term viability:

*   **Long-Term Fiscal Sustainability**: The plan must account for severe long-term pressures from an aging population. Age-related expenditures (pensions, healthcare, and long-term care) are projected to rise from 17% of GDP in 2024 to 26% by 2070, representing the second-largest increase in the EU.
*   **Investment in Innovation and Digital Transition**: To combat low R&D intensity, the plan supports reforms via the [[Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF)]] and [[Cohesion Policy]], including the establishment of [[Science Malta]] (Xjenza Malta) to enhance institutional strength in research.
*   **Labor Market and Social Fairness**: The plan addresses severe labor shortages and skills mismatches by promoting inclusion, particularly targeting the gender employment gap (13.4 percentage points) and improving conditions for non-EU nationals.
*   **Green Transition**: Despite a low renewable energy share in electricity (17% in 2024), the plan commits to reducing fossil fuel subsidies (which accounted for 1.6% of GDP) and accelerating the deployment of renewable energy and circular economy measures to meet 2030 and 2050 climate targets.

### Institutional Oversight
The implementation of this plan is monitored under the [[European Semester]] framework. The [[Malta Fiscal Advisory Council]] (MFAC) plays a key role in providing independent advice, although the [[European Commission]] noted concerns regarding its independence and effectiveness in prior assessments. The [[Central Bank of Malta]] continues to operate within the [[ECB]] framework, ensuring monetary policy stability while the government executes fiscal adjustments.

### Implications for Sovereign Credit
The endorsement strengthens Malta's policy credibility by providing a clear fiscal trajectory and demonstrating commitment to EU strategic priorities. It mitigates risks associated with high net expenditure growth and reinforces the country's strong [[External Liquidity]] position, characterized by a net international investment position of over 100% of GDP. However, continued vigilance is required regarding corruption perceptions, judicial efficiency, and the effective execution of the [[Recovery and Resilience Plan]] to sustain credit ratings and investor confidence.
