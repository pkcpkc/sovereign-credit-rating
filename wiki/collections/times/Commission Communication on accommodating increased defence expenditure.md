# Commission Communication on accommodating increased defence expenditure

## Event Details

On **March 19, 2025**, the **European Commission** published a Communication detailing mechanisms within the reformed **EU Economic Governance Framework** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263) to accommodate member states' increased defence expenditure. This guidance established the operational framework for the **National Escape Clause**, allowing countries to deviate from standard net expenditure limits without triggering immediate fiscal sanctions, provided such spending is essential for national security and EU defence capabilities.

The practical application of this Communication was realized shortly thereafter in **Belgium**:

*   **April 30, 2025**: Belgium formally requested the activation of the **National Escape Clause** to finance its increased defence budget, citing security imperatives. This request aligned with the Commission's March guidance.
*   **Contextual Background**: At the time of the request, Belgium was facing severe fiscal pressure, with a projected deficit of 5.4% of GDP in 2025 and public debt at 104.7% of GDP. Despite this, the country sought to increase defence spending to 1.7% of GDP.
*   **Regulatory Alignment**: Belgium simultaneously submitted its **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (2025–2029)** on March 18, 2025, which incorporated the defence spending increase under the escape clause provisions outlined in the Commission's Communication.
*   **Commission Endorsement**: On **May 21, 2025**, the Commission adopted a Recommendation endorsing Belgium’s medium-term fiscal-structural plan, effectively validating the use of the escape clause for defence purposes as compliant with the new EU fiscal rules.

The Commission Communication thus served as the critical regulatory bridge allowing Belgium to balance its high debt trajectory and structural fiscal weaknesses (such as ageing costs and low productivity) with its geopolitical commitment to increased defence spending, without breaching EU fiscal discipline rules.
