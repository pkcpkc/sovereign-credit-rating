# Publication of Monetary Authorities Rating Methodology

## Event Details

On **September 11, 2013**, **[[S&P Global Ratings]]** published the "Monetary Authorities Rating Methodology," establishing a standardized framework for assessing the creditworthiness of monetary authorities, including central banks and monetary unions. This methodology was designed to evaluate the ability and willingness of these entities to service their financial obligations to nonofficial (commercial) creditors.

The 2013 publication serves as a foundational component of S&P's broader sovereign rating framework. It was later integrated into the updated **[[S&P Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)]]**, effective December 18, 2017. The 2017 update retained the core principles of the monetary authority assessment while refining the overall sovereign rating structure, particularly regarding the interaction between sovereign ratings and monetary authority ratings within monetary unions (e.g., the European Central Bank).

Key aspects of the methodology include:
*   **Integration with Sovereign Ratings**: For monetary authorities of monetary unions, ratings are determined by a weighted average of member sovereign ratings, adjusted by the monetary assessment of the authority itself.
*   **Monetary Assessment Pillar**: The framework evaluates monetary policy credibility, exchange rate regimes, and institutional transparency as part of the five-pillar sovereign assessment (Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, and Monetary).
*   **Rating Uplifts**: The methodology allows for potential rating uplifts for monetary authorities, ranging from zero to four notches above the sovereign rating, depending on the monetary flexibility and credibility of the central bank or union.

This publication followed the "Principles of Credit Ratings" (2011) and preceded the major overhaul of the sovereign methodology in 2017, which further clarified the distinction between local and foreign currency rating caps and adjusted fiscal metrics to focus on net debt.
