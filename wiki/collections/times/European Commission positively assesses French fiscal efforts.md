# European Commission positively assesses French fiscal efforts

## Event Details

On **2025-06-04**, the [[European Commission]] issued a positive assessment of France's fiscal efforts, marking a significant milestone in the country's compliance with EU fiscal rules. This endorsement came amidst a broader context of economic uncertainty and rising sovereign debt, validating the French government's commitment to fiscal consolidation under the [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan]] (MTFSP).

### Context and Preceding Events
The assessment followed a period of domestic political negotiation and fiscal adjustment:
*   **2025-02**: A political compromise was reached on the 2025 budget, signaling a shift towards fiscal responsibility.
*   **2025-03**: The French government published an action plan to enhance public finance execution, aiming to address past fiscal slippages.
*   **2024-12**: [[Moody's]] had previously downgraded France's credit rating to Aa3, highlighting concerns over rising debt levels (113.1% of GDP in 2024) and fiscal execution risks.

### Key Elements of the Assessment
The European Commission's positive view was based on France's demonstrated capacity for fiscal adjustment and its roadmap to reduce the general government deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. Key factors influencing this decision included:
1.  **Fiscal Consolidation Strategy**: France's commitment to a frontloaded structural fiscal effort, prioritizing spending rationalization over tax increases, given the already high tax-to-GDP ratio.
2.  **Political Consensus**: The broad political support built around the fiscal plans under EU rules, reducing the risk of policy reversal.
3.  **Debt Sustainability Path**: The alignment of the MTFSP with EU requirements to place public debt on a credible downward trajectory in the medium term.

### Implications and Next Steps
This assessment provided credibility to France's fiscal stance, supporting its sovereign credit outlook and market confidence. It set the stage for subsequent international reviews, including the [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV consultation, where staff discussions concluded in May 2025 and the Executive Board concluded its review in July 2025. The IMF also emphasized the need for the "well-designed, credible package of fiscal measures" that the European Commission had begun to recognize.

The positive assessment reinforced the importance of implementing structural reforms to boost productivity and addressing local government fiscal oversight to ensure long-term sustainability.
