# Constitutional Court Ruling on Unconstitutional Restriction of Legal Aid

## Event Details

In October 2025, the [[Constitutional Court]] of Austria issued a landmark ruling declaring that statutory restrictions on legal aid in administrative proceedings were unconstitutional. This decision addresses a significant barrier to access to justice, particularly in the context of recent judicial reforms and increased court fees.

The ruling was a response to systemic challenges identified in the 2025 Rule of Law Report, where high court fees—marked by an approximate 23% average increase effective April 1, 2025—were cited as a major impediment to fair judicial processes. The Constitutional Court found that limiting legal aid in these specific administrative contexts violated constitutional guarantees regarding the right to effective legal protection.

Following the October 2025 decision, suspensions of the previously restricted legal aid provisions were mandated to take effect by March 2026. This timeline allows for necessary legislative adjustments and administrative preparations to ensure the justice system can accommodate the restored access to legal representation without compromising judicial efficiency. The decision is part of broader efforts to balance the "Justice 3.0" digitalization initiatives with substantive access-to-justice reforms, aiming to maintain high public perception of judicial independence (currently rated at 86% by the general public) while addressing structural vulnerabilities in the administrative court system.
