# Approval of comprehensive fiscal consolidation package (VAT, CIT, and financial transaction tax).

## Event Details

In **October 2024**, the Slovak government approved a comprehensive fiscal consolidation package aimed at addressing persistent budget imbalances and high structural deficits. The package included increases in **Value Added Tax (VAT)**, **Corporate Income Tax (CIT)**, and the introduction of a **financial transaction tax**. This decision was made in the context of a recorded economic growth of 2.1% and a fiscal deficit of 5.3% of GDP in 2024, which remained above the EU excessive deficit procedure threshold.

The consolidation measures were part of the broader **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)** for Slovakia, which was subsequently endorsed by the **Council of the European Union** in **January 2025**. The European Commission's **2025 Country Report** highlighted that while the consolidation package was a necessary step to stabilize public finances, the structural deficit remained high due to permanent spending increases in areas such as pensions, healthcare, and military salaries.

The implementation of the VAT reform contributed to rising inflation pressures, with inflation expected to reach 4.0% in 2025. Despite the consolidation efforts, the European Commission projected that the general government deficit would remain elevated at 4.9% in 2025 and rise to 5.1% in 2026, with public debt projected to reach 63.0% of GDP by 2026. The package was thus seen as an initial phase of fiscal adjustment rather than a complete solution to Slovakia's long-term sustainability challenges, which are further compounded by demographic ageing and rigid budget composition.
