# IMF Staff Report Completion for 2025 Article IV Consultation

## Event Details

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) completed its 2025 Article IV Consultations with Hungary and Italy, marking significant milestones in the surveillance of these member states' economic health. These completions occurred in mid-2025, following intense policy discussions and staff assessments that highlighted divergent economic trajectories despite shared challenges regarding global uncertainty and inflation.

### Hungary: A Challenging Juncture

For [[Hungary]], the IMF staff report was completed on **July 25, 2025**, following a mission that concluded discussions in Budapest on June 17, 2025. The consultation characterized the Hungarian economy as being at a "challenging juncture," noting that output had stagnated over the past three years with GDP contracting in early 2025. Inflation remained persistently above the Magyar Nemzeti Bank’s (MNB) 3 percent target, driven by core services inflation and external price pressures.

Key findings from the Hungarian consultation included:
*   **Fiscal Adjustment Necessity**: The IMF recommended additional fiscal measures to meet budget targets, with a cumulative adjustment projected for 2026-2027 to bring the deficit below 3 percent of GDP. Current policies were projected to yield a higher deficit (4.5-4.9 percent of GDP).
*   **Debt Sustainability Risks**: Public debt was projected to rise to 79 percent of GDP by 2030, with risks stemming from high interest expenses and significant contingent liabilities from State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs).
*   **Monetary Policy**: The MNB was advised to maintain a tight policy stance, keeping the base rate at 6.5 percent into 2026 to anchor inflation expectations, while phasing out distortive price and margin controls.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Critical recommendations focused on boosting productivity, enhancing energy security, and improving governance to unlock suspended EU funds (€19 billion) amidst rule of law concerns.

The Hungarian authorities acknowledged the challenges but expressed confidence in their Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) and recent investments in electric vehicle (EV) and battery production.

### Italy: Resilience Amid Structural Challenges

For [[Italy]], the IMF staff report was completed on **July 1, 2025**, following policy discussions that concluded with the authorities on May 28, 2025, and the Executive Board's final conclusion on July 18, 2025. The consultation highlighted Italy's notable economic resilience, with real GDP growing by 0.7 percent in 2024, supported by National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) investments.

Key findings from the Italian consultation included:
*   **Fiscal Overperformance**: Italy achieved a primary surplus of 0.4 percent of GDP in 2024, better than projected. The IMF recommended reaching a 3 percent primary surplus by 2027 to ensure a declining debt trajectory.
*   **Structural Challenges**: Persistent issues included low productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-EU average female labor force participation.
*   **Banking Sector Resilience**: The banking sector showed record profits and sound asset quality, though vulnerabilities among less significant institutions remained.
*   **External Sector**: Italy's current account turned to a surplus in 2024, supported by lower energy imports and robust tourism, strengthening its Net International Investment Position.

Both consultations underscored the importance of sustained fiscal consolidation and structural reforms to address long-term growth constraints, whether through governance improvements in Hungary or productivity enhancements in Italy.
