# Act adopted on amicable settlements of cases to reduce backlogs

## Event Details

On December 19, 2024, Belgium adopted an act focused on facilitating amicable settlements of cases as a strategic measure to reduce judicial backlogs. This legislative development occurred within a broader context of significant challenges facing the Belgian justice system, particularly regarding the excessive length of proceedings and structural resource deficiencies.

The adoption of this act aligns with ongoing efforts by the [[Belgian Government]] and the [[High Council for Justice]] to improve judicial efficiency, which remains a critical concern identified in the [[2025 Rule of Law Report]]. Specifically, the [[Court of Appeal of Brussels]] has been highlighted as having severe backlogs, with the [[High Council for Justice]] publishing reports in late 2024 to address these delays. The push for alternative dispute resolution mechanisms through amicable settlements is part of the government's response to recommendations from the [[Belgian Court of Audit]] and the [[European Commission]] regarding the need for faster case processing and better management of judicial resources.

This measure is seen as a step toward mitigating the impact of long-standing efficiency issues, although it operates alongside other initiatives such as the digitalisation of justice and the transfer of budgetary management powers to the judiciary. The act reflects the government's intent to address the structural bottleneck of case processing times, which affects both the public and private sectors, as noted by the relatively high percentage of companies expressing confidence in investment protection but facing delays.
