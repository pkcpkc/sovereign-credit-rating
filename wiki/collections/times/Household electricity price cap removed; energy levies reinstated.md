# Household electricity price cap removed; energy levies reinstated

## Event Details

On **January 1, 2025**, the Austrian government removed the household electricity price cap and reinstated energy levies, marking a significant shift in the country's approach to energy market stabilization following the period of energy price shocks. This policy adjustment was part of a broader fiscal consolidation strategy undertaken by the new coalition government (ÖVP-SPÖ-NEOS), which took office in March 2025 after the September 2024 elections.

According to the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation**, this removal of support measures contributed to inflationary pressures in 2025. Headline inflation averaged 3.2% in 2025, exceeding the Euro Area average, largely due to core services inflation and these one-off energy price effects. The IMF noted that while inflation remained elevated, it was projected to converge toward the 2% target by end-2026, contingent on continued disinflationary fiscal policies and wage moderation.

The reinstatement of levies and removal of caps were aimed at reducing the fiscal deficit, which had widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, pushing public debt to 81.2%. The IMF emphasized that while near-term consolidation measures were announced for 2025-2026, substantial further fiscal adjustment would be required over the medium term to stabilize the debt ratio against rising pressures from demographic aging, defense spending, and the green transition. The policy change reflected the government's commitment to fiscal consolidation, albeit within a challenging economic environment characterized by two successive years of recession (2023 and 2024) and weak growth prospects for 2025 (-0.1%).
