# Publication of the 2025 Country Report on Hungary

## Event Details

On **2025-06-04**, the [[European Commission]] published the **2025 Country Report on Hungary** (SWD(2025) 217 final), outlining a challenging economic landscape characterized by subdued growth, persistent inflationary pressures, and significant structural vulnerabilities. The report covers data up to 2024 and provides forecasts for 2025-2026, highlighting that while GDP growth is projected to rebound slowly in 2026—driven by new manufacturing capacities in the electric vehicle sector—the immediate outlook remains uncertain due to weak external demand and domestic policy uncertainty.

### Key Findings and Economic Assessment

*   **Fiscal Position and Debt Sustainability**: The report identifies high medium-term fiscal sustainability risks. Public debt rose to **73.5% of GDP in 2024**, with elevated debt-servicing costs due to implicit interest rates close to 6%. Although the government deficit decreased from 6.7% in 2023 to **4.9% in 2024**, it is projected to remain elevated at 4.6% in 2025 and 4.7% in 2026. Net expenditure growth in 2025 is projected at **6.1%**, exceeding EU recommendations, though partially justified by defense spending under the national escape clause. The Commission criticized the lack of a realistic medium-term budgetary framework.
*   **Growth and Inflation**: GDP growth was sluggish at **0.5% in 2024**, following a contraction of 0.8% in 2023. Forecasts indicate growth of **0.8% in 2025** and a pickup to **2.5% in 2026**, primarily driven by EV battery manufacturing. Inflation declined significantly from a peak of 17.0% in 2023 to **3.7% in 2024**, but picked up in early 2025 due to underlying price pressures and rising food costs.
*   **Structural Vulnerabilities**: Labour productivity has stagnated for 15 years, remaining among the lowest in the EU. This is driven by low-skilled employment increases rather than innovation. The business environment is described as distorted by heavy state intervention, including subsidized lending (38.5% of new SME loans), administratively controlled mortgage rates, and high sector-specific taxes. Regulatory volatility and the frequent use of emergency decrees hinder long-term business planning and investment.
*   **Energy Security**: Hungary remains heavily dependent on Russian fossil fuels (>70% of gas, >80% of oil). While solar power generation surged to 25% of electricity production, the grid lacks flexibility and storage. Progress on wind energy is stalled, and fossil fuel subsidies remain high at 1.01% of GDP.
*   **Social Challenges**: The at-risk-of-poverty or social exclusion (AROPE) rate increased to **20.2% in 2024**, with rising poverty among children and vulnerable groups. Social protection adequacy is low, and healthcare spending remains among the lowest in the EU at 4.1% of GDP.

### Institutional and Political Context

The report highlights concerns regarding **policy credibility and institutional strength**. Weak anti-corruption frameworks and high perceived corruption (78% of businesses) limit trust. The use of "state of danger" emergency powers limits parliamentary oversight and increases political unpredictability. Furthermore, the independence and capacity of the [[Hungarian Fiscal Council]] are noted as limited, and opaque public fund management structures lack effective oversight.

### EU Funds and Rule of Law

Implementation of the **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)** has stalled, with **0% of milestones achieved as of mid-2025**. Delays are attributed to anti-corruption deficiencies and weak control mechanisms. Access to Cohesion Policy funds is also limited by unfulfilled enabling conditions related to the rule of law, including concerns over judicial independence and beneficial ownership transparency.
