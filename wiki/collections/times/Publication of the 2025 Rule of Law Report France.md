# Publication of the 2025 Rule of Law Report: France

## Event Details

On 8 July 2025, the [[European Commission]] published the 2025 Rule of Law Report: France Country Chapter (SWD(2025) 910 final), a staff working document assessing the state of the rule of law in [[France]]. The report highlights significant progress in judicial independence, anti-corruption frameworks, and media transparency, while noting persistent challenges in judicial efficiency, legislative consultation, and the implementation of [[European Court of Human Rights]] judgments.

Key developments assessed in the report include the ongoing implementation of the 20 November 2023 law reforming the status of magistrates, which introduced measures such as the repeal of age limits for admissions and 360° evaluations for court presidents. The [[Superior Council of the Magistracy]] has actively defended judicial independence against public attacks on judges, particularly those involving election-related decisions.

The justice system is undergoing extensive digitalization, with the [[Portalis]] tool being deployed across various jurisdictions, including labor courts, and online access to court decisions expanding. However, judicial efficiency remains a concern, with average resolution times for civil and commercial cases remaining high (354 days in 2023) and particularly long for bribery cases (1,335 days).

In the anti-corruption sphere, France has strengthened its framework through the reorganization of the [[Anti-Corruption Agency]] (AFA) and increased resources for the [[High Authority for Transparency in Public Life]] (HATVP). New legislative tools, including Law Warsmann No. 582 (2024), have been passed to improve corruption prosecution and asset recovery. However, disclosure requirements for lobbyists still do not apply to top executive officials.

Media pluralism and freedom have seen notable improvements, particularly in media ownership transparency, with the preparation of a single national database listing direct or indirect owners and ultimate beneficial owners. The proposed reform of public broadcasting governance, involving the creation of a holding company, [[France Médias]], to oversee public broadcasters (including [[France Télévisions]], [[Radio France]], and [[INA]]), has sparked debate regarding editorial independence and triggered strikes.

Regarding checks and balances, legislative simplification efforts are welcomed by businesses, but stakeholders criticize the use of accelerated legislative procedures (used in 27 out of 39 draft bills in 2024), which contributes to legal uncertainty. Citizen consultation processes, such as the [[Agora]] application, have been interrupted since June 2024.

France continues to address a backlog of pending [[European Court of Human Rights]] judgments, with 26 leading judgments pending implementation as of 1 January 2025. The implementation rate for leading judgments from the past 10 years was 66%, with an average pending time of 3 years.

The civic space in France is considered narrowed due to budgetary restrictions impacting civil society organizations, though the financial environment remains generally favorable. Reports of intimidation and attacks against human rights defenders continue.
