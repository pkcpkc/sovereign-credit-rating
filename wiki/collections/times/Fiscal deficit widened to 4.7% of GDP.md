# Fiscal deficit widened to 4.7% of GDP

## Event Details

In 2024, Austria's fiscal deficit widened significantly to 4.7% of GDP, driven by weak economic activity, lagged inflation effects, and one-off expenditures. This deterioration occurred against the backdrop of a second successive year of recession, with Real GDP falling by 1.3% in 2024 following a 0.9% contraction in 2023. The economic downturn was primarily triggered by high energy prices, rapid interest rate hikes, and weakened domestic and external demand. Consequently, public debt rose to 81% of GDP, prompting the International Monetary Fund (IMF) to recommend a bold fiscal consolidation package. This package includes pension reforms, limits on public-sector wage increases, and increased reliance on property, inheritance, and excise taxes to put the debt ratio on a declining path. The deficit expansion highlighted challenges in maintaining [[Fiscal Policy]] stability amidst [[Macroeconomic Stability]] pressures and rising spending needs related to defense, the green transition, and demographic aging.
