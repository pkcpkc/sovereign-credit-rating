# Successive year of recession (Real GDP fell by 1.3%)

## Event Details

In 2024, the Austrian economy experienced its second successive year of recession, with Real GDP falling by 1.3%. This contraction followed a 0.9% decline in 2023, marking two consecutive years of economic downturn. The recession was primarily driven by the lingering effects of the energy price shock, rapid monetary tightening, and heightened global trade uncertainty.

The economic downturn had significant fiscal implications, causing the fiscal deficit to widen sharply to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, up from previous levels due to weak economic activity, lagged inflation effects, and one-off expenditures. Consequently, public debt rose to 81% of GDP. The IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation highlighted these challenges, noting that the combination of high energy prices, interest rate hikes, and weak domestic and external demand suppressed growth.

Despite the 2024 recession, the IMF projected a recovery beginning in 2026, driven by the unwinding of household savings, monetary easing, and fiscal expansion in key trading partners like Germany. However, medium-term growth prospects remain constrained by demographic aging, sluggish productivity growth, and rising spending pressures from defense commitments, the green transition, and an aging population.
