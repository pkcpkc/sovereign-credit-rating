# Submission of France's medium-term fiscal-structural plan

## Event Details

On **October 31, 2024**, France submitted its medium-term fiscal-structural plan to the [[European Commission]], marking a critical step in its engagement with the reformed EU economic governance framework. This submission occurred against a backdrop of significant economic and political challenges. The fragmented political landscape following the 2024 general elections had led to unprecedented delays in budget adoption and reduced fiscal ambitions, complicating the implementation of necessary structural reforms.

At the time of submission, France was facing a deteriorating fiscal position, with a general government deficit of 5.8% of GDP in 2024 and public debt rising to 113% of GDP. The plan outlined a seven-year fiscal adjustment period aimed at ensuring fiscal sustainability and addressing high fiscal sustainability risks, including aging costs and rising interest payments. The submission was intended to replace the previous excessive deficit procedure, with the goal of restoring fiscal stability through robust spending reviews and targeted reforms.

The [[Council of the European Union]] subsequently endorsed the plan on **January 21, 2025**, and issued a recommendation to end the excessive deficit situation in France. The Commission’s 2025 Country Report emphasized that strict adherence to this endorsed plan and effective spending reviews were necessary to address the projected slight deficit reduction in 2025 and to mitigate the risks associated with high public expenditure and debt burden. The plan also sought to support structural reforms to boost potential growth, address labor market weaknesses, and improve competitiveness, while ensuring that fiscal consolidation efforts did not undermine essential investments in the green and digital transitions.
