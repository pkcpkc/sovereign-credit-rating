# IMF Executive Board Concludes 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy

## Event Details

On July 18, 2025, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) Executive Board completed its 2025 [[Article IV Consultation]] with [[Italy]]. The consultation process involved policy discussions with Italian authorities, which concluded on May 28, 2025, followed by the completion of the [[IMF]] staff report on July 1, 2025. The final conclusion was announced by [[Helge Berger]], Head of the European Department, and [[Rishi Goyal]], Head of the Strategic Policy Unit.

The IMF Executive Board commended [[Italy]]'s economic resilience and strong fiscal performance in 2024, noting a return to a primary surplus of 0.4 percent of GDP driven by improved tax compliance and robust labor market conditions, including a record-high employment rate. Real GDP growth reached 0.7 percent in 2024, supported by investments from the [[National Recovery and Resilience Plan]] (NRRP) and net exports. However, the Board emphasized the urgency of accelerating structural reforms to address persistent challenges, including sluggish productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-EU average female labor force participation.

Key recommendations from the consultation include reaching a primary surplus of 3 percent of GDP by 2027 through measures such as improving tax compliance, rationalizing tax expenditures, and streamlining pension-related spending. The [[IMF]] stressed that full implementation of the NRRP, combined with comprehensive structural reforms focusing on productivity, labor supply, and the green transition, is essential to durably lift growth and offset demographic headwinds.

Fiscal risks remain significant, with public debt-to-GDP ratio standing at 135.3 percent. While the banking sector has strengthened with sound asset quality and adequate capital buffers, vulnerabilities among less significant institutions and the sovereign-bank nexus require close monitoring. Downside risks include escalating trade tensions, regional conflicts, and macro-critical climate-related shocks. The report highlights the importance of maintaining medium-term fiscal-structural plan alignment with EU fiscal frameworks to ensure a clear downward trajectory for public debt.
