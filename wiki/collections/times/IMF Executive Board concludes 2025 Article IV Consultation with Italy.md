# IMF Executive Board concludes 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy

## Event Details

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) Executive Board concluded the 2025 Article IV Consultation with Italy on **July 18, 2025**. The subsequent Press Release and Staff Report were published on **July 22, 2025**, following discussions with Italian officials that took place from **May 14 to May 28, 2025**. The Staff Report itself was completed on **July 1, 2025**.

The consultation, led by IMF Staff Team Head [[Lone Christiansen]], with Department Heads [[Helge Berger]] and [[Rishi Goyal]], and reviewed by Executive Director for Italy [[Riccardo Ercoli]], assessed Italy’s economic and financial landscape amidst a backdrop of global uncertainty.

### Economic Performance and Outlook
Italy’s economy demonstrated notable resilience in the early part of 2025, with real GDP growing by 0.7% in 2024, supported by investments from the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) and net exports. Employment reached record-high levels. The IMF projects growth to moderate to 0.5% in 2025 before recovering to 0.8% in 2026. Headline inflation, which averaged 1.1% in 2024, is expected to average 1.7% in 2025 and converge to the European Central Bank’s 2% target in 2026. Downside risks include global trade tensions, geopolitical conflicts, and potential delays in NRRP implementation.

### Fiscal Policy and Debt Sustainability
In 2024, Italy achieved a fiscal deficit of 3.4% of GDP and returned to a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP, outperforming earlier projections. However, public debt remained high at 135.3% of GDP at the end of 2024.
*   **IMF Recommendation:** The staff advised a more front-loaded fiscal adjustment to reach a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027, aiming to place public debt on a firm downward trajectory.
*   **Authorities' Position:** The Italian authorities, including [[Giancarlo Giorgetti]], Minister of Economy and Finance, committed to a gradual seven-year fiscal adjustment under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP). They target a deficit below 3% by 2026 and dispute the need for additional immediate measures, emphasizing structural reforms to boost growth instead.
*   **Sovereign Risk:** The IMF assessed sovereign debt risk as moderate overall but high in the medium to long term due to aging-related costs and mechanical fiscal indicators.

### Structural Reforms and Productivity
Key structural challenges persist, including weak productivity growth, rapid population aging, and below-EU-average female labor force participation.
*   **NRRP Implementation:** The implementation of the NRRP has accelerated, with 54% of milestones achieved by mid-2025, although spending execution remains sluggish. The deadline for meeting NRRP milestones and targets is **June 30, 2026**.
*   **Reform Agenda:** The IMF stressed the need for post-NRRP reforms to boost human capital, improve the business climate, and increase R&D investment, which remains low at 1.3% of GDP.

### Financial Sector Stability
The Italian banking sector showed improved soundness in 2024, characterized by high profitability, sound asset quality, and low Non-Performing Loans (NPLs) of 2.7%. Capital buffers are adequate, and the Systemic Risk Buffer (SyRB) was increased to 1%, with full implementation required by **June 30, 2025**.
*   **Vulnerabilities:** Concerns remain regarding the sovereign-bank nexus, which, while reduced, is still sizable. Vulnerabilities persist among some Less Significant Institutions (LSIs). The IMF called for continued vigilance on loan quality, particularly for firms exposed to trade shocks.
*   **AML/CFT:** The IMF recommended strengthening the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) framework and addressing transnational aspects of corruption.

### External Sector
Italy posted a current account surplus of 1.1% of GDP in 2024, driven by lower energy imports and strong exports. The Net International Investment Position (NIIP) improved to a positive 15.3% of GDP.
*   **Assessment:** The IMF assessed Italy’s external position as weaker than warranted by fundamentals, recommending further fiscal consolidation and productivity-boosting reforms. The Italian authorities disagreed, viewing the position as broadly in line with fundamentals.

### Climate and Energy Security
Italy faces significant climate risks due to its reliance on imported energy and exposure to extreme weather. The government is advancing the "Mattei Plan" to secure clean energy sources. The IMF emphasized accelerating the transition to renewables and enhancing climate adaptation measures to meet 2030 targets.
