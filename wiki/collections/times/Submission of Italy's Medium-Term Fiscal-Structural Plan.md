# Submission of Italy's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan

## Event Details

**Date:** October 15, 2024

On October 15, 2024, the Italian government submitted its **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP)** to the **European Commission**. This submission marked a critical milestone in Italy's engagement with the reformed **Stability and Growth Pact**, outlining a seven-year fiscal adjustment path designed to ensure long-term debt sustainability and align national fiscal policy with EU guidelines.

### Key Objectives and Content
The MTFSP was developed in the context of Italy's high public debt levels, which were projected to rise to 136.7% of GDP in 2025 due to lagged borrowing needs from previous tax credits and high debt service costs. The plan aims to:
*   Establish a credible path toward debt reduction through disciplined net expenditure growth.
*   Address structural vulnerabilities, including sluggish productivity growth, low R&D investment, and judicial inefficiencies.
*   Support the implementation of structural reforms backed by the **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRP)**.

### Outcome and Endorsement
Following the submission, the European Council reviewed the plan and issued a **Council Recommendation** on **January 21, 2025**. This recommendation:
1.  Formally endorsed Italy's MTFSP.
2.  Concluded the **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)** against Italy, signaling recognition of the country's commitment to fiscal consolidation.
3.  Highlighted Italy's return to a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP in 2024, driven largely by the phase-out of the "Superbonus" housing renovation tax credits.

### Broader Economic Context
The submission occurred against a backdrop of moderate economic growth (0.7% real GDP growth in 2024) and stabilizing inflation. While the fiscal framework provided a stable anchor, the **European Commission's 2025 Country Report** noted persistent challenges, including:
*   **Regional Disparities:** Significant economic divergence between the North and the South (Mezzogiorno).
*   **Structural Bottlenecks:** Judicial inefficiency and high perceived corruption rates hindering investment.
*   **Demographic Pressures:** Rising pension costs due to an aging population, threatening long-term fiscal sustainability.

The MTFSP serves as the primary instrument for Italy's fiscal surveillance under the new EU rules, with the **Parliamentary Budget Office (PBO)** playing a key role in monitoring the accuracy of budgetary forecasts and the implementation of the plan.
