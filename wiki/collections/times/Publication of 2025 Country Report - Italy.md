# Publication of 2025 Country Report - Italy

## Event Details

The **[[European Commission]]** published its 2025 Country Report on Italy on **2025-06-04**, providing a comprehensive assessment of the country's economic, social, and structural policies. The report highlights a mixed economic landscape characterized by moderate growth and improved fiscal indicators, yet overshadowed by persistent structural vulnerabilities and high public debt.

### Economic Performance and Outlook
- **GDP Growth**: Italy's real GDP growth stabilized at **0.7% in 2024**, driven by investment financed through the [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] (RRP) and net external demand. Private consumption remained subdued as households focused on rebuilding savings. Growth is expected to remain moderate in 2025-2026 due to global trade uncertainties and weak productivity.
- **Inflation**: Inflation is projected to stabilize around the [[European Central Bank]]'s 2% target in 2025-2026, supported by normalizing energy prices and moderate wage growth, which helps preserve competitiveness.

### Fiscal Dynamics and Debt Sustainability
- **Primary Surplus**: Italy achieved a **primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP in 2024**, the first since 2019. This improvement was primarily driven by the phase-out of the "Superbonus" housing renovation tax credits and energy support measures, rather than structural revenue gains.
- **Debt Trajectory**: Despite the primary surplus, the **public debt-to-GDP ratio is projected to rise to 136.7% in 2025**. This increase is attributed to lagged borrowing needs from past tax credits, high debt service costs (interest expenditure approx. 3.9% of GDP), and the interest-growth differential.
- **Policy Framework**: The [[Council of the European Union]] endorsed Italy's **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP)** in January 2025, formally ending the [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]]. The MTFSP outlines a seven-year fiscal adjustment path aimed at ensuring long-term debt sustainability.

### Structural Challenges and Reforms
- **Productivity and Investment**: Italy faces sluggish productivity growth due to insufficient R&D investment (private R&D is half the EU average), limited venture capital markets, and low tertiary education attainment.
- **Judicial System**: While backlogs are decreasing under RRP reforms, the justice system remains a bottleneck for investment, with trial durations among the longest in the EU. Corruption is perceived as a significant obstacle by 50% of businesses.
- **Labor Market**: Employment rates reached record highs, but labor force participation remains low, particularly among women (57.4% vs. 70.8% EU average) due to inadequate childcare and long-term care services. Skills mismatches are acute, especially in ICT and green sectors.
- **Regional Disparities**: Significant divergence persists between the North and the South ([[Mezzogiorno]]), with the latter lagging in GDP per capita, infrastructure, and service delivery.

### Financial Sector and External Position
- **Banking Sector**: The banking sector has strengthened resilience, with capital adequacy ratios at 20% and non-performing loans declining to 2.8%. However, banks remain heavily exposed to domestic sovereign debt, creating potential "doom loop" risks.
- **External Position**: Italy maintains a positive Net International Investment Position (NIIP) of approx. 12% of GDP. However, it relies on foreign capital inflows to finance deficits, and high gross external assets/liabilities create volatility risks.

### Green Transition and Energy
- **Clean Tech Leadership**: Italy is a leader in clean technology manufacturing, particularly in solar PV and wind.
- **Vulnerabilities**: The country faces high wholesale electricity prices due to a gas-heavy energy mix and significant taxes/levies. It is also highly vulnerable to climate risks (floods, droughts) and depends heavily on imports for critical raw materials and energy.
