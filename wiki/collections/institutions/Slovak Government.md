# Slovak Government

## Mandate & Role

The Slovak Government serves as the executive branch of Slovakia, led by Prime Minister [[Robert Fico]]. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk, the Government is responsible for fiscal policy, legislative initiation, and the implementation of international obligations. The 2025 Rule of Law Report highlights that the Government's conduct significantly impacts Slovakia's geopolitical stability, policy credibility, and accountability frameworks.

Key areas of influence and risk include:
*   **Legislative Process:** The Government frequently utilizes fast-track legislative procedures, with 28% of laws in 2024 adopted this way, often bypassing stakeholder consultation and reducing policymaking stability.
*   **Rule of Law & Judicial Independence:** The Government faces criticism for low perceived judicial independence (32% among the public) and for not establishing sufficient safeguards for the dismissal of [[Judicial Council (Slovakia)]] members. Amendments to judges' laws introduced by the administration have raised concerns about potential political influence on the judiciary.
*   **Anti-Corruption Framework:** The Government oversaw the dissolution of specialized anti-corruption entities, including the [[National Crime Agency (NAKA)]] and the Special Prosecution Office (SPO), leading to operational inefficiencies and a drop in investigated corruption cases. The Prosecutor General’s broad power to annul final decisions remains a critical accountability threat.
*   **Media Freedom:** The Government enacted the Act on [[Slovak Television and Radio (STVR)]], introducing a governance structure that raises concerns about the politicization of public service media. Additionally, amendments to the Freedom of Information Act, driven by the executive, introduced fees and longer response times, facing constitutional challenges.
*   **International Obligations:** The Government demonstrates weak enforcement of international judicial decisions, with the number of pending leading judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] rising from 31 to 33 between January and June 2025.

The Government engages in ongoing dialogue with the [[European Commission]] to address rule of law issues, and while it has aligned criminal codes with the EU PIF Directive, significant risks remain regarding institutional checks and balances and civil society space.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **2025 Rule of Law Report Engagement:** Subject of the [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Rule of Law Report, which noted limited to no progress in addressing key rule of law concerns. The Government is involved in intensive dialogue with the EU to resolve these issues.
*   **Legislative Initiatives (2024-2025):**
    *   Adopted amendments to Criminal Codes (2024) to align with the EU PIF Directive, later adjusted in December 2024.
    *   Enacted the Act on Slovak Television and Radio (STVR) in July 2024.
    *   Adopted laws amending NGO functioning acts in April 2024, increasing reporting obligations and pressure on civil society.
    *   Introduced amendments to the Freedom of Information Act entering into force in March 2025.
    *   Proposed constitutional amendments in April 2025, raising concerns about the primacy of EU law, which were postponed after the first reading.
*   **Anti-Corruption Strategy:** The Government is preparing a new National Anti-Corruption Strategy for 2024-2029 following the closure of specialized investigative bodies.
*   **Whistleblower Compliance:** In October 2024, the [[Office for the Protection of Whistleblowers]] fined the Ministry of Interior (under the Government) for procedural violations, highlighting accountability mechanisms in action.
