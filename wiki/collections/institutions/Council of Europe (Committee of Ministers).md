# Council of Europe (Committee of Ministers)

## Mandate & Role

The Council of Europe (Committee of Ministers) serves as the decision-making body of the Council of Europe, responsible for supervising the execution of judgments and decisions of the [[European Court of Human Rights]] (ECtHR). In the context of sovereign credit rating and country risk analysis, the Committee of Ministers plays a critical role in monitoring member states' compliance with international human rights standards and rule of law obligations. Its supervision of structural issues identified by the ECtHR serves as an indicator of institutional accountability and policy credibility. The Committee's involvement signals potential governance risks when member states, such as Belgium, face prolonged delays or insufficient action in implementing leading judgments.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Supervision of ECtHR Judgments**: The Committee of Ministers actively supervises the implementation of ECtHR judgments. As of January 1, 2025, Belgium had 17 leading judgments pending implementation, with the Government remaining under supervision for structural issues identified in the *Camara v. Belgium* case. This ongoing supervision highlights risks related to the rule of law and compliance with final court rulings.
*   **Rule of Law Monitoring**: The Committee works in tandem with other bodies like [[GRECO]] (Group of States against Corruption) to assess anti-corruption frameworks and integrity policies. The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report references the Committee’s role in overseeing structural compliance issues, noting that limited progress in addressing ECtHR-identified problems constitutes a threat to policy credibility.
*   **Advisory Frameworks**: The Committee contributes to the broader European human rights protection system, influencing standards related to access to public documents and civic space, including the Convention on Access to Official Documents, which Belgium has signed but not yet ratified.
