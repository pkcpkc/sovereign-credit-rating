# European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO)

## Mandate & Role

The European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO) is a decentralized European Union agency responsible for investigating, prosecuting, and bringing to judgment crimes against the European Union's financial interests. In the context of sovereign credit risk and country analysis, the EPPO serves as a critical component of the anti-corruption framework and judicial independence infrastructure. It operates alongside national bodies such as the French National Financial Prosecutor (PNF) and the Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) to combat financial crimes, including fraud and corruption related to EU funds, thereby supporting institutional strength and policy credibility.

In member states like Slovakia, the EPPO functions within a complex national anti-corruption architecture. While the EPPO provides an independent EU-level mechanism for protecting financial interests, its national counterparts and cooperating bodies face significant challenges. For instance, in Slovakia, the dissolution of specialized national anti-corruption entities (NAKA and SPO) has weakened domestic capacity, making the EPPO's role as a counterbalance to national institutional weaknesses increasingly pertinent for assessing overall accountability and rule of law safeguards.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Cooperation with National Authorities**: The EPPO works in conjunction with national financial prosecutors, such as the PNF in France, to investigate complex financial crimes. Its effectiveness contributes to the broader assessment of a country's anti-corruption framework and the protection of public assets.
- **Sovereign Credit Relevance**: The presence and operational effectiveness of the EPPO are considered strength factors in evaluating the financial sector health and accountability of member states. It supports the recovery of misused public funds and enhances confidence in investment protection by law.
- **Institutional Context**: Listed as a key institution in the 2025 Rule of Law Report on France, highlighting its role in the EU's broader justice and anti-corruption architecture.
- **Context of National Anti-Corruption Frameworks (Slovakia)**: In the context of the 2025 Rule of Law Report on Slovakia, the EPPO is identified as a key institution within the anti-corruption framework. Its relevance is heightened by the fragmentation of Slovakia’s national anti-corruption efforts, including the closure of specialized entities like the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Bureau for Combating Crime (UBOK). The EPPO serves as a reference point for effective independent investigation, contrasting with domestic issues such as the broad powers of the Prosecutor General and low perceived judicial independence.
