# National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC)

## Mandate & Role

The National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) is the primary Italian government body responsible for overseeing the integrity framework and implementing anti-corruption measures within the public administration. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, ANAC serves as a critical institution for assessing policy credibility and institutional strength. Its effectiveness directly influences Italy's risk profile regarding public procurement integrity, regulatory transparency, and the rule of law. ANAC operates within the broader Italian institutional checks and balances framework, working to mitigate corruption risks that could otherwise impact economic stability and investment protection.

## Key Activities & Reports

**2025 Developments and Strategic Updates:**
*   **National Anti-Corruption Plan (NACP):** ANAC updated the National Anti-Corruption Plan in January 2025. This update included the issuance of new guidelines specifically tailored for small municipalities, aiming to extend anti-corruption standards to the local level of government.
*   **Integrity Guidelines:** The Authority issued specific guidelines addressing "revolving doors" (the movement of individuals between public and private sectors) and strengthened whistleblowing protections. These measures aim to enhance accountability and reduce conflicts of interest in public service.
*   **Resource Strengthening:** Recent assessments note that ANAC has strengthened its human resources, improving its operational capacity to monitor and enforce anti-corruption regulations.

**Contextual Risk Factors and Legislative Environment:**
*   **Public Procurement Risks:** Despite ANAC's efforts, public procurement remains identified as a high-risk area for corruption in Italy. This is a persistent threat factor in sovereign credit risk assessments, indicating that while institutional frameworks exist, enforcement and systemic vulnerabilities remain significant.
*   **Legislative Gaps:** ANAC operates in a landscape with unresolved legislative challenges, particularly regarding the lack of progress in establishing an operational lobbying register and regulating political party financing. The absence of these transparency mechanisms limits the overall effectiveness of the anti-corruption framework, even as ANAC strengthens its specific mandates.
*   **Judicial Context:** ANAC's work is intersected by broader judicial developments, including the Constitutional Court's validation (May 2025) of the law abrogating the offence of "abuse of public office." This legislative change impacts the legal toolkit available for addressing administrative misconduct, a domain where ANAC's preventive guidelines play a compensatory role.

**Stakeholder and International Oversight:**
*   ANAC's performance is monitored in international rule of law reports, such as the European Commission's Rule of Law Report, which cites its activities as a strength in Italy's accountability framework.
*   The Authority's work is relevant to international anti-corruption bodies such as [[GRECO]] (Group of States Against Corruption) and [[Transparency International]], which evaluate Italy's compliance with international standards.
