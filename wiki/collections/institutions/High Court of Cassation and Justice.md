# High Court of Cassation and Justice

## Mandate & Role

The **High Court of Cassation and Justice** (HCCJ) is the highest court in Romania, serving as a critical component of the country's justice system and a focal point for rule of law assessments within the **European Union**. Its primary mandate involves ensuring the uniform application of the law and overseeing judicial discipline. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk, the HCCJ plays a pivotal role in maintaining **judicial independence**, combating **corruption**, and upholding the **rule of law**.

According to the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Romania**, the HCCJ is central to the assessment of **institutional strength** and **policy credibility**. The court's actions directly impact the effectiveness of the **anti-corruption framework**, particularly through its interpretation of criminal procedures such as the **statute of limitations**. While the broader justice system has made progress in implementing recommendations from the **Venice Commission** regarding the **Justice Laws**, the HCCJ's recent jurisprudence has introduced systemic risks related to impunity and compliance with **EU law**, affecting investor confidence and **legislative quality** perceptions.

## Key Activities & Reports

The HCCJ's recent activities have significantly influenced the legal landscape in Romania, particularly concerning high-level corruption cases and procedural law:

- **Interpretation of *Lex Mitior* and Statute of Limitations**: The HCCJ issued critical decisions (e.g., Decision 37/2024 and Decision 16/2024) interpreting the principle of *lex mitior* (retroactive application of more lenient laws) to the statute of limitations. These rulings have led to the discontinuation of numerous corruption cases and the annulment of convictions, raising serious concerns about **impunity** and the effectiveness of the **National Anti-Corruption Directorate** (DNA).
- **Referral to Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU)**: In April 2025, the HCCJ sought clarification from the CJEU (Case C-280/25 *Lin II*) regarding the compatibility of its case-law on the statute of limitations with **EU law**. This referral highlights ongoing tensions between national judicial interpretations and European legal standards.
- **Judicial Independence and Transparency**: The court's role is closely monitored regarding the transparency of disciplinary sanctions and promotions. While the Ministry of Justice has shown openness to IT system reforms to guarantee **judicial independence**, the perception of the HCCJ's independence remains average among the public and businesses.
- **Compliance with Venice Commission Recommendations**: The HCCJ operates within a framework where legislative steps are being explored to extend mandates of high-ranking prosecutors, aligning with efforts to strengthen safeguards following the implementation of the **Justice Laws** and **Venice Commission** recommendations.
