# European Investment Bank

## Mandate & Role

The European Investment Bank (EIB) serves as the European Union's long-term lending institution, supporting EU policy objectives through financing investment projects. In the context of sovereign credit rating and risk surveillance, the EIB is recognized as a key institutional actor within the EU's economic governance framework. Its role is particularly significant in supporting member states through the absorption of EU funds, such as the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF), and facilitating structural reforms and the green transition. The EIB’s involvement is cited as a strength in maintaining institutional strength and supporting external balances through well-capitalized financial sector interactions.

While the specific EIB lending operations are not detailed in the provided summary, the institution is explicitly referenced as a core entity within the [[Slovakia]] national institutional landscape and the broader EU economic governance system. Its activities are intertwined with:

- **External Balance Support**: The EIB contributes to stabilizing external balances in member states like Slovakia by providing access to EU funding alongside cohesion funds.
- **Institutional Monitoring**: As part of the EU's institutional framework, the EIB operates alongside the [[European Commission]], [[European Central Bank]], and the [[Council of the European Union]] to ensure compliance with EU legal frameworks regarding fiscal and structural reforms.
