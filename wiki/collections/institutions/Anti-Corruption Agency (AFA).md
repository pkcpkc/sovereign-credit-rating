# Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)

## Mandate & Role

The Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) is an independent public authority in France responsible for preventing, detecting, and sanctioning corruption, influence peddling, conflicts of interest, and illicit enrichment among public agents and private entities. It plays a critical role in the French anti-corruption framework and is recognized by international bodies, including the European Commission, as a key institution contributing to policy credibility and institutional strength. The AFA’s work supports sovereign credit profiles by enhancing governance quality, ensuring accountability, and mitigating corruption risks in public procurement and large-scale projects.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Institutional Reorganization**: In the 2024–2025 period, the AFA underwent structural reorganization, dividing its operations into distinct departments for the public and private sectors to enhance specialized oversight.
*   **National Anti-Corruption Plan**: The agency is central to the implementation of the new 2024–2029 National Anti-Corruption Plan, expected to be adopted in summer 2025. This plan aims to strengthen institutional responses to integrity risks and improve consistency in prosecution.
*   **Compliance Oversight**: The AFA continues to exercise oversight over entities subject to anti-corruption compliance obligations, working in conjunction with the High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP) and the National Financial Prosecutor (PNF).
*   **Resource Allocation**: While the agency has seen strengthened institutional capacity, broader budgetary constraints within the justice and anti-corruption sector remain a potential threat to its operational efficiency.
