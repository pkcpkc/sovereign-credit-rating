# International Monetary Fund (IMF)

## Mandate & Role

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) serves as a critical reference institution in the global financial architecture, particularly regarding sovereign credit risk and economic surveillance. While the IMF itself does not issue commercial credit ratings, its methodologies and assessments are foundational to the frameworks used by major rating agencies. Specifically, S&P Global Ratings integrates IMF-related concepts into its Sovereign Rating Methodology, utilizing the five-pillar framework (Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, and Monetary) to assess a sovereign's ability and willingness to service financial obligations. The IMF's focus on debt sustainability and economic resilience directly influences how rating agencies evaluate fiscal assessments and external liquidity.

Through mechanisms such as Article IV Consultations and the Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP), the IMF provides detailed country-specific analyses that inform global market perceptions of sovereign risk. These assessments cover key areas including fiscal consolidation, public debt sustainability, structural reforms, productivity growth, and financial stability. The IMF’s work on debt sustainability underpins the fiscal assessment pillars used by rating agencies, including the analysis of net general government debt and contingent liabilities from the banking sector. The Fund also emphasizes the shift towards productivity-driven, socially, and environmentally sustainable development models, particularly in small open economies facing capacity constraints.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Influence on Rating Methodologies**: The IMF's analytical frameworks inform key components of the S&P Sovereign Rating Methodology, particularly in the areas of institutional assessment (policymaking effectiveness, transparency) and fiscal assessment (debt sustainability, contingent liabilities).
- **Debt Sustainability Analysis**: The IMF’s work on debt sustainability underpins the fiscal assessment pillars used by rating agencies, including the analysis of net general government debt and contingent liabilities from the banking sector.
- **Monetary and External Assessment**: Concepts central to IMF surveillance, such as monetary policy credibility, exchange rate regimes, and external liquidity, are explicitly scored in sovereign rating methodologies (e.g., the shift to using Current Account Payments in external ratios reflects broader macroeconomic assessments aligned with IMF standards).
- **Article IV Consultation: France (2025)**: The **2025 Article IV Consultation with France** highlighted an economy demonstrating resilience despite high public debt (113.1% of GDP) and fiscal slippages. The report emphasized the need for credible, frontloaded fiscal consolidation under the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. It also addressed risks from geoeconomic fragmentation, weak productivity growth, and local government fiscal oversight.
- **Article IV Consultation: Poland (2024/2025)**: The **2024 Article IV Consultation with Poland** (released Jan 2025) assessed an economy recovering robustly driven by domestic demand and Next Generation EU (NGEU) funds, but facing significant fiscal pressures. Key findings included:
    - **Fiscal Deficits & EDP**: The general government deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024, triggering the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP). Public debt is projected to rise to ~65% of GDP by 2029.
    - **Consolidation Strategy**: The IMF supported the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) aiming for a deficit of 2.9% of GDP by 2028, but recommended frontloading consolidation in 2025 to support disinflation.
    - **Monetary Policy**: The IMF commended the National Bank of Poland’s (NBP) prudent stance, urging continued tightness until wage growth decelerates, with rate cuts potentially starting mid-2025.
    - **Financial Sector**: The banking sector is well-capitalized, though risks persist from CHF mortgage litigation and WIBOR disputes. The IMF recommended eliminating distortions like the bank asset tax that favors public debt.
    - **Structural Challenges**: Long-term growth is clouded by population aging and the need for climate transition reforms. Refugee integration (approx. 976,000 Ukrainians) has positively impacted the labor market.
- **Article IV Consultation: Malta (2024)**: The **2024 Article IV Consultation with Malta** highlighted the country’s remarkable economic performance driven by export-oriented services (tourism, online gaming), while warning of capacity constraints in infrastructure and labor markets. Key recommendations included:
    - **Fiscal Consolidation**: Phasing out broad fixed-energy price policies (which account for 20% of the fiscal deficit) in favor of targeted subsidies to redirect resources toward investment and innovation.
    - **Financial Stability**: Addressing substantial real estate exposure (70% of bank loans) by tightening macroprudential policies, specifically raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer (sSyRB).
    - **Structural Reforms**: Developing a clear roadmap for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reform aligned with the EU’s Pillar II directive and enhancing productivity through educational and digital initiatives.
    - **Governance**: Continuing to strengthen the Anti-Money Laundering/Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) framework and advancing judicial reforms.
- **Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP)**: The IMF conducts FSAPs to identify potential vulnerabilities in the financial sector.
    - **France (2025)**: Confirmed the banking sector's resilience with high capital ratios (19.8%) and strong liquidity, noting risks from non-bank financial intermediaries.
    - **Malta (2024 Context)**: Highlighted robust banking capitalization (Tier 1 ratio ~21%) and strong liquidity buffers, but flagged risks from real estate concentration and cybersecurity.
