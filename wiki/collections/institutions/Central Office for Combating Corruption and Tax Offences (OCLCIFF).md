# Central Office for Combating Corruption and Tax Offences (OCLCIFF)

## Mandate & Role

The Central Office for Combating Corruption and Tax Offences (OCLCIFF) is a specialized judicial investigation unit within the French justice system, tasked with combating corruption and tax offenses. It operates as a key component of France's anti-corruption framework, working alongside other independent and credible regulatory bodies such as the Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) and the High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP). The OCLCIFF contributes to the country's policy credibility and institutional strength by enforcing legal guarantees against financial crimes, although its effectiveness is subject to broader systemic challenges, including resource constraints and high case resolution times.

## Key Activities & Reports

The OCLCIFF functions within a justice system that faces significant efficiency challenges, particularly regarding the duration of case resolutions. While France maintains a relatively low corruption perception score, the OCLCIFF's work is impacted by the high average duration for resolving bribery cases, which stood at 1,335 days as of the 2025 reporting period. This inefficiency has prompted government-led "flash missions" to propose solutions for improving judicial efficiency. The office operates in conjunction with the National Financial Prosecutor (PNF), which is described as independent and effective in combating financial crimes. The OCLCIFF's activities are part of the broader institutional response to corruption and integrity risks, supporting the implementation of the 2024-2029 National Anti-Corruption Plan. Despite these efforts, budgetary restrictions impacting justice and anti-corruption bodies remain a threat to optimal performance.
