# Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)

## Mandate & Role

The Constitutional Tribunal is Poland's supreme constitutional court, tasked with reviewing the conformity of statutes and other normative acts with the Constitution, international treaties, and national law. In the context of sovereign credit risk and rule of law analysis, the Tribunal serves as a critical check on executive power and a guarantor of legal certainty. However, its operational legitimacy and authority have been severely compromised by political polarization and government resistance, creating a persistent "constitutional crisis."

As of 2025, the Tribunal faces significant threats to its institutional strength and credibility due to the government's refusal to publish its judgments, stemming from disputes over the legitimacy of judges appointed by the reformed National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) after 2017. This conflict represents a major geopolitical and political stability threat, as it undermines the separation of powers and triggers ongoing infringement procedures by the European Commission and scrutiny from the Court of Justice of the European Union. The Tribunal's inability to enforce its rulings effectively contributes to low confidence in investment protection and legislative instability among international investors.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Legislative Reform and Status**: Amendments to the laws governing the Constitutional Tribunal and the NCJ were adopted by Parliament in 2025 but remained pending before the Constitutional Tribunal itself due to a presidential referral. These reforms aim to resolve the dispute over judge appointments but have not yet entered into force.
- **Judgment Publication Dispute**: The Polish government continues to refuse to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, asserting that the body lacks legitimacy due to the disputed appointment mechanisms. This refusal is a central element of the ongoing rule of law crisis and hinders legal transparency.
- **EU Infringement Procedures**: The Tribunal is the subject of active infringement proceedings by the European Commission, highlighting the tension between domestic constitutional authorities and EU institutions. An Advocate General opinion regarding the infringement case was issued in March 2025.
- **Institutional Leadership**: The President of the Constitutional Tribunal is identified as Bogdan Święczkowski, a figure closely associated with the political appointments during the previous administration, further polarizing perceptions of the Tribunal's independence.
- **Pending Legal Challenges**: The Tribunal's pending review of the new NCJ and Constitutional Tribunal laws determines the immediate future of the judicial reform agenda, including the classification and potential removal of approximately 1,600 judges appointed by the post-2017 NCJ.
