# S&P Global Ratings

## Mandate & Role

S&P Global Ratings is a leading credit rating agency that provides standardized frameworks for assessing the creditworthiness of sovereign governments and monetary authorities. Its primary mandate involves evaluating the ability and willingness of entities to service financial obligations to nonofficial (commercial) creditors. The agency utilizes a comprehensive **Five-Pillar Assessment Framework** to determine sovereign credit ratings, integrating analyses of **Institutional Assessment** (policymaking effectiveness, debt payment culture), **Economic Assessment** (GDP per capita, growth prospects), **External Assessment** (liquidity, currency status), **Fiscal Assessment** (net debt burden, contingent liabilities), and **Monetary Assessment** (policy credibility).

A critical aspect of S&P's operational mandate is the integration of significant **analyst discretion** within its structured framework. While the methodology provides indicative ratings based on quantitative and qualitative pillars, it allows for "supplemental adjustment factors" (e.g., event risk, extreme liquidity weakness) and final rating overrides. This discretionary component has led to critiques regarding the **replicability of ratings**, prompting academic initiatives such as the "Citizen Science" project by [[Alex Stomper]] at [[HU Berlin]] to document discrepancies between the formal methodology and actual rating outcomes in European countries.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)**: Published on December 18, 2017, this methodology replaced the 2014 version and integrated the 2013 Monetary Authorities methodology. Key updates include:
  - Limiting the local-currency rating uplift to one notch above foreign-currency ratings.
  - Shifting the initial fiscal assessment metric to the change in *net* general government debt (gross debt minus liquid financial assets) as a percentage of GDP.
  - Refining external metrics by using Current Account Payments (CAP) in specific ratios and updating classifications for reserve and actively traded currencies.
  - Incorporating **Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment (BICRA)** to analyze contingent liabilities from the banking sector.
- **Monetary Authority Ratings**: For monetary unions (e.g., ECB), ratings are derived from a weighted average of member sovereign ratings, adjusted for the monetary assessment of the authority.
- **Academic & Analytical Scrutiny**:
  - **Citizen Science Initiative (2024)**: Presented by [[Alex Stomper]] at [[HU Berlin]], this initiative applies the S&P 2017 methodology to European countries to test the replicability of sovereign credit ratings. It highlights how subjective elements like "cohesive civil society" and discretionary adjustments create opacity in the final rating determination.
  - **Detailed Pillar Analysis**: Academic overviews emphasize that **Fiscal Assessment** is the "heart" of the process, split into flows (flexibility) and stocks (debt burden), while **Institutional Assessment** caps ratings based on "debt payment culture" and external security risks.
