# IMF

## Mandate & Role

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) serves as a global organization focused on fostering international monetary cooperation, securing financial stability, and facilitating economic growth. A core component of its mandate is **Economic Surveillance**, primarily conducted through **[[IMF Article IV Consultation]]** processes. During these consultations, the IMF staff assesses member countries' economic and financial policies, macroeconomic stability, and institutional strength. The institution provides policy advice and assessments regarding fiscal sustainability, monetary policy credibility, debt sustainability analysis (DSA), and external sector vulnerabilities, often engaging in constructive dialogue with national authorities to guide structural reforms and fiscal adjustments.

The IMF also plays a critical data and analytical role for the global credit rating industry. Its datasets and methodologies are foundational for external assessments, particularly regarding **reserve-currency flexibility** (derived from IMF [[COFER]] data), which is a key variable in the **[[Fitch Ratings]]** Sovereign Rating Model (SRM). Furthermore, the IMF collaborates with rating agencies on macro-prudential and climate risk frameworks, such as the integration of **Climate Vulnerability Signals** and the **International Liquidity Ratio (ILR)**, ensuring that sovereign credit assessments reflect comprehensive external financing flexibility and shock vulnerability.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Hungary 2025 Article IV Consultation**: Completed the staff report and Executive Board assessment for Hungary in August 2025. Key findings highlighted stagnant output, elevated inflation, and public debt risks.
  - **Policy Recommendations**: Advocated for additional fiscal adjustment to ensure debt sustainability, recommending a structural primary surplus of ~1.75% of GDP. Advised maintaining a tight monetary stance into 2026 and phasing out distortive price and margin controls.
  - **Risk Assessment**: Identified "moderate" risk of sovereign stress, citing high gross financing needs and contingent liabilities from state-owned enterprises. Highlighted downside risks from deepening geoeconomic fragmentation and potential loss of €19 billion in suspended EU funds due to rule of law concerns.
  - **Methodologies Used**: Applied Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA), External Sector Assessment, Growth at Risk (GaR) Model, and Financial Soundness Indicators.

- **Slovak Republic 2025 Article IV Consultation**: Concluded by the IMF Executive Board in March 2025. The assessment focused on an economy recovering from headwinds but facing significant fiscal challenges, including a 5.8% GDP deficit in 2024 that triggered an Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) by the European Commission.
  - **Policy Recommendations**: Endorsed the Slovak government’s four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028) aimed at reducing the deficit to ~2.2% of GDP. Staff advised prioritizing expenditure-based savings (e.g., reducing subsidies, targeting social spending) over further revenue hikes. Recommended strengthening the macroprudential framework, including adopting a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer, and implementing structural reforms to diversify the economy away from automotive dependence.
  - **Risk Assessment**: Identified "moderate" overall risk of sovereign stress, but "high" long-term risk due to aging-related costs (projected to increase fiscal burdens by 5.6% of GDP by 2050). Key downside risks included trade policy uncertainty, geoeconomic fragmentation, and governance concerns potentially slowing EU fund disbursements. The banking sector was deemed resilient with strong capital buffers, though vulnerabilities persist in commercial real estate (CRE) and mortgage refixations.
  - **Methodologies Used**: Applied Article IV Consultation, External Sector Assessment (EBA-lite), Financial Stability Assessment Program (FSAP), and Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA).
