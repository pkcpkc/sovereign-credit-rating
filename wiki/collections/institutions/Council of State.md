# Council of State

## Mandate & Role

The **Council of State** is a supreme administrative court and judicial institution present in multiple European jurisdictions, notably **Belgium** and **France**, functioning as a critical component of the rule of law framework. In **Belgium**, it serves as one of the highest courts, performing judicial review and advisory functions to ensure administrative legality. In **France**, it acts as the highest administrative court, providing legal checks on executive power and contributing to the stability of the legal framework. Across these contexts, the Council of State is integral to the institutional checks and balances mechanisms evaluated by the **European Commission** in its **Rule of Law Reports**, supporting policy credibility and accountability by reviewing the legality of administrative actions and legislation.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Belgian Context**: The Council of State is a primary institution reviewed in the **European Commission**'s assessment of Belgium’s justice system. It operates alongside the **Constitutional Court** and the **Court of Cassation** to form the apex judicial structure. Its role is central to monitoring judicial independence and efficiency, although it operates within a system facing structural challenges such as resource deficiencies.
- **French Context**: As part of the **French** institutional landscape, the Council of State contributes to the strong legal framework for checks and balances. It operates in conjunction with other key bodies such as the **Constitutional Council** and the **Court of Cassation**. Its existence supports the "stable democratic institutions" cited as a strength in France's geopolitical and political stability.
- **Rule of Law Compliance**: In both countries, the Council of State's operations are part of the broader evaluation of judicial independence, efficiency, and the effectiveness of administrative justice in resolving disputes between citizens and the state.
