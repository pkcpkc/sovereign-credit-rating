# Constitutional Court (Italy)

## Mandate & Role

The Constitutional Court of Italy serves as the supreme arbiter of constitutional law, ensuring that legislation and governmental actions comply with the Italian Constitution. Within the context of sovereign credit risk and rule of law analysis, the Court plays a critical role in maintaining institutional checks and balances, upholding the separation of powers, and validating the legal framework surrounding anti-corruption measures and judicial integrity. Its decisions directly impact policy credibility and institutional strength by confirming or striking down laws that affect public administration, media freedom, and political financing.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Validation of Anti-Corruption Legal Changes**: In May 2025, the Constitutional Court declared the law abrogating the offence of "abuse of public office" compliant with the Constitution. This decision was noted as a strength in policy credibility, providing legal certainty to a significant change in criminal law affecting public officials.
- **Constitutional Review Context**: The Court operates within a broader judicial landscape undergoing significant reform, including the draft constitutional reform to separate the careers of judges and prosecutors (advanced in Parliament in January 2025). While the Court itself did not rule on this specific draft in the reported period, the judiciary's strong opposition highlights the Court's potential future role in adjudicating the constitutional boundaries of judicial autonomy and representation.
- **Institutional Checks and Balances**: The Court is a key entity referenced in assessments of Italy’s institutional strength. Its functioning contributes to the high perceived judicial independence among the general public and companies, a factor cited as a strength for geopolitical and political stability.
