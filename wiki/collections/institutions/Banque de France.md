# Banque de France

## Mandate & Role

The [[Banque de France]] serves as the national central bank of [[France]]. As a key institution within the [[Eurozone]], it operates under the monetary policy framework established by the [[European Central Bank]], ensuring price stability and contributing to macroeconomic stability. Its independence and robust liquidity position are cited as strengths in maintaining financial sector health and access to Eurozone liquidity.

During the [[IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation with France]], the Banque de France was recognized for its role in maintaining strong financial oversight and a robust macroprudential policy toolkit. The institution benefits from the general strength of the [[Eurozone]]'s monetary policy, which helps maintain low and stable inflation, a key factor in France's price stability profile. Under the leadership of Governor [[François Villeroy de Galhau]], the Banque de France works closely with the [[Ministry of Finance]] and the [[High Council for Financial Stability (HCSF)]] to monitor systemic risks, including those arising from geoeconomic fragmentation and cyber threats.

## Key Activities & Reports

The Banque de France is integral to France's implementation of the [[Reformed EU Economic Governance Framework]] and the execution of the [[medium-term fiscal-structural plan]]. Its operations are closely monitored in the context of the [[2025 Country Report - France]] and the [[IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation with France]].

Key activities and findings related to the Banque de France include:

*   **Financial Sector Assessment:** The 2025 [[Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP)]], conducted jointly by the [[IMF]] and the [[European Commission]], confirmed the resilience of the French banking sector. The Banque de France oversees banks that maintain healthy capital buffers (19.8% regulatory capital ratio) and strong liquidity coverage ratios.
*   **Risk Monitoring:** While the banking sector remains resilient, the Banque de France monitors specific threats such as weak bank profitability due to net interest margin compression, high corporate indebtedness, and rising bankruptcies among SMEs. The institution also plays a role in monitoring non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs) and cyber/climate risks.
*   **Monetary and Fiscal Coordination:** The Banque de France's role in maintaining financial stability supports the broader macroeconomic environment necessary for the [[High Council of Public Finance]] and the [[Cour des Comptes]] to oversee fiscal consolidation efforts. The institution's independence is a strength in the sovereign credit rating analysis, operating within a strong [[European Central Bank]] framework.
