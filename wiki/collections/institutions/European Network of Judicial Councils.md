# European Network of Judicial Councils

## Mandate & Role

The European Network of Judicial Councils (CEJIL) is an international body referenced in the context of European Union rule of law monitoring and sovereign credit risk assessment. Its primary role relates to upholding **judicial independence** and ensuring the proper functioning of **institutional checks and balances** within member states. In the context of sovereign credit ratings, the CEJIL serves as a benchmark or reference point for evaluating the autonomy and safeguards of national judicial bodies, such as the [[Judicial Council (Slovakia)]]. Effective operation and adherence to CEJIL standards are critical for maintaining the credibility of a country's legal framework, which directly impacts **policy credibility and institutional strength** factors in credit analysis.

## Key Activities & Reports

The CEJIL is monitored as part of the broader assessment of **rule of law safeguards** in EU member states, specifically highlighted in the [[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Slovakia]].

*   **Slovakia Context**: The network is cited in the evaluation of the **Slovak Judicial Council**. Key findings note a lack of progress in establishing sufficient safeguards for the dismissal of Judicial Council members, particularly those not elected by judges. This deficiency is viewed as a threat to **accountability** and **judicial independence**, contributing to low public perception of judicial independence (32%).
*   **Rule of Law Dialogue**: The CEJIL is part of the institutional landscape under scrutiny during the intensive dialogue between the [[European Commission]] and the [[Slovak Government]]. Issues regarding the expanded role of national Judicial Councils in disciplinary proceedings are assessed against European standards promoted by networks like CEJIL.
*   **Institutional Strength**: Failures to align national judicial council operations with CEJIL recommendations are categorized as threats to **policymaking stability** and **debt payment culture**, as they signal potential political interference in the justice system.
