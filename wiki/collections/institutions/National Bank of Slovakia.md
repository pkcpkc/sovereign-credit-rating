# National Bank of Slovakia

## Mandate & Role

The National Bank of Slovakia (NBS) serves as the central bank of Slovakia, operating within the Eurozone framework under the governance of the [[European Central Bank]]. While monetary policy is determined by the ECB, the NBS retains a critical mandate regarding robust supervisory and macroprudential independence. It plays a vital role in maintaining financial sector stability, overseeing a banking sector characterized by high capitalization and liquidity. The institution supports the broader economic environment by managing the implications of Euro currency status, eliminating exchange rate volatility, and ensuring price transparency. Recent assessments, including the [[IMF]] 2025 Article IV Consultation, highlight the NBS's strengthened supervisory powers and its role in anchoring medium-term inflation expectations, despite temporary inflation surges driven by fiscal policy.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Financial Sector Supervision**: The NBS oversees a well-capitalized and liquid banking sector. Key indicators monitored include a high Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio of 17.7%, low non-performing loans (1.9%), and strong liquidity coverage ratios (exceeding 194-200%). Stress tests, conducted in conjunction with the [[Financial Stability Assessment Program]], confirm the sector's resilience to severe shocks.
- **Macroprudential Risk Monitoring**: The NBS monitors specific vulnerabilities, including stress in the commercial real estate (CRE) sector—particularly the office segment—and the refixation risk associated with household mortgages in the coming years. The [[IMF]] has recommended that the NBS strengthen its macroprudential framework, including the adoption of a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer, to mitigate these risks.
- **Eurozone Integration & Policy Coordination**: As part of the Eurosystem, the NBS benefits from ECB liquidity backstops and contributes to the stability of the Eurozone member economy. The institution operates with high data adequacy and transparency (SDDS Plus), facilitating effective coordination with fiscal authorities during periods of [[Fiscal Consolidation]] and structural reform.
