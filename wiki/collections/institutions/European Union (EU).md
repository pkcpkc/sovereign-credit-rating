# European Union (EU)

## Mandate & Role

The European Union (EU) serves as a supranational organization overseeing macroeconomic governance and fiscal sustainability among its member states. A primary aspect of this role involves enforcing fiscal discipline through frameworks such as the **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)**, which is activated when member states' fiscal deficits exceed established thresholds (e.g., the 3% of GDP limit). The EU also facilitates economic recovery and structural transformation through financial instruments like the **Next Generation EU (NGEU)**, which provides grants and loans to support member states in post-pandemic recovery, digital transformation, and climate goals. Additionally, the EU sets broader fiscal rules and frameworks that member states must align with to ensure long-term fiscal sustainability and stability within the Union.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) Enforcement**: The EU monitored **Poland**'s fiscal trajectory and triggered the Excessive Deficit Procedure in 2024 after Poland's general government deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP. This action compelled Polish authorities to develop a **Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)** aimed at reducing the deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028.
- **Fiscal Framework Alignment**: The EU updated its fiscal framework, requiring member states to amend national stabilizing expenditure rules to align with new EU standards. Poland has taken steps to align its fiscal governance with these frameworks.
- **Next Generation EU (NGEU) Fund Allocation**: The EU provides significant funding through NGEU, which is a key driver of near-term economic growth in Poland. The absorption of these funds is projected to support GDP growth, although it also contributes to increased import demand and potential current account deficits in the medium term.
- **Sovereign Debt Standards**: The EU maintains benchmarks for public debt sustainability, with member states aiming to keep debt levels compatible with EU averages. Poland's public debt, while below the EU average, is projected to rise toward 65% of GDP, necessitating adherence to EU consolidation guidelines.
