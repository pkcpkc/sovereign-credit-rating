# Council of the European Union

## Mandate & Role

The **Council of the European Union** acts as a central pillar in the European Union's economic governance framework, specifically within the reformed EU Economic Governance Framework. Its primary role involves the endorsement of member states' fiscal plans and the issuance of recommendations regarding macroeconomic stability and fiscal sustainability. The Council works in conjunction with the [[European Commission]] to monitor compliance with fiscal rules, assess national medium-term fiscal-structural plans, and make decisions on Excessive Deficit Procedures (EDP). Its decisions directly impact sovereign credit ratings by signaling institutional strength, policy credibility, and the likely trajectory of fiscal consolidation for member states.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Endorsement of Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plans**: The Council plays a decisive role in validating national fiscal strategies.
    - **France**: On **2025-01-21**, the Council endorsed France's medium-term fiscal-structural plan, accepting a seven-year adjustment period for France to address its high public debt (113% of GDP in 2024) and deficit issues.
    - **Hungary**: On **2025-03-18**, the Council issued a Recommendation endorsing Hungary's medium-term fiscal-structural plan. Despite this endorsement, the European Commission noted in its 2025 Country Report that Hungary lacks a realistic medium-term budgetary framework and that the plan faces challenges from frequent use of emergency powers and weak policy credibility.
    - **Poland**: On **2025-01-21**, the Council endorsed Poland's medium-term fiscal-structural plan. This plan aims to reduce Poland's general government deficit, which reached 6.6% of GDP in 2024, below the 3% Treaty reference value by 2028. The endorsement came alongside the initiation of the excessive deficit procedure, acknowledging the complexity of Poland's fiscal adjustment due to rising defense expenditure (projected to reach 2.8% of GDP in 2025) and the use of the "national escape clause" flexibility.
    - **Slovakia**: On **2025-01**, the Council endorsed Slovakia's Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan. This endorsement provides a critical anchor for Slovakia's fiscal consolidation efforts, which are challenged by a high structural deficit (5.3% in 2024) and rising debt (projected to reach 63.0% of GDP by 2026). The endorsement supports Slovakia's commitment to multi-year fiscal planning despite significant institutional backsliding and rule of law concerns.
- **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) Recommendations**: The Council issues formal recommendations to member states to end excessive deficit situations.
    - **France**: On **2025-01-21**, it issued a recommendation to end the excessive deficit situation in France, contingent on strict adherence to the endorsed fiscal plan and effective spending reviews.
    - **Poland**: The Council initiated the excessive deficit procedure for Poland on **2025-01-21**, citing the high budget deficit and rising public debt (projected at 58.0% of GDP in 2025). The procedure requires Poland to submit an Annual Progress Report on fiscal plan implementation by **2025-04-30**.
    - **Slovakia**: Slovakia continues to face an excessive deficit situation. Despite a consolidation package approved in October 2024 (including VAT and CIT hikes), the general government deficit remained high at 5.3% of GDP in 2024. The Council's oversight remains critical as the deficit is projected to remain elevated at 4.9% in 2025 and rise to 5.1% in 2026 due to rigid spending on pensions and healthcare.
- **Extension of Reform Deadlines**: The Council manages the timeline for structural reforms. It extended the deadline for the implementation of reforms underpinning France's fiscal adjustment to **2025-04-30**, reflecting the political and administrative complexities faced by the member state.
- **Oversight of Institutional Integrity**: The Council, through the [[European Semester]] and coordination with the Commission, monitors rule of law conditions that impact economic stability. In Slovakia's case, the Council's framework enforces compliance despite severe governance deficits, including the dissolution of anti-corruption agencies and the Civil Service Council, which undermine policy credibility and transparency.
