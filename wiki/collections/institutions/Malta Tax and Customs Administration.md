# Malta Tax and Customs Administration

## Mandate & Role

The **Malta Tax and Customs Administration** is the primary government body responsible for tax collection, customs enforcement, and fiscal compliance in Malta. In the context of sovereign credit analysis and macroeconomic stability, the administration plays a critical role in ensuring **fiscal sustainability** and **debt sustainability** by managing the country's low tax-to-GDP ratio (26.6% in 2024, significantly below the EU average of 39%). Its effectiveness is directly linked to the government's ability to narrow the fiscal deficit (projected to decline from 3.7% of GDP in 2024 to 2.8% in 2026) and stabilize public debt below 50% of GDP.

The administration faces specific challenges related to **aggressive tax planning** and revenue leakage. Notably, it manages a large **VAT compliance gap** (25.9% vs EU 7%), indicating significant opportunities for revenue enhancement. It is also tasked with enforcing regulations against **aggressive tax planning**, particularly concerning outbound payments and the treatment of non-domiciled companies, areas identified by the European Commission as carrying high risks for revenue loss. Furthermore, the administration operates within a broader regulatory framework scrutinized for **anti-money laundering (AML)** compliance, where weak enforcement has been cited as a threat to policy credibility.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Tax Revenue Collection & Compliance**: The administration is instrumental in driving the strong revenue growth expected to support Malta's fiscal consolidation. Its activities focus on closing the VAT compliance gap and improving corporate income tax collection, which constitutes a heavy reliance for Maltese tax revenues.
*   **Anti-Aggressive Tax Planning Enforcement**: Implementation of measures to curb aggressive tax planning schemes, including the pending introduction of withholding taxes on outbound payments. This is a key structural reform recommended by the [[European Commission]] to improve **institutional strength** and **policy credibility**.
*   **Fiscal Surveillance & Data Provision**: Provides critical data to the **Malta Fiscal Advisory Council (MFAC)** and [[Eurostat]] for monitoring **fiscal performance and flexibility**. While the MFAC is noted to have limited independence, the tax administration's data accuracy is vital for the [[Malta]]'s Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan.
*   **AML and Regulatory Enforcement**: Works to strengthen the enforcement of anti-money laundering regulations, addressing weaknesses highlighted in the [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Country Report. This is crucial for maintaining **policy credibility** and mitigating risks associated with the country's high financialization and exposure to global capital flows.
