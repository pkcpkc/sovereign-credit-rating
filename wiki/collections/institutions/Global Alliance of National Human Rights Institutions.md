# Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions

## Mandate & Role

The **Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions (GANHRI)** serves as the primary global network and oversight body for National Human Rights Institutions (NHRIs). In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, GANHRI plays a critical role in assessing the institutional strength, independence, and accountability of member states' human rights frameworks.

GANHRI is responsible for the accreditation of NHRIs based on their compliance with the **Paris Principles**. This accreditation status is a key indicator of **institutional strength** and **accountability** for countries like **Romania**. For instance, the stalled accreditation process for Romania's NHRIs—specifically the [[Romanian Institute for Human Rights]] and the [[People's Advocate]]—cited by the European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report, highlights significant risks regarding **policy credibility** and **geopolitical and political stability**. Challenges such as questions regarding independence, severe budget cuts, and staffing shortages directly impact the perceived reliability of a nation's human rights safeguards, which are integral to broader rule-of-law assessments affecting creditworthiness.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Accreditation Reviews**: GANHRI conducts periodic reviews of NHRIs to ensure adherence to the Paris Principles. In **Romania**, the accreditation process has been stalled due to specific inquiries from GANHRI concerning the independence and resources of the [[Romanian Institute for Human Rights]] and the [[People's Advocate]]. This stagnation is noted as a lack of progress in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Romania**, signaling potential weaknesses in **checks and balances**.
*   **Monitoring Institutional Independence**: GANHRI monitors the operational capacity of NHRIs. The report notes that the [[Romanian Institute for Human Rights]] faces severe budget cuts and staffing shortages, risking its functionality. This lack of resources undermines the NHRI's ability to act as an effective check on government power, a factor relevant to **accountability** metrics in sovereign risk analysis.
