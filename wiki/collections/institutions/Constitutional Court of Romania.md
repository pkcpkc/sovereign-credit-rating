# Constitutional Court of Romania

## Mandate & Role

The Constitutional Court of Romania serves as the supreme judicial body responsible for ensuring the conformity of laws and other normative acts with the Constitution. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and rule of law assessment, the Court acts as a critical check on legislative and executive power, directly influencing perceptions of institutional strength, policy credibility, and accountability. Its rulings significantly impact the anti-corruption framework, media freedom, and political stability, thereby affecting investor confidence and regulatory predictability.

## Key Activities & Reports

**Rulings on Anti-Corruption and Asset Declarations**
- **2025 Ruling on Asset Declarations**: In May 2025, the Court ruled that asset declaration requirements for spouses and children of public officials were unconstitutional. This decision is cited as a threat to policy credibility and institutional strength, as it weakened the transparency of the asset declaration system managed by the [[National Integrity Agency]], potentially reducing accountability and increasing corruption risks.
- **2018 Statute of Limitations**: A landmark ruling in June 2018 regarding Article 155(1) contributed to a legal void concerning the statute of limitations, which later interacted with subsequent legislation and judicial interpretations (such as *lex mitior* rulings by the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]]) to create systemic risks regarding impunity in high-level corruption cases.

**Electoral Integrity and Political Stability**
- **2024 Presidential Election Cancellation**: In December 2024, following the first round of presidential elections in November 2024, the Constitutional Court annulled the results due to irregularities. This decision led to a repeated election in May 2025, highlighting the Court's role as a final arbiter in political disputes but also underscoring threats to geopolitical and political stability, including electoral irregularities and transparency issues.

**Legislative Oversight**
- The Court’s activities are integral to the broader judicial independence landscape. While the [[Superior Council of the Magistracy]] and [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]] handle day-to-day justice administration, the Constitutional Court’s decisions on the constitutionality of Justice Laws and anti-corruption measures set the boundaries for the rule of law framework evaluated by institutions like the [[European Commission]] and the [[Venice Commission]].
