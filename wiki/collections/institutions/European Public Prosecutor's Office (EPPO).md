# European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)

## Mandate & Role

The **European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)** serves as an independent decentralized structure within the European Union, mandated to investigate, prosecute, and bring to judgment crimes against the Union's financial interests. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the EPPO represents a critical pillar of the **anti-corruption framework** and **institutional checks** in member states such as **Belgium** and **Italy**.

The EPPO's operational capacity and independence are monitored as part of the **Rule of Law** assessment. Effective cooperation between the EPPO and national authorities is viewed as a strength in mitigating corruption risks, which directly impacts **policy credibility** and **institutional strength**. Conversely, allegations of political interference, information leaks, or lack of coordination with national bodies are identified as threats to the integrity of the justice system and the effectiveness of anti-corruption measures. In Italy, the EPPO operates within a broader landscape where the **National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC)** has strengthened resources, though legislative gaps in lobbying and political financing remain concerns.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Anti-Corruption Monitoring in Belgium**: The EPPO is referenced in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Belgium** as a key institution within the anti-corruption landscape. Its performance and the nature of its cooperation with national prosecutors are assessed to determine the robustness of Belgium's anti-corruption framework. The report highlights the continued **cooperation between the Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC)** and the prosecution service as a strength, underscoring the importance of seamless judicial cooperation in handling high-level corruption cases.
*   **Institutional Context in Italy**: The EPPO is listed as a relevant institution in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Italy**. While the report primarily focuses on the **National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC)** updating the National Anti-Corruption Plan and issuing guidelines on "revolving doors," the EPPO's presence contributes to the overall integrity mechanisms. The report notes that public procurement remains a high risk area for corruption, a domain where the EPPO's mandate to protect the EU's financial interests is particularly relevant.
*   **Risk Mitigation and Judicial Independence**: The existence and functional independence of the EPPO contribute to the **high level of perceived judicial independence** among the general public and companies in member states, thereby supporting the overall stability of the **justice system**. This perception is noted as a strength in both Belgium and Italy, although Italy faces additional challenges regarding the length of judicial proceedings and staff shortages.
