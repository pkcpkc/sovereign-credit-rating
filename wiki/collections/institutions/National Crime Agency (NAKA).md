# National Crime Agency (NAKA)

## Mandate & Role

The National Crime Agency (NAKA) is a specialized Slovak government body mandated to investigate high-level corruption and organized crime. Its operational effectiveness and institutional independence are critical indicators for sovereign credit rating assessments, specifically under the **policy credibility and institutional strength** and **accountability** factors. The existence and functional integrity of NAKA serve as a key safeguard against corruption, directly influencing perceptions of **political polarization** and **rule of law risks** in Slovakia.

## Key Activities & Reports

NAKA's operational status is a focal point in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Slovakia**. Key developments impacting the agency include:

- **Dissolution and Institutional Risk**: The dissolution of NAKA, alongside the Special Prosecution Office (SPO), is identified as a significant threat to accountability. This structural change has led to:
    - A measurable drop in investigated corruption cases.
    - Operational inefficiencies and delays in investigations.
    - A shortage of specialized investigators.
- **Impact on Country Risk**: The closure of NAKA contributes to a **fragmented anti-corruption institutional framework** and weak enforcement mechanisms, negatively affecting **business climate perception** due to heightened rule of law risks.
- **Strategic Context**: The dissolution occurred despite the alignment of criminal codes with the EU PIF Directive, highlighting a divergence between legislative compliance and practical enforcement capacity. A new **National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2024-2029** is in preparation, which may address the vacuum left by NAKA's dissolution.
