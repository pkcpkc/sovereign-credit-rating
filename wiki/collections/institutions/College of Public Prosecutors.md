# College of Public Prosecutors

## Mandate & Role

The **College of Public Prosecutors** is a key institutional body within the Belgian justice system, representing the prosecution service. It plays a critical role in the administration of justice and the enforcement of laws, particularly in the context of anti-corruption efforts. The College collaborates closely with the **Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC)** to handle corruption cases. It is also involved in the broader governance of the judiciary, including discussions regarding the transfer of budgetary management powers from the executive branch to the judiciary, although the High Council for Justice and Court of Cassation have criticized linking these transfers to disciplinary reforms. The institution faces challenges related to structural resource deficiencies, unfilled vacancies due to unattractive working conditions, and concerns regarding potential political influence and information leaks in sensitive investigations.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Anti-Corruption Cooperation**: Continues to cooperate with the **Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC)**. Concerns have been raised regarding information leaks and potential political interference in high-level corruption cases handled by this partnership.
- **Judicial Reform and Budgeting**: The Government intends to transfer budgetary management powers for justice administration to the judiciary, a move that impacts the College. The College is part of the institutions whose independence is perceived highly by the public (64%) and companies (72%).
- **Specialised Prosecution**: Plans are in place for the creation of a specialised prosecution service for financial crime, alongside a new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF).
- **Efficiency and Backlogs**: The College is implicated in the broader issues of judicial efficiency, including long-standing backlogs and excessive length of proceedings, particularly noted in the Court of Appeal of Brussels.
