# Malta Tax and Customs Administration (MTCA)

## Mandate & Role

The Malta Tax and Customs Administration (MTCA) serves as the primary government body responsible for the administration and collection of taxes and customs duties in Malta. Its role is critical to the country's fiscal performance and sustainability, acting as the operational arm for implementing fiscal consolidation measures. The MTCA is tasked with enhancing revenue collection efficiency, ensuring compliance with international tax standards, and supporting the government's broader macroeconomic objectives.

Key aspects of its mandate include:
- **Revenue Administration**: Improving the efficiency of tax collection to support fiscal consolidation targets under the EU framework.
- **Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Reform**: Managing the transition towards new tax structures, specifically the implementation of the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive. The MTCA is responsible for developing and disseminating clear roadmaps for CIT reform to guide taxpayers and investors, thereby preventing revenue loss and reducing distortions between domestic and multinational firms.
- **Fiscal Reorientation**: Supporting the shift from broad energy subsidies to targeted mechanisms, ensuring that fiscal space is effectively reclaimed and redirected toward investment, innovation, and public services.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **IMF Article IV Consultation (2024)**: The MTCA was a key participant in discussions regarding Malta's economic outlook. The IMF endorsed recommendations for the MTCA to focus on revenue administration reforms to improve collection efficiency.
- **Implementation of EU Pillar II**: The MTCA is central to the deferred implementation of the EU Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive, with a deadline set for the end of 2029. The administration is urged to provide clear guidance to maintain investor confidence and protect the tax base.
- **Energy Price Reform**: The MTCA supports the gradual phasing out of fixed-energy price policies, which currently account for a significant portion (20%) of the fiscal deficit. This involves recalibrating tax mechanisms to allow for more targeted subsidies and improved fiscal flexibility.
