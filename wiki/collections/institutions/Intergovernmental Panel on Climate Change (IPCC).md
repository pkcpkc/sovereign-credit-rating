# Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)

## Mandate & Role

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) serves as the scientific body providing comprehensive assessments on the physical science basis of climate change, its impacts, and future risks. In the context of sovereign credit risk analysis, the IPCC's scientific findings underpin the assessment of physical and transition climate risks utilized by rating agencies.

Specifically, the IPCC is referenced by **Fitch Ratings** within its *Sovereign Rating Criteria* (effective September 2025) as a foundational source for understanding climate vulnerability. Fitch utilizes insights derived from such scientific assessments to inform its **Sovereign Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)**. This screening tool aggregates physical risks (e.g., heatwaves, floods, sea-level rise) and transition risks (e.g., fossil fuel dependence) to identify sovereigns with high exposure to climate-related challenges. Sovereigns with a Climate.VS score of 50 or higher in 2035 are subject to additional analytical scrutiny, potentially impacting their sovereign credit ratings via the Qualitative Overlay (QO) mechanism.

## Key Activities & Reports

The IPCC does not directly issue credit ratings or financial reports. However, its scientific assessments are integral to the risk modeling frameworks of credit rating agencies.

*   **Climate Risk Modeling Basis:** The IPCC's projections on physical climate events and transition dynamics provide the baseline data for **Fitch Ratings'** **Sovereign Climate Risk Model (SCRM)** and **Relative Climate Risk Module (RCRM)**.
*   **Risk Screening Criteria:** Data and methodologies aligned with IPCC findings are used to calculate the **Climate.VS** scores (0-100 scale for 2025, 2035, and 2050). These scores determine whether a sovereign requires deeper analysis regarding:
    *   **Physical Risks:** Exposure to heat, drought, storms, floods, and sea-level rise.
    *   **Transition Risks:** Dependence on fossil fuels and the cost of green energy transitions.
*   **Rating Impact:** If climate risks identified through these IPCC-informed metrics are deemed material and not fully captured by the standard Sovereign Rating Model (SRM), Fitch may apply notch adjustments via the Qualitative Overlay (QO) under "Other structural factors."
