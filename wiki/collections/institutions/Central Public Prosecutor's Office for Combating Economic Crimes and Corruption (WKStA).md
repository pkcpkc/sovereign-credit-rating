# Central Public Prosecutor's Office for Combating Economic Crimes and Corruption (WKStA)

## Mandate & Role

The **Central Public Prosecutor's Office for Combating Economic Crimes and Corruption (WKStA)** is a specialized prosecutorial body within the Austrian justice system, dedicated to combating economic crimes and corruption. Its role is critical to Austria's anti-corruption framework and the overall rule of law, serving as a primary enforcement mechanism for the National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2023-2025.

The WKStA operates under the broader prosecutorial system where prosecutorial autonomy is a key concern for international observers. While the office itself functions with stable resources, its independence is structurally linked to the ongoing debate regarding the powers of the **Minister of Justice**, who retains the authority to issue instructions in individual cases. The effectiveness and independence of the WKStA are closely monitored as part of the EU's assessment of Austria's justice system integrity.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Prosecutorial Independence Reforms**: The establishment of an independent Federal Prosecution Office, intended to enhance the autonomy of bodies like the WKStA, was committed to in the March 2025 Government Programme. As of mid-2025, this reform remains in the drafting stage with limited progress. Concerns persist that the Minister of Justice continues to issue instructions to prosecutors (31 cases examined by the Council of Directives in 2024), potentially impacting the WKStA's operational independence.
*   **Case Handling Efficiency**: The WKStA contributes to the broader justice system's efficiency metrics. However, disposition times for bribery cases have seen a significant increase, rising to an average of 518 days, indicating potential resource or procedural bottlenecks in complex corruption cases.
*   **Resource Allocation**: Resources for the WKStA remain stable, though assessments note that additional posts are needed to effectively manage the workload and meet the demands of the National Anti-Corruption Strategy.
*   **Collaboration with Oversight Bodies**: The WKStA operates alongside the **Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK)**, which detected 83 potential corruption cases in 2024. The interaction between investigative findings (e.g., the July 2024 investigative commission confirming past political influence on prosecutorial actions) and prosecutorial decisions is a key area of scrutiny.
