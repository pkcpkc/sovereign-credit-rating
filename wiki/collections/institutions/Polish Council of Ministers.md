# Polish Council of Ministers

## Mandate & Role

The **Polish Council of Ministers** serves as the central executive authority responsible for Poland's fiscal policy, economic stability, and compliance with European Union regulations. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the Council is the primary entity accountable for managing public finances, implementing structural reforms, and addressing macroeconomic imbalances.

Key responsibilities highlighted in recent economic surveillance include:
*   **Fiscal Consolidation**: Leading the implementation of the medium-term fiscal-structural plan endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]] to address the excessive deficit procedure (EDP). The Council aims to reduce the general government deficit from 6.6% of GDP (2024) to below 3% by 2028.
*   **Defense and Public Investment**: Balancing high and rising defense spending (projected at 2.8% of GDP in 2025) with the need for public investment and fiscal adjustment, utilizing the "national escape clause" flexibility within the new EU fiscal framework.
*   **Regulatory Governance**: Managing a complex and volatile regulatory environment that has been identified as a deterrent to investment. The Council is tasked with improving regulatory stability, increasing the use of impact assessments, and limiting the use of fast-track legislative procedures.
*   **Structural Reforms**: Overseeing critical reforms in the pension system (addressing low statutory retirement ages and sustainability), healthcare modernization, and the transition of the labor market to address skills shortages and demographic decline.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (2025)**: Submitted to and endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]] in January 2025. This plan outlines the path for fiscal consolidation amidst high defense expenditures and rising public debt (projected 58.0% of GDP in 2025).
*   **Annual Progress Report (2025)**: Submitted in April 2025 to report on the implementation of the fiscal-structural plan, demonstrating progress in fiscal adjustment measures and revenue generation.
*   **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) Implementation**: Managing the execution of Poland’s RRP, with 25% of milestones and targets fulfilled by mid-2025. Focus areas include green transition, digitalization, and infrastructure development.
*   **Legislative Initiatives**: Enactment of legislation establishing the independent [[Fiscal Council]] (effective 2026) to enhance policy credibility and institutional strength. Implementation of the Pillar 2 Directive on global minimum corporate tax in January 2025.
*   **Energy and Industrial Policy**: Promoting Poland as a clean tech manufacturing hub, particularly in battery production (60% of Europe’s lithium batteries), while addressing challenges related to high electricity prices and fossil fuel reliance.
