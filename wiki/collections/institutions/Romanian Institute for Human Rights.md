# Romanian Institute for Human Rights

## Mandate & Role

The **Romanian Institute for Human Rights** (IRMS) is a national body mandated to protect and promote human rights in Romania. In the context of sovereign credit rating factors and rule of law assessments, the Institute serves as a key indicator of **institutional strength**, **accountability**, and **civil society space**. Its operational capacity and independence are critical components of the country's governance framework.

According to the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the Institute's functionality is currently compromised by severe **budget cuts** and **staffing shortages**. These resource constraints directly threaten its ability to perform its mandate effectively, raising concerns regarding the quality of human rights oversight and the overall health of civil society in Romania.

## Key Activities & Reports

The primary recent development concerning the Institute involves its **accreditation process** with the **Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions** (GANHRI). As of mid-2025, this process is stalled. Key issues identified include:

*   **Independence Concerns**: GANHRI has raised questions regarding the institutional independence of the Romanian Institute for Human Rights.
*   **Resource Constraints**: Severe budget cuts and staffing shortages have been cited as major risks to the Institute's functionality.
*   **Joint Accreditation Context**: The accreditation status is also linked to the **People's Advocate** (Ombudsman), as both are National Human Rights Institutions in Romania. The lack of progress in accrediting these bodies is noted as a negative factor in the rule of law assessment, reflecting broader challenges in transparency and institutional credibility.

The Institute's struggles are contextualized within a **narrowed civil society space**, where civil society organizations (CSOs) face administrative barriers and attacks. While the government adopted the **Strategy for Open Government 2025-2030** to address these challenges, the specific impact on the Institute's operational capacity remains a concern for rule of law indicators.
