# Hungarian Fiscal Council

## Mandate & Role

The **Hungarian Fiscal Council** serves as an independent advisory body mandated to provide non-binding opinions on the budgetary forecasts and fiscal sustainability of Hungary. Its primary role is to enhance the transparency and credibility of public finance management by monitoring compliance with fiscal rules and assessing medium-term fiscal trajectories.

However, recent assessments by the [[European Commission]] highlight significant weaknesses in the institution's operational capacity. The Council is cited as having **limited independence and capacity**, which undermines its effectiveness as a check on executive fiscal power. This limitation is identified as a key threat to **policy credibility and institutional strength** within Hungary's sovereign credit risk profile, alongside weak anti-corruption frameworks and the frequent use of emergency decrees that bypass standard parliamentary oversight.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Fiscal Sustainability Assessment**: The Council is tasked with reviewing the government's budgetary plans and debt sustainability analyses. However, its impact is constrained by the broader context of **high regulatory volatility** and **frequent legislative amendments** that hinder long-term fiscal planning.
*   **Institutional Criticism**: The **2025 Country Report on Hungary** by the [[European Commission]] explicitly lists the "Limited independence and capacity of the Hungarian Fiscal Council" as a threat to policy credibility. This critique is part of a broader concern regarding **weak checks and balances** due to the persistent use of "state of danger" emergency powers, which limits parliamentary and institutional oversight of fiscal measures.
*   **Context of Fiscal Risks**: The Council operates in an environment characterized by **high medium-term fiscal sustainability risks**, with public debt reaching 73.5% of GDP in 2024 and a government deficit of 4.9%. The Council's limited capacity is seen as exacerbating the lack of a realistic medium-term budgetary framework.
