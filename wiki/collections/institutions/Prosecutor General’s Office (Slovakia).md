# Prosecutor General’s Office (Slovakia)

## Mandate & Role

The Prosecutor General’s Office (Slovakia) is a central government body responsible for the direction of criminal prosecution in Slovakia. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, its role is critical as a primary mechanism for enforcing anti-corruption frameworks and ensuring the rule of law.

Recent assessments indicate that the office possesses broad discretionary powers, including the authority to annul final decisions and interfere in ongoing cases, particularly in high-level corruption matters. These powers are identified as a significant threat to institutional accountability and the independence of the judiciary, contributing to risks related to policy credibility and institutional strength. The office operates within a fragmented anti-corruption framework, where its actions—or inactions—are closely monitored by international bodies such as the [[European Commission]] and the [[European Court of Human Rights]].

## Key Activities & Reports

Based on the *2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Slovakia*, the following activities and issues are attributed to or directly impact the Prosecutor General’s Office:

- **Interference in High-Level Cases**: The Prosecutor General continues to utilize powers to annul final judicial decisions, including in high-profile corruption cases. This practice is cited as a major impediment to the effective and independent investigation of high-level corruption.
- **Impact on Institutional Trust**: The use of these powers contributes to low perceived judicial independence (32% among the public) and weak enforcement of the rule of law. It undermines the accountability pillar of sovereign credit rating factors, specifically regarding threats to institutional checks and balances.
- **Fragmentation of Anti-Corruption Efforts**: The office operates in an environment where specialized anti-corruption entities, such as the National Crime Agency ([[NAKA]]) and the Specialized Prosecutor’s Office ([[SPO]]), have been dissolved. This dissolution has led to a drop in investigated corruption cases and operational inefficiencies, shifting the burden and scrutiny onto the general prosecutorial system.
- **International Scrutiny**: The office's conduct is a subject of intensive dialogue with the [[European Commission]]. The lack of progress in addressing concerns about prosecutorial independence and the annulment of decisions remains a key finding in EU rule of law monitoring.
