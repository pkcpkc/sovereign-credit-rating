# Kúria (Hungary)

## Mandate & Role

The **Kúria** serves as the highest judicial body in Hungary, responsible for the uniform application of law and the oversight of judicial practice. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and rule of law assessments, the Kúria is a critical institutional actor whose independence and operational integrity are key indicators of legal certainty and policy credibility.

According to the **2025 Rule of Law Report**, the Kúria continues to enforce uniform case law effectively. However, the institution operates within a broader judicial environment characterized by significant pressures on judicial independence, including reported undue pressure on judges regarding internal debates. The Kúria's role is increasingly scrutinized in light of broader institutional reforms, such as the raising of judicial age limits and changes to appointment procedures, which have raised concerns about political influence over the judiciary.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Case Allocation Transparency**: The Kúria is cited as a positive example within the Hungarian judiciary regarding transparency. Unlike lower-instance courts, where case allocation systems lack transparency and allow for discretionary manipulation by court managers, case allocation at the Kúria is functioning well with high transparency.
*   **Judicial Reform Context**: The Kúria operates amidst significant turbulence in the Hungarian judicial system. Key events affecting its environment include:
    *   The resignation of the President of the **National Judicial Council (Hungary)** in December 2024 following judicial protests.
    *   The withdrawal of the National Judicial Council from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms in January 2025.
    *   The adoption of the Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law in December 2024, which altered judicial age limits (minimum age raised to 35, service allowed until age 70).
    *   A finding by the Budapest Regional Court in March 2025 that the Kúria President unlawfully relieved a judge, highlighting ongoing tensions regarding administrative authority within the court.
*   **Rule of Law Monitoring**: The Kúria's performance and independence are monitored by international bodies such as the **European Commission**, the **Venice Commission**, and the **Council of Europe**, which have expressed concerns over the "chilling effect" on the judiciary and the lack of effective consultation in recent reforms.
