# European Public Prosecutor's Office

## Mandate & Role

The European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) serves as a decentralized European Union prosecution office established to protect the EU's financial interests by investigating and prosecuting crimes against the Union's budget. In the context of sovereign credit rating and country risk analysis, the EPPO acts as a critical pillar of the anti-corruption framework and institutional strength within member states. Its operational effectiveness serves as a proxy for the integrity of the judicial system and the capacity to enforce accountability against high-level public officials.

## Key Activities & Reports

**Operational Status in Poland (2025)**
*   **Full Operational Capacity:** As of January 1, 2025, the EPPO became fully operational in Poland. This milestone is cited in the *2025 Rule of Law Report* as a significant strength in Poland's anti-corruption framework.
*   **Anti-Corruption Infrastructure:** The EPPO's operation complements domestic efforts, including the planned establishment of a new Central Office for Combating Corruption within the police (replacing the Central Anti-Corruption Bureau).
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Despite the EPPO's operational status, systemic risks remain in Poland, including the absence of a comprehensive national anti-corruption strategy, a lack of lobbying rules, and broad immunities for top executives that continue to hinder high-level corruption prosecutions.

**Methodological Context**
*   The EPPO's activities are monitored under the **Rule of Law Report Methodology**, specifically within the assessment of **accountability** and **anti-corruption frameworks**.
