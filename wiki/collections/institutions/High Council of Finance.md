# High Council of Finance

## Mandate & Role

The **High Council of Finance** (Haut Conseil des finances / Hoge Raad van Financiën) is a Belgian government body responsible for monitoring fiscal rules and providing independent advice on economic and fiscal matters. Its primary role within the sovereign credit rating framework involves ensuring the credibility of national fiscal projections and monitoring compliance with budgetary constraints.

According to the **2025 Country Report - Belgium** by the [[European Commission]], the High Council of Finance is identified as a critical component of Belgium's policy credibility and institutional strength. However, the report highlights significant weaknesses in its operational capacity. Specifically, the High Council of Finance is noted to **lack sufficient autonomy, staff, and resources** to effectively monitor fiscal rules. This under-resourcing contributes to the broader weakness in Belgium's national fiscal framework, characterized by loose budgetary coordination between federal and federated entities.

## Key Activities & Reports

The High Council of Finance is involved in the oversight of Belgium's fiscal planning and reporting processes, including:

*   **Monitoring Fiscal Rules:** The institution is tasked with verifying government projections against independent forecasts. The 2025 report notes discrepancies between government projections and Commission forecasts on net expenditure, suggesting gaps in the effectiveness of current monitoring mechanisms.
*   **Advisory Role in Medium-Term Planning:** The Council's assessments feed into the **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan** (MTP) submitted by Belgium. The effectiveness of the HCF in validating the realism of these plans is hampered by its resource constraints, which the European Commission cites as a threat to policy credibility.
*   **Coordination Challenges:** The institution operates within a fragmented federal structure, where weak budgetary coordination between federal and regional governments prevents effective monitoring of compliance. The HCF's limited capacity exacerbates the difficulty in enforcing fiscal discipline across all levels of government.
