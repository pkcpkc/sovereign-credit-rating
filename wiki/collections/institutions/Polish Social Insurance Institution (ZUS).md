# Polish Social Insurance Institution (ZUS)

## Mandate & Role

The **Polish Social Insurance Institution (ZUS)** is a key government body responsible for the administration of Poland's social security system, including pensions, healthcare contributions, and labor market benefits. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, ZUS is a central entity for assessing **fiscal sustainability**, particularly regarding the long-term viability of the pension system.

The institution faces significant challenges linked to demographic shifts, including an aging population and demographic decline, which exert pressure on public finances. Key issues under ZUS's purview include:
- **Pension Sustainability**: Managing the risks associated with a low statutory retirement age for women (60 years) and special pension schemes, which contribute to rising public spending and debt burdens.
- **Labor Market Dynamics**: Addressing skills shortages and the tight labor market (with unemployment at a historic low of 2.9% in 2024) while dealing with a shrinking working-age population.
- **Fiscal Consolidation**: Supporting broader fiscal adjustment measures aimed at reducing the general government deficit (which reached 6.6% of GDP in 2024) and public debt levels.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Pension System Reform Discussions**: ZUS is involved in ongoing discussions and reforms aimed at improving the sustainability of the pension system, potentially by increasing the effective retirement age to mitigate the impact of demographic changes on the [[Fiscal Council]]'s medium-term fiscal-structural plans.
- **Healthcare System Coordination**: Collaborating on the reform of the healthcare system via the [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]], addressing its hospital-centric nature and insufficient preventive care, which impacts overall social spending efficiency.
- **Labor Market Data & Policy Implementation**: Providing data and administrative support for addressing the disability employment gap (which widened to 35.6% in 2024) and supporting vocational education and training (VET) initiatives to close skills shortages in STEM and manufacturing sectors.
- **Fiscal Plan Monitoring**: Supporting the [[European Commission]] and [[Polish Council of Ministers]] in monitoring the implementation of the medium-term fiscal-structural plan, particularly regarding net expenditure growth and the impact of social benefits on the deficit trajectory under the [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]].
