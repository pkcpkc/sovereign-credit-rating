# European Court of Justice

## Mandate & Role

The [[European Court of Justice]] (ECJ) serves as the supreme court of the [[European Union]], ensuring uniform interpretation and application of EU law. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the ECJ is a critical institutional actor for safeguarding the rule of law, which directly impacts policy credibility, institutional strength, and legal predictability. Its mandate includes resolving disputes between EU Member States, interpreting EU law, and ensuring that national measures align with EU directives and principles, thereby maintaining the integrity of the EU's legal and economic framework.

## Key Activities & Reports

Recent risk assessments highlight the ECJ's relevance to the following areas:

- **Rule of Law Safeguards**: The ECJ is cited as a key institution in the monitoring of rule of law adherence in Member States such as [[Slovakia]]. While the [[European Commission]] identifies threats related to potential constitutional amendments challenging EU law primacy, the ECJ remains the ultimate arbiter of such conflicts.
- **Implementation of EU Directives**: The alignment of national criminal codes with EU directives, such as the Protection of the European Union’s Financial Interests (PIF) Directive, is monitored for compliance. For instance, Slovakia adopted amendments to its Criminal Codes to align with the PIF Directive, a process overseen by the broader EU legal framework enforced by the ECJ.
- **Judicial Independence and Governance**: The ECJ's jurisprudence informs standards for judicial independence. Reports note low perceived judicial independence in Slovakia (32% public perception) and risks to the independence of the [[Judicial Council (Slovakia)]], areas where ECJ precedents often set the benchmark for EU member compliance.
- **Anti-Corruption and Financial Integrity**: The ECJ works in tandem with bodies like the [[European Public Prosecutor Office (EPPO)]] and the [[European Anti-Fraud Office (OLAF)]] to enforce rules against fraud and corruption. Dissolution of specialized anti-corruption entities in Slovakia (NAKA, SPO) has been flagged as a risk, with the ECJ framework providing the legal basis for demanding effective anti-corruption mechanisms.
