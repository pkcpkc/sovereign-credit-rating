# Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK)

## Mandate & Role

The **Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK)** is an Austrian government body responsible for the detection and investigation of potential corruption cases within the public sector. It plays a critical role in Austria's **anti-corruption framework**, contributing to the country's overall governance integrity and rule of law standards. Its activities are monitored as part of the **European Commission**'s assessment of institutional checks and balances and policy credibility. The BAK operates within a broader ecosystem that includes the **Central Public Prosecutor's Office for Combating Economic Crimes and Corruption (WKStA)** and aligns with international standards set by **GRECO** and **Transparency International**.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Corruption Case Detection**: In 2024, the BAK detected **83 potential corruption cases**, highlighting its active role in identifying irregularities in public administration.
- **National Strategy Implementation**: The BAK contributes to the implementation of the **National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2023-2025**, which aims to strengthen preventive and repressive measures against corruption.
- **Performance Context**: While the BAK's operational output is significant, the broader Austrian anti-corruption landscape faces challenges, such as limited progress on asset declaration rules for Members of Parliament and weak lobbying transparency frameworks, which indirectly affect the environment in which the BAK operates.
- **Resource and Oversight**: The BAK functions alongside the **Austrian Court of Audit** and the **Ombudsman Board**, which provide additional oversight on party finance and human rights, respectively. The effectiveness of these bodies is cited as a strength in Austria's institutional accountability.
