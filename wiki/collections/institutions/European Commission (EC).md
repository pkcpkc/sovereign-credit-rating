# European Commission (EC)

## Mandate & Role

The European Commission (EC) serves as a primary economic surveillance and fiscal policy oversight body within the European Union. It is responsible for monitoring the macroeconomic stability and fiscal performance of member states, ensuring compliance with EU fiscal frameworks, and guiding structural reforms. The EC works in coordination with the [[International Monetary Fund (IMF)]] and the [[European Central Bank (ECB)]] to assess country risks, particularly regarding fiscal consolidation, debt sustainability, and the implementation of structural reforms such as corporate tax directives and climate transition strategies.

## Key Activities & Reports

The EC's activities include the monitoring of national fiscal plans against EU ceilings and the issuance of recommendations for structural adjustments. Key interactions and oversight areas include:

- **Fiscal Surveillance and Excessive Deficit Procedure**: The EC monitors compliance with EU fiscal rules. For instance, [[Malta]] is projected to enter the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure in 2025, prompting EC oversight on the country's commitment to fiscal consolidation, with a target structural deficit reduction to 2.6% of GDP by 2027.
- **Tax Policy and Structural Reform Oversight**: The EC oversees the implementation of the EU’s Pillar II Minimum Tax Directive. [[Malta]] has deferred implementation until 2029, requiring alignment with EC guidelines to prevent revenue loss and distortions. The EC also monitors progress on long-term fiscal frameworks addressing aging-related spending and climate transition costs.
- **Macroeconomic Coordination**: The EC collaborates with the [[IMF]] and [[ECB]] in regular consultations (such as the IMF Article IV Consultation) to provide a unified view on economic stability, financial sector resilience, and external balance assessments.
