# College of Courts and Tribunals

## Mandate & Role

The **College of Courts and Tribunals** is a key institution within the Belgian justice system, responsible for the administration and management of the court system. Its role is central to ensuring **[[Judicial efficiency]]** and **[[Judicial independence]]**, which are critical factors in sovereign credit rating assessments regarding institutional strength and policy credibility.

According to the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Belgium** by the **[[European Commission]]**, the College plays a pivotal role in the ongoing reforms aimed at strengthening the judiciary's autonomy. A significant development involves the Government's intention to transfer **budgetary management powers** for the justice administration from the executive branch to the judiciary, with the College likely being the primary beneficiary or implementer of these powers. This shift is viewed as a strength for institutional stability, although it faces scrutiny regarding its linkage to disciplinary reforms by bodies such as the **[[High Council for Justice]]** and the **[[Court of Cassation]]**.

The College is also involved in addressing structural resource deficiencies, including the management of staff vacancies and working conditions, which are cited as barriers to filling judicial positions. Furthermore, the College operates within a framework criticized for shortcomings in the **digitalisation of justice**, including a lack of overarching strategy and fraud risks, as reported by the **[[Belgian Court of Audit]]**.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Budgetary Management Reform**: Participating in the transition of budgetary management powers from the executive to the judiciary, a move intended to enhance judicial independence and institutional stability.
- **Addressing Judicial Backlogs**: Collaborating with the **[[High Council for Justice]]** to address excessive length of proceedings and backlogs, particularly in the **[[Court of Appeal of Brussels]]**. The High Council published reports on case durations and specific court backlogs to inform these efforts.
- **Judicial Vacancies and Staffing**: Managing the publication of open positions for magistrates and court staff to increase transparency, while grappling with challenges in filling vacancies due to unattractive working conditions and salaries.
- **Justice Digitalisation**: Operating within a digitalisation environment noted for serious shortcomings, including poor management and fraud risks, prompting government action plans sent to the **[[Belgian Court of Audit]]**.
