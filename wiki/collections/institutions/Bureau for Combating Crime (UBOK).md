# Bureau for Combating Crime (UBOK)

## Mandate & Role

The Bureau for Combating Crime (UBOK) is a specialized law enforcement entity in Slovakia tasked with investigating corruption and organized crime. In the context of sovereign credit rating and rule of law analysis, UBOK serves as a critical component of the national anti-corruption framework. Its operational effectiveness and independence are key indicators of policy credibility and institutional strength.

Recent assessments indicate that the dissolution or significant weakening of specialized anti-corruption entities, including UBOK and the National Crime Agency (NAKA), has led to a "fragmented anti-corruption institutional framework." This structural change is cited as a threat to accountability, resulting in a drop in investigated corruption cases, operational inefficiencies, delays, and a shortage of specialized investigators. The lack of effective, independent investigation mechanisms for high-level corruption represents a significant risk factor for the country's institutional stability.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Investigation Capacity**: UBOK's capacity to investigate high-level corruption cases has been negatively impacted by the closure of specialized entities and the lack of specialized investigators.
- **Impact on Case Volume**: The restructuring has contributed to a measurable decrease in the number of corruption cases investigated, undermining the deterrent effect of the justice system.
- **Strategic Context**: The entity operates within the broader scope of the new National Anti-Corruption Strategy 2024-2029, which is currently in preparation to address these institutional gaps.
