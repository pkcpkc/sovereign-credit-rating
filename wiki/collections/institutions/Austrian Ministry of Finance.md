# Austrian Ministry of Finance

## Mandate & Role

The Austrian Ministry of Finance is the primary government body responsible for formulating and implementing Austria's fiscal policy, overseeing public debt management, and ensuring macroeconomic stability. It plays a central role in sovereign credit risk management by adhering to high standards of data quality and transparency, notably through adherence to the Special Data Dissemination Standard Plus (SDDS Plus). The Ministry works in close coordination with the [[Oesterreichische Nationalbank]] and the [[International Monetary Fund]] to maintain policy credibility and institutional strength.

Key responsibilities include:
- Designing and executing fiscal consolidation measures to stabilize public debt and reduce fiscal deficits.
- Implementing structural reforms aimed at boosting productivity, labor supply, and competitiveness.
- Managing the country's external financial position and ensuring debt sustainability.
- Collaborating with the [[Financial Market Authority]] on financial sector stability, including addressing risks in commercial real estate and banking capitalization.

## Key Activities & Reports

The Ministry has been actively engaged in the **2025 Article IV Consultation** with the [[International Monetary Fund]], concluding in July 2025. Key activities and policy directions highlighted in this process include:

- **Fiscal Consolidation Efforts**: In response to a fiscal deficit that widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024 and public debt rising to 81.2%, the Ministry, under Minister [[Christian Karner Marterbauer]], has announced near-term consolidation measures for 2025-2026. The goal is to reduce the deficit below 2% of GDP in the medium term to counteract pressures from demographic aging, defense spending, and green transition costs.
- **Structural Reform Implementation**: The Ministry is advancing reforms to boost labor supply, particularly among women and the elderly, to offset demographic drag. This includes expanding childcare and pension reforms.
- **Regulatory Adjustments**: The Ministry oversees the transition of borrower-based mortgage lending measures (KIM-V), which lapsed in July 2025, and supports the implementation of the Commercial Real Estate buffer (SSyRB) to mitigate financial sector risks.
- **Policy Coordination**: The Ministry coordinates with the [[European Commission]] to align with EU fiscal rules and engages with domestic think tanks like [[WIFO]] for economic analysis and forecasting.
