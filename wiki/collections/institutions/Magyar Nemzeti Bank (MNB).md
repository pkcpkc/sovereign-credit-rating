# Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB)

## Mandate & Role

The **Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB)** serves as the central bank of Hungary, primarily responsible for maintaining price stability and ensuring financial system stability. According to the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation, the MNB has maintained an independent monetary policy stance focused on anchoring inflation expectations, keeping the base rate at 6.5% since September 2024 to durably return inflation to the 3% target by 2027. 

The institution operates under a legal framework that supports operational independence, though this is noted to face challenges from political interference via regulatory caps on prices, fees, and margins, which the IMF argues distort market signals and weaken monetary policy transmission. Beyond its primary mandate, the MNB also pursues secondary goals, including environmental sustainability, which the IMF notes may distract from the core price stability mandate. The MNB utilizes a flexible exchange rate regime to absorb external shocks, though it has occasionally intervened via FX swap facilities to stabilize the Forint amidst volatility linked to global risk sentiment and fiscal uncertainty.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Monetary Policy Stance:** The MNB maintained a restrictive, data-dependent monetary policy throughout 2025. The IMF staff report supports maintaining this tight stance into 2026 to combat persistent inflation, particularly in core services driven by wage pressures.
*   **Regulatory Interventions:** The MNB has implemented various regulatory measures, including interest rate caps, price controls, and margin caps. The IMF highlights that these measures, while intended to protect consumers, distort market signals, defer rather than eliminate inflationary pressures, and undermine the effectiveness of monetary policy transmission.
*   **Financial Sector Oversight:** As part of its mandate, the MNB monitors the health of the banking sector. In 2025, the sector was assessed as well-capitalized (Capital Adequacy Ratio of 20.5%) and liquid, with historically low non-performing loan ratios. However, the MNB faces challenges regarding the "sovereign-bank nexus," where banks' growing exposure to sovereign debt amplifies macro-financial risks, and rising corporate sector risks amid high interest rates.
*   **Exchange Rate Management:** The MNB manages a floating exchange rate regime. Despite the automatic adjustment mechanism, the Forint faces depreciation pressure due to global risk shifts. The MNB has adjusted FX swap facilities to manage volatility, indicating a readiness to intervene when necessary to stabilize rates.
*   **Inflation Targeting:** The MNB aims for a 3% inflation target. As of mid-2025, inflation remained above target at 4.4-4.6%, driven by core services inflation (7.6%). The MNB's policies are aligned with IMF recommendations to keep policy tight until inflation converges to the target, expected by 2027.
