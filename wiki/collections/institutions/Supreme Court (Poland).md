# Supreme Court (Poland)

## Mandate & Role

The Supreme Court of Poland serves as the highest court in the land for common and administrative justice, playing a critical role in the enforcement of the rule of law and judicial independence within the European Union. In the context of sovereign credit risk and institutional stability, the Court is a focal point of the ongoing rule of law crisis between Poland and EU institutions. Its structural integrity and operational independence are key indicators of policy credibility and institutional strength.

Recent developments highlight the Court's centrality to legislative reforms aimed at addressing historical disputes over judicial appointments. Specifically, a draft law proposed in April 2025 aims to dismantle the **Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs** (ICE), a chamber established during the previous political administration that was widely criticized by the [[European Commission]] and the [[Venice Commission]] for undermining judicial independence. The restructuring is part of the government's Action Plan on the Rule of Law to align Poland with EU standards.

The Court's legitimacy and operational environment are further complicated by broader constitutional disputes involving the [[National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)]] and the [[Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)]]. While amendments to laws governing these bodies have been adopted by Parliament, they remain pending review by the Constitutional Tribunal due to presidential referral. The ongoing refusal of the Polish government to publish certain Constitutional Tribunal judgments exacerbates tensions, affecting Poland's geopolitical stability and policy credibility scores.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Structural Reform Initiatives (2025)**: The Ministry of Justice proposed a draft law to dismantle the Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs within the Supreme Court. This move is intended to restore judicial independence and comply with EU rule of law recommendations.
*   **Status of Judges Review**: The Court is implicated in a draft law addressing the status of approximately 1,600 judges appointed at the request of the post-2017 composition of the [[National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)]]. This legislation proposes a classification system and potential removal procedures, pending consultation with the [[Venice Commission]].
*   **Rule of Law Compliance**: The European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report identifies the Supreme Court's structural reforms as a key area of progress, though notes that substantial work remains to fully align with EU standards. The Court operates within a context of 54 pending leading judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]] as of June 2025, reflecting broader systemic challenges in the judicial system.
