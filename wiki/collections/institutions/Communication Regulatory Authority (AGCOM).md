# Communication Regulatory Authority (AGCOM)

## Mandate & Role

The Communication Regulatory Authority (AGCOM) is Italy’s independent media regulator. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, AGCOM serves as a key institutional check and balance regarding media freedom and pluralism. According to the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Italy**, AGCOM is recognized for functioning independently and being financially equipped, which represents a strength in Italy’s institutional framework. Its independence contributes to the credibility of the policy environment and the protection of fundamental freedoms, although broader concerns regarding media freedom, such as the safety of journalists and access to judicial information, persist in the country.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Regulatory Independence:** AGCOM functions as an independent entity with adequate financial resources, as noted in the European Commission's 2025 assessment.
*   **Contextual Challenges:** While AGCOM itself is functioning well, the broader media landscape it oversees faces challenges, including governance issues with the public service broadcaster [[Radiotelevisione Italiana (RAI)]], stalled defamation reforms, and threats to journalist safety (including alleged spyware use).
*   **Rule of Law Assessment:** AGCOM's performance is monitored as part of the "Media pluralism" pillar in the EU Rule of Law mechanism, contributing to the evaluation of Italy's institutional checks and balances.
