# National Bank of Romania

## Mandate & Role

The **National Bank of Romania (NBR)** is the central bank of Romania, primarily responsible for ensuring price stability and maintaining financial system stability. It operates under a credible inflation-targeting framework and manages the exchange rate regime, which is characterized by a managed float against the Euro to maintain relative stability. The NBR possesses strong institutional autonomy, which is generally respected, contributing to the overall policy credibility of the country.

Key aspects of its mandate include:
*   **Monetary Policy:** Implementing policies to anchor inflation expectations. While inflation remains elevated (5.8% in 2024, projected around 5% in 2025), the NBR's policies are credited with anchoring expectations despite pressures from fiscal deficits.
*   **Exchange Rate Management:** Managing the Lei to remain relatively steady against the Euro. This managed float regime provides a degree of monetary policy autonomy while mitigating unsustainable peg risks, though it faces devaluation pressures due to persistent current account deficits.
*   **Financial Stability Oversight:** Monitoring the health of the banking sector, which is noted for being well-capitalized (CET1 ratio of 20%) with low non-performing loans. The NBR manages sovereign debt structures to maintain favorable local currency debt implications.

## Key Activities & Reports

The NBR is cited as a key institution in Romania's macroeconomic landscape, particularly regarding:
*   **Inflation Control:** Actively working to reduce inflation from peaks of 10% in 2023 to 5.8% in 2024.
*   **External Liquidity Management:** Maintaining official reserve assets at 20.8% of GDP to provide a buffer against external shocks and finance current account deficits (which widened to 8.4% of GDP in 2024).
*   **Sovereign Debt Management Coordination:** Supporting the government in maintaining market access, evidenced by the timely issuance of sovereign green bonds.
