# Slovak Parliament (National Council)

## Mandate & Role

The Slovak Parliament (National Council) serves as the primary legislative body of Slovakia, responsible for adopting laws that shape the country's legal, economic, and political framework. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and rule of law analysis, the Parliament's activities are critical indicators of **policy credibility**, **institutional strength**, and **accountability**. Its legislative practices, including the use of fast-track procedures and the adoption of constitutional amendments, directly influence investor confidence, business climate perceptions, and the effectiveness of checks and balances within the state. The Parliament's role in overseeing the executive branch and ensuring the alignment of national laws with EU directives is a key factor in assessing geopolitical stability and policy sustainability.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Legislative Efficiency and Transparency**: The Parliament has frequently utilized fast-track legislative procedures, with 28% of laws in 2024 adopted through this mechanism. This bypasses standard consultation processes, raising concerns about limited stakeholder involvement and reducing transparency in the law-making process. This practice is cited as a threat to policymaking stability and democratic accountability.
- **Constitutional Amendments and EU Law Primacy**: In April 2025, the Parliament held the first reading of proposed constitutional amendments that raised significant concerns regarding the primacy of EU law. These proposals were eventually postponed but highlight political polarization and risks related to potential challenges against European Union legal frameworks.
- **Media Freedom and Pluralism**: The Parliament played a central role in adopting the Act on Slovak Television and Radio (STVR) in July 2024, which introduced a new governance structure for public service media. This has raised concerns about the politicization of media and threats to media pluralism. Additionally, amendments to the Freedom of Information Act, which entered into force in March 2025, introduced fees and longer response times, restricting public access to information and facing constitutional challenges.
- **Civil Society Regulation**: In April 2024, the Parliament adopted a law amending NGO functioning acts, imposing increased reporting obligations on non-governmental organizations. This has been identified as a measure that increases pressure on civil society and reduces the space for human rights advocates, thereby weakening institutional checks and balances.
- **Anti-Corruption and Judicial Oversight**: The Parliament has been involved in legislative changes affecting the anti-corruption framework, including the dissolution of specialized entities like the National Crime Agency (NAKA) and the Office for the Protection of Whistleblowers' oversight capabilities. It also enacted amendments to criminal codes (February 2024 and December 2024) to align with the EU PIF Directive, though concerns remain about safeguards against misuse of laws such as "abuse of law" for judges.
