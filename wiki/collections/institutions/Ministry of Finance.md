# Ministry of Finance

## Mandate & Role

The [[Ministry of Finance]] is the primary government body responsible for formulating and implementing fiscal policy, managing public finances, and ensuring compliance with national and international economic governance frameworks.

In [[Belgium]], it plays a central role in maintaining [[fiscal sustainability]] and [[debt sustainability]] by overseeing the national budget, executing the [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan]], and coordinating with regional entities despite challenges posed by [[institutional fragmentation]]. It works with advisory bodies like the [[High Council of Finance]], the [[Federal Planning Bureau]], and the [[National Bank of Belgium]] to monitor fiscal rules.

In [[France]], the Ministry (led by Minister for the Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty [[François Lombard]] and Minister for Public Accounts [[Guillaume de Montchalín]]) is tasked with addressing high public debt levels and fiscal slippages. It coordinates with the [[Banque de France]], the [[High Council for Public Finances]], and the [[High Council for Financial Stability (HCSF)]] to ensure macroeconomic stability and financial sector resilience. The Ministry is responsible for implementing structural reforms to boost productivity, managing the [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP)]], and addressing long-term pressures from aging demographics, pension costs, and defense spending, while engaging directly with international institutions like the [[International Monetary Fund]] and the [[European Commission]].

In [[Slovakia]], under Minister [[Kamenický]], the Ministry is responsible for executing an ambitious four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028) aimed at reducing the fiscal deficit from 5.8% of GDP in 2024 to approximately 2.2% by 2028. It manages the [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]] initiated by the [[European Commission]] and coordinates with the [[National Bank of Slovakia]] and the independent [[Council for Budgetary Responsibility]] to enhance policy credibility and institutional strength. The Ministry focuses on expenditure rationalization, improving governance to facilitate EU fund disbursement, and addressing long-term fiscal risks posed by demographic aging.

## Key Activities & Reports

### Belgium
*   **Submission of Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP):** The Ministry submitted the MTP covering 2025–2029 on March 18, 2025. This plan commits to a seven-year fiscal adjustment period, incorporating structural reforms in pensions, taxation, and labour markets to address the projected rise in public debt to 126.4% of GDP by 2035. The IMF supports this plan, recommending an annual structural primary balance reduction of approximately 0.6 percentage points of GDP until 2031 to bring the deficit durably below 3% of GDP and reduce debt to ~78% of GDP.
*   **Activation of National Escape Clause:** On April 30, 2025, the Ministry requested and successfully activated the [[National Escape Clause]] within the new EU governance framework to accommodate increased defence spending (projected at 1.7% of GDP in 2025), allowing for a deviation from net expenditure limits while maintaining compliance with the broader fiscal roadmap.
*   **IMF Article IV Consultation Engagement:** The Ministry participated in the [[IMF]] 2025 [[Article IV Consultation]], culminating in a Staff Report and Press Release in March 2025. Key discussions involved presenting the new government's agenda for fiscal consolidation and structural reforms, including plans to rationalize current spending (social benefits and public wages) while preserving public investment. The Ministry addressed IMF concerns regarding [[wage indexation]], labor productivity, and the need for coordinated federal-regional efforts on climate targets.
*   **Fiscal Coordination & Monitoring:** The Ministry manages the complex coordination between federal and regional governments to monitor compliance with fiscal rules, although it faces threats from the lack of a fully developed multiannual fiscal planning framework and insufficient autonomy of the [[High Council of Finance]]. The Ministry has outlined plans to revitalize the fiscal council and enforce binding burden-sharing rules to enhance accountability.
*   **Reporting on Economic Developments:** The Ministry contributes data and policy assessments to the [[European Commission]]'s [[2025 Country Report - Belgium]], addressing issues such as the widening government deficit (4.5% of GDP in 2024, projected 5.4% in 2025) and the implementation of the [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]]. It also highlights the risks associated with high labor tax wedges and declining labor-cost competitiveness.

### France
*   **2025 IMF Article IV Consultation & FSAP:** The Ministry engaged in the 2025 [[Article IV Consultation]] and the [[Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP)]] with the [[IMF]]. Discussions concluded in May 2025, with the Executive Board concluding the consultation in July 2025. The Ministry presented the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), aiming to bring the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. The IMF recommended a frontloaded structural fiscal effort of 1.1% of GDP in 2026, prioritizing spending rationalization over tax increases.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation & Budget Compromise:** Following fiscal slippages in 2023 and 2024, the Ministry reached a political compromise on the 2025 budget in February 2025. In March 2025, an action plan to enhance public finance execution was published. The Ministry is committed to frontloaded structural fiscal efforts to address the general government gross debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024.
*   **Financial Stability Oversight:** Working with the [[Banque de France]] and [[High Council for Financial Stability (HCSF)]], the Ministry monitors financial sector risks identified in the FSAP, including weak bank profitability due to net interest margin compression, high corporate indebtedness, and risks from non-bank financial intermediaries. The banking sector maintains healthy capital buffers (19.8% regulatory capital ratio) and strong liquidity.
*   **Structural Reforms & Productivity:** The Ministry is advancing structural reforms to boost labor productivity, which remains below pre-pandemic levels. Focus areas include leveraging AI and green technologies, improving education, and promoting less fragmented careers. The Ministry also oversees the implementation of the 2023 pension reform (raising retirement age to 64) to mitigate long-term demographic costs.
*   **Local Government Fiscal Oversight:** The Ministry is addressing fiscal slippages contributed by local governments by enhancing fiscal oversight, clarifying competencies, and adopting multi-year budgetary frameworks. The [[High Council for Public Finances]] reinforces monitoring and forecasting, while the Courts of Auditors provide critical efficiency reports.
*   **Sovereign Credit Rating Response:** In response to Moody's downgrade of France's credit rating to Aa3 in December 2024, the Ministry emphasized the deep and liquid debt market, low short-term sovereign debt distress risk, and the commitment to EU fiscal rules. The Ministry highlighted the robust cyber risk framework and AML/CFT regime as strengths in institutional stability.

### Slovakia
*   **2025 IMF Article IV Consultation:** The Ministry, led by Minister [[Kamenický]], engaged in the 2025 [[Article IV Consultation]] with the [[IMF]]. Discussions concluded in January 2025, with the Executive Board concluding the consultation in March 2025. The Ministry presented a four-year fiscal consolidation plan (2025–2028) aiming to reduce the deficit to 2.2% of GDP by 2028. The IMF supported the agenda but noted the need for additional expenditure-side measures to meet medium-term targets and address rising public debt (projected to reach 74.3% of GDP by 2030).
*   **Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) Management:** In response to the [[Excessive Deficit Procedure]] initiated by the [[European Commission]] in July 2024 due to a fiscal deficit of 5.8% of GDP in 2024, the Ministry implemented consolidation measures. These included reliance on revenue-based measures (e.g., financial transaction tax) and expenditure rationalization, such as reducing subsidies and targeting social spending.
*   **Governance and EU Fund Absorption:** The Ministry focuses on strengthening governance frameworks to address corruption vulnerabilities and judicial independence concerns, which are critical for maintaining investor confidence and ensuring the effective absorption of [[EU Funds]]. The Ministry works alongside the [[Council for Budgetary Responsibility]] to enhance transparency and data adequacy (SDDS Plus).
*   **Structural Reform Implementation:** To mitigate structural headwinds, the Ministry is tasked with diversifying the economy away from heavy dependence on the automotive sector. Initiatives include boosting innovation, increasing labor force participation to counter adverse demographic trends, and addressing long-term fiscal sustainability risks from aging populations, which could increase fiscal burdens by 5.6% of GDP by 2050 without reform.
*   **Financial Sector Coordination:** The Ministry coordinates with the [[National Bank of Slovakia]] to monitor financial stability. While the banking sector remains resilient with high capital adequacy, the Ministry addresses vulnerabilities in commercial real estate and household debt through macroprudential frameworks, including the adoption of countercyclical capital buffers.
