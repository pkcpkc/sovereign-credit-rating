# National Judicial Council (Hungary)

## Mandate & Role

The [[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]] serves as a key oversight body within the Hungarian justice system, responsible for safeguarding judicial independence and managing administrative aspects of the judiciary. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the Council's independence and operational integrity are critical indicators of institutional strength and the rule of law.

Recent assessments, including the **2025 Rule of Law Report**, highlight significant pressures on the Council’s independence. These include:
- **Political Influence and Resignations**: The resignation of the Council President in December 2024 following judicial protests against government reforms.
- **Withdrawal from Reform Agreements**: The Council withdrew from a four-party agreement on judicial reforms in January 2025, citing ineffective consultation processes.
- **Legislative Impact**: Adoption of the Fourteenth Amendment to the Fundamental Law and Act XLIX of 2025, which altered age limits for judges (minimum age 35, service until age 70) and introduced reforms perceived as undermining judicial autonomy.
- **Case Allocation Concerns**: While the [[Kúria (Hungary)]] maintains high transparency in case allocation, the Council has faced criticism for failing to improve transparency in lower-instance courts, where discretion by court managers impedes impartiality verification.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **2025 Rule of Law Report (Hungary Chapter)**: The [[European Commission]] identified the National Judicial Council as a focal point for concerns regarding judicial independence, noting stagnation in addressing transparency deficits and increased political pressure.
- **Four-Party Agreement Withdrawal (January 2025)**: The Council officially withdrew from the agreement concerning judicial reforms and salary increases, marking a deterioration in dialogue between the judiciary and the executive branch.
- **Judicial Reform Implementation**: Oversight of the implementation of Act XLIX of 2025, detailing judicial reforms that the [[Venice Commission]] and other international bodies have scrutinized for potential violations of judicial independence standards.
