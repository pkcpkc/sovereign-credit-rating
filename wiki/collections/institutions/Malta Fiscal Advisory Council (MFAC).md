# Malta Fiscal Advisory Council (MFAC)

## Mandate & Role

The Malta Fiscal Advisory Council (MFAC) serves as an independent fiscal council tasked with monitoring and assessing Malta's fiscal policies to ensure compliance with the [[Stability and Growth Pact]] and national fiscal rules. Its primary role is to enhance **[[fiscal sustainability]]** and **[[debt sustainability]]** by providing objective analysis of the government's budgetary plans and execution.

However, recent assessments by the [[European Commission]] highlight significant concerns regarding the institution's operational effectiveness. The MFAC is cited as having "limited independence and effectiveness," which negatively impacts Malta's overall **[[policy credibility and institutional strength]]**. This perceived weakness is considered a risk factor in sovereign credit analysis, as it undermines the robustness of fiscal surveillance and accountability mechanisms within the Maltese governance framework.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Fiscal Surveillance:** The MFAC is expected to monitor adherence to the [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan]], which was endorsed by the [[European Council]] in January 2025. This plan aims to provide a clear fiscal trajectory amid high net expenditure growth.
- **Compliance Monitoring:** The institution plays a role in the context of the [[European Semester]] process, although its impact is currently viewed as constrained by the broader institutional challenges in Malta, including lengthy judicial proceedings and perceived corruption risks.
- **Risk Assessment Context:** The MFAC's activities are scrutinized in light of Malta's specific fiscal vulnerabilities, such as low tax revenue reliance on corporate income tax, significant VAT compliance gaps, and long-term pressures from an aging population projected to increase age-related expenditure from 17% to 26% of GDP by 2070.
