# Federal Planning Bureau

## Mandate & Role

The **Federal Planning Bureau** (FPB) is a key Belgian government institution responsible for providing independent macroeconomic forecasting and economic analysis. It plays a critical role in the country's fiscal governance and sovereign credit profile by serving as the primary source of independent macroeconomic projections used to assess the sustainability of public finances.

In the context of sovereign credit ratings and economic surveillance, the FPB is cited by the [[European Commission]] as a strength of Belgium's policy credibility and institutional strength. Its independent forecasting capacity contrasts with weaknesses in other domestic oversight bodies, such as the [[High Council of Finance]], which lacks sufficient autonomy and resources. The FPB's data and projections are integral to the evaluation of Belgium's [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan]] and its compliance with the [[EU Economic Governance Framework (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263)]].

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Independent Macroeconomic Forecasting**: The FPB produces regular macroeconomic forecasts that serve as a benchmark for government budget planning. These forecasts are critical for assessing [[fiscal sustainability]] and [[debt sustainability]]. The Commission notes discrepancies between government projections and Commission forecasts, highlighting the importance of the FPB's independent stance.
*   **Support for Fiscal-Structural Planning**: The FPB's analytical work underpins the submission and assessment of Belgium's [[Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan]] (2025-2029). It provides the economic context for structural reforms related to [[pension reform]], [[tax reform]], and [[labour market participation]].
*   **Analysis of Structural Trends**: The Bureau contributes to the analysis of long-term economic challenges affecting [[Belgium]], including:
    *   [[Ageing costs]] and their impact on public expenditure.
    *   [[Productivity growth]] trends and innovation diffusion.
    *   The impact of the [[tax-wedge on labour]] on [[competitiveness]] and formal economic activity.
