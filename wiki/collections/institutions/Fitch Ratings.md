# Fitch Ratings

## Mandate & Role

Fitch Ratings is a global credit rating agency involved in assessing sovereign credit risk. Its primary mandate is to assess the capacity and willingness of sovereign issuers to honor their debt obligations, distinguishing between Local-Currency (LC) and Foreign-Currency (FC) Issuer Default Ratings (IDRs).

In the context of Hungary's economic landscape, Fitch serves as a key market participant whose assessments influence risk premia and bond spreads. The agency monitors factors such as debt sustainability, fiscal performance, and institutional strength to determine creditworthiness. Fitch's methodology is anchored in four analytical pillars: **Structural Features**, **Macroeconomic Performance/Policies/Prospects**, **Public Finances**, and **External Finances**.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Sovereign Rating Methodology (Effective Sept 2025)**: Fitch utilizes a quantitative **Sovereign Rating Model (SRM)** based on Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression of 18 key variables. This model serves as the starting point for ratings, with **Structural Features** carrying the highest weight (53.7%), followed by Public Finances (19.1%), External Finances (17.3%), and Macroeconomic Performance (9.8%). The SRM output is adjusted via a **Qualitative Overlay (QO)** to account for factors such as geopolitical risk, financial sector stability, and policy credibility, with adjustments capped at ±3 notches.
*   **Climate Risk Integration**: Fitch has introduced **Sovereign Climate Vulnerability Signals (Climate.VS)** as a screening tool. Sovereigns with a Climate.VS score of 50 or higher in 2035 are subject to additional analysis regarding physical risks (e.g., heatwaves, floods) and transition risks (e.g., fossil fuel dependence).
*   **Hungary Sovereign Assessment**: Fitch is cited alongside the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) and [[S&P Capital IQ]] in the [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV Consultation for Hungary. The agency's perspective is relevant to the assessment of high risk premia and widening bond spreads, which indicate market concerns regarding Hungary's debt affordability.
*   **Market Sentiment Indicator**: The presence of Fitch in the context highlights the role of private credit rating agencies in signaling fiscal risks, particularly concerning Hungary's elevated public debt (73.5% of GDP in 2024) and the potential loss of EU funding.
*   **Financial Sector Monitoring**: Fitch employs the **Macro-Prudential Indicator (MPI)** and **Banking Systemic Indicator (BSI)** to monitor macro-prudential risks.
*   **Recovery Ratings**: For issuers rated 'B+' or below, Fitch incorporates a view on potential recoveries via Recovery Ratings (RRs) to notch instrument ratings up or down from the IDR.
