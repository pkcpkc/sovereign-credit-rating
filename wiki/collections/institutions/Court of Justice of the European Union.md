# Court of Justice of the European Union

## Mandate & Role

The **Court of Justice of the European Union** (CJEU) serves as the supreme judicial authority of the European Union, tasked with ensuring the uniform interpretation and application of EU law. In the context of sovereign credit rating and country risk analysis, the CJEU plays a critical role in safeguarding the rule of law, judicial independence, and the effective oversight of national authorities. It acts as a key counterbalance to member state governments, particularly in cases involving the violation of EU principles, such as the independence of the judiciary, anti-corruption frameworks, the protection of fundamental rights, and the integrity of the internal market. Its interventions and rulings are monitored as indicators of institutional strength, legal certainty, and the resilience of checks and balances within member states, which directly influence geopolitical and political stability assessments.

## Key Activities & Reports

The CJEU is referenced in the **2025 Rule of Law Reports** for **Hungary**, **Malta**, and **Poland** as a pivotal entity in the EU's response to systemic rule of law concerns. Key activities and contexts include:

### Hungary: Systemic Rule of Law Challenges
- **Rule of Law Enforcement**: The Court is invoked in the context of addressing deteriorating judicial independence and weak institutional checks and balances in Hungary. The European Commission's report highlights the CJEU's role in challenging national measures that undermine legal certainty, such as the extensive use of emergency powers and the lack of effective oversight in secret surveillance.
- **Judicial Independence Cases**: The Court is relevant to ongoing disputes regarding the independence of the Hungarian judiciary, including reforms to judicial appointments, the resignation of the National Judicial Council President, and the enforcement of uniform case law by the *Kúria*. The CJEU's potential involvement in assessing the compatibility of these national reforms with EU law is a significant risk factor.
- **Anti-Corruption and Oversight**: The CJEU is part of the broader ecosystem of accountability, alongside bodies like GRECO and the Venice Commission, to address the high perception of corruption in Hungary. The Court's jurisdiction may be sought to challenge the lack of track record in high-level corruption convictions and the obstacles faced by the Integrity Authority.
- **Civil Society and Media Freedom**: The Court is implicated in protecting civil society space and media pluralism, which are under threat from regulatory pressure, discriminatory taxes, and investigations by the Sovereignty Protection Office. The CJEU's stance on measures restricting foreign funding for CSOs and affecting media freedom is critical for assessing political stability.

### Malta: Investor Citizenship and Institutional Integrity
- **Investor Citizenship Scheme Ruling**: On **April 29, 2025**, the CJEU ruled that Malta’s investor citizenship scheme is contrary to EU Law. The Court characterized the scheme as the "commercialisation" of Union citizenship. This ruling is a critical indicator of past regulatory weaknesses and financial sector health risks, following a referral by the European Commission in 2023.
- **Judicial Efficiency and Independence**: While perceived judicial independence in Malta remains high, the CJEU's oversight context is relevant to ongoing constitutional reforms involving the appointment of the Chief Justice. The Court's role supports the broader EU mechanism for monitoring the independence of the justice sector, although primary challenges in Malta currently relate to procedural efficiency and backlog.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework**: The CJEU’s jurisprudence informs the assessment of Malta’s anti-corruption efforts, particularly regarding the independence of prosecutorial authorities (Office of the Attorney General) and the effectiveness of bodies like the Permanent Commission Against Corruption.

### Poland: Constitutional Crisis and Judicial Independence
- **Constitutional Tribunal Infringement**: The CJEU is central to the ongoing constitutional crisis in Poland. The Government continues to refuse to publish Constitutional Tribunal judgments, asserting the body lacks legitimacy, which has triggered an ongoing infringement procedure by the European Commission. In **March 2025**, an Advocate General delivered an opinion on the Constitutional Tribunal infringement case, further escalating the legal pressure on Warsaw to align with EU rule of law standards.
- **Judicial Independence Reforms**: The Court’s role is critical in evaluating the compatibility of Polish legislative amendments regarding the National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) and the Constitutional Tribunal. While Parliament adopted laws addressing these bodies, they remain pending before the Constitutional Tribunal. The CJEU monitors the implementation of the Action Plan on the Rule of Law, specifically regarding the status of judges appointed by the post-2017 NCJ and the restructuring of the Supreme Court (including the dismantling of the Chamber of Extraordinary Control).
- **Prosecution Service Independence**: The CJEU provides the legal framework under which the separation of the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General is being assessed. Although a draft law was approved in late 2024 incorporating Venice Commission recommendations, the final implementation and its alignment with EU standards regarding the functional independence of the prosecution service remain under scrutiny.
- **Media Freedom and Civil Society**: The Court's jurisprudence supports the evaluation of media law reforms aimed at implementing the European Media Freedom Act, particularly concerning the independence of the media regulator (KRRiT). The CJEU’s stance on measures affecting civil society space, which was re-rated from "obstructed" to "narrowed" in March 2025, remains a key factor in assessing Poland’s institutional health.
