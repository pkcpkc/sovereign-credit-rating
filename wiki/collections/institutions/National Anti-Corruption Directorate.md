# National Anti-Corruption Directorate

## Mandate & Role

The National Anti-Corruption Directorate (DNA) is the primary Romanian institution responsible for investigating and prosecuting high-level corruption cases. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the DNA serves as a critical pillar of the **anti-corruption framework** and **accountability** mechanisms. Its effectiveness directly influences perceptions of **policy credibility** and **institutional strength**. While the agency maintains operational stability and a positive record in securing convictions against high-ranking officials, its impact is subject to systemic legal challenges, particularly regarding recent case-law on the **statute of limitations** issued by the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]], which has led to the discontinuation of numerous cases and annulment of convictions.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Implementation of Anti-Corruption Strategy**: The DNA is a central actor in the execution of the **2021-2025 National Anti-Corruption Strategy**, which the European Commission reports is on track. A new strategy is currently under preparation based on ex-post evaluations of the current framework.
- **Prosecutorial Operations**: The Directorate maintains a stable occupancy rate in its prosecution services despite existing vacancies. It continues to secure convictions in high-level corruption cases, contributing to the strength of Romania's accountability mechanisms.
- **Legal Challenges and Impunity Risks**: Recent rulings by the [[High Court of Cassation and Justice]] regarding *lex mitior* have significantly impacted the DNA's caseload, leading to the annulment of convictions due to expired limitation periods. This has raised concerns about impunity and compatibility with **European Union** law, prompting the HCCJ to seek clarification from the [[Court of Justice of the European Union]].
- **Institutional Safeguards**: Efforts are underway to strengthen safeguards for high-ranking prosecutors, including legislative steps to extend mandates from three to five years, aiming to bolster **judicial independence** and reduce political pressure on the agency.
