# Bank of Italy

## Mandate & Role

The **Bank of Italy** serves as the central bank of the Italian Republic and is a key component of the **European System of Central Banks** (ESCB) alongside the **European Central Bank** (ECB). Its primary mandate includes conducting monetary policy within the Eurozone, maintaining price stability, and ensuring the stability of the financial system.

In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the Bank of Italy operates under the independent mandate of the ECB, which helps mitigate risks of direct fiscal dominance. The institution plays a critical role in monitoring the health of the domestic banking sector, which has shown improved resilience with strong capital adequacy ratios and declining non-performing loans (2.7%-2.8%). However, the Bank oversees a sector with high exposure to domestic sovereign debt, creating a potential "doom loop" risk between bank health and sovereign debt sustainability. The Bank of Italy also contributes to macroeconomic surveillance by providing data and analysis on inflation, which is expected to stabilize around the ECB’s 2% target, and on structural economic indicators such as productivity growth and labor market dynamics.

A key strength cited in international assessments, such as the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation, is the Bank's strong supervisory capacity. The Bank is instrumental in the establishment of the national macroprudential policy authority and plays a central role in strengthening the **Anti-Money Laundering/Counter-Financing of Terrorism** (AML/CFT) framework, addressing vulnerabilities among Less Significant Institutions (LSIs).

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Monetary Policy Implementation**: Executes ECB monetary policy decisions, contributing to the stability of the Euro as a global reserve currency, which lowers borrowing costs for Italy compared to a standalone national currency scenario.
- **Financial Sector Supervision**: Monitors the Italian banking sector's exposure to sovereign debt and ensures compliance with capital requirements. The sector's profitability has recovered, supporting internal capital generation. The Bank oversees the activation of the **Systemic Risk Buffer** (SyRB), increased to 1%, and monitors vulnerabilities among Less Significant Institutions (LSIs) and firm exposures to potential trade shocks.
- **Macroeconomic Analysis**: Provides critical data on Italy’s economic performance, including GDP growth (0.7% in 2024), inflation trends (projected to average 1.7% in 2025 and converge to 2% in 2026), and external liquidity positions. Italy maintains a positive Net International Investment Position (NIIP) of approx. 15.3% of GDP in 2024, a metric closely monitored by the Bank.
- **Fiscal-Monetary Coordination**: While independent, the Bank operates in an environment with high public debt (135.3% of GDP in 2024) and significant interest expenditure. The Bank's role in managing liquidity and debt servicing mechanics is crucial for maintaining confidence in Italy’s debt sustainability, especially given the high share of sovereign debt held by domestic households and institutions.
- **Regulatory Framework Enhancement**: Collaborates with other financial authorities to address transnational aspects of corruption and enhance inter-agency collaboration on corruption detection, as highlighted in recent IMF reports.
