# Ministry for National Economy

## Mandate & Role

The **Ministry for National Economy** is the primary Hungarian government body responsible for formulating and implementing fiscal policy, managing public debt, and overseeing economic stability. In the context of sovereign credit risk and international surveillance, the Ministry is the key authority engaged in dialogue with the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) and the [[European Commission]]. It bears responsibility for the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), deficit reduction targets, and structural reforms aimed at improving productivity and competitiveness. The Ministry manages significant fiscal challenges, including elevated public debt levels (73.5% of GDP in 2024), high gross financing needs, and contingent liabilities from state-owned enterprises (SOEs). It also navigates complex geopolitical and regulatory environments, including the suspension of €19 billion in [[European Union]] funds due to rule of law concerns, and the implementation of regulatory measures such as price and interest caps that have been criticized by the IMF for distorting market signals.

## Key Activities & Reports

The Ministry was a central participant in the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation** for Hungary, concluding discussions with IMF staff in June 2025. Key activities and responsibilities highlighted in the IMF Staff Report include:

- **Fiscal Adjustment and Debt Sustainability**: The Ministry is tasked with implementing additional fiscal adjustments estimated at ~2% of GDP over 2025-2028 to ensure debt sustainability and reduce the fiscal deficit below 3% by 2027. This involves rationalizing spending (phasing out energy subsidies and administrative overhead) and enhancing revenue quality.
- **Regulatory Policy**: The Ministry oversees regulatory measures including price, interest, and margin caps. The IMF has advised phasing out these distortive measures to improve monetary policy transmission and market efficiency.
- **State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs)**: The Ministry manages the state’s presence in key sectors. The IMF highlights risks related to SOE liabilities, government guarantees (14% of GDP), and off-balance sheet financing vehicles, urging improved transparency and monitoring.
- **Structural Reforms**: The Ministry is responsible for reforms to boost productivity, enhance energy security, and reduce barriers to factor mobility. These reforms are critical for unlocking suspended [[European Union]] funds and addressing stagnant growth.
- **Engagement with International Bodies**: The Ministry engages in constructive dialogue with the [[International Monetary Fund]] and faces scrutiny from the [[European Commission]] regarding rule of law compliance and access to EU funding.
