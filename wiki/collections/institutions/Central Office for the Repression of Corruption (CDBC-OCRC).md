# Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC)

## Mandate & Role

The Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC) is a specialized Belgian government body responsible for investigating high-level corruption cases. It plays a critical role in Belgium's anti-corruption framework, cooperating with the prosecution service to address structural issues identified by international bodies such as GRECO and the European Commission. The institution is central to maintaining institutional integrity, though its operations are subject to scrutiny regarding potential political interference and information leaks in sensitive investigations.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Investigative Operations**: The CDBC-OCRC handles high-level corruption cases, where allegations of information leaks and undue political influence have been noted in the 2025 Rule of Law Report.
- **Cooperation with Prosecution**: The office continues to cooperate with the prosecution service, which is cited as a strength in maintaining geopolitical and political stability despite broader institutional challenges.
- **Structural Reforms and Context**: While the CDBC-OCRC operates within the current framework, the Belgian Government has announced the creation of a new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) and a specialised prosecution service for financial crime. These developments aim to address gaps in the existing anti-corruption structures, though concerns remain about the efficacy of current integrity policies and the verification of asset declarations.
- **International Monitoring**: The performance and integrity of the CDBC-OCRC are monitored as part of the broader assessment of Belgium's compliance with rule of law standards, including supervision by the Council of Europe regarding structural issues identified by the European Court of Human Rights.
