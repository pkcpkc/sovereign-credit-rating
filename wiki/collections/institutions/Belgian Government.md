# Belgian Government

## Mandate & Role

The Belgian Government is the executive branch of the Federal State of Belgium, responsible for implementing laws, managing public finances, and overseeing key state institutions including the justice system and anti-corruption frameworks. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk, the Government plays a central role in maintaining geopolitical and political stability, ensuring policy credibility, and upholding institutional checks and balances. Its actions directly influence sovereign credit rating factors such as institutional strength, accountability, and the rule of law, which are critical for investor confidence and debt sustainability.

Key areas of responsibility include:
- **Judicial Administration**: Managing the budget and resources of the justice system, with recent intentions to transfer budgetary management powers from the executive to the judiciary to enhance independence.
- **Anti-Corruption and Integrity**: Overseeing the implementation of integrity policies for public office holders, including asset declarations and lobbying regulations, though progress in these areas remains limited.
- **Human Rights and Civic Space**: Ensuring compliance with international obligations, including judgments from the [[European Court of Human Rights]], and protecting civic freedoms such as the right to demonstrate and access to public documents.
- **Media Regulation**: Supporting the operational independence of media regulators and public service broadcasters while navigating budgetary constraints.

## Key Activities & Reports

The Government's activities and performance are closely monitored through the [[European Commission]]'s Rule of Law Reports and assessments by international bodies such as GRECO and the [[Council of Europe]]. Key recent activities and challenges include:

- **Rule of Law Compliance**: The Government faces criticism for limited progress in ensuring compliance with final court rulings, with 17 leading [[European Court of Human Rights]] judgments pending implementation as of January 2025. It remains under supervision by the [[Council of Europe]] (Committee of Ministers) for structural issues identified in the *Camara v. Belgium* case.
- **Justice System Reform**: Efforts to address backlogs and improve efficiency are ongoing, including addressing criticisms from the [[Belgian Court of Audit]] regarding the digitalisation strategy of the justice department. The Government has taken steps to decriminalise defamation and introduce harsher penalties for crimes against journalists.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework**: While cooperation between the [[Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (CDBC-OCRC)]] and prosecution services continues, concerns persist regarding political interference and information leaks in high-level corruption cases. The Government has planned the creation of a new financial investigation service (FIOD-SRFF) but has not yet finalised draft lobbying rules or binding "revolving door" regulations.
- **Political Stability**: The period between June 2024 and February 2025 was characterised by a caretaker Government, highlighting vulnerabilities related to political fragmentation and delays in government formation.
- **Civic Space and Rights**: The Government's handling of civic space has been criticised, with proposals for judicial demonstration bans raising concerns about restrictions on the right to protest. The Federal Human Rights Institute (FIRM-IFDH) and other bodies monitor these developments.
