# Financial Supervision Authority (KNF)

## Mandate & Role

The Financial Supervision Authority (KNF) is the primary regulatory body responsible for overseeing financial stability and the banking sector in Poland. Its role is critical in maintaining the resilience of the financial system, which the IMF assesses as well-capitalized and liquid, with buffers above regulatory requirements. The KNF monitors key risks to financial stability, including litigation risks arising from Swiss Franc (CHF) mortgages and potential disputes regarding the WIBOR benchmark interest rate. The authority also addresses distortions in private credit provision, such as those caused by tax incentives favoring public debt over private lending, and oversees the management of litigation risks through the establishment of significant provisions by banks.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Banking Sector Oversight**: The KNF ensures that Polish banks maintain low non-performing loan ratios and strong profitability. It monitors the impact of external shocks and domestic fiscal policies on bank balance sheets.
- **Risk Management**: Supervision includes tracking specific legal risks, such as outstanding CHF mortgage litigations and potential WIBOR disputes, ensuring that banks have adequate provisions to cover these liabilities.
- **Policy Recommendations**: In the context of the 2024 Article IV Consultation, the KNF's regulatory environment is evaluated regarding distortions like the bank asset tax. The IMF has recommended eliminating such distortions to support a nascent credit recovery and allow mortgage credit holidays to expire.
- **Financial Stability Monitoring**: The KNF contributes to the assessment of financial sector health, highlighting strengths such as ample foreign exchange reserves and strong bank capitalization, while identifying threats like slow recovery in corporate credit.
