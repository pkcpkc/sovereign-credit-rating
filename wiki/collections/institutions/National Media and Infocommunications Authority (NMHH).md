# National Media and Infocommunications Authority (NMHH)

## Mandate & Role

The National Media and Infocommunications Authority (NMHH) serves as the primary regulatory body for media and infocommunications in Hungary. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the NMHH is a critical entity for assessing institutional quality, rule of law adherence, and political stability. Its mandate involves overseeing media pluralism and enforcing communications regulations; however, it is frequently cited in international assessments as a case study for weakened institutional independence and potential political capture.

## Key Activities & Reports

According to the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Hungary** issued by the **European Commission**, the NMHH has faced significant scrutiny regarding its functional independence:

*   **Lack of Functional Independence**: The European Commission notes that there has been no progress in ensuring the functional independence of the NMHH. The regulator is perceived as lacking autonomy from government influence, which undermines the rule of law framework in Hungary.
*   **State Advertising Distortion**: The NMHH operates within a media landscape where state advertising is disproportionately channeled to pro-government outlets, such as **KESMA**. This uneven distribution contributes to a distorted media environment, affecting media pluralism and creating risks related to political stability and transparency.
*   **Impact on Civil Society and Media**: The regulatory environment overseen by the NMHH contributes to increased pressure on journalists. This includes smear campaigns, selective denial of access to public events, and a broader climate of fear, exacerbated by parallel investigations by the **Sovereignty Protection Office** into journalists and media entities for alleged "foreign influence."
*   **Public Service Media Oversight**: The NMHH's regulatory context extends to public service media, which remains financially and politically dependent on the government, further highlighting the limitations of independent oversight in the Hungarian media sector.
