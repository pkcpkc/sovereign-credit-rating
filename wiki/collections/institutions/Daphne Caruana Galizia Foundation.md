# Daphne Caruana Galizia Foundation

## Mandate & Role

The **Daphne Caruana Galizia Foundation** is a civil society organization established in the wake of the assassination of journalist [[Daphne Caruana Galizia]]. Its primary mandate involves advocating for the rule of law, judicial independence, and anti-corruption measures in **Malta**. The Foundation serves as a critical voice in civil society, monitoring government accountability and pushing for transparency in public affairs. In the context of sovereign credit risk and institutional strength, the Foundation represents the civil society pillar that highlights gaps in political integrity, media freedom, and checks and balances. It actively engages with international bodies such as the [[European Commission]] and the [[Venice Commission]] to underscore risks related to the commercialization of citizenship, lack of transparency in ministerial asset declarations, and threats to independent media governance.

## Key Activities & Reports

The Foundation is prominently cited in the **2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta** as a key stakeholder in the ongoing assessment of Malta’s democratic institutions. Its activities and advocacy efforts contribute to the following areas of concern identified by the [[European Commission]]:

- **Anti-Corruption Oversight**: The Foundation highlights the inadequacy of integrity measures for high-level politicians, noting that ministers' asset declarations remain unpublished and conflict of interest rules are limited. It critiques the weak capacity of the [[Permanent Commission Against Corruption]] (PCAC) and the lack of a robust track record of final judgments in high-level corruption cases.
- **Media Freedom and Pluralism**: The Foundation advocates for the safeguarding of independent governance of public service media, which remains at "very high risk" for political bias. It supports legislative alignment with the [[European Media Freedom Act]] and the protection of journalists, noting that pending legislation to improve access to official documents remains stalled.
- **Checks and Balances**: The Foundation has criticized legislative reforms that limit citizen-led magisterial inquiries, arguing that these changes hinder accountability for high-level offenses. It monitors the narrowing of civil society space and the delayed establishment of a [[National Human Rights Institution]].
- **Investor Citizenship Scheme**: The Foundation has been a vocal critic of the investor citizenship scheme, which the [[Court of Justice of the European Union]] ruled illegal in April 2025, aligning with the Foundation's long-standing advocacy against the "commercialisation" of Union citizenship.
