# Ministry of Finance (Romania)

## Mandate & Role

The Ministry of Finance (Romania) is the primary government body responsible for designing and implementing national fiscal policy, managing public finances, and ensuring compliance with EU economic governance frameworks. In the context of sovereign credit risk, the Ministry plays a central role in fiscal consolidation efforts, particularly through the formulation and execution of the **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)**. The Ministry is tasked with addressing macroeconomic imbalances, including reducing the government deficit (which reached 9.3% of GDP in 2024) and managing public debt levels (54.8% of GDP in 2024). It works in coordination with the [[National Bank of Romania]] to maintain monetary-fiscal stability and with EU institutions, notably the [[European Commission]], to align national budgets with the reformed EU economic governance framework.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTP)**: The Ministry submitted the MTP in October 2024, which was endorsed by the [[European Council]] in January 2025. This plan outlines a seven-year fiscal adjustment period (2025-2031) aimed at restoring fiscal sustainability. Key measures include net expenditure caps, wage and pension freezes, and structural reforms to increase recurrent revenues.
*   **Fiscal Consolidation Measures**: In late 2024, the Ministry implemented emergency measures to save 2.0% of GDP in 2025, including freezing public sector wages and pensions and removing certain tax exemptions. These actions were taken in response to the widening government deficit and to meet the targets set in the MTP.
*   **Tax Framework Review**: The Ministry is responsible for the ongoing review of the tax system to address inefficiencies, such as the high VAT gap (33.7% in 2024) and low tax revenue as a percentage of GDP (26.2%). Challenges include frequent legislative changes and administrative capacity issues.
*   **EU Coordination and Reporting**: The Ministry engages with the [[European Commission]] regarding the **European Semester** process, submitting annual stability and convergence programs. It addresses Council Recommendations, such as those for correcting the excessive deficit, and works to implement reforms under the **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)**, although implementation has faced delays due to administrative bottlenecks.
*   **Debt Management**: The Ministry oversees sovereign debt issuance, including sovereign green bonds, and manages the government's borrowing needs. While public debt has risen rapidly, it remains below the EU average, and the Ministry aims to maintain sustainable debt dynamics through fiscal adjustments.
