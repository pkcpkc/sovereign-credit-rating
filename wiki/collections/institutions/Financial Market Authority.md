# Financial Market Authority

## Mandate & Role

The **Financial Market Authority** (FMA) is the primary independent supervisory body for the financial sector in **Austria**. Its mandate encompasses ensuring the integrity, stability, and transparency of Austrian financial markets. The FMA is recognized as a pillar of institutional strength, contributing significantly to **Sovereign Credit Rating Factors** through its role in maintaining robust regulatory oversight. Its independence is cited as a key strength in assessments of policy credibility and accountability, providing a stable framework for financial supervision that supports the broader economic resilience of the country.

## Key Activities & Reports

The **Financial Market Authority** plays a critical role in monitoring systemic risks, particularly within the banking sector. Key areas of oversight and regulatory activity include:

- **Commercial Real Estate (CRE) Monitoring:** The FMA closely monitors rising **Non-Performing Loans (NPLs)** in the commercial real estate sector. This surveillance is vital for assessing financial sector health and potential contingent liabilities for the sovereign, as highlighted in the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation**.
- **Macroprudential Oversight:** The Authority oversees the implementation of prudent lending standards and macroprudential measures, such as the Systemic Stability Reserve Buffer (SSyRB). In July 2025, borrower-based measures for residential real estate (KIM-V) lapsed, and the CRE buffer became effective, a transition closely watched by international bodies like the **International Monetary Fund**.
- **Banking Sector Stability:** The FMA's oversight contributes to the high capitalization levels of Austrian banks (CET1 ratio of 17.7%), ensuring they maintain strong liquidity buffers and profitability.
- **Regulatory Reporting:** The Authority is referenced in the **2025 Country Report - Austria** by the **European Commission** and the **IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation** as part of the institutional framework supporting the stability of the Austrian banking sector. The IMF has recommended that the FMA continue to make prudent lending standards permanent to prevent a resurgence of macro-financial risks.
