# Venice Commission

## Mandate & Role

The **Venice Commission**, formally the European Commission for Democracy through Law, serves as the advisory body of the **[[Council of Europe]]** on constitutional matters. Its primary mandate involves providing expert opinions on constitutional legislation, focusing on the rule of law, judicial independence, and institutional strength. In the context of sovereign credit rating and country risk analysis, the Commission acts as a critical external validator of institutional credibility. Its assessments of legal frameworks and democratic standards influence perceptions of governance quality, policy stability, and the effectiveness of checks and balances within member states. The Commission's recommendations are frequently integrated into national reform processes, particularly in resolving constitutional crises, ensuring the independence of prosecution and judicial services, and validating the compliance of Justice Laws with European standards.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Judicial Independence Assessment in France**: The Venice Commission is cited as a key reference institution in the **[[European Commission]]**'s **[[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on France]]**. Its expertise informs the evaluation of judicial independence, a core component of institutional strength and policy credibility in France.
*   **Institutional Strength Validation in France**: In the assessment of **[[France]]**, the Commission's standards contribute to the analysis of "checks and balances" and the "independent judiciary supported by the President of the Republic," which are identified as strengths in geopolitical and political stability.
*   **Constitutional and Legislative Review in France**: The Commission reviews legal frameworks such as the status of magistrates and anti-corruption structures (e.g., **[[Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM)]]**, **[[Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)]]**) to ensure they meet democratic standards.
*   **Hungary Judicial Reforms (2025)**: On June 16, 2025, the Venice Commission issued an opinion on judicial and prosecutorial amendments in **[[Hungary]]**. This occurred amid significant tensions regarding judicial independence, including the resignation of the President of the **[[National Judicial Council (Hungary)]]** and reforms that raised judicial age limits. The Commission's role serves as a benchmark for evaluating the democratic legitimacy of these reforms and the extent to which they undermine or uphold the rule of law, impacting Hungary's institutional credibility and legal certainty.
*   **Malta Rule of Law Monitoring (2025)**: The Venice Commission is listed as a key institution in the **[[European Commission]]**'s **[[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta]]**. It serves as a reference point for evaluating Malta's constitutional reforms, particularly those concerning the justice sector and the appointment of the Chief Justice. The Commission's standards are relevant to the assessment of Malta's ongoing efforts to enhance judicial independence and implement European Court of Human Rights judgments, which are critical factors in determining the country's geopolitical stability and policy credibility.
*   **Poland Judicial Independence and Prosecution Reforms (2025)**: The Venice Commission played a central role in Poland's **[[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Poland]]**. Key activities included:
    *   **Consultation on Judicial Status**: Providing consultation on draft legislation addressing the status of approximately 1,600 judges appointed by the post-2017 **[[National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)]]**, involving classification systems and potential removal mechanisms.
    *   **Prosecution Service Independence**: Its recommendations were incorporated into the draft law separating the office of the Minister of Justice from the Prosecutor General, a significant step toward ensuring the functional independence of the prosecution service.
    *   **Supreme Court Structure**: Advising on reforms to the **[[Supreme Court (Poland)]]**, including the dismantling of the Chamber of Extraordinary Control and Public Affairs to strengthen judicial independence.
    *   **Constitutional Crisis Context**: The Commission's standards serve as a backdrop for the ongoing constitutional crisis in Poland, where the government continues to refuse to publish **[[Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)]]** judgments, citing lack of legitimacy, while the Commission's input helps frame EU infringement procedures and domestic legislative adjustments.
*   **Romania Justice Laws Implementation (2025)**: The Venice Commission provided critical benchmarking for Romania's judicial reforms, specifically regarding the **Justice Laws** enacted in 2022.
    *   **Validation of Justice Laws**: The **[[European Commission]]**'s **[[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Romania]]** highlights the completion of the process to implement Venice Commission recommendations on these laws as a significant strength for Romania's geopolitical and political stability.
    *   **Safeguards for Prosecutors**: The Commission's opinions guided legislative steps to extend mandates of high-ranking prosecutors and strengthen safeguards, contributing to improved policy credibility.
    *   **Judicial Independence Metrics**: While Romania progressed on Venice Commission recommendations, the Commission's standards remain a reference for addressing persisting challenges in perceived judicial independence and transparency in disciplinary sanctions.
*   **Slovakia Constitutional and Judicial Oversight (2025)**: The Venice Commission is identified as a key institution in the **[[European Commission]]**'s **[[2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Slovakia]]**.
    *   **Constitutional Amendments**: The Commission provides the standard for evaluating proposed constitutional amendments in Slovakia, which raised concerns regarding the primacy of EU law. These amendments were postponed after the first reading, with the Commission's guidelines serving as a critical reference for ensuring compliance with European democratic standards.
    *   **Judicial Independence Framework**: The Commission's standards are relevant to the assessment of Slovakia's **[[Judicial Council (Slovakia)]]**, particularly concerning unresolved safeguards for the dismissal of its members. The lack of progress in aligning these safeguards with Venice Commission recommendations contributes to the low perceived judicial independence and institutional risks noted in country risk analysis.
    *   **Institutional Checks and Balances**: The Commission's framework informs the analysis of institutional weaknesses, such as the broad powers of the **[[Prosecutor General’s Office (Slovakia)]]** to annul final decisions, which undermines the rule of law and policy credibility.
