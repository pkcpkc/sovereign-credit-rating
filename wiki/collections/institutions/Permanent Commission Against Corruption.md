# Permanent Commission Against Corruption

## Mandate & Role

The **Permanent Commission Against Corruption** (PCAC) is a Maltese government body mandated to monitor and combat corruption within the public sector. It plays a critical role in the national anti-corruption framework, which is a key determinant in sovereign credit risk assessments and rule of law evaluations. 

Despite its mandate, the PCAC has been identified as having **weak capacity to achieve concrete results**. The institution operates with limited part-time staff and has faced significant scrutiny regarding its operational readiness. Notably, the PCAC has **never referred a case for prosecution**, raising concerns about its effectiveness in ensuring institutional accountability. Its limited impact contributes to the high perceived levels of corruption in Malta (Corruption Perceptions Index score of 46/100), which poses a threat to policy credibility and institutional strength.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Operational Limitations**: The Commission has cited insufficient resources as a barrier to achieving its objectives. It lacks a robust track record of final judgments or successful prosecutions in high-level corruption cases.
- **National Anti-Fraud and Corruption Strategy**: The PCAC is involved in the implementation of this strategy; however, there is noted **lack of transparency** in its execution.
- **Rule of Law Monitoring**: The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report highlights the PCAC's inability to demonstrate concrete results, contrasting it with the increased resources in investigative bodies like the Financial Crime Investigation Department (FCID) and the Office of the Attorney General.
- **Institutional Gaps**: The Commission's limited oversight mechanisms and failure to prosecute have been cited as threats to accountability, particularly regarding high-level politicians and officials whose asset declarations remain unpublished.
