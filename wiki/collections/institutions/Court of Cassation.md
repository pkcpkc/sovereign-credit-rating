# Court of Cassation

## Mandate & Role

The **Court of Cassation** is one of the highest judicial authorities in both **Belgium** and **France**, playing a critical role in maintaining the integrity, independence, and efficiency of the justice systems in these jurisdictions. In the context of sovereign credit risk and institutional strength, the Court acts as a key pillar of the rule of law framework. It contributes to **policy credibility and institutional strength** by upholding judicial independence, ensuring the government respects legal boundaries, and enforcing final court judgments.

In **Belgium**, the Court is central to the discourse on judicial independence, which remains high in public perception. It actively engages in structural reforms aimed at improving efficiency and management, while participating in inter-institutional checks and balances to enforce compliance with judicial decisions.

In **France**, the Court operates within a complex landscape of ongoing reforms. While the 2023 reform of the status of magistrates aims to enhance judicial independence and diversity, perceived judicial independence remains average. The Court functions amidst challenges such as personal attacks on magistrates (particularly regarding election-related cases) and significant case backlogs. Its performance and efficiency are key indicators monitored in the **European Commission**’s **2025 Rule of Law Report**, assessing France’s institutional strength, accountability, and adherence to the rule of law.

## Key Activities & Reports

### Belgium
- **Enforcement of Court Rulings**: On **July 19, 2024**, the Court of Cassation issued a **joint memorandum** alongside the other two highest courts in Belgium. This document formally required the **Belgian Government** to respect final court judgments, addressing concerns about **compliance with court rulings** and highlighting limited progress in ensuring public authorities adhere to final decisions.
- **Judicial Reform Critique**: The Court has voiced criticism regarding the Government's intention to transfer budgetary management powers of the justice administration from the executive to the judiciary. Specifically, the Court of Cassation, along with the **High Council for Justice**, criticized the proposal for linking these managerial changes to disciplinary reforms, arguing that such links could undermine judicial independence.
- **Addressing Backlogs and Efficiency**: The Court operates within a broader justice system facing structural resource deficiencies and long-standing efficiency issues, such as excessive length of proceedings. Its role is integral to ongoing reforms funded by the **Recovery and Resilience Facility** aimed at improving statistical collection and reducing delays.
- **Rule of Law Monitoring**: The Court's operations are monitored in the **European Commission**’s **2025 Rule of Law Report**, assessing the justice system's performance under the four pillars of the rule of law, particularly in relation to political party financing, lobbying transparency, and anti-corruption frameworks.

### France
- **Judicial Independence Under Pressure**: Despite the implementation of the 2023 magistrate status reform, perceived judicial independence remains average. Magistrates face severe personal attacks and threats, particularly after rulings on national elections. The **Superior Council of the Magistracy** and the **President of the Republic** have actively reaffirmed judicial independence in response to these pressures.
- **Digitalization and Efficiency Challenges**: Progress continues in digitalizing judicial procedures (e.g., the **Portalis** tool), but magistrates report slow implementation and technical issues. Case resolution times remain high (354 days for civil/commercial cases; 1,335 days for bribery cases). This has prompted government-led "flash missions" (published May 2025) to propose solutions and explore out-of-court dispute resolution.
- **Rule of Law Monitoring**: The Court's standing is assessed in the **European Commission**’s **2025 Rule of Law Report**, which highlights the court system's role in checks and balances, legislative stability, and accountability. The report notes that while the legal framework is strong, practical implementation faces hurdles, including high regulatory burdens for SMEs and legal uncertainty due to rapid legislative changes.
