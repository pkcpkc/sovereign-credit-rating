# High Council for Justice

## Mandate & Role

The **High Council for Justice** is a key institution within the Belgian justice system, tasked with overseeing judicial management and promoting judicial independence. Its primary role involves addressing structural inefficiencies within the courts, such as backlogs and the excessive length of proceedings. The Council plays a critical part in the dialogue between the judiciary and the executive branch, particularly regarding the proposed transfer of budgetary management powers for the justice administration from the executive to the judiciary.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Addressing Judicial Backlogs**: In November 2024, the High Council for Justice published a specific report analyzing and addressing case backlogs at the Court of Appeal of Brussels. This activity is part of broader efforts to improve judicial efficiency, a persistent concern in Belgium's rule of law landscape.
*   **Judicial Efficiency and Statistics**: The Council has published data on criminal case durations to highlight efficiency issues. It is involved in initiatives funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility aimed at improving statistical collection within the justice system.
*   **Budgetary and Disciplinary Reforms**: The Council has actively criticized the government's approach to linking efficiency reforms with disciplinary measures. While the government intends to transfer budgetary management to the judiciary, the High Council for Justice, alongside the Court of Cassation, has raised concerns about the implications of these reforms for judicial independence and autonomy.
*   **Recruitment and Resource Management**: The Council is involved in efforts to address structural resource deficiencies, including the publication of open positions for magistrates and court staff to improve transparency in recruitment, although vacancies often remain unfilled due to unattractive working conditions.
