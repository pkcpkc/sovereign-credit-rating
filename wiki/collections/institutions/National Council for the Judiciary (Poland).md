# National Council for the Judiciary (Poland)

## Mandate & Role

The National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) is the constitutional body responsible for safeguarding the independence of the courts and judges in Poland. It plays a central role in the country's rule of law framework and is a key institution under surveillance by the [[European Commission]] regarding judicial independence and institutional strength.

In the context of sovereign credit rating and country risk analysis, the NCJ is critical to assessing **policy credibility** and **institutional strength**. Controversies surrounding its composition and appointments are cited as significant threats to legal certainty and investment protection, contributing to a "constituent crisis" and political polarization. The legitimacy of the NCJ's post-2017 composition remains disputed, affecting the perceived independence of the judiciary and the rule of law environment.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Judicial Appointments and Legitimacy Disputes**: The NCJ's post-2017 composition has been the subject of ongoing disputes. Approximately 1,600 judges were appointed at the request of this composition. The [[European Commission]] and [[Venice Commission]] have highlighted concerns regarding the independence of these appointments. In 2025, the [[Ministry of Justice]] proposed a draft law to address the status of these judges, involving a classification system and potential removal, pending consultation with the Venice Commission.
- **Legislative Reforms and Constitutional Review**: Legislation amending the laws governing the NCJ was adopted by the Polish Parliament but remains pending before the [[Constitutional Tribunal (Poland)]] due to a presidential referral. These reforms aim to align the body with EU rule of law standards and address long-standing concerns raised in the Action Plan on the Rule of Law.
- **Impact on Rule of Law Metrics**: The NCJ is a focal point in the **2025 Rule of Law Report**, where its disputed appointment processes are cited as a threat to policy credibility. The resolution of NCJ-related issues is considered essential for restoring confidence in judicial institutions and mitigating risks associated with legislative instability.
