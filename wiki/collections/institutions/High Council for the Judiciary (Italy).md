# High Council for the Judiciary (Italy)

## Mandate & Role

The **High Council for the Judiciary** (Consiglio Superiore della Magistratura) is the primary constitutional body responsible for ensuring the independence of the judiciary in Italy. It plays a critical role in the administration of the justice system, including the recruitment, assignment, promotion, and disciplinary measures for magistrates. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and country risk analysis, the Council serves as a key indicator of **judicial independence**, **institutional checks and balances**, and the efficiency of the **justice system**. Its actions and opinions influence perceptions of **rule of law** stability, particularly regarding the separation of powers and the autonomy of the judiciary from political interference.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Implementation of Justice Reform (2024–2025)**: Following the adoption of legislative decrees in March 2024 implementing a comprehensive reform of the justice system, the High Council has actively taken forward tasks under the implementing legislation. This includes efforts to address persistent staff shortages and improve **judicial efficiency**.
- **Opposition to Constitutional Reform (January 2025)**: The Council issued a critical opinion on the draft constitutional reform aimed at separating the careers of judges and prosecutors. It argued that the reform undermines judicial autonomy and proper representation within the Council. Despite this opposition, the **Chamber of Deputies** approved the draft in January 2025, leading to further parliamentary scrutiny and reservations from judicial bodies.
- **Digitalisation of Criminal Courts (2025)**: Supported the mandatory use of the digital case management system (*APP*) in criminal courts and prosecutors' offices, which entered into force on January 1, 2025. While aimed at reducing the case backlog, technical challenges led to derogations for some courts.
- **Regulation of Judicial Appointments (2024)**: Approved a directive concerning out-of-tenure appointments for magistrates in September 2024, contributing to the management of judicial personnel and recruitment efforts to address a reported 17% shortage of magistrates.
