# Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (Poland)

## Mandate & Role

The Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (Poland), also known by its Polish acronym CBA, is a specialized law enforcement agency responsible for combating corruption within the public sector. In the context of sovereign credit risk and rule of law assessment, the Bureau plays a critical role in enforcing accountability and deterring high-level corruption, which are key factors in evaluating Poland's institutional strength and policy credibility. However, the Bureau's operational future is currently under significant review, with legislative plans to dissolve the agency and integrate its functions into a new Central Office for Combating Corruption within the police structure. This structural change is viewed through the lens of the **European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)**, which became fully operational in Poland in 2025, alongside ongoing concerns regarding the lack of a comprehensive national anti-corruption strategy.

## Key Activities & Reports

*   **Structural Dismantling and Integration**: According to the *2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Poland*, a new Central Office for Combating Corruption is scheduled to be established within the police, effectively dissolving the Central Anti-Corruption Bureau. This transition aims to streamline anti-corruption efforts but raises questions about institutional continuity and independence.
*   **Investigation of Large-Scale Cases**: Despite structural uncertainties, the Bureau has been involved in investigating large-scale corruption cases involving public officials and politicians. These activities contribute to the "accountability" factor in sovereign credit ratings, although systemic challenges remain.
*   **Public Procurement Oversight**: The Bureau's efforts intersect with risks in **public procurement**, an area identified as highly vulnerable to corruption due to significant single-bid rates and potential bid-rigging. Investigations into the misuse of EU funds and procurement irregularities are part of the broader enforcement landscape.
*   **Interaction with International Bodies**: The Bureau's performance and the broader anti-corruption framework are monitored by international entities such as **GRECO** (Group of States Against Corruption) and **Transparency International**, which track compliance with anti-corruption standards and perception indices.
*   **Legislative Context**: The Bureau operates in a environment characterized by broad immunities for top executives, which hinders effective criminal accountability. The absence of lobbying rules and a cohesive anti-corruption strategy limits the efficacy of enforcement actions, as noted in the European Commission's assessment.
