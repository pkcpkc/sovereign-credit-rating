# Public Service Commission

## Mandate & Role

The **Public Service Commission** is a key institutional body within the Maltese framework responsible for the governance and integrity of the civil service. In the context of sovereign credit risk and rule of law analysis, the Commission serves as a critical node in the **Checks and Balances** and **Institutional Accountability** pillars. Its effectiveness and independence are indicators of the broader strength of public administration, which directly impacts policy credibility, fiscal management, and the efficiency of legal certainty. While specific operational details are not extensively detailed in the provided summary, its inclusion in the EU Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Report alongside the **Office of the Attorney General**, **Permanent Commission Against Corruption**, and **National Audit Office** underscores its relevance to the country's institutional health and democratic stability.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Rule of Law Monitoring**: The Public Service Commission is identified by the **European Commission** in the *2025 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Malta* as a key institution within the Maltese justice and anti-corruption architecture.
- **Contextual Challenges**: The broader environment in which the Commission operates faces significant threats, including perceived high levels of corruption in the public sector (CPI score 46/100) and weaknesses in public procurement integrity. These systemic issues indirectly pressure the Public Service Commission to ensure strict adherence to meritocratic and transparent hiring and management practices to mitigate **Contingent Liabilities** arising from public funds mismanagement.
- **Institutional Independence**: The Commission's role contributes to the high perceived judicial independence among the general public and companies, a strength noted in Malta's geopolitical and political stability profile.
