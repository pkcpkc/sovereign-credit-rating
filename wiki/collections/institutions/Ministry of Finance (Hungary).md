# Ministry of Finance (Hungary)

## Mandate & Role

The Ministry of Finance (Hungary) is the primary government body responsible for formulating and implementing fiscal policy, managing public debt, and overseeing the national budget. In the context of sovereign credit risk and economic surveillance, the Ministry plays a central role in Hungary's compliance with EU fiscal rules, though its operations are characterized by significant regulatory volatility and limited institutional independence.

Key aspects of its mandate and operational context include:
- **Fiscal Policy Formulation**: The Ministry manages the state budget, which has been adopted early in the year with frequent revisions, reducing reliability and policy predictability. It oversees a tax base that is narrow, with heavy reliance on consumption taxes, and implements sector-specific taxes (e.g., on banking, retail, energy).
- **Debt Management**: The Ministry manages public debt, which rose to 73.5% of GDP in 2024. It faces high debt-servicing costs due to implicit interest rates close to 6% and manages high gross financing needs.
- **EU Compliance & Funding**: The Ministry is responsible for submitting Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plans to the [[Council of the European Union]] and managing the implementation of EU funds, including the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) and Cohesion Policy funds. However, implementation has stalled (0% milestones achieved as of mid-2025) due to anti-corruption deficiencies and weak control mechanisms.
- **Regulatory Environment**: The Ministry operates within a framework characterized by frequent regulatory changes and the use of emergency decrees, which reduce policy credibility. It also oversees opaque public fund management structures, including off-budget public interest trusts and funds that operate with limited oversight.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan**: Submitted and endorsed by the [[Council of the European Union]] in March 2025, aiming to provide a medium-term budgetary framework despite criticisms regarding its realism.
- **Budget Submission**: The 2026 budget law was submitted to Parliament in May 2025, projecting a government deficit of 4.9% of GDP (decreased from 6.7% in 2023) and net expenditure growth of 6.1% in 2025.
- **EU Funds Implementation**: Overseeing the stalled implementation of the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) and Cohesion Policy funds, with access limited by unfulfilled rule of law conditions.
- **Fiscal Sustainability Management**: Addressing high medium-term sustainability risks driven by aging population pressures on pension spending and persistent primary deficits.
