# Ministry of Economy and Finance

## Mandate & Role

The Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), led by Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti, is the primary government body responsible for Italy's fiscal policy, public finance management, and the coordination of structural reforms. In the context of sovereign credit ratings and economic surveillance, the MEF plays a critical role in formulating and executing the national budget, managing public debt, and ensuring compliance with European Union fiscal frameworks, including the reformed Stability and Growth Pact.

Key responsibilities and roles identified in recent assessments include:
- **Fiscal Strategy and Planning**: Leading the development of the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP), which outlines the country's fiscal adjustment path and structural reform agenda. The MTFSP was submitted in October 2024 and endorsed by the EU Council in January 2025, marking the end of the Excessive Deficit Procedure. The MEF advocates for a gradual seven-year fiscal adjustment, targeting a deficit below 3% of GDP by 2026, contrasting with IMF recommendations for a more front-loaded approach to achieve a 3% primary surplus by 2027.
- **Debt Management**: Overseeing the management of Italy’s high public debt levels (135.3% of GDP at end-2024). The ministry works to mitigate debt sustainability risks by managing interest expenditures, maintaining relatively long average debt maturity, and leveraging the deep domestic bond market—supported by strong retail appetite for government bonds—to reduce reliance on foreign capital.
- **Structural Reform Coordination**: Implementing reforms aimed at improving productivity, judicial efficiency, and labor market dynamics. The MEF coordinates with other ministries, the [[Ministry of Justice]], and the [[Italian Parliament]] to ensure milestones are met for the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) and digital/green transitions.
- **Fiscal Surveillance and Transparency**: Collaborating with the independent Parliamentary Budget Office (PBO) to enhance the credibility of budgetary forecasts and improve transparency in public procurement through digitalization. The ministry also oversees tax policy administration, focusing on improving tax compliance, broadening the tax base, and addressing the tax wedge on labor to disincentivize informal employment.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP)**: The MEF prepared and submitted Italy’s MTFSP in October 2024. This plan commits to a seven-year fiscal adjustment path to ensure debt sustainability, aiming to keep net expenditure growth below recommended maximums and gradually reduce the public debt ratio.
- **Budgetary Execution and Primary Surplus**: In 2024, the MEF oversaw a fiscal outturn that resulted in a primary surplus of 0.4% of GDP and a deficit of 3.4% of GDP, the first primary surplus since 2019. This was achieved largely through the phase-out of the "Superbonus" housing renovation tax credits, energy support measures, and continued revenue efforts.
- **Engagement in Article IV Consultations**: The MEF actively engages with the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF) during Article IV consultations. In the 2025 consultation, the MEF and Italian authorities discussed the pace of fiscal consolidation, with the IMF recommending a more aggressive adjustment to firmly place debt on a downward trajectory, while the authorities emphasized the importance of growth-enhancing structural reforms and a gradual consolidation path.
- **Compliance with EU Fiscal Rules**: The ministry actively engages with the [[European Commission]] and the [[Council of the European Union]] under the European Semester process. Its adherence to fiscal rules and the successful endorsement of the MTFSP helped conclude the Excessive Deficit Procedure against Italy in January 2025.
- **Public Debt Servicing**: Maintaining a strong record of servicing sovereign debt without default. The MEF manages high interest expenditures (approx. 3.9% of GDP) and navigates refinancing risks associated with Italy’s high debt-to-GDP ratio and sensitivity to interest rate shocks.
- **Tax Policy and Compliance**: Addressing issues related to the tax wedge on labor and tax evasion. Recent assessments note improved tax compliance and a broadening of the tax base as strengths, though challenges remain in disincentivizing informal employment.
