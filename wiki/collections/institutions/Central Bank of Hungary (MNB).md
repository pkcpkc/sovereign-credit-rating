# Central Bank of Hungary (MNB)

## Mandate & Role

The Central Bank of Hungary (MNB) is the national central bank responsible for maintaining price stability and overseeing the stability of the financial system. While it maintains operational autonomy in many technical aspects, its effectiveness is significantly constrained by political pressures and state interventions. The MNB operates within a flexible exchange rate regime, which allows for adjustment but exposes the economy to volatility due to inflation differentials with the Eurozone. A key challenge for the MNB is the limited independence of its monetary policy framework, as subsidized loan schemes and administratively controlled mortgage rates distort financial market pricing and limit the bank's ability to effectively manage inflation and liquidity.

## Key Activities & Reports

- **Inflation Management**: The MNB has successfully driven a significant decline in inflation, which fell from a peak of 17.0% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2024. However, inflation picked up again in Q1 2025 due to underlying price pressures, rising food prices, and high construction costs, posing ongoing challenges to price stability.
- **Financial Sector Oversight**: The MNB supervises a banking sector that is well-capitalized (CET1 ratio of 18.8%) and highly profitable, with low non-performing loan (NPL) ratios and strong liquidity coverage. However, systemic risks persist due to the high concentration of government bonds in bank assets and the distortion of credit allocation through subsidized lending schemes.
- **Monetary Policy Constraints**: The effectiveness of MNB's monetary policy is undermined by discretionary state intervention in business transactions and administratively controlled mortgage rates. These interventions limit the transmission mechanism of monetary policy and reduce overall policy predictability.
- **Exchange Rate Regime**: The MNB manages a flexible exchange rate, which serves as a buffer for external shocks but faces volatility risks stemming from the divergence in inflation rates between Hungary and the Eurozone.
